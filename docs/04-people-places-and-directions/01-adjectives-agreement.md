# Adjectives and agreement

## The idea

An **adjective** is a word that describes a noun: "a small cat", "she is tired". You met a few in *canadien* and *canadienne*. In French an adjective **agrees** with its noun. That means it changes its ending to match the noun's gender (masculine or feminine) and number (one or several).

### The basic rule

Start from the masculine singular form, the one in the dictionary. For the feminine, add *-e*. For the plural, add *-s*.

| | masculine | feminine |
|---|---|---|
| singular | *petit* | *petite* |
| plural | *petits* | *petites* |

*Un petit chat*, *une petite ville*, *les petits chats*, *les petites villes*. Listen to the sound change. In *petit* /pəti/ the *t* is silent, and in *petite* /pətit/ you say it. The feminine ending shows you a consonant you could not hear before. The plural *-s* is silent, as in [Plurals, des and les](../02-sounds-spelling-and-everyday-things/05-plurals-and-des-les.md).

If the masculine already ends in *-e*, the feminine looks the same: *un homme triste*, *une femme triste*. Adjectives such as *jeune*, *drôle* ("funny") and *triste* ("sad") work this way. *Sympa* ("nice, friendly", short for *sympathique*) has one form for both genders and takes *-s* in the plural: *des collègues sympas*.

### Endings that change more

Some endings change a little more in the feminine. Learn each pattern with one example.

| Masculine ending | Feminine | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *-eux* | *-euse* | *heureux*, *heureuse* /øʁø/, /øʁøz/ |
| *-if* | *-ive* | *actif*, *active* |
| *-er* | *-ère* | *cher*, *chère* |
| *-on* | *-onne* | *bon*, *bonne* |
| *-el* | *-elle* | *naturel*, *naturelle* |
| *-il* | *-ille* | *gentil*, *gentille* |
| *-s* or *-t* | *-sse* or *-tte* (some) | *gros*, *grosse* |

Plurals follow the basic rule, with two extras. A masculine already ending in *-s* or *-x* does not change: *un homme heureux*, *des hommes heureux*. An ending in *-al* becomes *-aux*: *national*, *nationaux*. Adjectives ending in *-eau* take *-x*: *beau*, *beaux*.

### Where the adjective goes

Most adjectives come after the noun: *une femme intelligente*, *un café chaud*. A small group comes before the noun. A memory trick is **BAGS**, four ideas:

- Beauty: *beau*, *joli*
- Age: *jeune*, *vieux*, *nouveau*
- Goodness: *bon*, *mauvais*
- Size: *grand*, *petit*, *gros*

So you say *un grand bureau*, *une jolie photo*, *un bon ami*, *un mauvais jour*. *Long* and *court* ("long", "short") can go before or after the noun, so use them after at first.

Three of these adjectives have an extra masculine form used before a vowel sound:

| Before a consonant | Before a vowel | Feminine | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| *beau* /bo/ | *bel* /bɛl/ | *belle* /bɛl/ | *beaux*, *belles* |
| *nouveau* /nuvo/ | *nouvel* /nuvɛl/ | *nouvelle* | *nouveaux*, *nouvelles* |
| *vieux* /vjø/ | *vieil* /vjɛj/ | *vieille* | *vieux*, *vieilles* |

*Un bel hôtel*, *un nouvel employé*, *un vieil ami*. The extra form sounds like the feminine, so your mouth does not have to stop between the words. Before a vowel, a final consonant also links to the next word: *un petit ami* /œ̃ pətit ami/, *un grand ami* /œ̃ gʁɑ̃t ami/.

One more detail for later: in careful French *des* becomes *de* when an adjective comes before a plural noun. Partitive articles: du, de la, des<!-- link: ../06-habits-memories-and-food/01-partitive-articles.md --> explains it, and this lesson avoids that case.

### Adjectives after être

After *être*, the adjective matches the subject: *Elle est fatiguée*, *Ils sont contents*, *Nous sommes heureuses* (said by women). The position rules above only apply next to a noun.

## Hear it

Sana, who started a job in Toronto, describes her new colleagues to a friend (*tu*). Listen for the consonant you hear in the feminine forms: *jeune*, *sérieuse* /seʁjøz/.

```fr
Nadia: Alors, comment sont tes nouveaux collègues? | So, what are your new colleagues like?
Sana: Ils sont sympas! La patronne s'appelle Julie. Elle est jeune, mais très sérieuse. | They're nice! The boss is called Julie. She's young, but very serious.
Nadia: Et Dev? | And Dev?
Sana: Dev est un nouvel employé aussi. Il est grand, drôle et intelligent. | Dev is a new employee too. He's tall, funny and intelligent.
Nadia: Et le bureau? | And the office?
Sana: C'est un très beau bureau, mais je suis fatiguée! | It's a very nice office, but I'm tired!
```

## Try it

1. Make the adjective agree: *une femme (heureux)*, *des hommes (heureux)*, *une collègue (gentil)*, *des idées (intelligent)*.
2. Put the adjective in the right place: *un chat (petit)*, *une idée (bon)*, *des collègues (sympa)*, *un ami (vieux)*.
3. Say in French: "She is tired." "They (women) are happy." "He is tall."
4. Which form of "new" goes with *un collègue*, and which with *un employé*?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *une femme heureuse*, *des hommes heureux* (no change), *une collègue gentille*, *des idées intelligentes*.
2. *un petit chat*, *une bonne idée*, *des collègues sympas*, *un vieil ami*.
3. *Elle est fatiguée.* *Elles sont heureuses.* *Il est grand.*
4. *un nouveau collègue* (consonant sound) and *un nouvel employé* (vowel sound).

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Elle est jeune et sérieuse. | She is young and serious.
Il est grand et drôle. | He is tall and funny.
C'est un très beau bureau. | It's a very nice office.
Ils sont sympas. | They are nice.
Nous sommes fatiguées. | We are tired (women).
```

## Common mistakes

Putting every adjective before the noun, as in English. Most go after: *une femme intelligente*, not *une intelligente femme*.

Forgetting the feminine consonant. *Petit* is silent at the end, but *petite* ends in /t/, so a woman who is small is *petite*, and you say it.

Writing *beau* before a vowel. Say *un bel hôtel*, and write it the same way.

## Key terms

- **Adjective**: a word that describes a noun, such as *petit* or *heureuse*.
- **Agreement**: matching the gender and number of an adjective to its noun.
- **Masculine and feminine**: the two grammatical genders of French nouns and adjectives.
- **BAGS**: a memory trick for adjectives that go before the noun: beauty, age, goodness, size.
