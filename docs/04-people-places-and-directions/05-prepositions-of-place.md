## The idea

A **preposition** is a small word that links a noun to the rest of the sentence and often says where something is. You know *à*, *dans*, *sur* and *chez* already. This lesson adds more, and shows how they join with *le* and *les*.

### Where things are

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *dans* | in | *Le sac est dans la chambre.* |
| *sur* | on | *Le livre est sur la table.* |
| *sous* | under | *Le chat est sous la chaise.* |
| *devant* | in front of | *La voiture est devant l'hôtel.* |
| *derrière* | behind | *Le parc est derrière la banque.* |
| *entre* | between | *La pharmacie est entre la banque et le café.* |
| *à côté de* | next to | *à côté de la porte* |
| *près de* | near | *près de la maison* |
| *loin de* | far from | *loin du parc* |
| *en face de* | across from | *en face de l'hôtel* |
| *au coin de* | at the corner of | *au coin de la rue* |
| *au milieu de* | in the middle of | *au milieu du parc* |

The words that end in *de* (*à côté de*, *près de*, *loin de*, *en face de*, *au coin de*, *au milieu de*) join with the article that follows.

### De and le, de and les

*De* plus *le* becomes *du*, and *de* plus *les* becomes *des*. *De la* and *de l'* do not change.

| You think | You say |
|---|---|
| de + le parc | *du parc* |
| de + les magasins | *des magasins* |
| de + la banque | *de la banque* |
| de + l'hôtel | *de l'hôtel* |

So: *à côté du parc*, *près des magasins*, *en face de la banque*, *loin de l'hôtel*. You already saw *à* plus *le* become *au* and *à* plus *les* become *aux* in Aller and faire. This is the same idea for *de*. Watch out: *du* has another job that a later lesson explains, in Partitive articles: du, de la, des<!-- link: ../06-habits-memories-and-food/01-partitive-articles.md -->. For now, *du* only means *de* plus *le*.

### Cities, provinces and countries

For a city, use *à*: *à Montréal*, *à Toronto*, *à Québec*. To say where you are from, use *de*: *Je suis de Toronto*.

For a country or province, the preposition depends on its gender and first sound:

| Country | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| masculine, starts with a consonant | *au* (à + le) | *au Canada*, *au Maroc*, *au Québec* |
| feminine, or starts with a vowel | *en* | *en France*, *en Angleterre*, *en Inde*, *en Ontario* |
| plural | *aux* (à + les) | *aux États-Unis* |

The OQLF gives these rules for [countries](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/23897/la-syntaxe/les-prepositions/preposition-devant-un-nom/les-prepositions-devant-un-nom-de-pays) and for [provinces](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/24890/la-syntaxe/les-prepositions/preposition-devant-un-nom/les-prepositions-devant-un-nom-de-province-ou-de-territoire), with *en Ontario* and *au Québec* as examples. *L'Angleterre* is England. Two provinces are exceptions: *à l'Île-du-Prince-Édouard* and *à Terre-Neuve-et-Labrador*.

Watch out for *Québec*, which is both a province and a city. For the province, use *au Québec* (*Elle habite au Québec*). For the city, use *à Québec* like any other city (*Il travaille à Québec*). To say where you are from, *du Québec* is the province and *de Québec* is the city.

To say where you are from, use *de*, *d'*, *du* or *des*: *Je suis de Toronto*, *Elle est du Canada*, *Il est de France*, *Ils sont d'Inde*, *Nous sommes des États-Unis*. The pattern is the same as above: *du* for a masculine country, *des* for a plural one, and no article after *de* for a feminine country or a vowel.

### A Quebec note on streets

With *rue*, dictionaries give *dans la rue*. The OQLF says *sur la rue* is also common in Quebec and either choice is acceptable, while *boulevard* takes *sur* (*sur le boulevard*) ([OQLF: les prépositions devant le nom d'une voie de communication](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/25333/la-syntaxe/les-prepositions/preposition-devant-un-nom/les-prepositions-devant-le-nom-dune-voie-de-communication)). This book uses *sur la rue* in the dialogues because you will hear it all the time in Montreal, and you can say *dans la rue* too.

## Hear it

First a short description of a room, then a visitor in Montréal (*vous*) asks a passer-by where the pharmacy is. Listen for *du* in *en face du parc*.

```fr
Dans ma chambre, il y a un lit sous la fenêtre. | In my room, there is a bed under the window.
La table est à côté du lit. | The table is next to the bed.
Mon sac est sur la chaise, et le chat est sous la table. | My bag is on the chair, and the cat is under the table.

Touriste: Excusez-moi, madame. Il y a une pharmacie près d'ici? | Excuse me. Is there a pharmacy near here?
Passante: Oui. Elle est sur la rue Saint-Denis, en face du parc. | Yes. It's on rue Saint-Denis, across from the park.
Touriste: C'est loin? | Is it far?
Passante: Non. Elle est à côté de la banque, au coin de la rue. | No. It's next to the bank, at the corner of the street.
```

## Try it

1. Fill in: *Le chat est ___ la table* (under). *La pharmacie est ___ la banque et le café* (between). *Le parc est ___ l'hôtel* (behind).
2. Join the words: *à côté de + le parc*, *près de + les magasins*, *en face de + la banque*, *loin de + l'hôtel*.
3. Choose *à*, *en*, *au* or *aux*: *Elle habite ___ France.* *Il travaille ___ Montréal.* *Nous allons ___ États-Unis.* *Ils sont ___ Maroc.* *Elle habite ___ Ontario.*
4. Describe your room with two sentences using two different prepositions.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *sous*, *entre*, *derrière*.
2. *à côté du parc*, *près des magasins*, *en face de la banque*, *loin de l'hôtel*.
3. *en France*, *à Montréal*, *aux États-Unis*, *au Maroc*, *en Ontario*.
4. Example: *Mon lit est sous la fenêtre. Mon sac est sur la chaise.* Check that each sentence has a place word and the right article.

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Le livre est sur la table. | The book is on the table.
La banque est en face du parc. | The bank is across from the park.
La pharmacie est à côté de la banque. | The pharmacy is next to the bank.
J'habite à Toronto, au Canada. | I live in Toronto, in Canada.
Elle est de France. | She is from France.
```

## Common mistakes

Writing *de le parc*. French always joins them: *du parc*.

Using *à* with a country. Say *en France*, *au Canada*, *aux États-Unis*, and keep *à* for cities.

Saying *au États-Unis*. The name is plural, so it takes *aux*.

## Key terms

- **Preposition**: a small word that links a noun to the rest of the sentence, often to say where something is.
- **Contraction**: two words joined into one, such as *de* + *le* = *du*.
