# C'est or il est

<span class="tag tag-grammar">Grammar</span> <span class="tag tag-a1">A1</span>

After this lesson you can choose between *c'est* and *il est* or *elle est* to name, describe and give opinions, and tell *c'est* from *il y a*.

Before this: [Être and introducing yourself](../01-first-sounds-and-greetings/10-etre-and-introductions.md), [Adjectives and agreement](01-adjectives-agreement.md), [Avoir and il y a](../02-sounds-spelling-and-everyday-things/07-avoir-and-il-y-a.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

English uses "it is" and "he is" for many things. French splits them in two. *C'est* ("it is", "this is", "that is") **names** something or gives a general opinion. *Il est* and *elle est* **describe** a particular person or thing. You choose by what comes next.

### C'est: naming

Use *c'est* before a noun with an article, a name or a stressed pronoun:

- *C'est un livre.* *C'est le bureau de Mme Chen.*
- *C'est Julie.* *C'est moi.*

For several things or people, use *ce sont*: *Ce sont mes collègues*. In everyday speech many people also say *c'est* before a plural.

### Il est and elle est: describing

Use *il est*, *elle est*, *ils sont* or *elles sont* before an adjective that describes a particular person or thing, or before a job or nationality with no article:

- *Le bureau est grand. Il est beau.*
- *Julie est avocate. Elle est sympa.*
- *Il est médecin.* *Elle est canadienne.*

A job after *il est* has no article. If you add an adjective, you name a person and the article comes back, so you switch to *c'est*: *C'est un bon médecin*, *C'est une avocate canadienne*. Compare:

| Describing | Naming with an adjective |
|---|---|
| *Il est médecin.* | *C'est un bon médecin.* |
| *Elle est avocate.* | *C'est une très bonne avocate.* |

### C'est and a general opinion

When the opinion is about a whole idea and not a particular noun, use *c'est* and the masculine adjective: *C'est facile.* *C'est génial.* *C'est dommage.* ("That's a pity.") *Le travail est facile* uses *est* because the noun *le travail* is named. *Le travail? C'est facile* is a general comment on it.

A useful pattern is *c'est* plus an adjective plus *de* and an infinitive: *C'est difficile de trouver un appartement.*

### Il y a or c'est?

*Il y a* says that something exists or is there: *Il y a un café ici.* *C'est* tells you what something is, once you can see it: *C'est un café sympa.* You can use both in one answer: *Il y a un café. C'est un café sympa.*

### An exception

*Il est* is also used for the time: *Il est tard* ("It is late"), *Il est trois heures*. These use *il* with no particular noun behind it, which is called an **impersonal** use. You learned *il est* for the time in [Telling the time](../03-verbs-and-questions/07-telling-time.md), and it works the same way here.

## Hear it

Lucie gives M. Okafor a first-day tour of a government office in Ontario (*vous*). Listen for each *c'est* and each *il est* or *elle est*, and what follows it.

```fr
Lucie: Bonjour, monsieur Okafor. Voici votre bureau. | Hello, Mr. Okafor. Here is your office.
Okafor: Il est grand! C'est génial. | It's big! That's great.
Lucie: Et voici Mme Chen. Elle est avocate. C'est une très bonne avocate. | And here is Ms. Chen. She is a lawyer. She is a very good lawyer.
Okafor: Enchanté, madame. | Nice to meet you.
Lucie: Il y a un café gratuit ici. C'est utile! | There is free coffee here. That's useful!
Okafor: Et le travail? C'est difficile? | And the work? Is it hard?
Lucie: Non, c'est facile au début. | No, it's easy at the beginning.
Okafor: Et ce monsieur, qui est-ce? | And this gentleman, who is he?
Lucie: C'est Paul. Il est informaticien. Il est très sympa. | That's Paul. He is an IT specialist. He is very nice.
```

## Try it

1. Choose *c'est* or *il est/elle est*: *___ médecin.* *___ un médecin canadien.* *___ facile.* *La maison? ___ grande.* *___ Julie.* *___ tard.*
2. Say in French: "He is a nurse." "He is a good nurse."
3. Choose *il y a* or *c'est*: *Dans la rue, ___ un café.* *___ un café sympa.*
4. Say one thing about your own work or studies using *c'est* and one using *il est* or *elle est*.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Il est médecin.* *C'est un médecin canadien.* *C'est facile.* *Elle est grande.* *C'est Julie.* *Il est tard.*
2. *Il est infirmier.* *C'est un bon infirmier.*
3. *Dans la rue, il y a un café.* *C'est un café sympa.*
4. Example: *C'est difficile, mais c'est intéressant.* and *Mon bureau est petit. Il est joli.*

## Say it

```fr
C'est un bon médecin. | He is a good doctor.
Il est médecin. | He is a doctor.
C'est facile. | It's easy.
Elle est avocate. | She is a lawyer.
Il y a un café ici. C'est génial! | There is a café here. That's great!
```

## Common mistakes

Using *il est* before *un*, as in *il est un médecin*. When there is an article, use *c'est*.

Putting an article after *il est* with a job. Say *Il est médecin*, not *il est un médecin*.

Mixing up *il y a* and *c'est*. *Il y a* says something is there. *C'est* tells you what it is.
