# Colours, sizes and where adjectives go

<span class="tag tag-words">Words</span> <span class="tag tag-a1">A1</span>

After this lesson you can name colours and basic clothes, say a size, and shop for clothes with the right adjective order.

Before this: [Adjectives and agreement](01-adjectives-agreement.md), [Gender and articles](../02-sounds-spelling-and-everyday-things/04-gender-and-articles.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

Colours are adjectives, so they agree with the noun like the ones in the last lesson. They also follow the position rule: a colour always goes after the noun. You say *une robe rouge*, never *une rouge robe*.

### The colours

| Masculine | Feminine | Sound of the feminine |
|---|---|---|
| *rouge* | *rouge* | /ʁuʒ/ |
| *bleu* | *bleue* | /blø/ (same as masculine) |
| *vert* | *verte* | /vɛʁt/ |
| *jaune* | *jaune* | /ʒon/ |
| *noir* | *noire* | /nwaʁ/ |
| *blanc* | *blanche* | /blɑ̃ʃ/ |
| *gris* | *grise* | /gʁiz/ |
| *violet* | *violette* | /vjɔlɛt/ |
| *brun* | *brune* | /bʁyn/ |
| *rose* | *rose* | /ʁoz/ |

Listen to the feminine forms of *vert*, *gris*, *blanc* and *violet*: a consonant appears that you could not hear in the masculine, as in the last lesson. *Bleu* and *bleue* sound the same.

For the plural, add *-s*: *des robes rouges*, *des sacs bleus*. A masculine ending in *-s* does not change: *des manteaux gris*.

### Colours that never change

Some colour words are really nouns used as adjectives, for example *orange* ("an orange") and *marron* ("a chestnut"). They are **invariable**, which means they keep one form for every gender and number: *une robe orange*, *des chaussures marron*. *Brun* and *marron* both mean "brown". *Brun* agrees (*une veste brune*) and *marron* does not. The OQLF explains the rule for simple colour words: [Accord des adjectifs de couleur de forme simple](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/21721/la-grammaire/ladjectif/accord-de-ladjectif/adjectifs-de-couleur/accord-des-adjectifs-de-couleur-de-forme-simple). *Rose* is an exception and does agree: *des robes roses*.

To say "light" or "dark", add *clair* or *foncé* after the colour: *bleu clair*, *vert foncé*. The pair then stays invariable: *une veste bleu clair*, *des chaussures gris foncé*.

### Putting two adjectives together

When a noun has a BAGS adjective and a colour, the BAGS adjective goes before and the colour after: *une jolie robe bleue*, *un petit sac noir*, *un grand manteau gris foncé*.

### Clothes and sizes

Learn each noun with its article: *un manteau* (coat), *un pantalon* (pants), *une chemise* (shirt), *une robe* (dress), *une jupe* (skirt), *une veste* (jacket). *Des chaussures* (shoes) and *des bottes* (boots) are feminine plurals, so a colour after them has an *-s* when it varies.

*La taille* means "the size" of clothes. The question is *Quelle taille?*, and you answer with a size: *petite*, *moyenne* or *grande*. *Moyen* means "medium" and agrees with *taille*, which is feminine. You can also say a garment is *trop petit* or *trop grand* ("too small", "too big").

*Une couleur* is "a colour". To ask which one, say *Quelle couleur?*.

## Hear it

A customer and a clerk in a clothing shop in a Montréal mall use *vous*. Listen for *noir*, *gris foncé* and *bleu clair*, and for the price in words.

```fr
Vendeuse: Bonjour, madame. Vous cherchez quelque chose? | Hello. Are you looking for something?
Cliente: Bonjour. Oui, je cherche un manteau noir. | Hello. Yes, I'm looking for a black coat.
Vendeuse: Quelle taille? | What size?
Cliente: Moyenne, s'il vous plaît. | Medium, please.
Vendeuse: Voilà un manteau noir. Il y a aussi un manteau gris foncé. | Here is a black coat. There is also a dark grey coat.
Cliente: J'aime le gris foncé. C'est combien? | I like the dark grey. How much is it?
Vendeuse: Cent quarante dollars. | A hundred and forty dollars.
Cliente: C'est un peu cher. Vous avez une veste bleu clair? | It's a bit expensive. Do you have a light blue jacket?
Vendeuse: Oui, voilà. Elle est belle, non? Quatre-vingt-dix dollars. | Yes, here. It's nice, isn't it? Ninety dollars.
Cliente: Très bien. Je voudrais la veste, s'il vous plaît. | Very good. I'd like the jacket, please.
```

## Try it

1. Make the colour agree: *une robe (rouge)*, *des chaussures (blanc)*, *une jupe (vert)*, *des bottes (marron)*, *une chemise (violet)*.
2. Put the noun and the adjectives in order: *(bleu) (petit) sac*, *(jaune) (joli) robe*.
3. Say in French: "a dark green jacket", "light grey pants".
4. A clerk asks *Quelle taille?* You want a small coat. What do you say?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *une robe rouge*, *des chaussures blanches*, *une jupe verte*, *des bottes marron* (invariable), *une chemise violette*.
2. *un petit sac bleu*, *une jolie robe jaune*.
3. *une veste vert foncé*, *un pantalon gris clair*.
4. *Petite, s'il vous plaît.* or *Un petit, s'il vous plaît.* The first matches *taille*.

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Je cherche un manteau noir. | I'm looking for a black coat.
Quelle taille? Moyenne. | What size? Medium.
Il y a aussi une veste bleu clair. | There is also a light blue jacket.
C'est un peu cher. | It's a bit expensive.
Je voudrais la robe verte. | I'd like the green dress.
```

## Common mistakes

Putting the colour before the noun. A colour goes after: *un sac noir*.

Adding *-s* to *orange* or *marron* in the plural. They never change: *des sacs orange*.

Keeping *clair* and *foncé* separate from the colour. The pair is one unit, so *une veste bleu clair* has no *-e* on *bleu* or *clair*.
