# Asking for directions

<span class="tag tag-speak">Speak</span> <span class="tag tag-a1">A1</span>

After this lesson you can ask the way to a place, ask how far it is, and understand a short answer with *tout droit*, *à gauche*, *à droite* and *jusqu'à*.

Before this: [Prepositions of place](05-prepositions-of-place.md), [Asking questions](../03-verbs-and-questions/04-asking-questions.md), [Aller and faire](../03-verbs-and-questions/05-aller-and-faire.md), [Telling the time](../03-verbs-and-questions/07-telling-time.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

When you are lost, you ask a stranger, so you use *vous*. A few set phrases do almost all the work. Learn each one as a whole, the way you learned *je m'appelle*.

### Asking

| You say | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Excusez-moi, pour aller à la pharmacie, s'il vous plaît?* | Excuse me, how do I get to the pharmacy, please? |
| *Où est la station de métro?* | Where is the métro station? |
| *Où sont les toilettes?* | Where is the washroom? (plural in French) |
| *Il y a une banque près d'ici?* | Is there a bank near here? |
| *C'est loin?* | Is it far? |

*Pour aller à* is a chunk that means "to get to". Use *à la*, *à l'* or *au* after it, as in [Aller and faire](../03-verbs-and-questions/05-aller-and-faire.md): *pour aller au parc*, *pour aller à l'hôtel*. *Où est* uses one place, and *où sont* uses several.

### The answers: five chunks with *vous*

The person answering uses commands. A **command** (also called the **imperative**) tells someone what to do. French has special forms for this, and a later lesson covers them in The imperative<!-- link: ../07-pronouns-and-getting-things-done/04-imperative.md -->. For now, learn five words as chunks. They all end in *-ez* because the speaker is using *vous*:

| Chunk | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Continuez* | Keep going |
| *Tournez* | Turn |
| *Traversez* | Cross |
| *Prenez* | Take |
| *Allez* | Go |

*Prenez* comes from *prendre* ("to take"), which a later lesson teaches. Here you only need the chunk: *Prenez la rue Saint-Denis.* With a friend you will hear the same words without *-ez*: *tourne*, *continue*, *traverse*. They mean the same.

### Direction words

| French | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *tout droit* | straight ahead |
| *à gauche* | to the left |
| *à droite* | to the right |
| *jusqu'à* | as far as, until |
| *au feu* | at the traffic light |
| *au carrefour* | at the intersection |
| *la première rue*, *la deuxième rue* | the first street, the second street |

*Jusqu'à* plus *le* becomes *jusqu'au*, like *à* plus *le*: *jusqu'au feu*, *jusqu'à la banque*, *jusqu'à l'intersection*.

### How far?

You answer distance with minutes: *C'est à dix minutes à pied* ("It's ten minutes on foot"). Change *à pied* for *en voiture* (by car) or *en métro*: *C'est à vingt minutes en voiture*. Use the numbers from stage 2.

### Speaking in Quebec

You will also hear *les lumières* for traffic lights in Montréal. [The Quebec accent and street words](10-quebec-accent-and-street-words.md) explains that word.

## Hear it

A visitor asks a passer-by for a pharmacy and a métro station, on rue Saint-Denis in Montréal (*vous*). Listen for each direction word and for the distance.

```fr
Touriste: Excusez-moi, monsieur. Pour aller à la pharmacie, s'il vous plaît? | Excuse me, sir. How do I get to the pharmacy, please?
Passant: La pharmacie? Continuez tout droit jusqu'au feu. Tournez à droite. | The pharmacy? Keep going straight to the traffic light. Turn right.
Touriste: C'est loin? | Is it far?
Passant: Non, c'est à cinq minutes à pied. Elle est en face de la banque. | No, it's five minutes on foot. It's across from the bank.
Touriste: Merci. Et il y a une station de métro près d'ici? | Thank you. And is there a métro station near here?
Passant: Oui. Traversez la rue et prenez la deuxième rue à gauche. La station est à côté du parc. | Yes. Cross the street and take the second street on the left. The station is next to the park.
Touriste: Merci beaucoup, monsieur. Bonne journée! | Thank you very much, sir. Have a good day!
Passant: De rien. Bonne journée! | You're welcome. Have a good day!
```

## Try it

1. Say in French: "Excuse me, how do I get to the bank?" and "Is it far?"
2. Give the directions: "Keep going straight. Turn left at the light. The hotel is across from the park."
3. Say how long it takes: seven minutes on foot, fifteen minutes by car.
4. Role play: you are at a corner and need a métro station. Ask for it, then repeat back what you understood in three words.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Excusez-moi, pour aller à la banque, s'il vous plaît?* *C'est loin?*
2. *Continuez tout droit. Tournez à gauche au feu. L'hôtel est en face du parc.*
3. *C'est à sept minutes à pied.* *C'est à quinze minutes en voiture.*
4. Example: *Excusez-moi, il y a une station de métro près d'ici?* Then, for instance, *Tout droit, et à gauche.* Any three direction words from the answer are correct.

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Excusez-moi, pour aller à la pharmacie, s'il vous plaît? | Excuse me, how do I get to the pharmacy, please?
Continuez tout droit jusqu'au feu. | Keep going straight to the light.
Tournez à gauche, et tournez à droite. | Turn left, and turn right.
C'est à dix minutes à pied. | It's ten minutes on foot.
Il y a une station de métro près d'ici? | Is there a métro station near here?
```

## Common mistakes

Saying *où est les toilettes*. The plural needs *où sont*.

Adding *-ez* to a friend. Use *tourne* with *tu* and *tournez* with *vous*.

Forgetting *jusqu'au*. Say *jusqu'au feu*, not *jusqu'à le feu*.
