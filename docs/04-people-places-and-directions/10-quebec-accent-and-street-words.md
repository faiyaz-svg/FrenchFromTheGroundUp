## The idea

You learned standard sounds in stage 1. This lesson is about hearing the accent most Montrealers speak with. You do not have to copy it. You need to recognise it so that *tu* [t͡sy] and *jupe* [ʒʏp] still sound like words you know.

### The hiss: affrication

In Quebec, *t* and *d* before the sounds /i/ and /y/ (and before /j/ and /ɥ/) get a small hiss. *Tu* /ty/ sounds like [t͡sy], and *dix* /dis/ starts like [d͡z]. The OQLF says this happens inside words and becomes optional between two words ([OQLF: l'affrication](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/24467/la-prononciation/phenomenes-phonetiques/laffrication)). You heard this in stage 1, and it is normal all over Quebec.

### Loose vowels

The second feature is **laxing**. The tense vowels /i/, /y/ and /u/ become shorter, looser and more open when the syllable ends in most consonants. The OQLF writes them [ɪ], [ʏ] and [ʊ] ([OQLF: les voyelles i, ou et u](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/24527/la-prononciation/prononciation-de-certaines-lettres/prononciation-des-voyelles-phonetiques-i-ou-et-u)). Its examples include *jupe* [ʒʏp] and *soupe* [sʊp]. English has [ɪ] in "sit" and [ʊ] in "full".

| Word | Standard | Quebec |
|---|---|---|
| *jupe* (skirt) | /ʒyp/ | [ʒʏp] |
| *soupe* (soup) | /sup/ | [sʊp] |
| *ligne* (line) | /liɲ/ | [lɪɲ] |
| *limite* (limit) | /limit/ | [lɪmɪt] |

The loosening happens mainly in **closed syllables**, which end in a consonant sound such as /p t k b d g m n ɲ f s ʃ l/. The OQLF says the consonants /ʁ z v ʒ/ do not trigger it. A syllable that ends in a vowel, as in *tu* or *rue*, keeps the tense vowel. When you hear a short, open vowel in *ligne* or *jupe*, it is the same word and the same spelling. [Quebec vowels and diphthongs](../12-real-montreal/06-quebec-vowels-and-diphthongs.md) comes back to the Quebec vowel system in much more detail.

## Street words

Three words you know have simple Quebec uses. *Une rue* is a street, *un boulevard* is a wide main street, and *un coin* is a corner. *Une côte* is a street going up or down a hill. Usito, the Quebec dictionary, defines *côte* as « partie en pente d'une route » and gives it no Quebec or casual label.

Other words come up on any Montréal drive. Learn the standard form, then the word you hear.

| You hear or read | Meaning | Standard or note |
|---|---|---|
| *un stationnement* | parking lot, parking | the OQLF lists *stationnement* as preferred in Quebec and Canada and criticizes the borrowed *parking* ([OQLF](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/1298970/parc-de-stationnement)) |
| *un trottoir* | sidewalk | standard |
| *une piste cyclable* | bike path | standard |
| *une autoroute* | highway | standard |
| *un échangeur* | highway interchange | standard |
| *des travaux* | roadwork | standard |
| *un cône* | traffic cone | standard |
| *une ruelle* | back alley between buildings | Quebec and Canada; Usito says the OQLF endorses it ([Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/ruelle)) |
| *une auto*, *une voiture* | car | standard |
| *un char* | car | Quebec; Usito says it is criticized as a non-standard word for *voiture*, from English "car" ([Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/char)) |
| *une lumière* | traffic light | Quebec; Usito says it is criticized as a non-standard word for *feu*, from English "traffic light"; standard is *un feu* ([Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/lumi%C3%A8re)) |
| *un chum* | friend, boyfriend | Quebec, casual; Usito labels it familiar and says it is criticized as non-standard ([Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/chum)) |

*Un chum* can mean a friend, or a boyfriend or male partner. Context tells you which. The dictionary's criticism does not stop people using these words every day. It means you should say *voiture*, *feu* and *copain* in writing and at work, and recognise *char*, *lumière* and *chum* in speech. All three words are marked {qc} when they appear in a line below.

## Hear it

Two friends (*tu*) drive in Montréal in construction season. Listen for the three Quebec words marked {qc}: *chum*, *lumière* and *char*. Then a driver asks a clerk about parking (*vous*).

```fr
Léa: Regarde, il y a des cônes orange sur l'autoroute! | Look, there are orange cones on the highway!
Sam: Oui, il y a des travaux. On va être en retard. | Yes, there's roadwork. We're going to be late.
Léa: Pas de problème. Mon chum est déjà au restaurant. {qc} | No problem. My boyfriend is already at the restaurant.
Sam: Il y a un stationnement près du restaurant? | Is there a parking lot near the restaurant?
Léa: Oui, en face de la banque. On tourne à la prochaine lumière. {qc} | Yes, across from the bank. We turn at the next light.
Sam: Super. Mon char est petit, c'est facile. {qc} | Great. My car is small, it's easy.

Conducteur: Excusez-moi, madame. Il y a un stationnement près d'ici? | Excuse me. Is there a parking lot near here?
Employée: Oui, monsieur. Le stationnement est derrière l'hôtel. | Yes, sir. The parking lot is behind the hotel.
```

## Try it

1. Which two sounds does the hiss affect, and before which vowels?
2. Say *jupe* and *soupe* in the Quebec way, with a short, open vowel. Then say *tu* with a hiss.
3. Give the standard word for each: *char*, *lumière*, *chum*.
4. In an email to your manager, which would you write: *char* or *voiture*? Why?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. /t/ and /d/, before /i/ and /y/ (and the semi-vowels /j/ and /ɥ/).
2. [ʒʏp], [sʊp], [t͡sy]. Your vowels should be shorter and more open in the first two.
3. *voiture* or *auto*, *feu*, *copain* or *ami* (or *petit ami* for a partner).
4. *Voiture*. Both words exist, but Usito criticizes *char* as non-standard, so use *voiture* in writing.

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Il y a un stationnement derrière l'hôtel. | There is a parking lot behind the hotel.
Tournez à gauche au feu. | Turn left at the light.
Le trottoir est en face de la banque. | The sidewalk is across from the bank.
Il y a des travaux sur l'autoroute. | There is roadwork on the highway.
Une piste cyclable, s'il vous plaît? | A bike path, please?
```

## Common mistakes

Copying the Quebec vowels for every word. Keep the standard vowels in your own speech and learn to hear the others.

Writing *char*, *lumière* or *chum* in a formal text. Use *voiture*, *feu* and *copain* there.

Saying the loose vowel before /ʁ z v ʒ/. The OQLF says those consonants do not trigger the loosening, so *rouge* keeps its tense /u/.

## Key terms

- **Affrication**: the hiss that Quebec speakers add to *t* and *d* before /i/ and /y/.
- **Laxing**: making a tense vowel shorter, looser and more open, as Quebec does for /i/, /y/ and /u/ in closed syllables.
- **Closed syllable**: a syllable that ends in a consonant sound.
