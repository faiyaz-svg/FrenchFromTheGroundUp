# This, that, and moi, toi

## The idea

### This and that

A **demonstrative** is a word that points: "this book", "those chairs". French has four, and they agree with the noun like *le*, *la* and *les* do.

| | Before a noun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | *ce* | *ce vélo* |
| masculine before a vowel sound | *cet* | *cet ami*, *cet hôtel* |
| feminine | *cette* | *cette chaise* |
| plural | *ces* | *ces photos* |

*Cet* appears for the same reason as *bel* in lesson 1: two vowels would collide. *Cet* and *cette* sound the same, /sɛt/. *Ces amis* links with a /z/: /sɛz ami/.

The word means "this" or "that", and French does not choose between them. To be exact, add *-ci* ("here") or *-là* ("there") after the noun: *ce livre-ci* is the one near you, *ce livre-là* is the other. In speech, people often say *-là* even for the near one.

### Stressed pronouns

You know *je*, *tu*, *il* and the others, which sit next to a verb. French also has a second set, the **stressed pronouns**, which stand on their own or after a word like *avec*.

| Subject | Stressed | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *je* | *moi* /mwa/ | *avec moi* |
| *tu* | *toi* /twa/ | *pour toi* |
| *il* | *lui* /lɥi/ | *à lui* |
| *elle* | *elle* | *chez elle* |
| *nous* | *nous* | *avec nous* |
| *vous* | *vous* | *pour vous* |
| *ils* | *eux* /ø/ | *chez eux* |
| *elles* | *elles* | *avec elles* |

You use them in five places:

1. After a preposition (a small word such as *avec*, *pour*, *chez*, *à*): *Tu viens avec moi?*, *C'est pour toi.*
2. Alone, as an answer: *Qui? Moi.*
3. After *c'est*: *C'est lui.* In the plural: *Ce sont eux.*
4. To emphasize the subject: *Moi, j'aime ce café. Et toi?*
5. To say who owns something, with *à*: *Ce sac est à moi.*, *C'est à elle.*

Two handy patterns: *moi aussi* ("me too") and *moi non plus* ("me neither"). *Chez moi* means "at my place", and *chez toi* is "at your place". The same *chez* works with any person: *chez eux*, *chez Julie*.

### Four small words

*Même* means "same": *le même sac*. *Autre* means "other": *un autre sac*, *l'autre*. *Seul* means "alone" or "only", and it agrees: *Il est seul*, *Elle est seule*. *Un truc* is a casual word for "a thing", the way "stuff" or "thingy" is in English.

## Hear it

Two friends (*tu*) look at things at a flea market in Québec City, a market where people sell used items. Then one of them speaks to a seller (*vous*). Listen for *moi*, *toi* and *eux*, and for *ce* and *cet*. *Regarde* ("look") is a command; commands come in stage 7, so learn it as a chunk for now.

```fr
Zoé: Regarde ce vélo! Il est beau, non? | Look at this bike! It's nice, isn't it?
Malik: Oui, mais il est trop grand pour moi. | Yes, but it's too big for me.
Zoé: Et cette chaise-là? Elle est jolie. | And that chair? It's pretty.
Malik: Moi, j'aime ce livre-ci et ces photos. | I like this book and these photos.
Zoé: Moi aussi! Mais je n'aime pas ce sac. | Me too! But I don't like this bag.
Malik: Moi non plus. C'est un vieux truc. | Me neither. It's an old thing.

Zoé: Excusez-moi, madame, ce vélo est à vous? | Excuse me, is this bike yours?
Vendeuse: Non, il est à mon voisin. Moi, j'ai les livres et les sacs. | No, it's my neighbour's. I have the books and the bags.
Zoé: Ce livre, c'est combien? | How much is this book?
Vendeuse: Cinq dollars. Et ces photos sont pour vous. | Five dollars. And these photos are for you.
```

## Try it

1. Fill in *ce*, *cet*, *cette* or *ces*: *___ ami*, *___ amie*, *___ hôtel*, *___ chaises*, *___ café*.
2. Fill in the stressed pronoun: *Il habite chez ___.* (them, men) *C'est ___.* (him) *Ce sac est à ___.* (her) *Tu viens avec ___?* (me)
3. Answer with *moi aussi* or *moi non plus*: *J'aime ce café.* *Je n'aime pas ce film.*
4. Say in French: "this bike here and that bike there".

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *cet ami*, *cette amie*, *cet hôtel*, *ces chaises*, *ce café*.
2. *Il habite chez eux.* *C'est lui.* *Ce sac est à elle.* *Tu viens avec moi?*
3. *Moi aussi.* *Moi non plus.*
4. *ce vélo-ci et ce vélo-là*.

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Regarde cette photo! | Look at this photo!
Moi, j'aime ce vélo. Et toi? | I like this bike. And you?
C'est à moi. | It's mine.
Tu viens avec nous? | Are you coming with us?
Moi aussi. | Me too.
```

## Common mistakes

Writing *ce amie* or *ce hôtel*. Use *cette amie* and *cet hôtel*.

Using *je* or *tu* after a preposition. Say *avec moi*, not *avec je*.

Saying *c'est ils*. After *c'est*, use *eux* for men and *elles* for women: *ce sont eux*, *ce sont elles*.

## Key terms

- **Demonstrative**: a word that points to a noun, such as *ce*, *cette*, *ces*.
- **Stressed pronoun**: a pronoun that stands alone or follows a preposition, such as *moi* or *eux*.
- **Preposition**: a small word that links a noun to the rest of the sentence, such as *avec*, *pour*, *chez*.
