# Possessives and family

You need: nothing.

## The idea

A **possessive** is a word that says who owns or has something: "my", "your", "his". In French it is a small word in front of the noun, in the place of *le* or *un*. It agrees with the noun, not with the owner. That is the big difference from English.

### The table

| Owner | masculine | feminine | plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| my | *mon* | *ma* | *mes* |
| your (*tu*) | *ton* | *ta* | *tes* |
| his, her, its | *son* | *sa* | *ses* |
| our | *notre* | *notre* | *nos* |
| your (*vous*) | *votre* | *votre* | *vos* |
| their | *leur* | *leur* | *leurs* |

Look at the third row. *Son père* means "his father" or "her father". The word *son* agrees with *père* (masculine), and it says nothing about the owner. Context tells you whether it is his or hers. *Sa mère* is "his mother" or "her mother".

For *notre*, *votre* and *leur*, the singular has one form for both genders, and the plural takes *-s*: *notre maison*, *nos maisons*; *votre ami*, *vos amis*; *leur famille*, *leurs enfants*.

### A trap with vowels

A feminine noun that starts with a vowel sound takes *mon*, *ton* or *son*, not *ma*, *ta* or *sa*. You say *mon amie*, *ton école*, *son idée*. The reason is the same as *bel* in [Adjectives and agreement](01-adjectives-agreement.md): *ma amie* makes two vowels collide. The noun is still feminine. Write *mon amie*, with the *-e* on *amie*.

A final *n* links to a vowel: *mon ami* /mɔ̃n ami/. The plural *s* links too: *mes amis* /mɛz ami/.

### Saying who owns it with *de*

To say "Priya's brother", French puts the owner second: *le frère de Priya*. Before a vowel, *de* becomes *d'*: *la mère d'Amina*.

### Family words

| Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|
| *un père* | *une mère* |
| *un frère* | *une sœur* |
| *un fils* | *une fille* |
| *un mari* | *une femme* |
| *un grand-père* | *une grand-mère* |
| *un oncle* | *une tante* |
| *un cousin* | *une cousine* |
| *un neveu* | *une nièce* |

*Une femme* means "a woman" and also "a wife". *Une fille* means "a girl" and also "a daughter". *Des parents* means "parents" (mother and father) and also "relatives" in a wider sense, and context tells you which. *Un bébé* is masculine even for a girl. *La famille* is "the family". With *avoir* you can say how many you have: *J'ai deux frères*. After a negative, *un*, *une* and *des* become *de*: *Je n'ai pas de sœur*.

## Hear it

Priya, a new employee in Montréal, shows a family photo. First she speaks with Mme Fortin, an older colleague (*vous*). Then she speaks with Lucas, a friend (*tu*). Listen for *mon*, *ma* and *mes* at the start of each noun.

```fr
Fortin: C'est votre famille sur la photo, Priya? | Is that your family in the photo, Priya?
Priya: Oui, madame. Voici mon père, ma mère et mon frère Arjun. | Yes. Here are my father, my mother and my brother Arjun.
Fortin: Et qui est la petite fille? | And who is the little girl?
Priya: C'est ma nièce, la fille de mon frère. Elle a trois ans. | That's my niece, my brother's daughter. She is three.
Fortin: Vous avez des frères et des sœurs? | Do you have brothers and sisters?
Priya: J'ai un frère et une sœur. Ma sœur habite à Toronto. | I have a brother and a sister. My sister lives in Toronto.

Lucas: Ta famille est grande? | Is your family big?
Priya: Oui! J'ai deux oncles, trois tantes et beaucoup de cousins. | Yes! I have two uncles, three aunts and a lot of cousins.
Lucas: Tes parents habitent à Montréal aussi? | Do your parents live in Montreal too?
Priya: Non, mes parents habitent à Toronto avec leur chat. | No, my parents live in Toronto with their cat.
```

## Try it

1. Fill in the possessive: *___ père* (my), *___ amie* (my), *___ sœur* (his), *___ parents* (their), *___ enfants* (your, *vous*).
2. Say in French: "her brother", "our friends", "your (*tu*) aunt", "their house".
3. Name the relative: the brother of your father. The daughter of your aunt. The mother of your mother.
4. Say how many brothers and sisters you have, and one thing you do not have, in a negative sentence.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *mon père*, *mon amie*, *sa sœur*, *leurs parents*, *vos enfants*.
2. *son frère*, *nos amis*, *ta tante*, *leur maison*.
3. *mon oncle*, *ma cousine*, *ma grand-mère*.
4. Example: *J'ai un frère et deux sœurs. Je n'ai pas de cousin à Montréal.* Your answer is correct if it uses *de* after *pas*.

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Voici mon père et ma mère. | Here are my father and my mother.
C'est mon amie Léa. | That's my friend Léa.
Ta sœur habite à Toronto? | Does your sister live in Toronto?
Nos parents sont sympas. | Our parents are nice.
Je n'ai pas de frère. | I don't have a brother.
```

## Common mistakes

Matching the possessive to the owner as in English. *Son père* is "his father" or "her father", because *son* matches *père*.

Saying *ma amie*. Use *mon amie*, because *amie* starts with a vowel sound.

Forgetting that *un*, *une* and *des* turn into *de* after *ne ... pas*. Say *Je n'ai pas de sœur*.
