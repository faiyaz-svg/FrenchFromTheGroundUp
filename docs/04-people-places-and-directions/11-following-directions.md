# Following directions

<span class="tag tag-listen">Listen</span> <span class="tag tag-a1">A1</span>

After this lesson you can follow spoken directions in a city, pick out landmarks and distances, and write down the route in short notes.

Before this: [Asking for directions](08-asking-directions.md), [Prepositions of place](05-prepositions-of-place.md), [Metro maps and signs](09-metro-maps-and-signs.md). You need: paper and a pencil.

## The idea

When someone gives directions, they speak fast and they name landmarks. You do not need every word. You need the order: where you start, which way, how far, and what you will see at the end. This lesson trains you to catch those pieces.

### Landmarks

| French | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *une place* | a square, a plaza |
| *une église* | a church |
| *un monument* | a monument |
| *un fleuve* | a large river |
| *une montagne* | a mountain |
| *un panneau* | a sign, a road sign |
| *une côte* | a street on a hill |
| *un guide* | a guide |
| *un touriste* | a tourist |

*Un fleuve* is a river that flows into the sea. The Saint Lawrence (*le Saint-Laurent*) is the *fleuve* that runs past both Québec City and Montréal. *Une montagne* is any mountain, and in Montréal, *la montagne* also means the large hill in the middle of the city, *le mont Royal*. *Une côte* is a sloped part of a road, as in the last Quebec lesson.

The places in the dialogues below are real kinds of places, but the routes are made up for practice. Do not use them to find your way. Use a map.

### A listening plan

1. Listen once with the translation hidden, and do not write.
2. Listen again and write only the direction words in order. Use *G* for *gauche* and *D* for *droite*, and write numbers as digits.
3. Check your notes against the text.

Landmark words are usually near the end of a sentence, after the preposition: *en face de l'église*, *à côté du monument*. When you hear the preposition, listen closely to what follows.

## Hear it

A guide in Québec City talks to a tourist with a map (*vous*). *Regardez* ("look") is a *vous* command, like the chunks in the last directions lesson. Then two friends (*tu*) talk by phone: Inès tells Karim how to reach a restaurant in Montréal.

```fr
Guide: Bonjour! Regardez la carte. Nous sommes ici, devant l'église. | Hello! Look at the map. We are here, in front of the church.
Touriste: Où est le monument? | Where is the monument?
Guide: Il est au milieu de la place. Continuez tout droit, et prenez la côte à droite. | It is in the middle of the square. Keep going straight, and take the street on the right that goes up the hill.
Touriste: C'est loin? | Is it far?
Guide: Non, c'est à dix minutes à pied. Derrière la place, il y a le fleuve. | No, it's ten minutes on foot. Behind the square, there is the river.
Touriste: Il est très grand! | It's very big!
Guide: Oui. Le panneau est devant le monument. C'est utile pour les touristes. | Yes. The sign is in front of the monument. It's useful for tourists.

Inès: Allô Karim, tu es où? | Hi Karim, where are you?
Karim: Je suis devant l'église, sur la place. | I'm in front of the church, on the square.
Inès: D'accord. Tourne à gauche, et continue tout droit jusqu'au feu. | OK. Turn left, and keep going straight to the light.
Karim: Et après? | And then?
Inès: Le restaurant est au coin de la rue, en face d'un petit parc. | The restaurant is on the corner of the street, across from a small park.
Karim: Il y a un panneau rouge? | Is there a red sign?
Inès: Oui, c'est ça. | Yes, that's it.
Karim: Super, je suis tout près. À tout à l'heure! | Great, I'm very close. See you soon!
```

Replay the first dialogue and write the route in notes. A good set of notes is: *église, tout droit, côte D, 10 min, monument*.

## Try it

1. In the first dialogue, where do the speakers start?
2. Where is the monument, and on which side is the *côte*?
3. How far is it, and how do you travel?
4. In the second dialogue, what is the first direction Karim gets, and what colour is the restaurant's sign?
5. Write a route in notes from your door to the nearest park, using three direction words.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. In front of the church (*devant l'église*).
2. *Au milieu de la place.* The *côte* is on the right: *prenez la côte à droite*.
3. Ten minutes, on foot (*à dix minutes à pied*).
4. *Tourne à gauche* (turn left). The sign is red.
5. Example: *porte, G, tout droit, feu, D, parc*. Any three direction words are correct.

</details>

## Say it

Read the notes out loud as full sentences, as if you were the guide.

```fr
Nous sommes devant l'église. | We are in front of the church.
Continuez tout droit jusqu'à la place. | Keep going straight to the square.
Prenez la côte à droite. | Take the street on the right going up the hill.
Le monument est au milieu de la place. | The monument is in the middle of the square.
Le restaurant est en face du parc. | The restaurant is across from the park.
```

## Common mistakes

Trying to translate every word. Catch the direction words and landmarks first.

Mixing up *gauche* and *droite* under pressure. Write *G* and *D* beside each word as you hear it.

Hearing *place* and thinking "seat". In this lesson it means "square".

## In Quebec

The word *côte* is a normal word for a street on a hill. Usito, the Quebec dictionary, defines it as « partie en pente d'une route » ([Usito: côte](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/c%C3%B4te)), and gives it no casual label. Use it as you would in France.
