# Metro maps and signs

<span class="tag tag-read">Read</span> <span class="tag tag-a1">A1</span>

After this lesson you can read the signs in a métro station and the lines on a métro map, and say which way to go.

Before this: [Asking for directions](08-asking-directions.md), [Colours, sizes and where adjectives go](02-colours-sizes-and-position.md), [Telling the time](../03-verbs-and-questions/07-telling-time.md). You need: nothing. If you have a Montréal métro map open in another window, use it as you read.

## The idea

A métro system is built to be read quickly, so its signs use a few short words that repeat in every station. In Quebec the signs are in French. Learn the words below once and you can read most of a station.

### Words on signs

| Sign | Meaning | Where you see it |
|---|---|---|
| *Entrée* | entrance | street level |
| *Sortie* | exit | in the station, to leave |
| *Correspondance* | transfer to another line | tunnels and platforms |
| *Direction* | towards | platform |
| *Ouvert* / *Fermé* | open / closed | doors and shops |
| *Poussez* / *Tirez* | push / pull | doors |
| *Interdit* | forbidden | warnings |
| *Défense de* | it is forbidden to | warnings |
| *Les toilettes* | washrooms | a symbol and a word |
| *Un ascenseur* | elevator | a symbol and a word |
| *Un escalier* | stairs | a symbol and a word |
| *Un guichet* | ticket window | ticket area |
| *Un tourniquet* | turnstile | gates |

*Défense de* is followed by an infinitive, as in *défense de stationner* ("no parking"), and *interdit* by a noun, as in *interdit aux vélos* ("no bikes"). The two mean the same, and you can read them as "do not".

### Compass points

The four points are *le nord*, *le sud*, *l'est* and *l'ouest* (north, south, east, west). Signs in many cities say *direction nord* or give the last station of the line instead.

### Lines and directions

The Société de transport de Montréal (STM) runs the métro. As of 2026, its site lists four lines: the green line (*la ligne verte*), the orange line (*la ligne orange*), the yellow line (*la ligne jaune*) and the blue line (*la ligne bleue*) ([STM: métro](https://www.stm.info/en/info/networks/metro)). Green stations run from Angrignon to Honoré-Beaugrand. Two stations on the green line are transfer points: Lionel-Groulx connects to the orange line, and Berri-UQAM connects to the orange and yellow lines. The STM site also numbers the lines: 1 is green, 2 is orange, 4 is yellow and 5 is blue. Colours agree with the feminine noun *ligne*: *la ligne verte*, *la ligne bleue*. *Orange* never changes, and *jaune* is the same in both genders.

On a platform, *direction* is followed by the name of the last station on that line in your direction. *Direction Honoré-Beaugrand* tells you which side of the platform to wait on. You do not need to know whether that means east or west. You only need to match the name on the sign with the end station of your line on the map.

The STM site also lists the OPUS card under its fares pages. Check there for the way to pay today, because fares and cards change. Here you only need the vocabulary: *une carte OPUS*, *un titre* (a fare ticket or pass) and *un trajet* (a trip). *Un terminus* is the last station, or the end of a bus line.

## Hear it

This is a reading text, so first read it silently and then press play. It describes a trip on the green line. Find the line, the direction and the transfer.

```fr
Vous êtes à la station Angrignon, sur la ligne verte. | You are at Angrignon station, on the green line.
Vous allez à Berri-UQAM. | You are going to Berri-UQAM.
Prenez la direction Honoré-Beaugrand. | Take the direction Honoré-Beaugrand.
À Berri-UQAM, il y a une correspondance avec la ligne orange et la ligne jaune. | At Berri-UQAM, there is a transfer to the orange line and the yellow line.
Cherchez le mot Sortie. | Look for the word Sortie.
```

Now read some signs out loud. Say what each one means.

```fr
Entrée
Sortie
Correspondance, ligne orange
Direction Angrignon
Poussez
Tirez
Ouvert
Fermé
Interdit aux vélos
Défense d'entrer
Ascenseur
Les toilettes
```

## Try it

1. Match: *Poussez*, *Tirez*, *Entrée*, *Sortie* with "push", "pull", "way in", "way out".
2. A sign says *Correspondance, ligne orange*. What do you do if you want the orange line?
3. You want to go towards Angrignon on the green line. What sign do you look for?
4. Say in French: "Where is the elevator?" and "I'd like a ticket, please."

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Poussez* is push, *Tirez* is pull, *Entrée* is way in, *Sortie* is way out.
2. Follow the signs: it points to the transfer to the orange line.
3. *Direction Angrignon*.
4. *Où est l'ascenseur?* and *Un titre, s'il vous plaît.* or *Je voudrais un titre, s'il vous plaît.*

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Où est l'ascenseur? | Where is the elevator?
Où est la sortie? | Where is the exit?
Je voudrais un titre, s'il vous plaît. | I'd like a ticket, please.
Je cherche la ligne verte, direction Honoré-Beaugrand. | I'm looking for the green line, towards Honoré-Beaugrand.
La correspondance est à droite. | The transfer is on the right.
```

## Common mistakes

Confusing *poussez* and *tirez*. Both end in /e/. Remember that *poussez* sounds like "push".

Using *direction* as a place. It means "towards", so it is followed by the last station of the line.

Writing the colour wrongly after *ligne*. Say *la ligne verte*, not *la ligne vert*.
