# Indirect object pronouns

<span class="tag tag-grammar">Grammar</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can replace "to someone" with *lui*, *leur*, *me*, *te*, *nous* or *vous*, and choose between a direct and an indirect pronoun.

Before this: [Direct object pronouns](01-direct-object-pronouns.md), [Regular -er verbs](../03-verbs-and-questions/02-regular-er-verbs.md), [Regular -ir and -re verbs](../05-the-past-what-happened/01-regular-ir-and-re-verbs.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

Some verbs have a person after *à*: *je téléphone à Marc*, *elle répond à sa mère*, *il donne un livre à Léa*. The person is the **indirect object**: the one the action is aimed at, linked to the verb by *à*. In English you often say "to": "I give the book to Léa". The pronoun that replaces *à* + person is an **indirect object pronoun**.

### The pronouns

| Person | Pronoun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| to me | *me* (*m'*) | *Il me téléphone.* |
| to you (*tu*) | *te* (*t'*) | *Je te montre la photo.* |
| to him, to her | *lui* | *Je lui donne le billet.* |
| to us | *nous* | *Elle nous répond.* |
| to you (*vous*) | *vous* | *Je vous envoie un message.* |
| to them | *leur* | *Je leur téléphone.* |

Four of these are the same words as in the last lesson. Only the third person changes. *Lui* means "to him" and "to her", so it does not show gender. *Leur* means "to them" for men, women or a mix. You replace *à Marc* and *à Léa* with *lui*, and *à Marc et Léa* with *leur*.

The place is the same as for the direct pronoun: before the verb, before the infinitive, before the helper in the passé composé, with *ne ... pas* around the group: *Je lui téléphone. Je vais lui téléphoner. Je lui ai téléphoné. Je ne lui ai pas téléphoné.*

With an indirect object, the participle never changes: *Je lui ai parlé*, whether it is a man or a woman. The agreement rule from the last lesson is only for direct objects.

### Direct or indirect?

Look at what stands between the verb and the person. If there is a word *à*, the pronoun is *lui* or *leur*. If not, it is *le*, *la* or *les*.

| Verb | Pattern | Pronoun |
|---|---|---|
| *voir*, *aider*, *attendre*, *appeler* | verb + person | *Je le vois. Je l'aide. Je l'attends. Je l'appelle.* |
| *téléphoner*, *répondre*, *parler* | verb + *à* + person | *Je lui téléphone. Je lui réponds. Je lui parle.* |
| *donner*, *montrer*, *envoyer*, *prêter*, *rendre*, *expliquer*, *raconter*, *demander* | thing + *à* + person | *Je lui donne la clé. Je leur montre la ville.* |

*Appeler* and *téléphoner* both mean "to call", and the grammar is different: *Je l'appelle* but *Je lui téléphone*. Learn each verb with its pattern, as you learn its gender with a noun. *Demander* means "to ask": *Je lui demande l'heure* ("I ask him the time"). Here the person is the indirect object and the thing asked is the direct object.

### New verbs

These six verbs often come with an indirect object. *Montrer* ("to show") and *prêter* ("to lend") are regular -er verbs. *Expliquer* ("to explain") and *raconter* ("to tell a story") are too. *Rendre* ("to give back") is a regular -re verb: *je rends, tu rends, il rend, nous rendons, vous rendez, ils rendent*. *Envoyer* ("to send") is nearly regular. The *y* becomes *i* before a silent ending: *j'envoie, tu envoies, il envoie, nous envoyons, vous envoyez, ils envoient*. The participles are *montré*, *envoyé*, *rendu*.

*Dire* ("to say") also takes *à* + person, but it has its own lesson later in this stage, so it stays out for now.

## Hear it

Three friends plan a trip to Québec City. They use *tu*. Listen for *lui*, *leur* and *m'* in front of the verbs.

```fr
Inès: Qui donne les billets à Hugo? | Who gives the tickets to Hugo?
Karim: Je lui donne les billets ce soir. Je lui envoie aussi un message. | I give him the tickets tonight. I also send him a message.
Inès: Tu as parlé à Léa et à Marc? | Did you talk to Léa and Marc?
Karim: Oui, je leur ai parlé hier. Je leur ai montré l'hôtel. | Yes, I talked to them yesterday. I showed them the hotel.
Inès: Et ton ami de Québec? | And your friend from Quebec City?
Karim: Il m'a répondu ce matin. Il nous explique le chemin. | He answered me this morning. He's explaining the way to us.
Inès: Super. Je te rends l'argent demain. | Great. I'll give you the money back tomorrow.
```

Listen to *je leur ai parlé*: the group sounds like /ʒə lœʁ e paʁle/. Keep *leur* short, as one beat, and do not say a pause after it.

## Try it

1. Replace the person with *lui* or *leur*: *Je téléphone à Marc. Je donne la clé à Léa et à Sam. Elle répond à sa mère.*
2. Choose *le*, *la*, *les*, *lui* or *leur*: *Je ___ vois (Marie). Je ___ parle (Marie). Je ___ aide (mes parents). Je ___ téléphone (mes parents).*
3. Put in the passé composé, with a pronoun: *Je montre la photo à Léa. J'envoie un message à mes amis.*
4. Make it negative: *Je lui rends le livre. Je leur explique le problème.*
5. Say in French: "I'm going to send you a message." (to a friend)

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Je lui téléphone. Je leur donne la clé. Elle lui répond.*
2. *Je la vois. Je lui parle. Je les aide. Je leur téléphone.*
3. *Je lui ai montré la photo. Je leur ai envoyé un message.* (The participle does not agree with an indirect object.)
4. *Je ne lui rends pas le livre. Je ne leur explique pas le problème.*
5. *Je vais t'envoyer un message.*

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Je lui téléphone demain. | I'll call him (her) tomorrow.
Je leur montre la ville. | I'm showing them the city.
Il m'a répondu hier. | He answered me yesterday.
Je vais te rendre ton livre. | I'm going to give you back your book.
Elle nous raconte son voyage. | She tells us about her trip.
```

## Common mistakes

Using *le* or *la* for a verb with *à*: *Je la téléphone*. The verb takes *à*, so use *lui*: *Je lui téléphone*.

Using *lui* for a thing: *Je lui donne* means "I give him (her)". For a thing with *à*, you will need *y* (next lesson).

Making the participle agree with an indirect object: *Je leur ai parlés*. It stays *parlé*.

## Key terms

- **Indirect object**: the person an action is aimed at, linked to the verb by *à*.
- **Indirect object pronoun**: a pronoun that replaces *à* + a person: *me*, *te*, *lui*, *nous*, *vous*, *leur*.
