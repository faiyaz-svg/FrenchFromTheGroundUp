# At a hotel or an Airbnb

<span class="tag tag-speak">Speak</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can check in at a hotel or a rental, ask where things are, ask politely for something, and report a problem with the room.

Before this: [At the restaurant](../06-habits-memories-and-food/08-at-the-restaurant.md), [The imperative](04-imperative.md), [The pronouns y and en](03-y-and-en.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

A stay has four moments: you arrive, you ask about the basics, you ask for something, and you report anything that is wrong. The language is short and polite, and you already know most of the grammar.

### Arriving

At a hotel, the receptionist (*le réceptionniste*, *la réceptionniste*) speaks to you with *vous*. You give your booking: *J'ai une réservation au nom de Moreau.* ("I have a booking under the name Moreau.") *Au nom de* is a chunk. The desk is *la réception*. *Un séjour* is a stay, and people say *Bon séjour!* ("Enjoy your stay!") at the end of the check-in. A **traveller** is *un voyageur* or *une voyageuse*, and *un bagage* is a piece of luggage.

Rooms have *un lit double* (a double bed), *une salle de bain* (a bathroom), *un oreiller* (a pillow) and *une couverture* (a blanket). *Un étage* is a floor, and *au troisième étage* means "on the third floor". The ordinal *troisième* follows *deuxième* from stage 3.

With a private host, you hear *tu* more often, especially from younger hosts. The host says *Voici la clé* ("Here is the key") and shows you around.

### Asking for things politely

There are three polite patterns.

1. *Je voudrais* + noun: *Je voudrais une chambre calme.* *Je voudrais un oreiller.* This is a chunk from the café lesson.
2. *Pouvez-vous* + infinitive, with *vous* and a question: *Pouvez-vous m'apporter une couverture?* ("Can you bring me a blanket?") The pronoun *m'* comes before the infinitive, as you learned.
3. *Est-ce qu'il y a* + noun: *Est-ce qu'il y a un ascenseur? Est-ce que le wifi est gratuit?* With *en*: *Il y en a dans la chambre.*

*S'il vous plaît* goes at the end, and *merci* answers everything.

### Reporting a problem

To say that something does not work, use *ne ... pas marcher*: *Le wifi ne marche pas. La climatisation ne marche pas.* *La climatisation* is air conditioning. To say that a room is not as it should be, use *être* and an adjective: *La chambre est sale* ("dirty"), *la salle de bain n'est pas propre* ("clean"), *la chambre est bruyante* ("noisy"). The opposite of *bruyant* is *calme*. *Le bruit* is the noise.

The adjective agrees with the noun, as always: *un hôtel calme*, *une chambre bruyante*. After the problem, say what you want: *Pouvez-vous m'aider?* or *Vous avez une autre chambre?*

### Passwords and keys

*Le wifi* is the same word as in English, said /wifi/. *Un mot de passe* is a password, and it takes the letters and digits you hear: it helps to know the alphabet and the numbers from stages 1 and 2.

## Hear it

Chloé, 26, works at the reception of a hotel in Québec City. A guest checks in and calls later, with *vous*. Then a host and a guest in a rental use *tu*. Listen for the polite patterns and for *il y en a*.

```fr
Chloé: Bonjour, monsieur. Bienvenue à l'hôtel. | Hello, sir. Welcome to the hotel.
Voyageur: Bonjour. J'ai une réservation au nom de Moreau, pour deux nuits. | Hello. I have a booking under the name Moreau, for two nights.
Chloé: Oui, je la trouve. Une chambre avec un lit double, au troisième étage. | Yes, I see it. A room with a double bed, on the third floor.
Voyageur: Est-ce que le wifi est gratuit? | Is the wifi free?
Chloé: Oui. Le mot de passe est sur la carte de la chambre. Le déjeuner est de 7 h à 10 h. Bon séjour! | Yes. The password is on the room card. Breakfast is from 7 a.m. to 10 a.m. Enjoy your stay!

Voyageur: Allô, c'est la chambre trois cent cinq. La climatisation ne marche pas et la chambre est bruyante. | Hello, this is room 305. The air conditioning doesn't work and the room is noisy.
Chloé: Je suis désolée. Qu'est-ce que je peux faire pour vous? | I'm sorry. What can I do for you?
Voyageur: Pouvez-vous m'apporter une couverture et un oreiller? Et vous avez une chambre calme? | Can you bring me a blanket and a pillow? And do you have a quiet room?
Chloé: Oui, je vous envoie quelqu'un tout de suite. | Yes, I'm sending someone to you right away.

Hôte: Salut Alex, bienvenue! Voici la clé. | Hi Alex, welcome! Here is the key.
Alex: Merci! Le wifi, il y a un mot de passe? | Thanks! The wifi, is there a password?
Hôte: Oui, il est sur le frigo. Il y a des oreillers dans la chambre, il y en a deux. | Yes, it's on the fridge. There are pillows in the room, there are two.
Alex: Et la salle de bain? | And the bathroom?
Hôte: En face de la chambre. Ne fais pas de bruit après 22 h, s'il te plaît. | Across from the room. Don't make noise after 10 p.m., please.
```

## Try it

1. Check in: say you have a booking under your name, for three nights.
2. Ask politely: a blanket (*Pouvez-vous*), a quiet room (*Je voudrais*), and whether there is an elevator.
3. Report: the wifi does not work; the bathroom is dirty.
4. Use *en* or *y*: *Il y a des oreillers dans la chambre.* (say there are some) *Tu vas à la réception?* (answer yes)
5. Say in French: "Can you help me?" and "Enjoy your stay!"

## Common mistakes

Saying *Je veux une chambre*. It sounds blunt. Use *Je voudrais* or *Pouvez-vous*.

Forgetting the pronoun before the infinitive: *Pouvez-vous apporter moi une couverture?* Say *Pouvez-vous m'apporter une couverture?*

Writing *Le chambre est sale*. *Chambre* is feminine: *La chambre est sale.*
