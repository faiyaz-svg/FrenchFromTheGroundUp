# Direct object pronouns

<span class="tag tag-grammar">Grammar</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can replace a person or a thing with *le*, *la*, *l'*, *les*, *me*, *te*, *nous* or *vous*, put the pronoun in the right place, and know when the past participle changes.

Before this: [The passé composé with avoir](../05-the-past-what-happened/03-passe-compose-avoir.md), [Regular -er verbs](../03-verbs-and-questions/02-regular-er-verbs.md), [Saying no: negation](../03-verbs-and-questions/03-negation.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

Once you have said a noun, saying it again sounds heavy. *Nadia cherche la clé. Nadia trouve la clé.* In English we say "it". French does the same with a short word that stands in for the noun. A word that replaces a noun is a **pronoun**. This lesson covers the pronouns that replace a direct object.

### What a direct object is

The **direct object** is the person or thing that receives the action of the verb, with no word like *à* or *de* between the verb and the noun. To find it, ask "whom?" or "what?" after the verb. In *Je vois Marie*, whom do I see? Marie, so *Marie* is the direct object. In *Il prend le métro*, he takes what? The métro.

French does not always match English here. Four verbs you know take a direct object in French, where English uses a small word: *écouter* (listen to), *regarder* (look at), *chercher* (look for) and *attendre* (wait for). So you say *J'attends Marie*, with no word between the verb and *Marie*.

### The pronouns

| Person | Pronoun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| me | *me* (*m'*) | *Tu m'aides.* |
| you (one person, *tu*) | *te* (*t'*) | *Je t'appelle.* |
| him, it (masculine) | *le* (*l'*) | *Je le vois.* |
| her, it (feminine) | *la* (*l'*) | *Je la vois.* |
| us | *nous* | *Il nous attend.* |
| you (*vous*) | *vous* | *Je vous remercie.* |
| them | *les* | *Je les écoute.* |

*Le*, *la*, *me* and *te* drop their vowel before a verb that starts with a vowel or a silent *h*: *je l'aime*, *tu m'aides*, *je t'attends*. French has no separate object word for "it". *Le* means "him" or "it" for a masculine noun, and *la* means "her" or "it" for a feminine noun, so the gender of the noun you replace decides.

### Where the pronoun goes

The pronoun goes right before the verb, which is the opposite of English: *Je vois Marie* becomes *Je la vois*. Here are the three cases you need.

1. With one verb: the pronoun goes before it. *Je les regarde.*
2. With two verbs, a helper and an infinitive (*aller*, *pouvoir*, *vouloir*), the pronoun goes before the infinitive, the verb it belongs to: *Je vais l'appeler. Tu peux m'aider?*
3. In the passé composé, the pronoun goes before the helper: *Je l'ai appelée. Il nous a attendus.*

In the negative, *ne* goes before the pronoun and *pas* after the helper or the single verb: *Je ne le vois pas. Je ne l'ai pas trouvé. Je ne vais pas l'appeler.* The pronoun stays glued to its verb.

### The participle changes with a direct object before it

In stage 5 you learned that with *avoir* the participle does not agree with the subject. There is one case where it does change: when a direct object comes before the verb. A pronoun is exactly that. Then the participle agrees with the pronoun in gender and number: add *-e* for *la*, *-s* for *les*, *-es* for feminine plural.

| Sentence | Participle |
|---|---|
| *Où est le sac? Je l'ai trouvé.* | *trouvé* (masculine) |
| *Où est la clé? Je l'ai trouvée.* | *trouvée* (feminine) |
| *Les livres? Je les ai pris.* | *pris* (masculine plural) |
| *Les clés? Je les ai prises.* | *prises* (feminine plural) |

You can hear the change only when the participle ends in a consonant sound once the feminine *-e* is added: *pris* /pʁi/ and *prise* /pʁiz/, *mis* and *mise*, *fait* and *faite*. With *trouvé* and *trouvée* you hear no difference, so this is a spelling rule when you write. With *me*, *te*, *nous* and *vous* the same rule applies (*Elle m'a appelée* if the speaker is a woman), and you will see it written mostly in emails and texts.

## Hear it

Samir helps his friend Nadia move to a new apartment. They use *tu*. Listen for the short pronoun right before each verb: *les*, *la*, *l'*, *te*.

```fr
Nadia: Merci de m'aider, Samir. Tu as vu les boîtes? | Thanks for helping me, Samir. Did you see the boxes?
Samir: Oui, je les vois. Elles sont dans la cuisine. | Yes, I see them. They are in the kitchen.
Nadia: Et la table? Tu peux la prendre avec moi? | And the table? Can you take it with me?
Samir: Bien sûr. On la prend ensemble. | Of course. We take it together.
Nadia: Je cherche la clé de la voiture. Je ne la trouve pas. | I'm looking for the car key. I can't find it.
Samir: Tu l'as mise dans ton sac? | Did you put it in your bag?
Nadia: Ah oui, je l'ai trouvée! Merci. Je t'invite à manger après le déménagement. | Ah yes, I found it! Thanks. I'll treat you to a meal after the move.
Samir: Avec plaisir. Je t'attends dans la voiture. | With pleasure. I'll wait for you in the car.
```

Listen to *je l'ai mise*: the *s* of *mise* is pronounced /miz/. That sound tells you the pronoun *la* came before the verb.

## Try it

1. Replace the noun after the verb with a pronoun: *Je vois Marie. Tu aimes ce film? J'attends mes amis.*
2. Rewrite with the pronoun in the right place: *Je vais appeler Léa. Tu peux aider ta sœur?*
3. Make these negative: *Je les vois. Je l'ai trouvé.*
4. Fill in the participle ending, or leave it blank: *Les clés? Je les ai pris___. La clé? Je l'ai trouv___.*
5. Say in French: "I'm waiting for you." (to a friend)

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Je la vois. Tu l'aimes? Je les attends.*
2. *Je vais l'appeler. Tu peux l'aider?*
3. *Je ne les vois pas. Je ne l'ai pas trouvé.*
4. *Les clés? Je les ai prises. La clé? Je l'ai trouvée.*
5. *Je t'attends.*

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Je t'appelle ce soir. | I'll call you tonight.
Je vais l'aider avec le déménagement. | I'm going to help him (her) with the move.
Tu peux m'attendre? | Can you wait for me?
Je ne l'ai pas vu. | I didn't see him (it).
Les clés? Je les ai trouvées. | The keys? I found them.
```

## Common mistakes

Putting the pronoun after the verb, as English does: *Je vois la*. The pronoun goes before: *Je la vois*.

Putting the pronoun before the helper verb: *Je le vais appeler*. With *aller*, *pouvoir* and *vouloir* the pronoun goes before the infinitive: *Je vais l'appeler*.

Forgetting the agreement in writing: *Les clés? Je les ai pris*. A pronoun before the verb makes the participle agree: *Je les ai prises*.

## Key terms

- **Pronoun**: a short word that replaces a noun.
- **Direct object**: the person or thing that receives the action of the verb, with no *à* or *de* in between.
- **Object pronoun**: a pronoun that replaces an object, such as *le*, *la* or *les*.
- **Agreement of the participle**: with *avoir*, the past participle takes *-e*, *-s* or *-es* when a direct object stands before the verb.
