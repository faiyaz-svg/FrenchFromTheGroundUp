# At the doctor and the pharmacy

<span class="tag tag-speak">Speak</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can make an appointment, describe a symptom and say how long it has lasted, and understand a doctor's or pharmacist's short advice.

Before this: [The body, health and the pharmacy](06-body-health-and-pharmacy.md), [Vouloir, pouvoir and devoir](../06-habits-memories-and-food/02-vouloir-pouvoir-devoir.md), [The imperative](04-imperative.md). You need: nothing.

This lesson teaches the language of a visit. It is not medical advice.

## The idea

A visit has three moments: you book it, you explain what is wrong, and you listen to what to do. Each one uses phrases you can learn whole.

### Booking an appointment

*Je voudrais prendre un rendez-vous.* ("I'd like to make an appointment.") The person on the phone will ask when. *Vous êtes libre jeudi à 14 h 30?* and you answer *Oui, ça marche* or *Non, je suis occupé jeudi. Et vendredi?* *Une clinique* is a clinic. *Un rendez-vous* also means a date or a meeting; the situation tells you which.

### Saying what is wrong, and for how long

You already know how to say where it hurts. Now add the time. *Depuis* means "since" or "for", and it goes before a time word.

| Chunk | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *depuis hier* | since yesterday |
| *depuis lundi* | since Monday |
| *depuis ce matin* | since this morning |
| *depuis deux jours* | for two days |
| *Depuis quand?* | Since when? For how long? |

The verb after *depuis* stays in the present: *J'ai mal à la tête depuis deux jours.* ("I have had a headache for two days.") In English you use "have had", and in French you use the present because the pain is still there. Learn *depuis* + time as a chunk for now. Stage 8 explains it fully.

A few more phrases help: *Je suis tombé et j'ai mal au genou.* ("I fell and my knee hurts.") *J'ai une blessure à la main.* ("I have an injury to my hand.") *Je tousse la nuit.* ("I cough at night.")

### Understanding advice

The doctor or pharmacist often gives short commands, which you know from the imperative lesson: *Reposez-vous. Buvez de l'eau. Prenez ce médicament. Revenez dans trois jours.* The doctor may also say *Je vous donne une ordonnance* ("I'm giving you a prescription") or *C'est un symptôme de la grippe* ("It is a symptom of the flu").

*Un traitement* is a treatment. The medicine can be *un comprimé* (a tablet), *une pilule* (a pill) or *un sirop* (a syrup). *Un conseil* is a piece of advice, and it is singular in French: *un conseil*, *des conseils*. *Guérir* is "to get better, to heal" and is a regular -ir verb like *finir*: *Vous allez guérir.*

## Hear it

Anna phones a clinic to book a visit (*vous*). Then, in a walk-in clinic in Montréal, Dr. Nguyen sees a patient (*vous*). Listen for *depuis* and for the commands at the end.

```fr
Secrétaire: Clinique du Parc, bonjour. | Parc clinic, hello.
Anna: Bonjour. Je voudrais prendre un rendez-vous, s'il vous plaît. | Hello. I'd like to make an appointment, please.
Secrétaire: Vous êtes libre jeudi à 14 h 30? | Are you free Thursday at 2:30 p.m.?
Anna: Oui, ça marche. Merci beaucoup. | Yes, that works. Thank you very much.

Nguyen: Bonjour, madame. Qu'est-ce qui ne va pas? | Hello, madam. What's wrong?
Patiente: J'ai mal à la tête et je tousse depuis trois jours. | I have a headache and I've been coughing for three days.
Nguyen: Vous avez de la fièvre? | Do you have a fever?
Patiente: Oui, un peu. Je me sens très fatiguée. | Yes, a little. I feel very tired.
Nguyen: Vous prenez un médicament? | Are you taking a medicine?
Patiente: Non, rien. | No, nothing.
Nguyen: Je vous donne une ordonnance pour un sirop. Reposez-vous bien et buvez beaucoup d'eau. | I'm giving you a prescription for a syrup. Rest well and drink a lot of water.
Nguyen: Revenez dans trois jours. Vous allez guérir. | Come back in three days. You will get better.
Patiente: Merci, docteur. | Thank you, doctor.
```

*Qu'est-ce qui ne va pas?* is a chunk: learn it whole as "What's wrong?". Listen to *depuis trois jours* /dəpɥi tʁwa ʒuʁ/: the final *s* of *trois* is silent, so *trois* ends in /wa/.

## Try it

1. Say you have a stomach ache since this morning.
2. Say you cough since Monday, and that you feel tired.
3. Ask for an appointment, and answer *Vous êtes libre vendredi à 10 h?* with yes.
4. Understand the advice, then say in English what to do: *Prenez ce comprimé. Ne travaillez pas. Revenez demain.*
5. Say in French: "I fell, and my foot hurts." and "Since when?"

## Say it

You are the patient. Say your lines out loud, then change the body part and the number of days.

```fr
Je voudrais prendre un rendez-vous. | I'd like to make an appointment.
J'ai mal à la gorge depuis deux jours. | I've had a sore throat for two days.
Je me sens fatigué. | I feel tired.
Qu'est-ce que je dois faire? | What should I do?
Merci, docteur. | Thank you, doctor.
```

## Common mistakes

Using the past for something still true: *J'ai eu mal depuis deux jours*. After *depuis* the pain continues, so use the present: *J'ai mal depuis deux jours*.

Saying *des conseils* when you mean one piece of advice. One piece is *un conseil*.

Using *depuis* with a number of days but forgetting the number: *depuis jours*. Say *depuis deux jours*.
