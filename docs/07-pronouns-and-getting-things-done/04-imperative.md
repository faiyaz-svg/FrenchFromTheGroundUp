## The idea

The **imperative** (*l'impératif*) is the form of the verb you use to tell someone to do something: "Come here", "Sit down", "Don't touch". You already know five imperatives from the directions lesson: *tournez*, *continuez*, *traversez*, *allez*, *prenez*. This lesson shows how to build all of them.

### How to build it

The imperative has three forms: for *tu*, for *nous* ("let's") and for *vous*. Take the present tense and drop the subject pronoun.

1. *Tu* form: *Tu parles* becomes *Parle!* *Tu finis* becomes *Finis!* *Tu prends* becomes *Prends!*
2. *Nous* form: *Nous mangeons* becomes *Mangeons!* ("Let's eat!")
3. *Vous* form: *Vous attendez* becomes *Attendez!* This is the form for strangers, for several people, and for polite requests.

One spelling change: for -er verbs (and *aller*, *ouvrir* and *offrir*), the *tu* form loses its final *s*. *Tu parles* is *Parle!*, *tu vas* is *Va!*, *tu ouvres* is *Ouvre!* Verbs like *finir*, *prendre* and *dormir* keep the *s*: *Finis! Prends! Dors!*

Four verbs have forms of their own.

| Verb | *tu* | *nous* | *vous* |
|---|---|---|---|
| *être* | *sois* | *soyons* | *soyez* |
| *avoir* | *aie* | *ayons* | *ayez* |
| *aller* | *va* | *allons* | *allez* |
| *faire* | *fais* | *faisons* | *faites* |

*Sois à l'heure! N'aie pas peur! Faites attention!* The other forms are the regular present.

### The negative

For a negative command, put *ne* before the verb and *pas* after it, as in a normal sentence: *Ne parle pas! Ne mangez pas ici! N'ouvre pas la porte!*

### Commands with a pronoun

A pronoun in a command works differently in the positive and the negative.

In a negative command, the pronoun stays in front of the verb, as in the other sentences of this stage: *Ne le prends pas! Ne lui téléphone pas! N'y va pas!*

In a positive command, the pronoun goes after the verb, joined with a hyphen: *Prends-le! Aide-les! Téléphone-lui! Parlez-lui!* After the verb, *me* and *te* change to *moi* and *toi*: *Donne-moi la clé. Regarde-moi. Lève-toi.* This lesson uses one pronoun at a time. Two together (*Donne-le-moi*) come later.

The verbs that lose their *s* in the *tu* form get it back before *y* or *en*, so you can say it: *Vas-y!* /vazi/, *Parles-en!* /paʁlzɑ̃/, *Manges-en!*

### Reflexive verbs

With a reflexive verb (*se lever*, *se dépêcher*), the pronoun goes after the verb in the positive, with *toi*, *nous* or *vous*: *Lève-toi! Dépêchez-vous! Levons-nous!* In the negative it goes before: *Ne te lève pas! Ne vous inquiétez pas!*

*S'asseoir* ("to sit down") is irregular, so learn its commands as they are: *Assieds-toi! Asseyez-vous! Asseyons-nous!* *Se calmer* ("to calm down"): *Calme-toi!* *S'inquiéter* ("to worry"): *Ne t'inquiète pas!* The second *e* takes a grave accent in the present (*je m'inquiète*). *Bouger* ("to move") adds an *e* in the *nous* form: *Bougeons!* The *vous* form is regular: *Ne bougez pas!*

### Two politeness words

*Veuillez* + infinitive is a formal, polite request: *Veuillez attendre.* ("Please wait.") You will see it on notices and hear it in announcements. *Tiens!* and *Tenez!* mean "Here, take this": a friend says *Tiens*, a stranger gets *Tenez*. Use them when you hand something over.

## Hear it

A yoga instructor in Montréal speaks to her class with *vous*. Then two friends talk, and one gives advice with *tu*. Listen to the ending of each command and to the pronoun *toi* said right after the verb.

```fr
Professeure: Bonjour à tous. Asseyez-vous sur le tapis. | Hello everyone. Sit down on the mat.
Professeure: Fermez les yeux. Respirez lentement. Ne bougez pas. | Close your eyes. Breathe slowly. Don't move.
Professeure: Calmez-vous. Ne vous inquiétez pas. | Calm down. Don't worry.

Sana: Je suis en retard! Je n'ai pas mon manteau. | I'm late! I don't have my coat.
Amir: Dépêche-toi! Prends ton manteau, il fait froid. | Hurry! Take your coat, it's cold.
Sana: Où est ma clé? | Where is my key?
Amir: Tiens, la voilà. Ne t'inquiète pas, je t'attends. | Here, there it is. Don't worry, I'll wait for you.
Sana: Merci. Je suis prête. | Thanks. I'm ready.
Amir: Super. Allons-y! | Great. Let's go!
```

## Try it

1. Give the *tu* command and the *vous* command: *parler*, *finir*, *aller*.
2. Make each command negative: *Prends-le! Lève-toi! Téléphone-lui!*
3. Make each command positive with a pronoun: *Tu appelles Léa.* (*la*) *Vous aidez vos amis.* (*les*) *Tu me donnes la clé.*
4. Give the command for *être* (*tu*) and *avoir* (*vous*) in a sentence: *___ à l'heure!* *___ de la patience!*
5. Say in French, to a stranger: "Please wait." and "Don't worry."

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Parle! Parlez!* *Finis! Finissez!* *Va! Allez!*
2. *Ne le prends pas! Ne te lève pas! Ne lui téléphone pas!*
3. *Appelle-la! Aidez-les! Donne-moi la clé!*
4. *Sois à l'heure! Ayez de la patience!*
5. *Veuillez attendre.* *Ne vous inquiétez pas.*

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Prends-le! | Take it!
Ne t'inquiète pas. | Don't worry.
Asseyez-vous, s'il vous plaît. | Please sit down.
Vas-y! | Go ahead!
Veuillez attendre. | Please wait.
```

## Common mistakes

Keeping the *s* in the *tu* form of an -er verb: *Parles!* Say *Parle!* The *s* only comes back before *y* or *en*: *Vas-y!*

Putting the pronoun before the verb in a positive command. Put it after, with a hyphen: *Prends-le!* In the negative it goes first: *Ne le prends pas!*

Saying *Ne pas bouger*, a notice form, to a person. When you speak to someone, use *Ne bougez pas.*
