# A short booking email

<span class="tag tag-write">Write</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can write a short email of 50 to 70 words to a hotel, with a subject line, a greeting, a request, a thank you and a closing.

Before this: [At a hotel or an Airbnb](08-hotel-or-airbnb.md), [Vouloir, pouvoir and devoir](../06-habits-memories-and-food/02-vouloir-pouvoir-devoir.md). You need: a keyboard that types accents, or a pencil.

## The idea

An email to a business you do not know is written in *vous*, in neutral and polite French. It has a fixed shape, so you can write one by filling in blanks. The Quebec language office (the OQLF) gives these shapes in its writing guides, and this lesson follows them.

### The six parts

1. Subject line, with the label *Objet :* (a non-breaking space before the colon). It is short and says what the email is about: *Objet : Demande de réservation du 12 au 14 mars*.
2. Greeting. When you do not know who will read it: *Madame, Monsieur,*. The OQLF says *Madame,*, *Monsieur,* and *Bonjour,* are the most common openings, and that the unknown-reader form is *Madame, Monsieur,* ([OQLF: formules d'appel dans le courriel](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/24944/la-redaction-et-la-communication/redaction-administrative-et-commerciale/correspondance/courriel/formules-dappel-dans-le-courriel)). The next line starts with a capital letter. It discourages the exclamation mark after the greeting in administrative writing, so write *Bonjour,* and not *Bonjour!*
3. Request. Say who you are in one phrase, then what you want, with dates and numbers: *Je voudrais réserver une chambre avec un lit double pour deux nuits.*
4. Question. Use *Est-ce que* or *Pouvez-vous* with *confirmer* or *me dire*: *Pouvez-vous me confirmer le tarif?*
5. Thanks: *Merci pour votre réponse.*
6. Closing and name. The OQLF says the closing of an email is often shorter than in a letter and should fit your relationship with the reader ([OQLF: la salutation dans le courriel](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/24946/la-redaction-et-la-communication/redaction-administrative-et-commerciale/correspondance/courriel/formules-de-salutation-dans-le-courriel)). It puts *Cordialement* with the more personal closings, next to *Amicalement*, and lists *Meilleures salutations* and *Salutations distinguées* among the short formal ones. For a hotel you do not know, *Meilleures salutations* is a neutral choice. *Cordialement* is warmer, and it is also common in work emails and with someone you already write to. Put your name under it.

### Words for the request

*Disponible* means "available": *Est-ce qu'une chambre est disponible du 12 au 14 mars?* *Un tarif* is a price list or rate. *Un renseignement* is a piece of information (so *une demande de renseignement* is a request for information), and *une demande* is a request. *Confirmer* ("to confirm") is a regular -er verb. *Une pièce jointe* is an attachment: *Je vous envoie le formulaire en pièce jointe.*

Keep the tone neutral and polite, as the OQLF advises ([OQLF: rédaction des courriels](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/22794/la-redaction-et-la-communication/redaction-administrative-et-commerciale/correspondance/courriel/generalites-sur-la-redaction-du-courriel)). Use *je voudrais* and *pouvez-vous*, not *je veux*. Write dates as *du 12 au 14 mars*. Write the time as *14 h 30*.

### A model

```fr
Objet : Demande de réservation du 12 au 14 mars
Madame, Monsieur,
Je voudrais réserver une chambre avec un lit double pour deux nuits, du 12 au 14 mars. Est-ce qu'une chambre calme est disponible?
Pouvez-vous me confirmer le tarif et l'heure d'arrivée? Le wifi est-il gratuit?
Merci pour votre réponse.
Meilleures salutations,
Anna Roy
```

## Try it

1. Choose the greeting: to a hotel you do not know; to a man called Monsieur Roy; to a hotel clerk you have written to before.
2. Fix the subject line: *Réservation!!!*

## Say it

Read your email aloud twice.

```fr
Je voudrais réserver une chambre pour deux nuits. | I'd like to book a room for two nights.
Est-ce qu'une chambre est disponible? | Is a room available?
Pouvez-vous me confirmer le tarif? | Can you confirm the rate?
Merci pour votre réponse. | Thank you for your reply.
```

## Common mistakes

Using *tu* with a business: *Peux-tu me confirmer*. Use *vous*: *Pouvez-vous me confirmer*.

Writing *Bonjour!* at the top of a formal email. The OQLF discourages the exclamation mark here, so write *Bonjour,* and start the next line with a capital.

Forgetting the space before the colon in *Objet :*. French puts a non-breaking space before the colon.

## In Quebec

The OQLF calls an email *un courriel* (you met the word in stage 2). For emails to someone you know, the greeting can be *Bonjour,* with the first name. The OQLF says adding the family name to the greeting is increasingly common in emails ([OQLF: formules d'appel dans le courriel](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/24944/la-redaction-et-la-communication/redaction-administrative-et-commerciale/correspondance/courriel/formules-dappel-dans-le-courriel)).
