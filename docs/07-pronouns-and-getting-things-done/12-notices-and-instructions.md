# Notices and instructions

<span class="tag tag-read">Read</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can read a notice on a board or a sign, find what you must do and what is forbidden, and follow a short set of steps.

Before this: [Metro maps and signs](../04-people-places-and-directions/09-metro-maps-and-signs.md), [The imperative](04-imperative.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

A notice is short and has a job: it tells you something or asks you to do something. You can read most of them by checking three things: the heading, the verb form, and the numbers or dates. The notices in this lesson were written for this book. They imitate a notice board in a Montréal condo building (*un immeuble*, a building with several homes).

### The heading says the topic

*Un avis* is a notice. Under it, a heading names the topic: *Recyclage*, *Incendie*, *Jour férié*, *Règlement*. *Un jour férié* is a public holiday, a day when offices are closed. *Un règlement* is a rule, and *les règlements* are the rules of a building. *Un danger* is a danger and *un incendie* is a fire.

### The verb form says what to do

Notices use four forms for the same job.

| Form | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Imperative with *vous* | *Mettez le papier dans le bac.* | Put the paper in the bin. |
| *Merci de* + infinitive | *Merci de fermer la porte.* | Please close the door. |
| *Il est interdit de* + infinitive | *Il est interdit de fumer.* | Smoking is forbidden. |
| Infinitive alone, on signs | *Pousser. Tirer. Ne pas fumer.* | Push. Pull. Do not smoke. |

The infinitive on its own is the form of a general instruction to anyone who reads it. The OQLF notes that in administrative and commercial texts you can often choose between the imperative and the infinitive, and that the imperative has a more personal tone ([OQLF: distinction entre l'impératif et l'infinitif](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/24208/la-grammaire/le-verbe/modes/imperatif/distinction-entre-limperatif-et-linfinitif)). You already know *poussez* and *tirez* from the signs lesson. The negative infinitive puts both negative words before the verb: *Ne pas fumer.*

Two more words help. *Une consigne* is an instruction or a rule to follow, especially one for safety: *Les consignes de sécurité.* *Un mode d'emploi* is the instructions for using something.

### Numbers and dates

Check the numbers. They tell you when and how many: *après 22 h*, *du 12 au 14*, *lundi*. Read the day and the time before the rest.

### Recycling in Quebec

*Un bac* is a bin. *Le bac bleu* (the blue bin) is for recycling. Recyc-Québec says the recycling bin takes containers (except those with a deposit), packaging and printed matter ([Recyc-Québec: qu'est-ce qui va dans le bac](https://www.recyc-quebec.gouv.qc.ca/citoyens/mieux-recuperer/quest-ce-qui-va-dans-le-bac/)). *Le bac brun* (the brown bin) is for food waste, the *compost*. Recyc-Québec says more and more municipalities collect food waste with a brown bin, and that your options depend on the services your municipality offers ([Recyc-Québec: matières organiques](https://www.recyc-quebec.gouv.qc.ca/citoyens/matieres-organiques)). *Le recyclage* is recycling and *les déchets* are the waste, or the garbage.

## Hear it

The notice board is on the wall of the entrance hall. Read each notice, then play the audio and follow the line with your finger. Listen for the *vous* commands and for the numbers.

```fr
# Notice 1
Avis: Recyclage et déchets | Recycling and waste
Mettez le papier, le carton et les bouteilles dans le bac bleu. | Put paper, cardboard and bottles in the blue bin.
Mettez les déchets de cuisine dans le bac brun. | Put kitchen waste in the brown bin.

# Notice 2
Avis: Jour férié | Public holiday
Le bureau de l'immeuble est fermé lundi, jour férié. | The building office is closed on Monday, a public holiday.
En cas d'urgence, composez le 911. | In an emergency, dial 911.

# Notice 3
Avis: Incendie | Fire
En cas d'incendie, n'utilisez pas l'ascenseur. Utilisez l'escalier. | In case of fire, do not use the elevator. Use the stairs.

# Notice 4
Avis: Règlement | Rules
Il est interdit de fumer dans l'immeuble. | Smoking is forbidden in the building.
Merci de ne pas faire de bruit après 22 h. | Please don't make noise after 10 p.m.

# Mode d'emploi
Avis: Mode d'emploi de la porte d'entrée | Instructions for the front door
1. Appuyez sur le bouton. | 1. Press the button.
2. Entrez le code. | 2. Enter the code.
3. Poussez la porte. | 3. Push the door.
```

Listen to *il est interdit de* /il ɛt ɛ̃tɛʁdi də/ as one group, with a link between *est* and *interdit*.

## Try it

1. Notice 1: where do the bottles go? And the kitchen waste?
2. Notice 2: on which day is the office closed? Who do you call in an emergency?
3. Notice 3: what must you not use in a fire, and what must you use?
4. Notice 4: what is forbidden, and after what time must you be quiet?
5. Rewrite as a command with *vous*: *Ne pas utiliser l'ascenseur.* Then say in French: "Please close the door."

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. Bottles go in the blue bin, kitchen waste in the brown bin.
2. Monday. Dial 911.
3. Do not use the elevator. Use the stairs.
4. Smoking in the building is forbidden. Be quiet after 22 h.
5. *N'utilisez pas l'ascenseur.* *Merci de fermer la porte.*

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Il est interdit de fumer. | Smoking is forbidden.

Utilisez l'escalier. | Use the stairs.
Appuyez sur le bouton. | Press the button.
Mettez le papier dans le bac bleu. | Put the paper in the blue bin.
```

## Common mistakes

Reading *Ne pas fumer* as a question or a statement about a person. It is an instruction to anyone who reads it.

Using *vous* commands for a friend: *Mettez le papier dans le bac* is for a notice. To a friend, say *Mets le papier dans le bac.*

Mixing up *les déchets* (waste) with *le recyclage* (recycling). They go in different bins.

## In Quebec

*Bac brun* is the name Recyc-Québec uses for the food waste bin. Its own page on sorting says *bac de récupération* for the recycling bin, and this book uses the everyday name *bac bleu*. What your municipality collects depends on its services, so check your city's page. Signs that forbid something often start with *Défense de* or *Interdit*, as you saw in the signs lesson.
