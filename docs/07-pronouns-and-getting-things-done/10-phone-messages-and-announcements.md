# Phone messages and announcements

<span class="tag tag-listen">Listen</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can follow a recorded phone menu, understand a voicemail, catch the key details of an airport announcement (flight, place, time), and leave a short message.

Before this: [The imperative](04-imperative.md), [Hearing times and plans](../03-verbs-and-questions/11-hearing-times-and-plans.md). You need: headphones if you have them, and a pencil.

## The idea

A recorded voice goes by once, and you cannot ask it to repeat. The good news is that these messages are built from fixed phrases. If you know the frame, you only need to catch the new details: a number to press, a time, a gate.

### The phone menu

A menu uses the *vous* imperative to tell you what to do. Each choice has the same shape: *Pour* + what you want, then a command + *le* + number. *Pour prendre un rendez-vous, appuyez sur le 1.* ("To make an appointment, press 1.") *Pour une urgence, composez le 911.* *Appuyer* is "to press" and *composer* is "to dial". *Patientez* means "wait" and comes from *patienter*. *Veuillez patienter* is the polite form from the imperative lesson. *Une annonce* is any recorded or spoken announcement, and *un appel* is a call.

Other phrases return in many menus. *Vous avez joint la clinique* means "You have reached the clinic" (the verb is *joindre*, and you only need to recognise it). *Nous ne pouvons pas répondre* means "We cannot answer". *Laissez un message* is "Leave a message". *Nous vous rappelons* is "We will call you back": *rappeler* means "to call back". A voicemail is on *un répondeur*, an answering machine.

### Leaving a message

When you leave a message, keep it in the order name, reason, number, request: *Bonjour, c'est Anna Roy. Je voudrais prendre un rendez-vous. Mon numéro est le ___. Rappelez-moi, s'il vous plaît.* Say digits in pairs or one by one, as you practised in stage 2. With a friend, use *tu*: *Rappelle-moi ce soir.*

### Airport announcements

An announcement starts with *Mesdames et messieurs* ("Ladies and gentlemen"). Then it gives a flight number, a place and a time. *Un vol* is a flight, *un passager* is a passenger and *l'embarquement* (m.) is boarding. A frame to know: *Les passagers du vol 452 à destination de Toronto sont priés de se rendre à la porte 12.* ("Passengers for flight 452 to Toronto are asked to go to gate 12.") *Sont priés de* means "are asked to", and you only need to recognise it. A delay is *un retard*: *Il y a un retard de vingt minutes.*

The speaker reads slowly, but a hall adds echo, so listen for numbers first. Write them down as you hear them: 452, 12, 14 h 30.

### How to listen

1. Play the message once with no text. Write down every number you catch.
2. Play it again. Listen for the verb in the imperative (*appuyez*, *composez*, *patientez*, *laissez*) and write the number that follows.
3. Play it a third time with the translation. Check your numbers.
4. Say the key sentence after the voice.

All the messages below were written for this book. They are examples of the usual frames, not real clinic or airport texts.

## Hear it

The first message is a clinic's recorded menu, and the second is a message from a friend. The third and fourth are airport announcements. Listen for the number after *appuyez sur le*, for the verbs in the imperative, and for the flight and gate numbers.

```fr
Répondeur: Vous avez joint la clinique du Parc. Nous ne pouvons pas répondre pour le moment. | You have reached the Parc clinic. We cannot answer right now.
Répondeur: Pour prendre un rendez-vous, appuyez sur le 1. Pour une question sur un médicament, appuyez sur le 2. | To make an appointment, press 1. For a question about a medicine, press 2.
Répondeur: Pour une urgence, composez le 911. Pour laisser un message, veuillez patienter. | For an emergency, dial 911. To leave a message, please wait.

Marc: Salut Léa, c'est Marc. Je t'appelle pour samedi. Rappelle-moi ce soir, s'il te plaît. À plus tard. | Hi Léa, it's Marc. I'm calling about Saturday. Call me back tonight, please. See you later.

Annonce: Mesdames et messieurs, les passagers du vol quatre cent cinquante-deux à destination de Toronto sont priés de se rendre à la porte douze. | Ladies and gentlemen, passengers for flight 452 to Toronto are asked to go to gate 12.
Annonce: L'embarquement commence à quatorze heures trente. | Boarding starts at 2:30 p.m.

Annonce: Mesdames et messieurs, il y a un retard de vingt minutes pour le vol deux cent dix à destination d'Ottawa. Merci de patienter. | Ladies and gentlemen, there is a 20-minute delay for flight 210 to Ottawa. Thank you for waiting.
```

## Try it

1. In the clinic menu, which number do you press for an appointment? And for a question about a medicine?
2. What number do you dial for an emergency?
3. In Marc's message, what does he ask Léa to do, and when?
4. In the first announcement: flight number, city, gate and boarding time.
5. In the last announcement: which city, which flight, and how long is the delay?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. 1 for an appointment, 2 for a question about a medicine.
2. 911.
3. He asks her to call him back (*rappelle-moi*) tonight (*ce soir*).
4. Flight 452, Toronto, gate 12, 14 h 30.
5. Ottawa, flight 210, 20 minutes.

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Je voudrais laisser un message. | I'd like to leave a message.
Rappelez-moi, s'il vous plaît. | Please call me back.
Pour prendre un rendez-vous, appuyez sur le 1. | To make an appointment, press 1.
Veuillez patienter. | Please wait.
Le vol est à quatorze heures trente. | The flight is at 2:30 p.m.
```

## Common mistakes

Saying *Rappelez moi* in writing, without a hyphen. In a positive command, the pronoun is joined by a hyphen: *rappelez-moi*.

Mixing up *rappeler* ("to call back") and *se rappeler* ("to remember"), which looks the same. *Se rappeler* is reflexive.

Writing *Appuyez le 1*. You press *on* the number: *appuyez sur le 1*.

## In Quebec

A phone menu may begin with a choice of language, for example *Pour le service en français, faites le 1.* ("For service in French, press 1.") This sample was written for this book. *Faites le 1* means "press 1", and you will hear *faites* ("do, make") used this way in French.
