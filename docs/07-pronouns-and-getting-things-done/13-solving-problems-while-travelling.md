# Solving problems while travelling

<span class="tag tag-speak">Speak</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span> <span class="tag tag-review">Review</span>

After this lesson you can explain a travel problem (a missed train, a broken heater, a lost or stolen bag), ask for a fix politely, and understand the answer, using everything from this stage.

Before this: [Direct object pronouns](01-direct-object-pronouns.md), [The pronouns y and en](03-y-and-en.md), [The imperative](04-imperative.md), [At the doctor and the pharmacy](07-doctor-and-pharmacy.md), [At a hotel or an Airbnb](08-hotel-or-airbnb.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

This lesson has no new grammar. It brings back the stage in three scenes, and you play one side of each. A problem at a desk always has three steps, and each step uses a phrase you already know.

1. Say what happened. *J'ai raté mon train.* *Le chauffage est en panne.* *On m'a volé mon portefeuille.*
2. Say what you want. *Je voudrais changer mon billet.* *Pouvez-vous le réparer?* *Vous avez une autre chambre?*
3. Understand and answer. The agent gives a time, a number or a command: *Il y a un autre train à 16 h 15.* *Je vous donne la clé.* *Annulez votre carte.*

### Words for what went wrong

*Une panne* is a breakdown, and *en panne* means "out of order": *Le chauffage est en panne.* *Le chauffage* is the heating. *Cassé* (broken), *perdu* (lost) and *volé* (stolen) are participles used as adjectives. They agree with the noun: *une valise perdue*, *un sac volé*. *Une valise* is a suitcase. *On m'a volé mon sac* means "Someone stole my bag". *On* here means "someone" and *m'* is the indirect pronoun from lesson 2.

### Words for what you ask

*Réparer* is "to repair", *remplacer* is "to replace", *changer* is "to change" and *annuler* is "to cancel". All four are regular -er verbs. *Un échange* is an exchange: *Est-ce qu'un échange est possible?* Use the pronouns from this stage to avoid repeating the noun: *Pouvez-vous le réparer?* (the heater), *Je l'ai perdue.* (the suitcase), *Je voudrais la changer.* *Il y en a un autre?* ("Is there another one?")

## Hear it

Three scenes. In the first, Lucie, who is 40, missed her train in Ottawa and talks to a ticket agent (*vous*). In the second, a hotel guest calls the reception about the heater (*vous*). In the third, two friends talk about a stolen wallet (*tu*). Listen for the pronoun before each verb.

```fr
Lucie: Bonjour. J'ai raté mon train pour Montréal. | Hello. I missed my train to Montreal.
Agent: Quel train? | Which train?
Lucie: Le train de 14 h 30. Voici mon billet. Est-ce qu'un échange est possible? | The 2:30 p.m. train. Here is my ticket. Is an exchange possible?
Agent: Oui. Il y a un autre train à 16 h 15. | Yes. There is another train at 4:15 p.m.
Lucie: Parfait, je le prends. Je voudrais changer mon billet. | Great, I'll take it. I'd like to change my ticket.
Agent: Pas de problème. Le train part du quai trois. | No problem. The train leaves from platform three.

Client: Allô, c'est la chambre quatre cent douze. Le chauffage est en panne. Il fait très froid. | Hello, this is room 412. The heating is out of order. It is very cold.
Réception: Je suis désolée. Pouvez-vous patienter? Quelqu'un arrive dans dix minutes. | I'm sorry. Can you wait? Someone is coming in ten minutes.
Client: Pouvez-vous le réparer ce soir? Ou vous avez une autre chambre? | Can you repair it tonight? Or do you have another room?
Réception: Oui, la trois cent cinq. Je vous donne la clé. | Yes, number 305. I'm giving you the key.
Client: Merci. Je peux y aller tout de suite? | Thanks. Can I go there right away?

Amir: Sana, on m'a volé mon portefeuille dans le métro! | Sana, someone stole my wallet in the metro!
Sana: Ne t'inquiète pas. Va voir un agent. | Don't worry. Go and see an officer.
Amir: J'ai perdu ma carte de débit aussi. | I lost my debit card too.
Sana: Appelle ta banque. Annule ta carte. Je t'accompagne. | Call your bank. Cancel your card. I'll go with you.
```

## Try it

1. Say you missed your train, and ask whether an exchange is possible.
2. Say the heater is out of order and ask to repair it with a pronoun (*Pouvez-vous* + *le*).
3. Answer *Vous avez perdu votre valise?* with a pronoun, in the passé composé (feminine).
4. Tell a friend: someone stole your bag; call the bank (command).
5. Ask whether there is another train, and answer with *en*.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *J'ai raté mon train. Est-ce qu'un échange est possible?*
2. *Le chauffage est en panne. Pouvez-vous le réparer?*
3. *Oui, je l'ai perdue.* (*Valise* is feminine, and *la* comes before the verb, so *perdue*.)
4. *On m'a volé mon sac. Appelle la banque.*
5. *Il y a un autre train? Oui, il y en a un.*

</details>

## Say it

Pick a scene and play the traveller out loud. Say all of the traveller's lines, then change the problem.

```fr
J'ai raté mon train. | I missed my train.
Est-ce qu'un échange est possible? | Is an exchange possible?
Le chauffage est en panne. | The heating is out of order.
Pouvez-vous le réparer? | Can you repair it?
On m'a volé mon sac. | Someone stole my bag.
```

## Common mistakes

Using *je* with *volé*: *J'ai volé mon sac* means "I stole my bag". Use *on m'a volé mon sac* for "someone stole my bag".

Forgetting the pronoun and repeating the noun: *Pouvez-vous réparer le chauffage?* is correct but heavy. In speech, *Pouvez-vous le réparer?* is more natural.

Writing *en panne* with an adjective ending: *en pannes*. The phrase does not change.

## Key terms
