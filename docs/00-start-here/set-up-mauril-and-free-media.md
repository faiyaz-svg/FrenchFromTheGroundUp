# Set up Mauril and free media

Listening is 6 minutes of every routine. This page names the main free sources, starting with Mauril, and says how to use them at each level. The full list is on the [media page](../reference/media.md).

## Mauril

[Mauril](https://mauril.ca/fr/) is a free app and website from CBC and Radio-Canada, funded by the Government of Canada. It has short clips from Radio-Canada and CBC with comprehension quizzes. As of 2026 it has 8 levels, which Mauril maps to the CEFR like this:

| Mauril level | CEFR level |
|---|---|
| Débutant 1 and 2 | A1 |
| Débutant 3 and 4 | A2 |
| Intermédiaire 1 and 2 | B1 |
| Avancé 1 and 2 | B2 |

Mauril is designed for people in Canada, and its content is limited to Canada, so it may not work from abroad. Open the [web app](https://app.mauril.ca/) or install the app on your phone, take the placement test if you want a starting point, then stay one level below what feels comfortable. Easy listening, done daily, builds more than hard listening done twice a week.

## Listen and watch at this level

Each stage's index page ends with a section named "Listen and watch at this level". It has 3 to 5 sources for that stage, Quebec French first, each with one line on what to do with it. Pick one item and stay with it for the stage. Changing source every day costs you time finding something.

Catalogues change, and some titles are free only for a while. Each list was checked in November 2026. If a link fails or a title has a paid label, use another item from the same list, or from the media page.

## How to listen at each level

At A1, you will not follow most of what is said. That is fine. Pick a short clip (1 to 3 minutes) and play it three times: once for the mood, once for words you know, once with the French subtitles on. Repeat one phrase out loud.

At A2, use French subtitles and play the clip once without them first. Pause after a sentence you almost caught and replay it at slow speed if the player has that setting.

At B1, listen once without any text, write down three things you understood, then listen again with subtitles. Look up no more than five words per session, or listening turns into dictionary work.

At B2, listen to a full episode of 15 to 30 minutes, then retell it in 2 minutes out loud. Switch between Quebec and France sources to keep both accents familiar.

At every level, repeat a clip you liked a few days later. The second hearing is where you notice how much you gained.
