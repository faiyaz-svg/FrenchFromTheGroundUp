# The 30-minute routine

Every lesson has a practice routine that fills 30 minutes. You repeat it on several days, not once. This page explains the default routine, why each part is there and what to do on a busy day.

## The default routine

| Minutes | Task | What to listen for |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Anki: today's cards. Say each French word before you flip | One thing per card: the vowel, the silent final letter |
| 12 | The new lesson: read it, then do Try it | Where you hesitate |
| 6 | Listening: the stage's media list, or Mauril | One sound or one word the lesson named |
| 7 | Speaking out loud: shadow the Hear it lines, then do Say it | Your rhythm against the audio |

The minutes add up to 30. Each lesson's own table names the one thing to listen for in each row.

Two things change the table:

- Writing lessons take 4 minutes from listening or speaking and give them to the writing task.
- From B1 (stage 9), the lesson block is 10 minutes, and the 2 minutes saved go to extra reading or listening.

## Why each part is there

Anki (5 minutes) keeps old words alive. A word you saw once last week is gone by next week unless you meet it again, and the cards show it to you shortly before you would forget it.

The lesson (12 minutes) is the only part where something new arrives. Twelve minutes is short on purpose: you read one idea, try it and stop. Reading the same lesson for 40 minutes teaches less than reading it for 12 and using it for four days.

Listening (6 minutes) trains your ear on real speech. Your ear is slower than your eye in French, because the spelling hides sounds. The stage media list gives you sources at the right level, and [set up Mauril and free media](set-up-mauril-and-free-media.md) explains how to use them.

Speaking out loud (7 minutes) is the part people skip. Say the lines with the audio first (shadowing), then alone. Saying a sentence moves it from something you recognise to something you can produce.

## How many days per lesson

Plan on about 5 days per lesson. The first day is a read and a first try. On days 2 to 4 you repeat the routine and the Say it lines. On day 5 you check the Move on when boxes.

Repeat the routine on at least three days before you tick the boxes. A lesson that feels done after one day is usually not done: you recognise the page, and you can't yet say or hear the thing without it.

Some lessons need fewer days (a review lesson you already know) and some need more (the nasal vowels, the subjunctive). Let the boxes decide, not the calendar.

## Using the checklist boxes

Each box is a test you can run: a score on a drill, a line said at normal speed with no stops, a count. Run the test, and tick the box only if you pass it. If you miss one box, repeat only the part of the routine that trains it, then test again.

If you fail the same box three days in a row, go back one lesson, read its Common mistakes section and return. The cause is usually in an earlier lesson.

## Busy days

When you have less time, keep the order of importance: Anki first, then speaking, then listening, then the lesson.

- 15 minutes: Anki (5) and Say it (7), then 3 minutes of listening.
- 10 minutes: Anki (5) and one line of Say it said five times.
- 5 minutes: Anki only. Do not skip it for more than two days, or the pile of cards grows.

A short day still counts toward the five days. If you miss a day, start the next one where you stopped. Don't double up the next day.
