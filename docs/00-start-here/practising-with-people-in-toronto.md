# Practising with people in Toronto

The book trains your ears and mouth alone. Talking with people is where it pays off, and Toronto has several ways to do it. Everything here was checked in November 2026 (as of 2026), but schedules, prices and venues change often, so check each page before you go.

## Places to try

| What | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| [Toronto Metropolitan University French Conversation Table](https://www.torontomu.ca/tables) | No cost listed | A drop-in table for all French levels, led by a language facilitator. The page lists it for students, alumni, staff and faculty. Ask the university whether visitors can join. Check the dates: the fall 2026 sessions listed ended on December 2 |
| [Le French Meetup de Toronto](https://www.meetup.com/frenchmeetup/) | CA$3 per event, plus your own drinks | Evenings in French at pubs and cafés, all levels, and some online evenings. Check the dates on the page |
| [Diskuto](https://www.meetup.com/diskuto/) | Free, you buy something at the café | French conversation at a café near St. George station, all levels, listed for Thursday evenings. Check the dates and the venue |
| [Alliance Française de Toronto](https://www.alliance-francaise.ca/en/) | Courses are paid. Prices are not listed on the home page | Adult courses from A1 to B2, tests and cultural events. A free conversation club was not found, so ask them |
| [Toronto Public Library](https://tpl.ca/) | Free with a library card | Search the site for French learning resources, French books and e-books. Check which learning apps it offers now |

The library card is free for people who live, work or study in Toronto. Your public library may also lend graded readers (short books written for learners).

## Online language exchange

An exchange pairs two people who each want to learn the other's language. You speak French for half the time and English for the other half. Free apps and sites for this exist, and so do paid tutors on marketplaces. This book does not recommend one. If you try one, check its price, its privacy terms and how it treats new members, and meet in the app's own call tool first.

Meetup groups also run online exchanges. The French Meetup listed an online French and Spanish exchange evening in 2026.

## Starting a conversation as a beginner

You do not need a full sentence. Start with a greeting, say you are learning, and ask the other person to slow down. People are usually glad to help. Say the lines out loud before you go, and use the mic button when you are on the site.

```fr
Vous: Bonjour! Je m'appelle Sam. Je suis nouveau ici. | Hello! My name is Sam. I'm new here.
Vous: Je parle un peu français. | I speak a little French.
Vous: Vous pouvez parler plus lentement, s'il vous plaît? | Can you speak more slowly, please?
Vous: Pardon, je ne comprends pas. | Sorry, I don't understand.
Vous: Comment dit-on ça en français? | How do you say this in French?
Vous: Merci beaucoup, c'était bien! | Thank you very much, that was good!
```

Plan the first minute and stop when you are tired. Twenty minutes of real talk is a good session at A1. After each one, write down two words or phrases you wanted and did not have, and add them to Anki.
