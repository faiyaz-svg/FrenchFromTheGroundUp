# How to use this book

This book takes you from no French to CEFR level B2 (the level where you can follow most news and meetings and write a clear email). You study about 30 minutes a day. This page says what the book covers, how a lesson works and how long the whole thing takes.

## What the book covers

The book teaches standard French for speaking and writing. It also teaches you to understand Quebec French from the first stage: the accent, everyday words and casual speech. The page [standard and Quebec French](standard-vs-quebec-french.md) explains the choice.

It covers listening, speaking, reading and writing for daily life, travel, work and government French. It does not teach literature. There are no novels, poems or essays on literary analysis. If you want those later, B2 is a good point to start them.

It is not a replacement for talking with people. The book gives you the words and the habits, and [practising with people in Toronto](practising-with-people-in-toronto.md) lists ways to use them.

## Four lines and 16 stages

The map on the home page has four lines, one for each level from A1 to B2. Each line holds four stages, so there are 16 stages. Each stage has about 12 lessons, and the book has 205 lessons in all. Each lesson is a station on the map.

| Line | Level | Stages | What you can do at the end |
|---|---|---|---|
| Green | A1 | 1 to 4 | Greet people, order a coffee, ask for directions, introduce yourself |
| Orange | A2 | 5 to 8 | Talk about the past, habits and food, handle simple tasks and a visit to Quebec |
| Yellow | B1 | 9 to 12 | Talk about work and plans, give opinions, follow real Montreal speech |
| Blue | B2 | 13 to 16 | Argue a point, write formal emails, understand news and debates |

[Levels and goals](levels-and-goals.md) says what each level means in plain words and how it compares with job requirements.

## How a lesson works

Each lesson is a short read of 10 to 15 minutes, followed by a routine you repeat over several days.

1. **Read.** The lesson explains one idea. Every French line has a play button, so you hear it before you study it.
2. **Try it.** A few exercises, each with a closed answer box. Try first, then open the answer.
3. **Say it.** Lines to say out loud. The mic button listens and shows which words it heard.
4. **The routine.** A table with the 30-minute plan for the lesson: cards, the lesson, listening and speaking. [The 30-minute routine](the-30-minute-routine.md) explains each part.
5. **Move on when.** A short checklist. When every box is true, go to the next lesson.

Each lesson ends with a New words table. Those words become the Anki cards for the stage.

## Audio, voices and the English button

Every French line in a box on the page has a play button, and italic French words in a sentence can be clicked to hear them. The site uses your browser's voices, so the sound depends on your browser and your computer. The Voice control in the page header lets you pick a Quebec or France accent and a slow speed. [Set up voices and audio](set-up-voices-and-audio.md) walks through it. Do this before lesson 1.

From stage 11, the explanations are in French. Each French paragraph has an English version behind a button at the top of the page. The button shows "English" and then "Français" to switch back. Try the French first, then check.

## Practice and reference pages

The Practice section has drills that run in the browser: conjugation, le or la, fill in the blank, dictation, numbers, Quebec words and Say it. Use them when a Move on when box asks for a score. See the [practice page](../practice/index.md).

The Reference section holds the glossary, common mistakes, a media list and other lookup pages. Start at the [reference index](../reference/index.md).

## Progress

The [progress page](../progress.md) lists every lesson with a box. Tick a lesson when its Move on when boxes are true. On the website, ticks are saved in the browser you use, and the stations on the home map fill in. In VS Code, change `[ ]` to `[x]` in `docs/progress.md`.

## How long it takes

B2 is commonly estimated at about 550 to 650 hours of study in total. These are rough figures from language schools, and people vary a lot.

The book has 205 lessons. At about 5 days per lesson and 30 minutes a day, that is 1 025 days, or about 2.8 years. That is 512 hours of the routine, so the extra listening and conversation you do on top is what closes the gap to the commonly cited range. More listening each day shortens the time, and so does finishing a lesson in fewer days when the boxes are already true.

## Language of the book

Stages 1 to 8 are in English, with French in the boxes, tables and italics. Stages 9 and 10 have French instructions and headings, with English beside new ideas. From stage 11, explanations are in French with an English button. Reading French about French is part of reaching B1 and B2, so the switch is on purpose.
