# Vouloir, pouvoir and devoir

<span class="tag tag-grammar">Grammar</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can say what you want, what you can do and what you must do with *vouloir*, *pouvoir* and *devoir*, say "it is necessary" with *il faut*, and say that you could not, wanted to or had to do something in the past.

Before this: [Making plans](../03-verbs-and-questions/08-making-plans.md), [Saying no: negation](../03-verbs-and-questions/03-negation.md), [Irregular past participles](../05-the-past-what-happened/04-irregular-participles.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

In [Making plans](../03-verbs-and-questions/08-making-plans.md) you learned phrases such as *tu veux*, *on peut* and *je ne peux pas* as whole chunks. They come from three irregular verbs. A verb is **irregular** when its forms do not follow the usual pattern, so you learn each form. These three are called **modal verbs** (*les verbes modaux*) here: each one is followed by a second verb in the infinitive, the form that ends in *-er*, *-ir* or *-re*, as in *vouloir* or *manger*. The first verb changes with the subject. The second one stays in the infinitive.

*Je veux manger.* ("I want to eat.") *Tu peux venir.* ("You can come.") *Nous devons partir.* ("We have to leave.")

### The three verbs in the present

| Pronoun | *vouloir* (to want) | *pouvoir* (to be able to) | *devoir* (to have to) |
|---|---|---|---|
| *je* | *veux* | *peux* | *dois* |
| *tu* | *veux* | *peux* | *dois* |
| *il / elle / on* | *veut* | *peut* | *doit* |
| *nous* | *voulons* | *pouvons* | *devons* |
| *vous* | *voulez* | *pouvez* | *devez* |
| *ils / elles* | *veulent* | *peuvent* | *doivent* |

Listen for the pattern. *Je veux*, *tu veux* and *il veut* sound the same, /vø/, and so do *je peux*, *tu peux* and *il peut*, /pø/. The plural *ils veulent* /vœl/ and *ils peuvent* /pœv/ have the open vowel from *peur*, and the final *-ent* is silent. The *nous* and *vous* forms have the stem of the infinitive (*voul-*, *pouv-*, *dev-*).

### What each verb means

*Vouloir* is "to want": *Je veux un café.* *Tu veux venir?* *Pouvoir* is "to be able to", so it means both "can" and "may": *Je peux entrer?* ("May I come in?") *Devoir* is "to have to": *Je dois travailler.* Used with a noun it means "to owe": *Je dois dix dollars à Léa.* Used with a probable fact it means "must be": *Il doit être fatigué* ("He must be tired").

*Je voudrais* ("I would like") is the polite form of *je veux*. You know it from the café. The rule behind it comes in [The conditionnel for politeness](../09-work-and-the-future/03-conditionnel-politeness.md), so keep it as a chunk for now. *Je veux* alone can sound blunt when you order, so use *je voudrais* with people you do not know.

### Il faut

*Il faut* + infinitive means "it is necessary to" and is a way to say "you have to" without naming anyone: *Il faut manger.* ("One has to eat.") The verb is *falloir*, and it has only the *il* form. Watch out: *il ne faut pas* means "you must not", not "you don't have to": *Il ne faut pas parler ici.* ("You must not talk here.")

*Devoir* names a person, *il faut* does not. *Tu dois partir* and *Il faut partir* are close in meaning. With a noun, *il faut* means "one needs": *Il faut du temps.* ("It takes time.")

### In the past

In the passé composé you need the participles: *voulu*, *pu* and *dû*, with *avoir*. *J'ai voulu partir.* ("I wanted to leave.") *Je n'ai pas pu venir.* ("I could not come.") *Nous avons dû attendre.* ("We had to wait.") The accent on *dû* appears only in the masculine singular (the feminine is *due*), and *dû* is the form you need now.

### Préférer, souhaiter, espérer

*Préférer* ("to prefer") follows the *-er* pattern, with one spelling change. The *é* of the stem becomes *è* when the ending is silent: *je préfère*, *tu préfères*, *il préfère*, *ils préfèrent*, but *nous préférons*, *vous préférez*. The sounds change too: /e/ becomes /ɛ/. *Espérer* ("to hope") works the same way: *j'espère*. *Souhaiter* ("to wish") is regular and a little more formal: *Je souhaite partir.*

## Hear it

Listen for the same /ø/ in *veux* and *peux*, and for *je ne peux pas* said with the *ne* dropped in speech. The first dialogue uses *tu*, the second uses *vous*.

```fr
Inès: Tu veux manger chez moi samedi? | Do you want to eat at my place on Saturday?
Maya: Je voudrais bien, mais je ne peux pas. Je dois travailler. | I'd love to, but I can't. I have to work.
Inès: Tu travailles samedi soir? | You work on Saturday evening?
Maya: Oui, je suis infirmière. Il faut être à l'hôpital à 19 h. | Yes, I'm a nurse. I have to be at the hospital at 7 p.m.
Inès: Et dimanche? Tu peux venir dimanche? | And Sunday? Can you come on Sunday?
Maya: Oui, dimanche, ça marche. | Yes, Sunday works.
Inès: Je fais de la soupe. Tu préfères ça ou une salade? | I'm making soup. Do you prefer that or a salad?
Maya: Je préfère la soupe. | I prefer the soup.

Employé: Restaurant Le Quai, bonjour. | Le Quai restaurant, hello.
Maya: Bonjour. J'ai réservé une table pour samedi à 19 h. Je ne peux pas venir. | Hello. I booked a table for Saturday at 7 p.m. I can't come.
Employé: Vous voulez une autre date? | Do you want another date?
Maya: Oui, vendredi à 19 h, s'il vous plaît. | Yes, Friday at 7 p.m., please.
Employé: Pour combien de personnes? | For how many people?
Maya: Pour deux personnes. | For two people.
```

## Try it

1. Fill in the verb: *Nous ___ (vouloir) manger.* *Ils ___ (pouvoir) venir.* *Tu ___ (devoir) travailler.* *Vous ___ (pouvoir) répéter?*
2. Say "You have to work tomorrow" in two ways, once with *devoir* and once with *il faut*.
3. Put into the passé composé: *Je ne peux pas venir.* *Elle veut partir.* *Nous devons attendre.*
4. Write the right form of *préférer*: *Tu ___ le thé ou le café?* *Nous ___ la soupe.* *Elles ___ le café.*
5. What does *Il ne faut pas fermer la porte* mean?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Nous voulons manger.* *Ils peuvent venir.* *Tu dois travailler.* *Vous pouvez répéter?*
2. *Tu dois travailler demain.* *Il faut travailler demain.*
3. *Je n'ai pas pu venir.* *Elle a voulu partir.* *Nous avons dû attendre.*
4. *Tu préfères le thé ou le café?* *Nous préférons la soupe.* *Elles préfèrent le café.*
5. "You must not close the door." It does not mean "you don't have to".

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Je veux un café. | I want a coffee.
Tu peux venir samedi? | Can you come on Saturday?
Je dois travailler demain. | I have to work tomorrow.
Il faut être à l'hôpital à 19 h. | One has to be at the hospital at 7 p.m.
Je n'ai pas pu venir. | I could not come.
```

## Common mistakes

Putting the second verb in a conjugated form: *Je veux mange*. After *vouloir*, *pouvoir* and *devoir*, the second verb stays in the infinitive: *Je veux manger*.

Reading *il ne faut pas* as "you don't have to". It means "you must not".

Writing *je préfère* with *é* in every form. The accent is *è* before a silent ending: *je préfère*, *ils préfèrent*, but *nous préférons*.

## In Quebec

In casual speech, *je veux*, *je peux* and *je dois* lose their vowel: *j'veux*, *j'peux*, *j'dois*, and *ne* is dropped. This is common in many regions, including France. Do not write it in an email.

```fr
J'peux pas venir, j'dois travailler. {qc} | I can't come, I have to work.
J'veux un café. {qc} | I want a coffee.
```

## Key terms

- **Modal verb**: a verb such as *vouloir*, *pouvoir* or *devoir* that is followed by another verb in the infinitive.
- **Infinitive**: the form of a verb ending in *-er*, *-ir* or *-re*, as in *manger*.
- **Irregular verb**: a verb whose forms do not follow the usual pattern.
