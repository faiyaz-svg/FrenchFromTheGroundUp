# Dinner with friends

<span class="tag tag-speak">Speak</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span> <span class="tag tag-review">Review</span>

After this lesson you can host or be a guest at a dinner with friends in French: welcome people, offer food and drink, say what you bring, make a toast, thank the host, and talk about a past memory.

Before this: all of stage 6, especially [Partitive articles: du, de la, des](01-partitive-articles.md), [Vouloir, pouvoir and devoir](02-vouloir-pouvoir-devoir.md), [Food, shopping and quantities](03-food-shopping-and-quantities.md), [Imparfait or passé composé](06-imparfait-vs-passe-compose.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

This lesson has no new grammar. It brings the stage together in one evening, with *tu* for friends. Here is what each part of the dinner needs.

### Welcoming and offering

The host says *Bienvenue chez moi!* ("Welcome to my place!") and offers a drink. Use the partitive: *Tu veux du vin? Vous voulez de l'eau?* With several friends use *vous*, which here means "you all". Offers are polite with *voulez* or *tu veux*, and answers are polite with *je voudrais* and *s'il te plaît*. A drink before the meal is *un apéro*, a casual short form of *apéritif*. Do not confuse it with *un cinq à sept*, the late-afternoon gathering for drinks from [Table culture in Quebec](09-quebec-table-culture.md).

### Bringing something

A guest often brings food or drink: *J'apporte du vin et du fromage.* ("I'm bringing wine and cheese.") *Apporter* is a regular *-er* verb. The host can ask *Tu peux apporter du pain?* ("Can you bring some bread?"). Remember to use the partitive after *apporter* and *de* after a quantity: *une bouteille de vin*.

### The toast and the table

To make a toast, raise your glass and say *Santé!* ("Cheers!", literally "health"). *À table!* tells everyone to sit down for the meal (*le repas*). *Un hôte* is a host, and a woman is *une hôtesse*.

### Thanking and complimenting

After the meal, say what you liked: *C'était délicieux!* ("It was delicious!") *Quel bon repas!* ("What a good meal!") *Merci pour le repas.* ("Thank you for the meal.") The imparfait is natural here, because you describe how the meal was.

### Sharing and talking

*Partager* means "to share": *On partage la tarte?* ("Shall we share the tart?"). At dinner, friends tell stories about the past, so you use the imparfait for habits and the passé composé for events: *Chez mes grands-parents, on mangeait toujours de la tarte le dimanche.* *Hier, je me suis levée à 7 h pour cuisiner.*

One word of warning from the stage: if a friend does not eat meat or has an allergy, say so before the meal, and the host can plan: *Je suis végétarien. Je ne mange pas de viande.*

## Hear it

Listen for the partitive in each offer, and for the toast. Jasmine, 33, hosts three friends, Karim, Léa and Dev, in Ottawa. They all use *tu*, and *vous* when she speaks to the group.

```fr
Jasmine: Bienvenue chez moi! Vous voulez un apéro? Il y a du jus, de la bière et du vin blanc. | Welcome to my place! Do you want a drink? There is juice, beer and white wine.
Karim: Salut Jasmine! J'apporte du vin et du fromage. | Hi Jasmine! I'm bringing wine and cheese.
Jasmine: Merci! Léa, tu veux du vin? | Thanks! Léa, do you want some wine?
Léa: Moi, je voudrais de l'eau, s'il te plaît. J'ai soif. | I'd like some water, please. I'm thirsty.
Dev: Moi, je prends du vin blanc. Santé! | I'll have white wine. Cheers!
Tous: Santé! | Cheers!
Jasmine: À table! Aujourd'hui, il y a de la soupe, du poulet avec du riz et des carottes, et une tarte au citron. | To the table! Today there is soup, chicken with rice and carrots, and a lemon tart.
Dev: Je suis végétarien, mais je peux manger le riz et les carottes. | I'm vegetarian, but I can eat the rice and the carrots.
Jasmine: Pas de problème. Il y a aussi des pâtes aux champignons. Tu veux des pâtes? | No problem. There is also pasta with mushrooms. Do you want some pasta?
Dev: Oui, avec plaisir! | Yes, with pleasure!
Léa: Quel bon repas! C'était vraiment délicieux. | What a good meal! It was really delicious.
Karim: Tu cuisines très bien, Jasmine. | You cook very well, Jasmine.
Jasmine: Merci! Je me suis levée à 7 h pour cuisiner. Chez mes grands-parents, on mangeait toujours de la tarte le dimanche. | Thanks! I got up at 7 to cook. At my grandparents' place, we always ate tart on Sundays.
Léa: On partage la tarte au citron? | Shall we share the lemon tart?
Jasmine: Oui, bien sûr! | Yes, of course!
Karim: Merci pour le repas. C'était super. À bientôt! | Thanks for the meal. It was great. See you soon!
```

## Try it

1. Offer a guest wine, in two ways (one with *tu*, one with *vous*).
2. Say that you are bringing bread and a bottle of water.
3. Say in French that you do not drink wine and that you prefer water.
4. Put in the right tense: *Quand j'___ (être) petit, nous ___ (manger) chez ma grand-mère le dimanche.* *Hier, je me ___ (lever) à 7 h.*
5. Say a toast, and thank the host for the meal.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Tu veux du vin?* and *Vous voulez du vin?*
2. *J'apporte du pain et une bouteille d'eau.*
3. *Je ne bois pas de vin. Je préfère l'eau.*
4. *Quand j'étais petit, nous mangions chez ma grand-mère le dimanche.* *Hier, je me suis levé à 7 h.* (A woman writes *levée*.)
5. *Santé!* and *Merci pour le repas, c'était délicieux.*

</details>

## Common mistakes

Forgetting the partitive: *J'apporte vin*. Say *J'apporte du vin*.

Using *du* after a negative: *Je ne bois pas du vin*. Use *Je ne bois pas de vin*.

Asking *Tu veux de vin?* The offer needs the partitive: *Tu veux du vin?*

## In Quebec

*Santé!* is the toast in French generally. An *apéro* is a short form of *apéritif* and is a drink at home before the meal, while a *cinq à sept* is a gathering for drinks, often in a bar, in the late afternoon.
