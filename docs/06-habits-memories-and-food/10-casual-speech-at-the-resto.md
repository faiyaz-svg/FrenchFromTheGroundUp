# Casual speech at the resto

<span class="tag tag-listen">Listen</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can follow three friends who talk casually about where to eat in Montréal, recognise the casual forms (*c'est-tu*, *y'a*, *pis*, *tsé*) and six everyday words (*gars*, *fun*, *bouffe*, *en masse*, *correct*, *jaser*), and answer in short casual replies.

Before this: [Casual questions in Quebec](../03-verbs-and-questions/10-quebec-casual-questions.md), [At the restaurant](08-at-the-restaurant.md), [Table culture in Quebec](09-quebec-table-culture.md). You need: headphones, if you have them.

## The idea

This is a listening lesson. You listen first, with the English hidden, and you read afterwards. In stage 3 you learned the casual forms that Quebec speakers use with friends. Here you hear them in one long conversation about food. Everything in the main dialogue is casual and is marked {qc}. Casual does not mean wrong. It means a way of speaking you use with friends, and not in a written email or with a stranger at work. You learn it to understand, and the standard forms stay your own choice.

### What you will hear

First, the forms from stage 3. A question with the particle *-tu* sounds like the first two lines below, and the other forms are in the table.

```fr
C'est-tu cher? {qc} | Is it expensive? (standard: est-ce que c'est cher?)
T'as-tu faim? {qc} | Are you hungry? (standard: tu as faim?)
```

| You hear | Standard form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *y'a* | *il y a* | there is, there are |
| *chu* | *je suis* | I am |
| *pis* | *et*, *puis* | and, and then |
| *ben* | *bien* | well, really |
| *tsé* | *tu sais* | you know |
| *ouais*, *ouin* | *oui* | yes |

Three habits are also common. Speakers drop *ne* in the negative: *c'est pas bon* for *ce n'est pas bon*. They shorten *je* and *tu* before a vowel or a short word. And they add small filler words (*ben*, *tsé*, *pis*) while they think, much as English speakers say "well" and "you know".

### Six new words

| You hear | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *un gars* | a guy, a man (casual) |
| *le fun* | fun (from English, casual) |
| *la bouffe* | food (casual) |
| *en masse* | a lot, plenty (casual) |
| *correct* | fine, OK (casual) |
| *jaser* | to chat (casual) |

You can use *du fun* the way you use *du pain*: *On a du fun.* ("We are having fun.") *Correct* is also used on its own as a reply, as in *Correct!* ("OK!"), and it can mean "acceptable": *C'est correct* means "it's fine". *En masse* works like *beaucoup de*: *en masse de place*. In careful or written French, you would choose the standard words: *beaucoup de*, *bien*, *le plaisir*, *la nourriture*, *parler*.

### Short answers

In a casual conversation, answers are short: *Ouais.* *Ouin.* *Ben oui.* *Correct.* *Pas pire.* ("Not bad.") *Pas du tout.* *Pis?* ("And then?") You can use them with friends, but they are not for a first meeting.

### How to listen

Do not try to catch every word. In the first pass, listen for who wants what. In the second pass, listen for the casual forms in the table. In the third, read the French and check. Casual speech is faster, and the vowels are shorter.

## Hear it

Listen for *y'a*, *pis* and *c'est-tu* in three voices. The friends are Maude, Samir and Léo, in the Plateau. They all use *tu*, and every line is casual.

```fr
Maude: On mange où ce soir? {qc} | Where are we eating tonight?
Samir: Ben, j'sais pas. T'as-tu faim? {qc} | Well, I don't know. Are you hungry?
Maude: Ouais, chu ben fatiguée pis j'ai faim. {qc} | Yeah, I'm really tired and I'm hungry.
Léo: Y'a un resto sur le Plateau. La bouffe est super bonne. {qc} | There's a restaurant on the Plateau. The food is really good.
Samir: C'est-tu cher? {qc} | Is it expensive?
Léo: Non, c'est correct. Pis y'a en masse de place. {qc} | No, it's fine. And there's lots of room.
Maude: Ouais, mais la dernière fois, y'avait trop de bruit. {qc} | Yeah, but last time there was too much noise.
Samir: Tsé, on va jaser là-bas. On va avoir du fun. {qc} | You know, we'll chat there. We'll have fun.
Maude: Bon, ok. Pis toi, Samir, tu veux quoi? {qc} | Well, OK. And you, Samir, what do you want?
Samir: Moi, de la poutine! Pis une bière. {qc} | Me, poutine! And a beer.
Léo: Ouin, un gars qui a faim! {qc} | Yeah, a guy who's hungry!
```

### The same talk in standard French

Compare the first lines with the standard form. This is how you would say it with a person you do not know, in a written message, or in a formal setting.

```fr
Maude: Où est-ce que nous mangeons ce soir? | Where are we eating tonight?
Samir: Je ne sais pas. Tu as faim? | I don't know. Are you hungry?
Léo: Il y a un restaurant sur le Plateau. La nourriture est très bonne. | There is a restaurant on the Plateau. The food is very good.
Samir: Est-ce que c'est cher? | Is it expensive?
Léo: Non, c'est bien, et il y a beaucoup de place. | No, it's fine, and there is lots of room.
```

## Try it

Play the casual dialogue with the English hidden, then answer.

1. Who is tired and hungry? What does the person say?
2. What does Léo say about the price of the restaurant?
3. What is Maude's worry about the restaurant?
4. What does Samir want to eat and drink?
5. Give the standard French for the two casual questions and for the line about the room in the dialogue.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. Maude says that she is very tired and that she is hungry.
2. *C'est correct*, so it is fine, not too expensive.
3. Last time, there was too much noise (*trop de bruit*).
4. Poutine and a beer.
5. *Est-ce que c'est cher?* and *Il y a beaucoup de place.*

</details>

## Say it

Say the standard lines first, then repeat the casual line with the same rhythm.

```fr
Est-ce que c'est cher? | Is it expensive?
Il y a beaucoup de place. | There is lots of room.
C'est-tu cher? {qc} | Is it expensive?
Y'a en masse de place. {qc} | There's lots of room.
Ouais, c'est correct. {qc} | Yeah, it's fine.
```

## Common mistakes

Writing casual forms in an email or a formal message: *c'est-tu* or *y'a*. Write the standard forms, and keep the casual ones for speaking and for recognising.

Using casual words with a stranger, an older person or a manager. With them, use *vous* and the standard words.

Translating *correct* as "right". In casual Quebec speech it often means "fine" or "OK".

## Key terms

- **Casual speech**: the way people speak with friends, with shortened forms and fillers, not used in formal writing.
- **Filler word**: a small word such as *ben* or *tsé* that fills a pause.
