# Adverbs ending in -ment

<span class="tag tag-grammar">Grammar</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can turn an adjective into an adverb in *-ment* (*facile*, *facilement*), spell the *-amment* and *-emment* adverbs, use *bien*, *mal*, *mieux* and *vite*, and put an adverb in the right place.

Before this: [Adjectives and agreement](../04-people-places-and-directions/01-adjectives-agreement.md), [The imparfait](05-imparfait.md), [The passé composé with avoir](../05-the-past-what-happened/03-passe-compose-avoir.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

An **adverb** (*un adverbe*) is a word that tells how, when or how much. It describes a verb ("she speaks slowly"), an adjective ("very tired") or another adverb ("quite slowly"). An adverb never changes for gender or number, which makes it easy to use. You already know many: *très*, *trop*, *beaucoup*, *souvent*, *toujours*, *lentement*, *heureusement*, *évidemment*, *vraiment*. This lesson shows how most of the "how" adverbs are built.

### Building the adverb in -ment

For most adjectives, take the **feminine** form and add *-ment*.

1. Write the adjective in the feminine: *lent* gives *lente*, *facile* stays *facile*, *complet* gives *complète*.
2. Add *-ment*: *lentement*, *facilement*, *complètement*.

| Adjective | Feminine | Adverb |
|---|---|---|
| *rapide* | *rapide* | *rapidement* |
| *facile* | *facile* | *facilement* |
| *probable* | *probable* | *probablement* |
| *certain* | *certaine* | *certainement* |
| *seul* | *seule* | *seulement* |
| *complet* | *complète* | *complètement* |
| *général* | *générale* | *généralement* |
| *normal* | *normale* | *normalement* |
| *heureux* | *heureuse* | *heureusement* |

In the English "slow" and "slowly", you add *-ly*. In French, the *-ment* goes after the feminine form, which is why the *-e* is in *seulement*.

When the masculine adjective already ends in a vowel other than *-e* (*-ai*, *-i*, *-u*), add *-ment* to the masculine: *vrai* gives *vraiment*, *poli* gives *poliment*, *absolu* gives *absolument*.

### Adjectives ending in -ant and -ent

When the adjective ends in *-ant*, replace it with *-amment*. When it ends in *-ent*, replace it with *-emment*. Both are said /amɑ̃/, the same sound, although the spellings differ: *constant* gives *constamment* and *évident* gives *évidemment* /evidamɑ̃/. Listen for it in *évidemment* and *récemment*. Two adjectives in *-ent* keep the feminine rule: *lent* gives *lentement* and *présent* gives *présentement*.

### Adverbs that are not built this way

Some very common adverbs have their own form.

| Adjective | Adverb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *bon* | *bien* | *Il parle bien.* |
| *mauvais* | *mal* | *Elle joue mal.* |
| *meilleur* | *mieux* | *Je comprends mieux.* |
| *rapide* | *vite* (*rapidement* also exists) | *Il mange vite.* |

Watch out: *bon* and *bien* are not the same. *Bon* is an adjective and describes a noun: *un bon café*. *Bien* is an adverb and describes a verb: *Il parle bien*. The same goes for *mauvais* (adjective) and *mal* (adverb). *Mieux* means "better" as an adverb: *Tu parles mieux qu'hier*. The full comparison, with *que*, is in [Comparatives](../08-comparing-duration-and-settling-in/01-comparatives.md).

Other adverbs have small spelling surprises: *gentil* gives *gentiment*, *bref* gives *brièvement*, and *énorme* gives *énormément*. These are exceptions you will meet, and you can learn them as they come.

Two more words come up every day. *Tellement* means "so" or "so much": *Je suis tellement fatigué.* *Tout à fait* means "completely" or "exactly", and alone it is a way to agree: *Tout à fait!*

### Where the adverb goes

With a simple tense, put the adverb right after the verb: *Il parle lentement.* *Je comprends bien.* Before an adjective, put it in front: *Il est complètement fatigué.* *C'est tellement difficile.*

With the passé composé, short common adverbs sit between the helper and the participle: *J'ai bien dormi.* *Elle a vite fini.* *Il a beaucoup mangé.* Longer *-ment* adverbs can go there or right after the participle: *Il a répondu rapidement* is the usual order, and *Il a rapidement répondu* is also correct. An adverb of the whole sentence, such as *heureusement* or *normalement*, can stand at the start, followed by a comma: *Heureusement, j'ai bien dormi.*

## Hear it

Listen for the /amɑ̃/ in *évidemment* and the stress on the last syllable of *rapidement*. Two friends, Nadia and Marc, talk about how an interview went. They use *tu*.

```fr
Nadia: Alors, ton entretien? Ça s'est bien passé? | So, your interview? Did it go well?
Marc: Oui, très bien! J'étais tellement fatigué, mais heureusement, j'ai bien dormi. | Yes, very well! I was so tired, but luckily I slept well.
Nadia: Tu as parlé lentement? | Did you speak slowly?
Marc: Oui, et j'ai répondu rapidement et facilement. | Yes, and I answered quickly and easily.
Nadia: Tu penses que tu vas avoir le travail? | Do you think you'll get the job?
Marc: Probablement. J'ai seulement besoin d'un peu de chance. | Probably. I only need a bit of luck.
Nadia: Tu vas réussir, certainement. On mange ensemble ce soir? | You'll succeed, for sure. Shall we eat together tonight?
Marc: Tout à fait! | Absolutely!
```

## Try it

1. Build the adverb: *lent*, *facile*, *heureux*, *complet*, *évident*.
2. *Bien* or *bon*? *Il parle ___.* *C'est très ___.* *Elle joue ___.* (Use *mal* in the last one.)
3. Put the adverb in the passé composé sentence: *Elle dort* + *bien*. *Nous mangeons* + *vite*.
4. Say in French: "I'm so tired."
5. Write the adverb for *gentil* and for *bref*.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *lentement*, *facilement*, *heureusement*, *complètement*, *évidemment*.
2. *Il parle bien.* *C'est très bon.* *Elle joue mal.*
3. *Elle a bien dormi.* *Nous avons vite mangé.*
4. *Je suis tellement fatigué(e).*
5. *gentiment*, *brièvement*.

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Il parle lentement. | He speaks slowly.
J'ai bien dormi. | I slept well.
Heureusement, j'ai bien dormi. | Luckily, I slept well.
Il est complètement fatigué. | He is completely tired.
Tout à fait! | Absolutely!
```

## Common mistakes

Writing *facilment* or *seulment*. The *-e* of the feminine stays: *facilement*, *seulement*.

Writing *evidentment*. For adjectives in *-ent*, the adverb is *-emment*: *évidemment*. For *-ant*, it is *-amment*: *constamment*.

Using *bon* for *bien*: *Il parle bon*. After a verb that describes how, use the adverb: *Il parle bien*.

## Key terms

- **Adverb**: a word that tells how, when or how much, and does not change.
- **Feminine form**: the form of an adjective used with a feminine noun, the base for adverbs in *-ment*.
