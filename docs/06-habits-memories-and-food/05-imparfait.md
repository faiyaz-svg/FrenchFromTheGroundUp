# The imparfait

<span class="tag tag-grammar">Grammar</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can build the imparfait of any verb you know, use *être* in the imparfait, and talk about habits, places and states in the past (*j'habitais à Gatineau*, *il faisait froid*, *c'était bien*).

Before this: [The passé composé with avoir](../05-the-past-what-happened/03-passe-compose-avoir.md), [Prendre, venir, boire and voir](../05-the-past-what-happened/02-prendre-venir-boire-voir.md), [Aller and faire](../03-verbs-and-questions/05-aller-and-faire.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

The passé composé tells what happened: one event, finished. The **imparfait** (*l'imparfait*) is the other everyday past tense. It does not tell an event. It paints the scene: what things were like, what you used to do, how you felt. English uses "used to", "would" and "was ...ing" for it: "We used to live in Gatineau", "It was cold", "I was reading".

The two tenses work together, and the next lesson explains how to choose. This lesson teaches the forms and the main uses of the imparfait.

### Building the imparfait

Follow three steps.

1. Take the *nous* form of the present: *nous parlons*, *nous finissons*, *nous vendons*, *nous faisons*.
2. Remove *-ons*. What is left is the **stem**: *parl-*, *finiss-*, *vend-*, *fais-*.
3. Add the imperfect endings: *-ais*, *-ais*, *-ait*, *-ions*, *-iez*, *-aient*.

| Subject | *parler* | *finir* | *vendre* | *faire* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *je* | *parlais* | *finissais* | *vendais* | *faisais* |
| *tu* | *parlais* | *finissais* | *vendais* | *faisais* |
| *il / elle / on* | *parlait* | *finissait* | *vendait* | *faisait* |
| *nous* | *parlions* | *finissions* | *vendions* | *faisions* |
| *vous* | *parliez* | *finissiez* | *vendiez* | *faisiez* |
| *ils / elles* | *parlaient* | *finissaient* | *vendaient* | *faisaient* |

Listen for the endings. *-ais*, *-ait* and *-aient* all sound like /ɛ/: *je parlais*, *il parlait* and *ils parlaient* are the same sound, /paʁlɛ/. The *nous* and *vous* endings have a /j/: *nous parlions* /nu paʁljɔ̃/ and *vous parliez* /vu paʁlje/. Compare with the present *nous parlons* and *vous parlez*, which have no /j/.

This method works for every verb you know, including the irregular ones: *prendre* (*nous prenons*) gives *je prenais*, *aller* (*nous allons*) gives *j'allais*, *boire* (*nous buvons*) gives *je buvais*, *avoir* (*nous avons*) gives *j'avais*, *voir* (*nous voyons*) gives *je voyais*, and *vouloir* gives *je voulais*.

### Être is the one exception

*Être* has no *nous* form that works (*nous sommes*). Its stem is *ét-*, and the endings are normal: *j'étais*, *tu étais*, *il était*, *nous étions*, *vous étiez*, *ils étaient*. Learn it as a set. It is the most common verb of the imparfait.

### Spelling points

Verbs in *-ger* keep the *e* before *a*, so that the *g* stays soft: *je mangeais*, *il mangeait*, *ils mangeaient*, but *nous mangions*, *vous mangiez* (the *i* already softens the *g*). The same goes for *voyager*. Verbs in *-cer* use *ç* before *a*: *je commençais*, *il commençait*, but *nous commencions*. Verbs in *-ier* have two *i* in the *nous* and *vous* forms: *nous étudiions*, *vous étudiiez*.

### What the imparfait is for

Use the imparfait for these things:

- **Habits** in the past: *Le samedi, on mangeait chez ma grand-mère.* ("On Saturdays we used to eat at my grandmother's.")
- **Descriptions** of people, places and weather: *Il faisait froid.* *Il y avait beaucoup de monde.* *Mon père était grand.*
- **States** of mind or body: *J'étais fatigué.* *Nous avions faim.*
- **Background** while something else happens, which you will see in the next lesson.

*C'était* ("it was"), *il y avait* ("there was, there were") and *il faisait* ("it was, of the weather") are the three forms you will use most.

Words that often go with the imparfait show a habit: *d'habitude* ("usually"), *chaque jour* ("every day"), *toujours*, *souvent*, *tous les jours*, *le samedi*. *Chaque* is always followed by a singular noun and never changes: *chaque semaine*, *chaque matin*.

## Hear it

Listen for the same /ɛ/ ending in *habitais*, *travaillait* and *était*. Aya is a young neighbour, so she uses *vous* with M. Fortin, a retired mechanic of 74 in Gatineau.

```fr
Aya: Monsieur Fortin, comment c'était, quand vous étiez petit? | Mr Fortin, what was it like when you were little?
Fortin: Nous habitions à Gatineau. Mon père travaillait dans un garage. | We lived in Gatineau. My father worked in a garage.
Aya: Et vous alliez à l'école à pied? | And did you go to school on foot?
Fortin: Oui, chaque matin, avec mon frère. Il faisait très froid en janvier. | Yes, every morning, with my brother. It was very cold in January.
Aya: Et après la classe? | And after class?
Fortin: D'habitude, j'allais au garage. Je regardais mon père travailler. | Usually I went to the garage. I watched my father work.
Aya: Vous aimiez ça? | Did you like that?
Fortin: Oui, j'adorais les voitures! Nous n'avions pas beaucoup d'argent, mais on était heureux. | Yes, I loved cars! We didn't have a lot of money, but we were happy.
Aya: Vous aviez des jouets? | Did you have toys?
Fortin: J'avais un vélo rouge. C'était mon jouet préféré. | I had a red bike. It was my favourite toy.
```

## Try it

1. Find the stem from the *nous* form: *nous finissons*, *nous faisons*, *nous prenons*.
2. Write the imparfait: *je (habiter)*, *tu (aller)*, *nous (manger)*, *vous (être)*, *ils (avoir)*, *elle (faire)*.
3. Write in French: "When I was a child, I played every day."
4. Write *nous commençons* in the imparfait, and say how it differs from the present.
5. Say in French: "It was cold." "There were a lot of cars."

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *finiss-*, *fais-*, *pren-*.
2. *j'habitais*, *tu allais*, *nous mangions*, *vous étiez*, *ils avaient*, *elle faisait*.
3. *Quand j'étais enfant, je jouais tous les jours.*
4. *Nous commencions.* The present *nous commençons* has *ç* before *o*. In the imparfait the *i* already softens the *c*, so no cedilla.
5. *Il faisait froid.* *Il y avait beaucoup de voitures.*

</details>

## Say it

```fr
J'habitais à Gatineau. | I lived in Gatineau.
Mon père travaillait dans un garage. | My father worked in a garage.
Il faisait froid et il y avait beaucoup de monde. | It was cold and there were a lot of people.
D'habitude, j'allais à l'école à pied. | Usually I went to school on foot.
Nous étions heureux. | We were happy.
```

## Common mistakes

Using the passé composé for a habit: *J'ai joué tous les jours*. For a repeated past action, use *je jouais tous les jours*.

Building the stem from the infinitive: *je finais*. Use the *nous* form: *nous finissons*, so *je finissais*.

Forgetting the soft *g*: *je mangais*. Write *je mangeais*.

## In Quebec

In fast casual speech, *j'étais* often sounds like *j'tais*. This is casual and heard in many regions. Write *j'étais*.

```fr
J'tais p'tit, j'habitais à Gatineau. {qc} | I was little, I lived in Gatineau.
```
