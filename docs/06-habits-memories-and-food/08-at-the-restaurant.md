# At the restaurant

<span class="tag tag-speak">Speak</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can book a table, order a meal, say what you do not eat, ask for advice and ask for the bill in a French-speaking restaurant.

Before this: [Ordering at a café](../02-sounds-spelling-and-everyday-things/08-ordering-at-a-cafe.md), [Partitive articles: du, de la, des](01-partitive-articles.md), [Vouloir, pouvoir and devoir](02-vouloir-pouvoir-devoir.md), [Food, shopping and quantities](03-food-shopping-and-quantities.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

A restaurant visit has four steps: you book, you order, you eat, and you pay. Each step has a few fixed phrases, and with *vous* and the grammar from this stage you can say all of them. In every dialogue here, the customer uses *vous* with the waiter, as people do with staff they do not know.

### Booking a table

You book with *je voudrais réserver une table pour* and the number of people, then the day and the time. The restaurant asks the name: *C'est à quel nom?* You answer *Au nom de Roy*, then spell it if needed, as in stage 1. *Une réservation* is a booking, and *réserver* is "to book".

### Reading the card

*La carte* is the list of dishes. A meal has three courses, and each has a name. *Une entrée* is a starter. *Un plat* is the main dish. *Un dessert* is the last course. Watch out: in North American English, "entrée" often means the main dish. In French, *une entrée* is always the first course.

To order, use *je vais prendre* ("I'm going to have") with the dish, or *je prends*. You may also say *comme entrée, je prends la soupe* ("as a starter, I'll have the soup"). To ask for help, use *Qu'est-ce que vous conseillez?* ("What do you recommend?"). *Conseiller* means "to advise". *Commander* means "to order": *Vous voulez commander?* ("Do you want to order?").

### Saying what you do not eat

Say *Je suis végétarien* (a man) or *Je suis végétarienne* (a woman) if you eat no meat. For a dish without something, use *sans*: *sans fromage*. For an allergy, say *J'ai une allergie au poisson* ("I have a fish allergy"). *Au* is *à* + *le*, and with *une allergie* it means "to": *une allergie au poisson*, *une allergie à la viande*, *une allergie aux fraises*. Tell the waiter before you order.

### How you like your meat

For a steak or a piece of beef, the waiter asks how you want it cooked.

| French | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *saignant* | rare, still red inside |
| *à point* | medium |
| *bien cuit* | well done |

*Cuit* is the participle of *cuire* ("to cook"). Say it as one phrase: *Le bœuf, à point, s'il vous plaît.*

### At the table and the bill

*Une fourchette* is a fork, *un couteau* is a knife, *une cuillère* is a spoon and *une serviette* is a napkin. To ask for something, say *Je peux avoir une fourchette, s'il vous plaît?* ("Can I have a fork, please?").

At the end, say *L'addition, s'il vous plaît.* The waiter may ask *Vous payez ensemble ou séparément?* ("Are you paying together or separately?"). *Payer* is an *-er* verb, and *nous payons* works as usual. You can also ask *Le service est compris?* ("Is the service included?"). *Le service* here is the service charge, a part of the price that goes to the staff.

In France, the law says that the menu prices include service, and that a tip is optional ([DGCCRF: le pourboire](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/dgccrf/les-fiches-pratiques/pourboire)). Customs in Quebec are different, and [Table culture in Quebec](09-quebec-table-culture.md) explains the tip.

## Hear it

Listen for the polite *vous* in each line, and for *je vais prendre*. First a phone call, then the table. Éric is a waiter of 35 in a Vieux-Montréal bistro, and the four customers use *vous* with him.

```fr
Éric: Bistro du Port, bonjour. | Bistro du Port, hello.
Client: Bonjour. Je voudrais réserver une table pour quatre, samedi à 19 h 30. | Hello. I'd like to book a table for four, Saturday at 7:30 p.m.
Éric: Oui, c'est possible. C'est à quel nom? | Yes, that's possible. Under what name?
Client: Au nom de Roy. | Under Roy.

Éric: Bonsoir. Voici la carte. Vous voulez boire quelque chose? | Good evening. Here is the menu. Would you like something to drink?
Client: Oui, une bouteille d'eau et deux verres de vin blanc. | Yes, a bottle of water and two glasses of white wine.
Éric: Vous avez choisi? | Have you chosen?
Client: Pas encore. Qu'est-ce que vous conseillez? | Not yet. What do you recommend?
Éric: Le poisson est délicieux ce soir. | The fish is delicious tonight.
Client: J'ai une allergie au poisson. | I have a fish allergy.
Éric: Alors, le poulet est très bon aussi. | Then the chicken is very good too.
Cliente: Moi, je suis végétarienne. Il y a un plat sans viande? | Me, I'm vegetarian. Is there a dish without meat?
Éric: Oui, il y a des pâtes aux champignons. | Yes, there is pasta with mushrooms.
Cliente: Très bien, je vais prendre les pâtes. Et le bœuf, à point, pour mon mari. | Very good, I'll have the pasta. And the beef, medium, for my husband.

Client: L'addition, s'il vous plaît. | The bill, please.
Éric: Vous payez ensemble ou séparément? | Are you paying together or separately?
Client: Ensemble. Le service est compris? | Together. Is the service included?
Éric: Non, le service n'est pas compris. | No, the service is not included.
```

## Try it

1. Say in French: "I'd like to book a table for two on Friday at 8 p.m."
2. Match each one to its meaning: *saignant*, *à point*, *bien cuit* with "well done", "rare", "medium".
3. Which word do you need: a fork, a knife, a spoon, a napkin?
4. Say that you are vegetarian (you can pick *végétarien* or *végétarienne*) and that you have a fish allergy.
5. Ask for the bill, and say that you are paying separately.

1. *Je voudrais réserver une table pour deux, vendredi à 20 h.*
2. *saignant* is rare, *à point* is medium, *bien cuit* is well done.
3. *une fourchette*, *un couteau*, *une cuillère*, *une serviette*.
4. *Je suis végétarien. J'ai une allergie au poisson.* A woman says *végétarienne*.
5. *L'addition, s'il vous plaît. Nous payons séparément.*

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Je voudrais réserver une table pour quatre. | I'd like to book a table for four.
Qu'est-ce que vous conseillez? | What do you recommend?
Je vais prendre le poulet, bien cuit. | I'll have the chicken, well done.
J'ai une allergie au poisson. | I have a fish allergy.
L'addition, s'il vous plaît. | The bill, please.
```

## Common mistakes

Calling the main course *l'entrée*. In French, *l'entrée* is the starter and *le plat* is the main dish.

Saying *Je veux un café* to the waiter. It is blunt. Use *je voudrais* or *je vais prendre*.

Forgetting *de* with a quantity: *une bouteille eau*. Use *une bouteille d'eau*.

## In Quebec

Some Quebec restaurants are marked *apportez votre vin* ("bring your own wine"). In these restaurants customers can bring their own alcohol, and the restaurant needs a permit with the "pour servir" option for it ([Éducaloi: what to bring](https://educaloi.qc.ca/curiosites/quel-alcool-choisir-pour-aller-dans-un-restaurant-apportez-votre-vin/)). Ask the restaurant what you may bring.
