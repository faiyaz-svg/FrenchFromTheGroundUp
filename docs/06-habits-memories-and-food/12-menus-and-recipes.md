# Menus and recipes

<span class="tag tag-read">Read</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can read a restaurant menu, read a simple recipe with its ingredients, measures and steps, and understand the verbs in the infinitive that give the instructions.

Before this: [Food, shopping and quantities](03-food-shopping-and-quantities.md), [At the restaurant](08-at-the-restaurant.md), [Partitive articles: du, de la, des](01-partitive-articles.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

A menu and a recipe are short texts with a fixed layout. Once you know the layout, you can read them even when a few words are new. This lesson shows each layout, teaches the cooking verbs, and gives you a menu and a recipe to read.

### Reading a menu

A menu groups the dishes under headings: *entrées* (starters), *plats* (main dishes), *desserts* and *boissons* (drinks). Each line gives the dish and its price, written as in stage 2: *12,50 $*. A dish name is usually a list of nouns, with *de*, *à la*, *au* or *aux* to join them: *pâtes aux champignons* is "pasta with mushrooms", and *tarte au citron* is "lemon tart". *Du jour* ("of the day") marks a dish that changes every day. A note such as *végétarien* tells you the dish has no meat. Read the whole line before you decide: a dish name sometimes has an ingredient you do not eat.

### Reading a recipe

A recipe has three parts: the title, the list of ingredients (*les ingrédients*), and the steps. The ingredients are written as a quantity, *de* and the food: *une pincée de sel*, *250 g de farine*, *un demi-litre de lait*. You know the quantity words from the food lesson. New ones are *une pincée* ("a pinch") and *une cuillère à soupe* ("a tablespoon"), also *une cuillère à café* ("a teaspoon").

The steps often use the **infinitive**, the form that ends in *-er*, *-ir* or *-re*, with no subject: *Couper les carottes.* ("Cut the carrots.") *Mélanger.* ("Mix.") You are not asked to conjugate these verbs. Treat them as vocabulary: each step starts with a verb that tells you what to do. Recipes can also use the *vous* command form (*coupez*, *mélangez*), which you will learn in The imperative<!-- link: ../07-pronouns-and-getting-things-done/04-imperative.md -->. The meaning is the same.

| Verb | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *éplucher* | to peel |
| *couper* | to cut |
| *mélanger* | to mix |
| *ajouter* | to add |
| *verser* | to pour |
| *chauffer* | to heat |
| *cuire* | to cook |
| *bouillir* | to boil |

You also need the tools: *un bol* (a bowl), *une poêle* (a frying pan), *une casserole* (a pot, a saucepan), and *un four* (an oven). *Un degré* is a degree: *Chauffer le four à 180 °C.* ("Heat the oven to 180 °C.") The symbol *°C* means "degrees Celsius", the scale used in Canada.

### Measures in Canada

Many Canadian recipes give both a metric amount and a cup: for example *250 ml (1 tasse) de lait* ([Fondation OLO, a Quebec recipe](https://fondationolo.ca/media/sqchbff3/fondation-olo-recette-crepes.pdf)). *Une tasse* is a cup, and a Canadian cup is 250 ml. Read both numbers if you see them, and use one scale.

## Hear it

First, a bistro menu. Listen for the prices and for each dish, and notice the *de*, *aux* and *au* that join the nouns.

```fr
Entrées
Soupe du jour 8,00 $ | Soup of the day
Salade de tomates 9,50 $ | Tomato salad
Plats
Poulet, riz et carottes 19,00 $ | Chicken, rice and carrots
Poisson au citron et pommes de terre 24,50 $ | Fish with lemon and potatoes
Pâtes aux champignons (végétarien) 17,00 $ | Pasta with mushrooms (vegetarian)
Desserts
Tarte au citron 8,50 $ | Lemon tart
Crème glacée 6,00 $ | Ice cream
```

Now a recipe for crêpes (thin pancakes). The ingredients first, then the steps. A recipe gives the verb first, and the verb does not change.

```fr
Crêpes
Ingrédients: | Ingredients:
250 g de farine | 250 g of flour
3 œufs | 3 eggs
Un demi-litre de lait | half a litre of milk
Une pincée de sel | a pinch of salt
Une cuillère à soupe de sucre | a tablespoon of sugar
Un peu de beurre | a little butter
Mettre la farine, le sel et le sucre dans un bol. | Put the flour, salt and sugar in a bowl.
Ajouter les œufs et mélanger. | Add the eggs and mix.
Verser le lait et mélanger. La pâte est prête. | Pour in the milk and mix. The batter is ready.
Chauffer une poêle avec un peu de beurre. | Heat a frying pan with a little butter.
Verser un peu de pâte dans la poêle. | Pour a little batter into the pan.
Cuire deux minutes, puis retourner la crêpe et cuire encore une minute. | Cook two minutes, then turn the crêpe and cook one more minute.
```

A second, very short recipe, for carrot soup.

```fr
Soupe de carottes
Éplucher les carottes et un oignon. | Peel the carrots and an onion.
Couper en petits morceaux. | Cut into small pieces.
Mettre dans une casserole avec de l'eau et du sel. | Put in a pot with water and salt.
Faire bouillir vingt minutes. | Boil for twenty minutes.
Ajouter du poivre. | Add pepper.
```

## Try it

1. Which dish on the menu is for a vegetarian? How much is the chicken dish?
2. A customer orders the chicken and the lemon tart. What is the total before tax?
3. Write the French verb for: to peel, to cut, to mix, to add.
4. In the crêpe recipe, which tool do you use to cook, and how long does the first side cook?
5. How many millilitres are in *une tasse*, and what does *180 °C* refer to?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Pâtes aux champignons*, marked *végétarien*. The chicken dish is 19,00 $.
2. 19,00 + 8,50 = 27,50 $, so *vingt-sept dollars cinquante*.
3. *éplucher*, *couper*, *mélanger*, *ajouter*.
4. *Une poêle*, and *deux minutes*.
5. 250 ml. *180 °C* is the oven temperature in degrees Celsius.

</details>

## Say it

Read these lines aloud, with the verb first and no subject.

```fr
Couper les carottes. | Cut the carrots.
Ajouter une pincée de sel. | Add a pinch of salt.
Chauffer une poêle avec un peu de beurre. | Heat a pan with a little butter.
Cuire deux minutes. | Cook for two minutes.
Je vais prendre la soupe du jour. | I'll have the soup of the day.
```

## Common mistakes

Reading *cuire* as a noun. It is a verb, "to cook", and it has the participle *cuit* from the restaurant lesson.

Treating *les pâtes* (pasta) and *la pâte* (batter, dough) as the same word. The singular is *la pâte*, and *les pâtes* is the plural for pasta.

Forgetting that *de* follows a quantity in a list of ingredients: *une pincée de sel*, not *une pincée du sel*.

## In Quebec

Some Quebec recipes give amounts in *tasses* next to millilitres, as in the Fondation OLO recipe linked above, and Canadian recipes use temperatures in degrees Celsius. A menu price is written with the dollar sign after the number, as in *9,50 $*.
