# Food, shopping and quantities

<span class="tag tag-words">Words</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can name common foods and shops, ask for a quantity with *de* (*un kilo de pommes de terre*, *une douzaine d'œufs*), and say what you buy with *acheter*.

Before this: [Partitive articles: du, de la, des](01-partitive-articles.md), [Numbers from 0 to 100](../02-sounds-spelling-and-everyday-things/06-numbers-0-100.md), [Regular -er verbs](../03-verbs-and-questions/02-regular-er-verbs.md). You need: nothing, or a real shopping list to translate.

## The idea

This lesson is mostly words, so learn them in groups and use each one in a sentence. Every food noun is given with its article. The partitive from the last lesson makes a list sound natural: *Je voudrais du beurre, du fromage et de la viande.*

### Foods

| Group | Words |
|---|---|
| Dairy and basics | *le beurre*, *le fromage*, *le lait*, *les œufs* (m.), *le pain*, *le riz*, *les pâtes* (f.), *le sucre*, *la farine* |
| Meat and fish | *la viande*, *le poulet*, *le bœuf*, *le jambon*, *le poisson* |
| Fruit | *une pomme*, *une banane*, *une fraise*, *un citron* |
| Vegetables | *un légume*, *une tomate*, *une carotte*, *une pomme de terre*, *un oignon*, *une laitue*, *un champignon* |
| Seasoning | *le sel*, *le poivre*, *l'huile* (f.), *le sirop d'érable* |

*Les pâtes* is always plural. *Une pomme de terre* has three words but names one thing, and its plural is *des pommes de terre*. Say *l'huile* /lɥil/ with the /ɥ/ from *huit*, and *oignon* /ɔɲɔ̃/ with the /ɲ/ of *gn*; the *i* is silent.

### Shops

| Shop | What you buy |
|---|---|
| *une épicerie* | a grocery store, a general food shop |
| *un supermarché* | a large store with every food group |
| *une boulangerie* | bread and pastries (*le pain*, *le croissant*) |
| *une boucherie* | meat |
| *un dépanneur* | a corner store open late (stage 2) |
| *un marché* | an outdoor or indoor market with stalls |

### Quantities with de

A **quantity word** says how much, and it is followed by *de* with no article: *un kilo de pommes de terre*, not *un kilo des pommes de terre*. Before a vowel, *de* becomes *d'*: *une douzaine d'œufs*.

| Quantity | Example |
|---|---|
| *un kilo*, *un demi-kilo*, *un gramme* | *un kilo de poulet*, *deux cents grammes de fromage* |
| *un litre* | *un litre de lait* |
| *un paquet*, *une boîte* | *un paquet de pâtes*, *une boîte de champignons* |
| *une bouteille*, *un verre*, *une tasse* | *une bouteille d'eau*, *un verre de vin* |
| *une tranche* | *une tranche de jambon* |
| *une douzaine* | *une douzaine d'œufs* |
| *beaucoup*, *assez*, *trop*, *un peu* | *beaucoup de sel*, *assez de pain*, *trop de sucre*, *un peu d'huile* |

*Une douzaine* is twelve, and it works like *un paquet*: *une douzaine d'œufs*, *une douzaine de pommes*. A quantity word has the gender it has on its own, so *une tranche* is feminine even when the food is *du jambon*.

### Kilograms, grams and pounds

In Canada, the price of bulk food must be shown in metric units, and a shop may add the price in imperial units too ([Measurement Canada: selling food by weight](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/mesures-canada/fr/node/720), checked 2026). So a price such as *4,99 $ le kilo* may appear next to a price per pound. The French word for a pound is *une livre* (about 454 g). Watch out: *une livre* is feminine and means "a pound". *Un livre* is masculine and means "a book".

For a price, say the figures as you did in stage 2: *3,50 $* is *trois dollars cinquante*. *Le kilo* after a price means "per kilo".

### Acheter

*Acheter* ("to buy") is an *-er* verb with the same spelling change as *préférer*, but here the vowel is *e*, and it becomes *è* when the ending is silent: *j'achète*, *tu achètes*, *il achète*, *nous achetons*, *vous achetez*, *ils achètent*. The silent endings sound alike /aʃɛt/; *nous achetons* /nuz aʃtɔ̃/ drops the vowel.

## Hear it

Listen for *de* after each quantity, and for the prices said in dollars and cents. Luc uses *vous* at the stalls, in the Marché du Vieux-Port in Québec City, and *tu* when he reads his own list to a friend.

```fr
Marchande: Bonjour, monsieur. Qu'est-ce que je vous sers? | Hello, sir. What can I get you?
Luc: Bonjour. Je voudrais un kilo de pommes de terre, s'il vous plaît. | Hello. I'd like a kilo of potatoes, please.
Marchande: Voilà. Et avec ça? | Here you are. And with that?
Luc: Une douzaine d'œufs et deux tomates. | A dozen eggs and two tomatoes.
Marchande: C'est tout? | Is that all?
Luc: Oui, c'est tout. C'est combien? | Yes, that's all. How much is it?
Marchande: Ça fait 11,50 $. | That comes to $11.50.
Luc: Voilà. Merci, bonne journée! | Here you are. Thank you, have a good day!

Luc: J'achète du pain, du beurre et du fromage. | I'm buying bread, butter and cheese.
Dev: Tu achètes de la viande? | Do you buy meat?
Luc: Oui, un demi-kilo de bœuf et du poulet. | Yes, half a kilo of beef and some chicken.
Dev: Et pour le souper? Du riz, des carottes, un oignon? | And for supper? Rice, carrots, an onion?
Luc: Oui, et de l'huile, du sel, du poivre. | Yes, and oil, salt, pepper.
```

## Try it

1. Add the quantity and *de*: *un litre ___ lait*, *une douzaine ___ œufs*, *un paquet ___ pâtes*, *une tranche ___ jambon*.
2. Say what you buy with *acheter*: *Nous ___ du pain.* *Elle ___ des fraises.* *Tu ___ du lait?*
3. In which shop do you buy bread? Meat? A litre of milk late at night?
4. Say in French: "a lot of salt", "a little oil", "enough rice".
5. Read these prices aloud in French: *3,50 $ le kilo*, *2,25 $*.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *un litre de lait*, *une douzaine d'œufs*, *un paquet de pâtes*, *une tranche de jambon*.
2. *Nous achetons du pain.* *Elle achète des fraises.* *Tu achètes du lait?*
3. *À la boulangerie.* *À la boucherie.* *Au dépanneur.*
4. *beaucoup de sel*, *un peu d'huile*, *assez de riz*.
5. *Trois dollars cinquante le kilo.* *Deux dollars vingt-cinq.*

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Je voudrais un kilo de pommes de terre. | I'd like a kilo of potatoes.
Une douzaine d'œufs, s'il vous plaît. | A dozen eggs, please.
J'achète du pain à la boulangerie. | I buy bread at the bakery.
Un demi-kilo de bœuf et un litre de lait. | Half a kilo of beef and a litre of milk.
C'est combien? Ça fait 11,50 $. | How much is it? That comes to $11.50.
```

## Common mistakes

Using the article after a quantity word: *un kilo des pommes de terre*. Use *de* alone: *un kilo de pommes de terre*.

Writing *j'achetes* or *il achete*. Write *è* when the ending is silent: *j'achète*, *il achète*.

Mixing *une livre* and *un livre*. The pound is feminine and the book is masculine.

## In Quebec

An *épicerie* is a grocery store, and *un dépanneur* is a corner store with longer hours.
