# A weekend in Quebec City

<span class="tag tag-speak">Speak</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span> <span class="tag tag-review">Review</span>

After this lesson you can tell a neighbour about a weekend trip in a 90-second story with the passé composé, time words and linking words, and answer follow-up questions.

Before this: [Telling about your weekend](08-telling-about-your-weekend.md), [The passé composé with être](05-passe-compose-etre.md), [The passé composé: no, questions and agreement](06-pc-negation-questions-agreement.md), [Past time words](07-past-time-words.md). ## The idea

This lesson has no new grammar. It brings together everything from stage 5 in one longer piece of speech. You will hear a couple tell a neighbour about a trip, then you tell your own trip. The story needs five things you have already learned, and the table shows where each one comes from.

### The frame for a 90-second story

Ninety seconds is about 10 to 12 sentences. Use the five steps from the weekend lesson, and add details at each step.

1. Frame: when you left, with whom, where you went and how.
2. Day one: three things in order, with *d'abord*, *ensuite*, *puis*.
3. Day two: two things, one with *être*.
4. One thing that went wrong or was surprising: use a negative or *malheureusement*.
5. The end: *enfin*, when you came back and what you thought.

### Five words for a trip

*Une auberge* is an inn or a small hotel. *Un bateau* is a boat. *Une excursion* is a short organised trip, and *une croisière* is a trip by boat, for pleasure. *Un traversier* is the Quebec word for a ferry that carries people and vehicles across the river. Usito marks it as a Quebec and Canadian word, and says the OQLF made it official ([Usito: traversier](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/traversier)). Its examples include ferries to Lévis, Tadoussac and Matane.

### Answering follow-up questions

A neighbour asks short questions, and you have met most of them: *Vous êtes allés où? Vous avez fait quoi? Avec qui? Vous êtes rentrés quand? Vous avez aimé?* Answer each in one sentence and add a detail.

## Hear it

Marc is 55 and Sylvie is 52. They live next door to Mme Bouchard, and they use *vous* with her. Listen for the order of events, for the helper verbs, and for the negative.

```fr
Bouchard: Bonjour, Marc, bonjour, Sylvie! Alors, la fin de semaine? | Hello, Marc, hello, Sylvie! So, how was the weekend?
Marc: Nous sommes allés à Québec. Nous sommes partis vendredi après-midi. | We went to Quebec City. We left on Friday afternoon.
Sylvie: Nous sommes arrivés à six heures. Nous avons dormi dans une petite auberge dans le Vieux-Québec. | We arrived at six. We slept in a small inn in Old Quebec.
Bouchard: Et samedi? Vous avez fait quoi? | And Saturday? What did you do?
Marc: D'abord, nous avons visité le Vieux-Québec. Ensuite, nous avons mangé une poutine. | First, we visited Old Quebec. Then we ate a poutine.
Sylvie: L'après-midi, nous avons pris le traversier pour Lévis. Puis, nous avons fait une croisière sur le fleuve. | In the afternoon, we took the ferry to Lévis. Then we went on a cruise on the river.
Bouchard: Et dimanche? | And Sunday?
Marc: Dimanche, nous avons fait une excursion. Malheureusement, nous avons eu froid. Nous n'avons pas patiné. | On Sunday, we went on an excursion. Unfortunately, we were cold. We didn't skate.
Sylvie: Nous avons rencontré des touristes gentils. Enfin, nous sommes rentrés dimanche soir. | We met some friendly tourists. Finally, we came back on Sunday night.
Bouchard: Vous avez aimé? | Did you like it?
Sylvie: Nous avons adoré! | We loved it!
```

### A model monologue

Here is one person telling the whole trip alone, in about 90 seconds at a normal pace.

```fr
La fin de semaine dernière, je suis allé à Québec avec ma femme. | Last weekend, I went to Quebec City with my wife.
Nous sommes partis vendredi après-midi et nous sommes arrivés à six heures. | We left on Friday afternoon and arrived at six.
Samedi, d'abord, nous avons visité le Vieux-Québec. | On Saturday, first, we visited Old Quebec.
Ensuite, nous avons mangé une poutine. | Then we ate a poutine.
L'après-midi, nous avons pris le traversier et nous avons fait une croisière. | In the afternoon, we took the ferry and went on a cruise.
Dimanche, nous avons fait une excursion. | On Sunday, we went on an excursion.
Malheureusement, nous avons eu froid. | Unfortunately, we were cold.
Nous n'avons pas patiné. | We didn't skate.
Enfin, nous sommes rentrés dimanche soir. | Finally, we came back on Sunday night.
Nous avons adoré notre fin de semaine. | We loved our weekend.
```

## Try it

1. Put the verbs in the passé composé: *Nous (partir) vendredi.* *Nous (prendre) le traversier.* *Nous (avoir) froid.* *Nous (ne pas patiner).*
2. Which helper does each verb take, *avoir* or *être*: *visiter*, *arriver*, *manger*, *rentrer*, *faire*?
3. Write three questions a neighbour could ask you about your weekend trip.
4. Record yourself telling a real or imagined trip for 90 seconds, then count your passé composé verbs. You need at least 8, including 3 with *être*, 1 negative and 3 time words.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Nous sommes partis vendredi.* *Nous avons pris le traversier.* *Nous avons eu froid.* *Nous n'avons pas patiné.*
2. *Visiter*, *manger* and *faire* take *avoir*. *Arriver* and *rentrer* take *être*.
3. Examples: *Vous êtes allés où? Vous avez fait quoi? Vous êtes rentrés quand?*
4. Play your recording and tick each requirement as you hear it. If one is missing, add a sentence and record again.

</details>

## Common mistakes

Switching to the present in the middle of a story: *Samedi, nous avons visité Québec, puis nous mangeons.* Keep the passé composé until the story is over.

Using *avoir* with *partir*, *arriver* or *rentrer*. These take *être*.

Forgetting to agree the participle when *nous* or *elles* is feminine: *nous sommes parties* for two women.
