# Prendre, venir, boire and voir

After this lesson you can use the present of *prendre*, *venir*, *boire* and *voir*, the verbs built on them (*comprendre*, *apprendre*, *revenir*, *devenir*), and say that something has just happened with *venir de* and an infinitive.

Before this: [Regular -ir and -re verbs](01-regular-ir-and-re-verbs.md), [Aller and faire](../03-verbs-and-questions/05-aller-and-faire.md), [Making plans](../03-verbs-and-questions/08-making-plans.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

These four verbs are **irregular**: each has forms of its own that you learn as a set. They are among the most frequent verbs in French, so they are worth the effort. They also share a pattern. Each one uses up to three stems: one for *je, tu, il, elle, on*, one for *nous* and *vous*, and one for *ils* and *elles*. If you know the three stems, you know the verb.

### Prendre (to take)

*Prendre* is also the verb for eating and drinking in a café: *je prends un café* means "I'll have a coffee". It follows the *vendre* pattern, except that the *d* disappears in the plural and the *n* doubles in *ils prennent*.

| Subject | *prendre* | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| *je* | *prends* | /pʁɑ̃/ |
| *tu* | *prends* | /pʁɑ̃/ |
| *il / elle / on* | *prend* | /pʁɑ̃/ |
| *nous* | *prenons* | /pʁənɔ̃/ |
| *vous* | *prenez* | /pʁəne/ |
| *ils / elles* | *prennent* | /pʁɛn/ |

Two other verbs end in *-prendre* and take the same forms: *comprendre* (to understand) and *apprendre* (to learn). You have seen *je ne comprends pas* as a chunk since stage 1. Now you can say *tu comprends*, *nous comprenons* and *ils comprennent*. In the same way, *j'apprends le français* means "I'm learning French".

### Venir (to come)

| Subject | *venir* | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| *je* | *viens* | /vjɛ̃/ |
| *tu* | *viens* | /vjɛ̃/ |
| *il / elle / on* | *vient* | /vjɛ̃/ |
| *nous* | *venons* | /vənɔ̃/ |
| *vous* | *venez* | /vəne/ |
| *ils / elles* | *viennent* | /vjɛn/ |

You already know *Tu viens?* from Making plans. *Revenir* (to come back) and *devenir* (to become) take the same forms: *je reviens*, *il devient*. With a place, *venir de* means "to come from": *Je viens de Toronto*, *Tu viens d'où?* ("Where are you from?"). *De* becomes *d'* before a vowel.

### Boire (to drink)

| Subject | *boire* | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| *je* | *bois* | /bwa/ |
| *tu* | *bois* | /bwa/ |
| *il / elle / on* | *boit* | /bwa/ |
| *nous* | *buvons* | /byvɔ̃/ |
| *vous* | *buvez* | /byve/ |
| *ils / elles* | *boivent* | /bwav/ |

The stem changes from *boi-* to *buv-* in *nous* and *vous*, and the singular forms are all pronounced /bwa/. Listen for the final /v/ in *ils boivent* /il bwav/: it is what separates it from *il boit* /il bwa/.

### Voir (to see)

| Subject | *voir* | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| *je* | *vois* | /vwa/ |
| *tu* | *vois* | /vwa/ |
| *il / elle / on* | *voit* | /vwa/ |
| *nous* | *voyons* | /vwajɔ̃/ |
| *vous* | *voyez* | /vwaje/ |
| *ils / elles* | *voient* | /vwa/ |

Here the stem becomes *voy-* in *nous* and *vous*, and *ils voient* sounds the same as *il voit*. Watch out for that last pair: the sound does not tell you whether there is one person or several, so the subject has to.

### Venir de and an infinitive

*Venir de* followed by an infinitive says that something has just happened. This is the **recent past**, and it uses the present of *venir*: *Je viens de finir* means "I have just finished". English says "have just"; French says that you "come from" doing it. Compare *Il vient manger* ("he is coming to eat") with *Il vient de manger* ("he has just eaten"). One small word changes the time.

## Hear it

Two coworkers in a Gatineau office are going to lunch, and they use *tu*. Listen for *Qu'est-ce que tu prends?* and for the difference between *je viens* and *je viens de*.

```fr
Amélie: Tu viens manger avec nous? | Are you coming to eat with us?
Karim: Oui, je viens de finir mon projet. Qu'est-ce que tu prends? | Yes, I've just finished my project. What are you having?
Amélie: Moi, je prends la soupe. Et toi? | I'm having the soup. And you?
Karim: Je prends un sandwich. Tu bois un café? | I'm having a sandwich. Are you drinking a coffee?
Amélie: Non, je bois un thé. Les autres collègues boivent un jus. | No, I'm drinking tea. The other colleagues are drinking juice.
Karim: Tu vois Paul là-bas? Il vient de Toronto. | Do you see Paul over there? He's from Toronto.
Amélie: Oui. Il apprend le français et il comprend bien. | Yes. He's learning French and he understands well.
Karim: Super! On revient pour la réunion à une heure. | Great! We come back for the meeting at one.
```

## Try it

1. Write the right form: *Nous ___ (prendre) le métro.* *Ils ___ (boire) un jus.* *Tu ___ (venir) à la réunion?* *Vous ___ (voir) le parc.*
2. Say in French: "She has just left." "I've just finished."
3. What is the difference between *Il vient manger* and *Il vient de manger*?
4. Fill in *comprendre*: *Je ne ___ pas.* *Nous ___ bien.* *Elles ___ le français.*
5. Which two forms of *voir* sound the same, one singular and one plural?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Nous prenons le métro.* *Ils boivent un jus.* *Tu viens à la réunion?* *Vous voyez le parc.*
2. *Elle vient de partir.* *Je viens de finir.*
3. *Il vient manger* means "he is coming to eat". *Il vient de manger* means "he has just eaten".
4. *Je ne comprends pas.* *Nous comprenons bien.* *Elles comprennent le français.*
5. *Il voit* /il vwa/ and *ils voient* /il vwa/.

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Je prends un café. | I'll have a coffee.
Tu viens avec nous? | Are you coming with us?
Nous buvons un thé. | We are drinking tea.
Vous voyez le parc? | Do you see the park?
Je viens de finir. | I've just finished.
```

## Common mistakes

Writing *ils prenent* with one *n*. The plural has two: *ils prennent*, *ils viennent*.

Using *nous boivons* or *vous boivez*. The stem becomes *buv-*: *nous buvons*, *vous buvez*.

Using *venir de* with a past tense after it, as in *je viens de fini*. After *venir de*, the verb stays in the infinitive: *je viens de finir*.
