# Everyday nouns in Quebec

## The idea

Quebec French has words that France does not use, and words that both use with different meanings. When you hear one, it is not a mistake. It is the French of the place. This lesson teaches you to understand these words at once. For your own speaking and writing, the book keeps teaching standard French, and tells you which Quebec words are casual. The main source for every word below is Usito, a dictionary of French as used in Quebec and Canada, whose free pages show a label for Quebec and Canadian use (Q/C). The label means a word is characteristic of Quebec French and widely used in Canada. It does not mean the word is wrong or informal.

### Clothes for winter

Winter in Montréal is long, so these words come up from November on.

| Quebec word | Meaning | Usually in France | Source note |
|---|---|---|---|
| *une tuque* | a soft winter hat, often woollen, often with a pompon | *un bonnet* | Q/C ([Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/tuque)) |
| *des mitaines* | mittens: warm gloves with a space only for the thumb | *des moufles* | Q/C sense; in France *mitaines* usually means gloves with bare fingertips ([Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/mitaine)) |
| *un foulard* | a long strip of cloth worn round the neck against the cold | *une écharpe* | Q/C sense ([Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/foulard)) |
| *un gilet* | a knit top with short or long sleeves | a cardigan, or a sleeveless vest | Q/C sense ([Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/gilet)) |
| *une camisole* | an undershirt, or a light top with no sleeves | *un débardeur* | Q/C for the undershirt sense; the sleeveless top has no label ([Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/camisole)) |
| *un bas* | a sock (also a stocking) | *une chaussette* | Usito lists *bas* as a synonym of *chaussette* ([Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/chaussette)) |

*Foulard* and *gilet* are words you may already know in their standard sense. In Quebec, a *foulard* is the thick scarf you wear in winter, and in France it is usually a light scarf. A *gilet* in Quebec is a knit top with sleeves, whereas in standard French it is a cardigan or a vest. If you hear *gilet*, look at the situation to know which one is meant.

### Things at home

| Quebec word | Meaning | Usually in France | Source note |
|---|---|---|---|
| *une débarbouillette* | a small square towel for washing your face and body | *un gant de toilette* | Q/C ([Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/d%C3%A9barbouillette)) |
| *une chaudière* | a bucket | *un seau* | Q/C; some people criticise it, and *seau* is the safe word in writing ([Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/chaudi%C3%A8re)) |
| *une sacoche* | a handbag | *un sac à main* | Q/C and casual ([Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/sacoche)) |
| *un frigidaire* | a fridge | *un réfrigérateur*, or *un frigo* in speech | casual; *Frigidaire* is a brand name ([Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/frigidaire)) |
| *une facture* | a bill for goods or services | the same | neutral ([Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/facture)) |

*Un frigo* is the short, casual form of *un réfrigérateur*, and *frigidaire* is a casual word that comes from a brand name. In an email to a landlord or a company, write *réfrigérateur*. *Sacoche* meaning "handbag" is also casual, and Usito notes that some people object to it. The word *sac à main* is neutral everywhere.

### Two words for time

*Présentement* means "right now", and it has no label in Usito. It is a normal word for "at the moment", Usito says it was long seen as typical of Quebec but now appears in the European press too, and *en ce moment* is another way to say it ([Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/pr%C3%A9sentement)).

*Tantôt* is the tricky one. In Quebec it can mean "a little while ago" or "in a little while", on the same day. *À tantôt* means "see you later". Usito marks this use Q/C ([Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/tant%C3%B4t)). The verb tense usually tells you which: *Il est parti tantôt* means "he left a while ago", and *Je pars tantôt* means "I'm leaving in a while". Usito says these uses are old-fashioned in France.

### Words you already know

Earlier lessons gave you four more Quebec words, and the same sources apply. *Magasiner* (to shop) is marked Q/C in [Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/magasiner). *Courriel* (email) is a word of Quebec origin from the 1990s ([Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/courriel)). *Une fin de semaine* is the Quebec word for the weekend, from [Bonjour-hi and other Montreal greetings](../01-first-sounds-and-greetings/09-bonjour-hi-montreal-greetings.md). *Un char* meaning "a car" is casual. Usito marks it Q/C and lists it among uses that people criticise as non-standard, so use *voiture* when you write ([Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/char)).

## Hear it

Emma and Léo share an apartment in Montréal, and they use *tu*. It is November, and they are sorting winter clothes and laundry. Lines with a casual word are marked. Listen for *tantôt* and for how each Quebec word sits in an ordinary sentence.

```fr
Emma: Présentement, je mets les manteaux et les bottes dans la chambre. Tu cherches quelque chose? | Right now I'm putting the coats and boots in the bedroom. Are you looking for something?
Léo: Oui, ma tuque et mes mitaines. | Yes, my hat and my mittens.
Emma: Tantôt, j'ai vu ta tuque sur le lit. Tes mitaines sont dans le sac. | A while ago, I saw your hat on the bed. Your mittens are in the bag.
Léo: Merci! Et mon foulard? | Thanks! And my scarf?
Emma: Il est sur la chaise avec ton gilet. | It's on the chair with your sweater.
Léo: Ça, ce n'est pas mon gilet, c'est ta camisole! | That's not my sweater, it's your tank top!
Emma: Où est mon autre bas? Je ne trouve pas mon bas rouge. | Where's my other sock? I can't find my red sock.
Léo: J'ai besoin d'une débarbouillette. Où sont les débarbouillettes? | I need a washcloth. Where are the washcloths?
Emma: Elles sont dans la chaudière, près de la porte. | They're in the bucket, near the door.
Léo: Il y a une facture pour toi sur le frigidaire. {qc} | There's a bill for you on the fridge.
Emma: Ah, la facture du téléphone! Ma sacoche est dans la chambre. {qc} | Ah, the phone bill! My handbag is in the bedroom.
```

## Try it

1. Match each Quebec word to its usual France word: *tuque*, *mitaines*, *foulard*, *camisole*, *débarbouillette* with *débardeur*, *bonnet*, *moufles*, *gant de toilette*, *écharpe*.
2. You write to your landlord about the fridge. Which word do you use: *frigidaire*, *frigo* or *réfrigérateur*?
3. Say in French: "I'm putting my mittens in my bag."
4. What does *tantôt* mean in *Il est parti tantôt* and in *Je pars tantôt*?
5. Which of these look like standard words but have a Quebec meaning: *chaudière*, *gilet*, *facture*, *foulard*?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *tuque* with *bonnet*, *mitaines* with *moufles*, *foulard* with *écharpe*, *camisole* with *débardeur*, *débarbouillette* with *gant de toilette*.
2. *Réfrigérateur*. The other two are casual.
3. *Je mets mes mitaines dans mon sac.*
4. "A while ago" in the first sentence (past tense), "in a while" in the second (present tense).
5. *Chaudière* (bucket), *gilet* (knitted top) and *foulard* (winter scarf). *Facture* means the same in both.

</details>

```fr
Je cherche ma tuque et mes mitaines. | I'm looking for my hat and my mittens.

Présentement, je suis occupé. | Right now I'm busy.
À tantôt! | See you later!
```

## Common mistakes

Using *frigidaire* or *sacoche* in a formal email. Both are casual. Write *réfrigérateur* and *sac à main*.

Treating a Quebec word as an error in France, or the reverse. Neither variety is wrong. The same word can mean something else: a *gilet* is not the same garment in Montréal and in Paris.

Reading *tantôt* only as "later". In Quebec, the verb tense decides whether it means earlier or later.

## Key terms

- **Regionalism**: a word or meaning used in one region of the French-speaking world, such as *tuque* in Quebec.
- **Register**: how formal or casual a way of speaking or writing is.
