# Listening to a story

<span class="tag tag-listen">Listen</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can follow a short story told in the passé composé, find who did what and when, and use the sequence words (*d'abord*, *ensuite*, *soudain*, *finalement*) to keep track of the order.

Before this: [The passé composé with avoir](03-passe-compose-avoir.md), [The passé composé with être](05-passe-compose-etre.md), [Past time words](07-past-time-words.md). You need: headphones if you have them, and a pencil.

## The idea

A story told out loud goes fast, and you cannot go back as you can on a page. The skill is to catch the frame first (who, where, when) and then follow the events in order. Every story in this lesson uses only the tenses you know: the passé composé with *avoir* and *être*. The new words are few, and the tricky ones are listed here before you listen.

### Words to know before you listen

*Un portefeuille* is a wallet and *un sac à dos* is a backpack. *Un agent* is a worker at a station, and *un policier* is a police officer. *Un étranger* is a stranger, a person you do not know. The same word can also mean a foreigner, and the situation tells you which. *Une surprise* is a surprise.

Three small words tell you how the speaker feels about what happens. *Heureusement* means "luckily", *malheureusement* means "unfortunately" and *finalement* means "in the end". Listen for them: they often come right before the part of the story that matters.

### How to listen

Use these steps for each story. The first time you listen, do not read the French or the English.

1. Set the voice to slow speed and Quebec in the page header.
2. Play the whole story once. Listen only for the frame: who is speaking, where the story happens and when it starts.
3. Play it again and listen for the time words: *mardi dernier*, *à midi*, *le soir*, *le lendemain*. Write each one down as you hear it.
4. Play it a third time, line by line, and say each line out loud after the voice. You should hear a participle after almost every *j'ai* and *je suis*.
5. Answer the questions without looking back. Then open the translation and check.
6. Play it at normal speed. Notice which lines you now follow without effort.

If you lose the thread, do not stop. Wait for the next time word and pick the story up from there. Time words and sequence words are your handholds.

### What to listen for in the passé composé

The helper verb is short and fast: *j'ai* /ʒe/, *il a* /il a/, *je suis* /ʒə sɥi/, *elle est* /ɛl ɛ/. The participle carries the meaning, so train your ear to wait for it. In an -er verb the participle ends in /e/ (*téléphoné*, *regardé*). With *être*, the agreement is silent, so *je suis allé* and *je suis allée* sound the same.

## Hear it

Aïcha is a 26-year-old student in Montréal. She tells a friend about a bad day with a happy ending. Listen for the frame, then for *malheureusement*, *le soir* and *finalement*.

```fr
Aïcha: Mardi dernier, j'ai pris le métro à huit heures du matin. J'ai mis mon portefeuille dans mon sac à dos. | Last Tuesday, I took the metro at eight in the morning. I put my wallet in my backpack.
Aïcha: Je suis descendue à ma station et je suis allée à l'université. | I got off at my station and went to the university.
Aïcha: À midi, j'ai ouvert mon sac à dos. Malheureusement, je n'ai pas trouvé mon portefeuille. | At noon, I opened my backpack. Unfortunately, I didn't find my wallet.
Aïcha: J'ai cherché partout. Rien! J'ai eu peur. | I looked everywhere. Nothing! I was scared.
Aïcha: D'abord, j'ai téléphoné à ma mère. Ensuite, je suis allée voir un agent de la station. L'agent n'a rien trouvé. | First, I called my mother. Then I went to see a station agent. The agent didn't find anything.
Aïcha: Le soir, tout à coup, un étranger a téléphoné. Il a trouvé mon portefeuille dans le métro. Il a vu ma carte avec mon numéro de téléphone. | In the evening, all of a sudden, a stranger called. He found my wallet in the metro. He saw my card with my phone number.
Aïcha: Le lendemain, je suis allée à la station. L'étranger est venu avec mon portefeuille. Un policier a regardé ma carte. | The next day, I went to the station. The stranger came with my wallet. A police officer looked at my card.
Aïcha: Finalement, j'ai pris mon portefeuille. Heureusement, l'étranger a été très gentil. Quelle surprise! | In the end, I took my wallet. Luckily, the stranger was very nice. What a surprise!
```

The second recording is the scene at the station on Wednesday. Everyone uses *vous*. Listen for who says *voici* and who says *merci*.

```fr
Agent: Bonjour. Vous cherchez quelque chose? | Hello. Are you looking for something?
Aïcha: Oui, j'ai perdu mon portefeuille mardi. Un monsieur a téléphoné. | Yes, I lost my wallet on Tuesday. A man called.
Policier: Voilà le monsieur. Il a trouvé votre portefeuille dans le métro. | Here is the man. He found your wallet in the metro.
Étranger: Bonjour. Voici votre portefeuille. | Hello. Here is your wallet.
Aïcha: Merci beaucoup! Quelle surprise. | Thank you very much! What a surprise.
```

## Try it

1. When did Aïcha take the metro, and what did she put in her backpack?
2. At what time did she see that something was missing?
3. Who did she call first, and who did she go to see after that?
4. How did the stranger know her phone number?
5. What happened the next day? Give two things.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. Last Tuesday (*mardi dernier*) at eight in the morning. She put her wallet in the backpack.
2. At noon (*à midi*).
3. She called her mother first (*d'abord*), then went to see a station agent (*ensuite*).
4. He saw her card with her phone number.
5. She went to the station, and the stranger came with the wallet. A police officer looked at her card, and she took her wallet.

</details>

## Say it

Retell the story in six short sentences, in order, with the sequence words. These lines are a model.

```fr
Mardi dernier, j'ai perdu mon portefeuille. | Last Tuesday, I lost my wallet.
D'abord, j'ai téléphoné à ma mère. | First, I called my mother.
Ensuite, je suis allée à la station. | Then I went to the station.
Le soir, un étranger a téléphoné. | In the evening, a stranger called.
Le lendemain, j'ai pris mon portefeuille. | The next day, I took my wallet.
Heureusement, il a été gentil. | Luckily, he was kind.
```

## Common mistakes

Trying to understand every word on the first play. The first listen is for the frame, and the details come on later plays.

Writing the wrong helper because you could not hear it. When a participle ends in /e/ or /i/ and the helper is unclear, listen for *je suis* or *il est*, which go with movement verbs, and *j'ai* or *il a*, which go with the others.

Confusing *heureusement* with *heureux*. *Heureusement* is "luckily" and goes with an event. *Heureux* is "happy" and goes with a person.
