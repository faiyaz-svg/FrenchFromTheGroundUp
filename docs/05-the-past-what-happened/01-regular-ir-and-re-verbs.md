# Regular -ir and -re verbs

<span class="tag tag-grammar">Grammar</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can use the present of the regular -ir verbs (*finir*, *choisir*), the regular -re verbs (*vendre*, *attendre*) and three common verbs that look like -ir verbs but work differently (*partir*, *sortir*, *dormir*).

Before this: [Regular -er verbs](../03-verbs-and-questions/02-regular-er-verbs.md), [Saying no: negation](../03-verbs-and-questions/03-negation.md), [Asking questions](../03-verbs-and-questions/04-asking-questions.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

Stage 3 taught the largest family of French verbs, the ones that end in *-er* in the infinitive (the dictionary form, such as *parler*). Two more families are still missing: verbs in *-ir* and verbs in *-re*. Each family has its own set of endings. The part before the ending is the **stem**: *parl-* in *parler*, *fin-* in *finir*, *vend-* in *vendre*. To use a verb, you keep a stem and add the ending that matches the subject.

A verb is **regular** when it follows the pattern of its family with no surprises. An **irregular** verb has its own forms, and you learn them one by one. Everything in this lesson is regular, in the sense that each verb follows a pattern you can learn once and reuse.

### The -ir verbs of the *finir* type

This is the big -ir family, and it has a sound you can hear. In the plural, the stem grows by *-iss-*.

| Subject | *finir* (to finish) | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| *je* | *finis* | /fini/ |
| *tu* | *finis* | /fini/ |
| *il / elle / on* | *finit* | /fini/ |
| *nous* | *finissons* | /finisɔ̃/ |
| *vous* | *finissez* | /finise/ |
| *ils / elles* | *finissent* | /finis/ |

Listen to the three singular forms: they sound the same, because the final *s* and *t* are silent. The plural is where you hear the difference between *il finit* /il fini/ and *ils finissent* /il finis/: the ending *-ent* is silent, but the /s/ of *-iss-* is not. Other verbs with the same pattern are *choisir* (to choose), *réussir* (to succeed), *remplir* (to fill) and *réfléchir* (to think something over). Each one is learned once, by its infinitive, and then conjugated like *finir*.

### The -re verbs of the *vendre* type

Here the endings are `-s, -s, (nothing), -ons, -ez, -ent`, and the stem keeps its *d* all the way.

| Subject | *vendre* (to sell) | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| *je* | *vends* | /vɑ̃/ |
| *tu* | *vends* | /vɑ̃/ |
| *il / elle / on* | *vend* | /vɑ̃/ |
| *nous* | *vendons* | /vɑ̃dɔ̃/ |
| *vous* | *vendez* | /vɑ̃de/ |
| *ils / elles* | *vendent* | /vɑ̃d/ |

The *d* is silent in the three singular forms and pronounced in the plural, so *il vend* /il vɑ̃/ and *ils vendent* /il vɑ̃d/ differ by one consonant. Verbs in the same family: *attendre* (to wait for), *répondre* (to answer), *perdre* (to lose), *entendre* (to hear) and *descendre* (to go down, to get off). The form *il vend* has no ending at all, so don't add a *t* to it.

Two of these verbs work with small words that English does not use. *Attendre* takes its object directly: *j'attends l'autobus* means "I wait for the bus", with no word for "for". *Répondre* uses *à* before what you answer: *je réponds à la question*, *il répond aux passagers* (*à* + *les* = *aux*, as in [Aller and faire](../03-verbs-and-questions/05-aller-and-faire.md)).

### Partir, sortir and dormir

These three verbs end in *-ir* but do not take *-iss-*. In the singular they lose the consonant before the ending, and the endings are *-s, -s, -t*.

| Subject | *partir* (to leave) | *sortir* (to go out) | *dormir* (to sleep) |
|---|---|---|---|
| *je* | *pars* | *sors* | *dors* |
| *tu* | *pars* | *sors* | *dors* |
| *il / elle / on* | *part* | *sort* | *dort* |
| *nous* | *partons* | *sortons* | *dormons* |
| *vous* | *partez* | *sortez* | *dormez* |
| *ils / elles* | *partent* | *sortent* | *dorment* |

Listen for the opposite of the *finir* type: here the singular is short, /paʁ/, and the plural is longer, /paʁt/ for *ils partent*. You will see the same two-step pattern again with other verbs, so notice it now. A quick test for a new -ir verb: if the plural form has *-iss-* (*nous choisissons*), it is the *finir* type. If it does not (*nous partons*), it is a verb like *partir*, and a dictionary or the Conjugation drill will tell you.

### Negatives and questions

Nothing changes from stage 3. *Ne* and *pas* wrap the verb: *je ne finis pas*, *elle n'attend pas*. A question is a rising voice, or *est-ce que* in front: *Est-ce que tu dors bien?*

## Hear it

Yves is a bus driver in Laval. In the first scene, he meets a new colleague and they use *vous*. In the second scene, he talks to a friendly coworker and they use *tu*. Listen for the silent endings in *il finit* and for the sounds in *vous dormez* and *ils descendent*.

```fr
Yves: Bonjour, je m'appelle Yves. Je suis chauffeur d'autobus à Laval. | Hello, my name is Yves. I'm a bus driver in Laval.
Nadia: Enchantée. Moi, je suis nouvelle ici. Vous travaillez tôt? | Nice to meet you. I'm new here. Do you work early?
Yves: Oui, je pars de la maison à quatre heures du matin. Je finis à midi. | Yes, I leave home at four in the morning. I finish at noon.
Nadia: Vous dormez bien? | Do you sleep well?
Yves: Pas beaucoup. Je dors tôt, à huit heures du soir. | Not a lot. I go to sleep early, at eight in the evening.
Nadia: Et les passagers? | And the passengers?
Yves: Ils descendent au terminus. J'attends cinq minutes et je réponds aux questions. | They get off at the terminus. I wait five minutes and I answer questions.

Marc: Tu finis à quelle heure aujourd'hui? | What time do you finish today?
Yves: Je finis à midi. Et toi? | I finish at noon. And you?
Marc: Moi, je finis à quatre heures. Tu veux un café? | I finish at four. Do you want a coffee?
Yves: Oui, avec plaisir. Tu choisis le café. | Yes, with pleasure. You choose the café.
Marc: D'accord. Je réfléchis... Il y a un petit café près d'ici. | OK. Let me think... There's a small café near here.
```

## Try it

1. Write the right form: *Nous ___ (finir) à midi.* *Ils ___ (attendre) l'autobus.* *Tu ___ (dormir) bien.* *Elle ___ (répondre) à la question.*
2. Write each sentence with *ne ... pas*: *Je sors ce soir.* *Vous choisissez un livre.* *Il perd sa clé.*
3. Which sentence do you hear in the plural: *Il finit* or *Ils finissent*? Say both and tell which sound shows the difference.
4. Put the correct verb in: *Je ___ (partir) à huit heures.* *Ils ___ (partir) à huit heures.*
5. Say in French: "I'm waiting for the bus."

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Nous finissons à midi.* *Ils attendent l'autobus.* *Tu dors bien.* *Elle répond à la question.*
2. *Je ne sors pas ce soir.* *Vous ne choisissez pas un livre.* *Il ne perd pas sa clé.*
3. *Ils finissent* is the plural. You hear the /s/ of *-iss-* at the end: /il finis/. *Il finit* ends on /i/.
4. *Je pars à huit heures.* *Ils partent à huit heures.*
5. *J'attends l'autobus.* There is no word for "for".

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Je finis à midi. | I finish at noon.
Tu choisis le café. | You choose the café.
Il vend des billets. | He sells tickets.
Nous attendons l'autobus. | We are waiting for the bus.
Ils partent à huit heures. | They leave at eight o'clock.
```

## Common mistakes

Writing *ils finient* instead of *ils finissent*. The *-iss-* stays in all three plural forms of a *finir* verb.

Adding *t* to *vendre* in the singular, as in *il vendt*. The forms are *je vends*, *tu vends*, *il vend*.

Using *-iss-* with *partir*: *nous partissons* does not exist. Say *nous partons*, *nous sortons*, *nous dormons*.

## Key terms

- **Stem**: the part of a verb left when you remove *-er*, *-ir* or *-re*.
- **Regular verb**: a verb that follows the pattern of its family.
- **Irregular verb**: a verb with its own forms, learned one by one.
