# Telling about your weekend

<span class="tag tag-speak">Speak</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can tell someone what you did last weekend in five or six sentences, in order, using the passé composé and the linking words *d'abord*, *ensuite*, *puis* and *enfin*, and you can answer the usual follow-up questions.

Before this: [The passé composé with avoir](03-passe-compose-avoir.md), [The passé composé with être](05-passe-compose-etre.md), [Past time words](07-past-time-words.md). You need: a quiet place to speak out loud.

## The idea

On Monday morning, someone at work asks *Et ta fin de semaine?* and expects a short story. A good weekend story has a beginning, a middle and an end, and each step uses the passé composé. This lesson gives you the pieces: a frame to follow, the new activity words, the questions you will be asked, and a model by a man named Hugo.

### A five-step frame

1. When: *La fin de semaine dernière*, or *Samedi*, or *Vendredi soir*. (*Fin de semaine* is the Quebec word. In France, people say *le week-end*.)
2. Where: *Je suis allé(e) à Ottawa* or *Je suis resté(e) à la maison*.
3. What happened, in order: *D'abord*, *ensuite*, *puis*
4. How it ended: *Enfin, nous avons dansé* or *Dimanche soir, je suis rentré*.
5. What you thought: *J'ai adoré.* *J'ai beaucoup aimé.* *J'ai bien dormi.*

*Enfin* means "finally" here, and it closes a list of steps. You know it from Rhythm and intonation as a general word, and now you use it as the last step in a series.

### New activity words

Some of these verbs are regular -er verbs: *visiter* (to visit a place), *danser* (to dance), *nager* (to swim), *patiner* (to skate), *skier* (to ski) and *rencontrer* (to meet someone, often for the first time). They all take *avoir*: *nous avons dansé*, *j'ai nagé*, *elle a skié*. *Visiter* is for places, not people: *j'ai visité Québec*, but *j'ai vu ma tante*.

Some are things you do with *faire*, using the participle *fait* from Irregular past participles: *faire une randonnée* (to go on a hike), *faire une promenade* (to go for a walk) and *faire un pique-nique*. *Nous avons fait une randonnée.* For places, you now have *un lac* (lake), *une plage* (beach), *la campagne* (the countryside) and *un chalet*. A *chalet* in Quebec is a country house or cottage by a lake or in the mountains. The first sense in Usito is not the Quebec one: a traditional building of the Alps and the Jura. So the two meanings are different ([Usito: chalet](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/chalet)).

### The questions you will hear

A friend or a colleague asks, and you can answer each one with a full sentence.

| Question | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Qu'est-ce que tu as fait la fin de semaine dernière?* | What did you do last weekend? |
| *Tu es allé où?* | Where did you go? |
| *Avec qui?* | With whom? |
| *Et ensuite?* | And then? |
| *Tu as aimé?* | Did you like it? |

*Avec qui* uses the question word *qui* from Asking questions. To keep the story going, say *Et ensuite?* and listen for the next step.

### Keeping it natural

Short sentences are fine. Start each step with the helper verb, as in *j'ai mangé*, *j'ai nagé*, *nous avons dansé*. Your listener expects it, and it gives you a moment to find the participle. If you need to think, say *alors* (so, well). If you forget a word, use one you do know: *j'ai marché* (I walked) instead of *j'ai fait une randonnée*.

## Hear it

Hugo is a teacher in Sudbury. On Monday he tells his colleague Marie about a weekend in a chalet in the Laurentides, in the mountains north of Montréal. They are friends and use *tu*. Listen for the order of the steps and for the sound *j'ai* or *nous avons* at the start of each one.

```fr
Marie: Salut, Hugo! Qu'est-ce que tu as fait la fin de semaine dernière? | Hi, Hugo! What did you do last weekend?
Hugo: Je suis allé dans les Laurentides avec ma famille. Nous sommes restés dans un chalet près d'un lac. | I went to the Laurentians with my family. We stayed in a chalet near a lake.
Marie: Super! Vous avez fait quoi? | Great! What did you do?
Hugo: Vendredi soir, nous sommes arrivés tard. Samedi, d'abord, nous avons skié le matin. | On Friday night, we arrived late. On Saturday, first, we skied in the morning.
Hugo: Ensuite, nous avons mangé au chalet. Puis, l'après-midi, nous avons patiné sur le lac. | Then we ate at the chalet. Then, in the afternoon, we skated on the lake.
Marie: Et dimanche? | And Sunday?
Hugo: Dimanche, nous avons fait une randonnée. Enfin, nous avons rencontré un voisin sympa. | On Sunday, we went on a hike. Finally, we met a nice neighbour.
Marie: Tu as bien dormi? | Did you sleep well?
Hugo: Oui! Je suis rentré dimanche soir, fatigué, mais content. J'ai adoré la campagne. | Yes! I came home Sunday night, tired but happy. I loved the countryside.
```

## Try it

1. Put these sentences in order to make a story, and add *d'abord*, *ensuite* or *enfin* where it fits: *Nous avons nagé.* *Nous sommes arrivés au lac.* *Nous avons fait un pique-nique.*
2. Fill in the helper and the participle: *Nous ___ (aller) à la plage.* *Elle ___ (nager) une heure.* *J'___ (patiner) sur le lac.*
3. How do you ask a friend "Where did you go last weekend?" and "With whom?"
4. Say your own weekend in four sentences, using *avoir* once and *être* once.
5. Say in French: "We had a picnic at the beach."

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Nous sommes arrivés au lac. D'abord, nous avons nagé. Ensuite, nous avons fait un pique-nique.* Other orders work if the story makes sense.
2. *Nous sommes allés à la plage.* *Elle a nagé une heure.* *J'ai patiné sur le lac.*
3. *Tu es allé où la fin de semaine dernière?* *Avec qui?* (A woman asking a woman says *Tu es allée où?*, with the same sound.)
4. Example: *Samedi, je suis allée au parc. J'ai marché une heure. Ensuite, j'ai mangé avec une amie. J'ai adoré.*
5. *Nous avons fait un pique-nique à la plage.*

</details>

## Say it

Read this role play aloud with a friend, or alone: say your own answers to the questions. The lines below are one possible set.

```fr
Qu'est-ce que tu as fait la fin de semaine dernière? | What did you do last weekend?
Samedi, je suis allé au lac avec des amis. | On Saturday, I went to the lake with some friends.
D'abord, nous avons nagé. Ensuite, nous avons fait un pique-nique. | First, we swam. Then we had a picnic.
Enfin, nous avons fait une promenade. | Finally, we went for a walk.
J'ai adoré. | I loved it.
```

## Common mistakes

Using *visiter* for a person: *j'ai visité ma tante*. Use *visiter* for places and *voir* for people: *j'ai vu ma tante*.

Using *j'ai allé* for "I went". Say *je suis allé*.

Starting every sentence with *et*: *et j'ai mangé, et j'ai dormi*. Use the linking words: *d'abord*, *ensuite*, *puis*, *enfin*.

## In Quebec

In Quebec, *un chalet* is a cottage in the country, usually near water or in the mountains. People often go to the chalet on *la fin de semaine*, and you can say *Je suis allé au chalet* without any other explanation. The Usito entry linked above gives the Quebec sense and the European one.
