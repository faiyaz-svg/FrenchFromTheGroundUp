# The passé composé: no, questions and agreement

<span class="tag tag-grammar">Grammar</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can say what you did not do, with *ne ... pas*, *jamais*, *rien*, *personne* and *pas encore*, ask questions in the passé composé, and know when the participle changes and when it does not.

Before this: [The passé composé with avoir](03-passe-compose-avoir.md), [The passé composé with être](05-passe-compose-etre.md), [Saying no: negation](../03-verbs-and-questions/03-negation.md), [Asking questions](../03-verbs-and-questions/04-asking-questions.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

In the passé composé, the verb has two parts, so you have to decide where the extra words go. The rule is simple: the negative words and the questions treat the helper verb (*avoir* or *être*) as "the verb". The participle comes after.

### Negation: ne and pas around the helper

*Ne* goes before the helper and *pas* goes after it, with the participle last: *Je n'ai pas mangé.* *Elle n'est pas arrivée.* *Ne* becomes *n'* before a vowel, which is the case for *ai*, *as*, *a*, *avons*, *est* and *êtes*.

After *pas*, the articles *un*, *une* and *des* become *de* or *d'*, as in stage 3: *J'ai vu un film* becomes *Je n'ai pas vu de film*.

| Meaning | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| not | *ne ... pas* | *Je n'ai pas dormi.* |
| not yet | *ne ... pas encore* | *Je n'ai pas encore fini.* |
| never | *ne ... jamais* | *Il n'a jamais voyagé en train.* |
| nothing | *ne ... rien* | *Je n'ai rien mangé.* |
| nobody | *ne ... personne* | *Je n'ai vu personne.* |
| no more | *ne ... plus* | *Il n'a plus travaillé ici.* |

Look at the position of *rien* and *personne*. *Rien* sits between the helper and the participle (*je n'ai rien vu*), like *pas* and *jamais*. *Personne* goes after the participle (*je n'ai vu personne*). Memory aid: *rien* behaves like *pas* and *jamais*, and *personne* goes last. *Pas encore* stays together: *je ne suis pas encore parti*.

In speech, many people drop *ne*. *J'ai pas mangé* is heard every day and is casual. In writing for work or school, keep *ne*.

### Questions

You have three ways to ask a question, and all three still work. The first is a rising voice: *Tu as mangé?* The second puts *est-ce que* in front: *Est-ce que tu as mangé?* The third, inversion, turns the helper and the pronoun around, with a hyphen: *Avez-vous mangé?* *Êtes-vous arrivé à midi?* You will hear inversion in formal and written French, and with *vous* in particular, so you need to understand it, but you can use the first two in everyday speech.

A question word goes in front: *Qu'est-ce que tu as fait?* ("What did you do?"), *Quand est-ce que tu es arrivé?*, *Pourquoi est-ce qu'il est parti?* In all of them, the participle stays in last place.

Short adverbs sit between the helper and the participle: *Tu as déjà mangé?* ("Have you eaten already?") and *Elle a bien dormi.* Place words such as *partout* ("everywhere") go after: *J'ai cherché partout.* *Quelque part* means "somewhere" and *nulle part* means "nowhere": *Je n'ai trouvé mon téléphone nulle part.*

### When does the participle change?

The rule is one line, and you know it already.

1. With *avoir*, the participle does not agree with the subject: *elle a mangé*, *elles ont mangé*.
2. With *être*, the participle agrees with the subject: *elle est partie*, *elles sont parties*.

That is all you need for now. A third case, with *avoir* and a direct object placed before the verb, exists in French and comes with the object pronouns in Direct object pronouns<!-- link: ../07-pronouns-and-getting-things-done/01-direct-object-pronouns.md -->. You will not meet it before then.

### How often

Four words say how often. *Tous les jours* means "every day", *parfois* and *de temps en temps* both mean "sometimes" ("from time to time"), and *rarement* means "rarely". They go with the present for habits: *Je téléphone à ma mère tous les jours.* *Il voyage rarement.* *Parfois, je prends le métro.* Habits in the past have their own tense in stage 6.

## Hear it

The first scene takes place at the airport. A traveller and a border officer in Montréal use *vous*, so the officer asks questions with inversion. In the second scene, two friends use *tu* and *est-ce que*. Listen for *n'ai rien*, *n'as vu personne* and *n'ai pas encore*.

```fr
Agent: Bonjour, madame. Vous êtes arrivée de Paris? | Hello, madam. Did you arrive from Paris?
Voyageuse: Oui, je suis arrivée ce matin. | Yes, I arrived this morning.
Agent: Avez-vous voyagé seule? | Did you travel alone?
Voyageuse: Oui, j'ai voyagé seule. J'ai travaillé et j'ai vu ma famille. | Yes, I travelled alone. I worked and I saw my family.
Agent: Avez-vous quelque chose à déclarer? | Do you have anything to declare?
Voyageuse: Non, je n'ai rien à déclarer. Voilà mon passeport. | No, I have nothing to declare. Here is my passport.
Agent: Merci. Bienvenue à Montréal. | Thank you. Welcome to Montreal.

Sam: Tu es sorti samedi? | Did you go out on Saturday?
Lou: Non, je ne suis pas sorti. Je suis resté à la maison. Je n'ai rien fait. | No, I didn't go out. I stayed at home. I did nothing.
Sam: Tu n'as vu personne? | You didn't see anyone?
Lou: Non, personne. Mais j'ai regardé un film. Est-ce que tu as déjà vu ce film? | No, nobody. But I watched a movie. Have you already seen this movie?
Sam: Non, je n'ai pas encore vu ce film. | No, I haven't seen it yet.
```

## Try it

1. Make each sentence negative with *ne ... pas*: *J'ai mangé.* *Elle est arrivée.* *Nous avons vu un film.*
2. Answer with the word in brackets: *Tu as mangé quelque chose?* (rien) *Tu as vu quelqu'un?* (personne) *Tu as déjà voyagé en train?* (jamais)
3. Change into a question with *est-ce que*: *Tu as dormi.* *Elles sont arrivées.* Then ask *Vous avez fini* with inversion.
4. Fill in the ending, or leave it blank if nothing changes: *Elle n'est pas parti___.* *Ils n'ont pas mangé___.*
5. Say in French: "I haven't finished yet."

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Je n'ai pas mangé.* *Elle n'est pas arrivée.* *Nous n'avons pas vu de film.* (*un* becomes *de* after *pas*.)
2. *Je n'ai rien mangé.* *Je n'ai vu personne.* *Je n'ai jamais voyagé en train.*
3. *Est-ce que tu as dormi?* *Est-ce qu'elles sont arrivées?* *Avez-vous fini?*
4. *Elle n'est pas partie.* *Ils n'ont pas mangé.* (With *être* the participle agrees. With *avoir* it does not.)
5. *Je n'ai pas encore fini.*

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Je n'ai pas dormi. | I didn't sleep.
Je n'ai rien mangé. | I didn't eat anything.
Je n'ai vu personne. | I didn't see anyone.
Est-ce que tu as déjà mangé? | Have you already eaten?
Qu'est-ce que vous avez fait? | What did you do?
```

## Common mistakes

Putting *pas* after the participle: *j'ai mangé pas*. Put *ne* before the helper and *pas* right after it: *je n'ai pas mangé*.

Putting *personne* in the middle: *je n'ai personne vu*. It goes last: *je n'ai vu personne*.

Keeping *un* after *pas*: *Je n'ai pas vu un film*. It becomes *de*: *Je n'ai pas vu de film*.

## In Quebec

Casual Quebec speech adds a small particle, *-tu*, to a question, so *Tu as mangé?* becomes the line below. It is spoken and casual, and you can hear it with friends. You do not write it.

```fr
T'as-tu mangé? {qc} | Have you eaten?
```
 The particle is taught in [Casual questions in Quebec](../03-verbs-and-questions/10-quebec-casual-questions.md).
