# The passé composé with avoir

<span class="tag tag-grammar">Grammar</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can say what you did, in the passé composé, with any regular verb that takes *avoir*, and put the time words *hier soir*, *d'abord*, *ensuite* and *puis* around it.

## The idea

Until now you have talked about the present and the near future. The **passé composé** is the tense you use in conversation to say what happened and is now finished: "I worked", "I have eaten", "she called". It is called *composé* ("composed") because it has two parts: a helper verb and a **past participle** (*le participe passé*). The helper is called an **auxiliary verb** (*un verbe auxiliaire*). For most verbs the auxiliary is *avoir*, and you already know its present: *j'ai, tu as, il a, nous avons, vous avez, ils ont*.

English has the same two-part build ("I have worked"), but English also has a separate form for finished events ("I worked"). French has one form for both: *j'ai travaillé* means "I worked", "I have worked" and "I did work".

### Step 1: the present of avoir

Write the right form of *avoir* for the subject: *j'ai*, *tu as*, *il a*, *elle a*, *on a*, *nous avons*, *vous avez*, *ils ont*, *elles ont*. Remember the liaison in *nous avons* /nuz avɔ̃/, *vous avez* /vuz ave/ and *ils ont* /ilz ɔ̃/.

### Step 2: the past participle

Take the infinitive and change the ending:

| Infinitive ends in | Participle ends in | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *-er* | *-é* | *parler* gives *parlé* |
| *-ir* (the *finir* type and *dormir*) | *-i* | *finir* gives *fini*, *dormir* gives *dormi* |
| *-re* | *-u* | *vendre* gives *vendu* |

The participle of an -er verb sounds the same as its infinitive: *parler* and *parlé* are both /paʁle/. So *je vais parler* ("I'm going to speak") and *j'ai parlé* ("I spoke") sound alike, and the helper verb is what tells you the time. Verbs like *manger* keep their *e* in the participle: *j'ai mangé*, with a /ʒ/ sound.

### Step 3: put the two parts together

The auxiliary comes first, then the participle: *J'ai mangé*, *tu as fini*, *il a vendu*, *nous avons attendu*, *vous avez répondu*, *elles ont dormi*.

| Subject | *parler* | *finir* | *vendre* |
|---|---|---|---|
| *je* | *j'ai parlé* | *j'ai fini* | *j'ai vendu* |
| *tu* | *tu as parlé* | *tu as fini* | *tu as vendu* |
| *il / elle / on* | *il a parlé* | *il a fini* | *il a vendu* |
| *nous* | *nous avons parlé* | *nous avons fini* | *nous avons vendu* |
| *vous* | *vous avez parlé* | *vous avez fini* | *vous avez vendu* |
| *ils / elles* | *ils ont parlé* | *ils ont fini* | *ils ont vendu* |

Short adverbs go between the two parts: *j'ai bien dormi*, *Tu as déjà mangé?*, *elle a trop travaillé*. Everything else comes after the participle: *j'ai regardé un film hier soir*.

### Telling a sequence

To say what happened first, then next, you need linking words. *D'abord* means "first", *ensuite* and *puis* both mean "then", and *avant* means "before". *Une fois* means "once" or "one time", and *la dernière fois* means "the last time". For when, use *hier matin* (yesterday morning) and *hier soir* (last night). *Hier* (yesterday) is from [Days, dates and big numbers](../03-verbs-and-questions/06-days-dates-and-big-numbers.md). Example: *La dernière fois, j'ai perdu ma clé.*

*D'abord, j'ai travaillé. Ensuite, j'ai mangé. Puis, j'ai regardé un match.* Each sentence is a finished event, one after another.

Written stories in some books use a third past form that is rarely spoken. You do not need it for conversation, and a later stage covers it.

## Hear it

Inès is a pastry chef in Québec City. She and her friend Léo use *tu*. Listen for *j'ai* at the start of most lines, and for the way the participle sounds the same as the infinitive.

```fr
Léo: Alors, tu as travaillé hier soir? | So, did you work last night?
Inès: Oui, j'ai travaillé jusqu'à huit heures. J'ai préparé trois gâteaux. | Yes, I worked until eight. I prepared three cakes.
Léo: Et après? | And afterwards?
Inès: D'abord, j'ai mangé une soupe. Ensuite, j'ai regardé un film. | First, I ate a soup. Then I watched a movie.
Léo: Tu as téléphoné à ta mère? | Did you call your mother?
Inès: Oui, j'ai téléphoné à ma mère. Elle a répondu tout de suite. | Yes, I called my mother. She answered right away.
Léo: Et tu as bien dormi? | And did you sleep well?
Inès: Oui, j'ai dormi huit heures. | Yes, I slept eight hours.
```

## Try it

1. Write the past participle of each verb: *regarder*, *choisir*, *attendre*, *voyager*, *réussir*.
2. Fill in the passé composé: *Nous ___ (manger) à midi.* *Elle ___ (finir) hier.* *Ils ___ (attendre) une heure.* *Tu ___ (téléphoner) à Marc?*
3. Put the words in order: *regardé / hier / j'ai / soir / un film*
4. Say in French: "I slept eight hours." "We sold the car."
5. Which sentence is about the future and which is about the past: *Je vais parler* or *J'ai parlé*?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *regardé*, *choisi*, *attendu*, *voyagé*, *réussi*.
2. *Nous avons mangé à midi.* *Elle a fini hier.* *Ils ont attendu une heure.* *Tu as téléphoné à Marc?*
3. *J'ai regardé un film hier soir.*
4. *J'ai dormi huit heures.* *Nous avons vendu la voiture.*
5. *Je vais parler* is the future (the near future from stage 4). *J'ai parlé* is the past.

</details>

## Say it

```fr
J'ai travaillé hier soir. | I worked last night.
D'abord, j'ai mangé. | First, I ate.
Ensuite, nous avons regardé un film. | Then we watched a movie.
Puis, il a téléphoné. | Then he called.
Vous avez bien dormi? | Did you sleep well?
```

## Common mistakes

Using the infinitive instead of the participle: *j'ai parler*. The two sound the same, so check the spelling: after *j'ai* write *-é*, *-i* or *-u*.

Putting the participle before *avoir*, or leaving out *avoir*. Say *j'ai mangé*, not *mangé* alone and not *je mangé*.

Using *être* as the helper because English says "I am finished". With the verbs in this lesson, *avoir* is the helper. The verbs that take *être* come in [The passé composé with être](05-passe-compose-etre.md).

## Key terms

- **Passé composé**: the tense for finished events, built from an auxiliary verb and a past participle.
- **Auxiliary verb**: the helper verb in a compound tense, *avoir* or *être*.
- **Past participle**: the verb form used with an auxiliary, such as *parlé*, *fini* or *vendu*.
