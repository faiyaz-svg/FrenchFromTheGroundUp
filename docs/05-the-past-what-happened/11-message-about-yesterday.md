# A message about yesterday

<span class="tag tag-write">Write</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can write a short message to a friend, of 50 to 70 words, that tells what you did yesterday in the passé composé, with time words, one movement verb and one negative.

Before this: [The passé composé with avoir](03-passe-compose-avoir.md), [The passé composé with être](05-passe-compose-etre.md), [The passé composé: no, questions and agreement](06-pc-negation-questions-agreement.md), [Past time words](07-past-time-words.md), [Texting to make plans](../03-verbs-and-questions/09-texting-to-make-plans.md). You need: a keyboard that types accents, or a pencil.

## The idea

A friend writes *Et ta journée?* and you answer in a few lines. This kind of message is casual, so you use *tu*, short sentences and no formal opening. It still has a shape: a greeting, a few events in order, one feeling and a question back. This lesson gives you the shape, a model and a template.

### Two words for parts of a day

*Une journée* is a day seen as time that passes: *une bonne journée*, *toute la journée* (all day). It is different from *un jour*, which is a day as a unit, as in *trois jours*. In the same way, *une matinée* is the morning as a stretch of time (*toute la matinée*, all morning), and *un matin* is a point in time (*hier matin*). *Un après-midi* is the afternoon. Use *hier matin* and *hier soir* for "yesterday morning" and "last night", and use *matinée* and *journée* to describe how a period went: *Ma matinée a été longue.*

### Two new adjectives, and agreement

*Fatigant* means "tiring" and *amusant* means "fun, amusing". They describe things, not people. *Une journée fatigante* has the feminine *-e*, because *journée* is feminine, as in Adjectives and agreement. *Un match amusant* has the masculine form. Compare *fatigué* (tired, a person) and *fatigant* (tiring, a thing or a day).

### Two verbs that you will need

*Oublier* (to forget) is a regular -er verb: *j'ai oublié mon téléphone*. *Rater* means "to miss" in the sense of "not catch": *j'ai raté l'autobus*. It also means "to fail", as in *rater un examen*. Both take *avoir*.

### A shape for the message

1. Greeting: *Salut Léa!*
2. Answer in one phrase: *Ma journée? Fatigante, mais amusante!*
3. Two or three events in order, each with a time word: *Hier matin*, *ensuite*, *le soir*
4. One negative: *Je n'ai pas cuisiné.*
5. A question back and a casual close: *Et toi? Bises.*

The model below follows these five steps. Notice that it has one *être* verb (*je suis arrivé*, *je suis rentré*) and one negative (*je n'ai pas cuisiné*).

### Check what you wrote

Read your message once for each of these:

1. Does every passé composé verb have a helper (*j'ai*, *je suis*)?
2. Did you write *-é*, *-i* or *-u* on the participles, and the right irregular ones (*fait*, *pris*, *vu*)?
3. Does the participle of each *être* verb agree with you (*je suis rentrée* if you are a woman)?
4. Do you have at least three time words?

## Hear it

Samir answers Léa's question *Et ta journée?* They are friends and use *tu*. Listen for the helper verbs and the time words as you read the message aloud, or play it.

```fr
Salut Léa! | Hi Léa!
Ma journée? Fatigante, mais amusante! | My day? Tiring, but fun!
Hier matin, j'ai raté l'autobus. Je suis arrivé en retard au bureau. | Yesterday morning, I missed the bus. I arrived late at the office.
Ensuite, j'ai travaillé jusqu'à midi et j'ai mangé avec Paul. | Then I worked until noon and ate with Paul.
L'après-midi, j'ai oublié mon téléphone au bureau. | In the afternoon, I forgot my phone at the office.
Le soir, je suis rentré à 18 h 30. Je n'ai pas cuisiné. J'ai regardé un match et j'ai dormi tôt. | In the evening, I got home at 6:30. I didn't cook. I watched a game and went to sleep early.
Et toi? Bises, Samir | And you? Hugs, Samir
```

Write times as in [Telling the time](../03-verbs-and-questions/07-telling-time.md): *18 h 30*, with a space before and after *h*.

## Common mistakes

Mixing *matin* and *matinée*, or *jour* and *journée*. Use *matin* and *jour* for a point or a count (*hier matin*, *trois jours*), and *matinée* and *journée* for the stretch of time (*toute la journée*).

Writing *fatigant* for a tired person. A person who is tired is *fatigué*. *Fatigant* describes the thing or the day that tires you.

Forgetting the accent: *j'ai oublie*, *j'ai rate*. The participles end in *é*: *oublié*, *raté*.

## In Quebec

A casual message in Quebec looks the same as above. Times are written with a space around the *h*, as in *18 h 30*, and [Telling the time](../03-verbs-and-questions/07-telling-time.md) gives the OQLF source for that rule.
