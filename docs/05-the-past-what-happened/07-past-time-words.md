# Past time words

<span class="tag tag-words">Words</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can place an event in time with *hier*, *avant-hier*, *la semaine dernière*, *il y a trois jours*, *la veille* and *le lendemain*, and link the steps of a story with *soudain* and *tout à coup*.

## The idea

A story in the past needs a clock. The passé composé tells you that something is finished, and the time word tells you when. This lesson gives you the words in two groups. The first group counts back from today. The second group counts from another moment in the story.

### Counting back from now

These words all take today as the starting point.

| French | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *hier* | yesterday |
| *avant-hier* | the day before yesterday |
| *la semaine dernière* | last week |
| *le mois dernier* | last month |
| *l'année dernière* | last year |
| *il y a trois jours* | three days ago |
| *récemment*, *dernièrement* | recently |
| *l'autre jour* | the other day |

*Dernier* (last) comes after the word for a period of time: *la semaine dernière*, *le mois dernier*, *l'année dernière*. For a weekday, you can say *samedi dernier*, "last Saturday". A weekday on its own often means the most recent one: *Samedi, je suis allé au parc* means "On Saturday I went to the park". With *le* in front, it means a habit: *Le samedi, je vais au parc* means "On Saturdays I go to the park".

### Il y a with a time

You know *il y a* as "there is": *il y a un parc*. With a length of time and a past event, the same two words mean "ago": *Il y a trois jours, j'ai vu Julie.* *J'ai habité à Toronto il y a deux ans.* The phrase can come first or last in the sentence. This is only a first look. Depuis, il y a and pendant<!-- link: ../08-comparing-duration-and-settling-in/03-depuis-il-y-a-pendant.md --> explains the whole family, with *depuis* and *pendant*.

### Counting from another moment in the story

*Hier* and *demain* count from today. When you tell a story about the past, you need words that count from a day in the story. *La veille* means "the day before" that moment, and *le lendemain* means "the next day". *Je suis arrivé lundi. Le lendemain, j'ai travaillé* means "I arrived on Monday. The next day, I worked", and the next day is Tuesday, whatever today is. Learn them as two partners: the day before and the day after.

*À cette époque* means "at that time" and sets the scene. You will mostly hear it before a past tense taught in stage 6. *Autrefois* means "in the past, in earlier times". *Longtemps* means "for a long time": *Nous avons parlé longtemps.* *Un souvenir* is a memory.

### Moves in the story

Stage 5 has already given you *d'abord, ensuite, puis, enfin*. Two more words show a sudden change: *soudain* (suddenly) and *tout à coup* (all of a sudden). They usually go at the start of the sentence, followed by a comma, and the event that follows is in the passé composé: *Soudain, la neige est tombée.*

A short way to see the pieces: *d'abord* opens, *ensuite* and *puis* continue, *soudain* breaks, *enfin* closes.

## Hear it

Mme Lavoie is 72. She tells her granddaughter, in the *tu* form, about her first winter in Montréal. Listen for *il y a cinquante ans*, *la veille*, *le lendemain* and *soudain*. Each event is in the passé composé, because this lesson tells a series of finished events.

```fr
Grand-mère: Il y a cinquante ans, je suis arrivée à Montréal en novembre. | Fifty years ago, I arrived in Montreal in November.
La veille de mon départ, j'ai téléphoné à ma mère. | The day before I left, I called my mother.
Le lendemain de mon arrivée, j'ai cherché un travail. | The day after I arrived, I looked for a job.
D'abord, j'ai habité chez ma cousine. | At first, I lived at my cousin's.
J'ai attendu une réponse deux semaines. | I waited two weeks for an answer.
Soudain, la neige est tombée. Tout à coup, j'ai eu froid. | Suddenly, the snow fell. All of a sudden, I was cold.
Un jour, un voisin est venu. Il a offert un thé à ma cousine. | One day, a neighbour came. He offered my cousin a tea.
Nous avons parlé longtemps avec lui. | We talked for a long time with him.
Dernièrement, j'ai trouvé une photo de ce premier hiver. C'est un souvenir. | Recently, I found a photo of that first winter. It's a memory.

Karim: Tu as vu Julie récemment? | Have you seen Julie recently?
Nina: Oui, je suis allée chez elle samedi dernier. Et toi? | Yes, I went to her place last Saturday. And you?
Karim: Moi, j'ai téléphoné à Julie avant-hier. | I called Julie the day before yesterday.
Nina: Il y a trois jours, j'ai vu son frère. | Three days ago I saw her brother.
```

## Try it

1. Write the French: "yesterday", "the day before yesterday", "last month", "two years ago".
2. Fill in: *Je suis arrivée lundi. Le ___, j'ai travaillé.* *J'ai fini mon travail jeudi. La ___, j'ai commencé.* (Hint for the second line: *commencé* means "started".)
3. What does *il y a* mean in *Il y a un parc* and in *Il y a deux jours, j'ai vu un film*?
4. Put the words in order of what happens first: *Soudain*, *d'abord*, *enfin*, *ensuite*.
5. Write three sentences about last week with three different time words from this lesson, with a passé composé verb in each.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *hier*, *avant-hier*, *le mois dernier*, *il y a deux ans*.
2. *Le lendemain* (the day after Monday). *La veille* (the day before Thursday).
3. "There is" in the first sentence, and "ago" in the second.
4. *D'abord*, *ensuite*, *soudain*, *enfin*. *Soudain* can fall at any point where something sudden happens.
5. Example: *La semaine dernière, j'ai travaillé tous les jours. Avant-hier, je suis allé au cinéma. Il y a trois jours, j'ai vu un ami.*

## Say it

```fr
Hier, j'ai travaillé. | Yesterday I worked.
La semaine dernière, nous sommes allés au parc. | Last week we went to the park.
Il y a trois jours, j'ai vu un film. | Three days ago I saw a movie.
Soudain, il a ouvert la porte. | Suddenly, he opened the door.
Le lendemain, elle est partie. | The next day, she left.
```

## Common mistakes

Putting *dernière* before the noun: *la dernière semaine* means "the final week". For "last week" say *la semaine dernière*.

Using *hier* for the day before a moment in a story. Say *la veille*.

Using *depuis* for "ago": *depuis trois jours, j'ai vu Julie*. For "ago" use *il y a*.

## Key terms

- **Time marker**: a word or phrase that places a verb in time, such as *hier* or *il y a trois jours*.
