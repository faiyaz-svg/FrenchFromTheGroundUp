# The passé composé with être

<span class="tag tag-grammar">Grammar</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can say what happened with the verbs of movement and change that take *être* as their helper (*aller*, *arriver*, *partir*, *sortir* and others), and make the participle agree with the subject.

Before this: [The passé composé with avoir](03-passe-compose-avoir.md), [Irregular past participles](04-irregular-participles.md), [Adjectives and agreement](../04-people-places-and-directions/01-adjectives-agreement.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

Most verbs form the passé composé with *avoir*. A small group uses *être* instead: *je suis allé*, *elle est arrivée*. These verbs mostly describe moving from one place to another, or changing state: going, coming, entering, falling, being born, dying. The rest of the build is the same as before: the present of the helper, then the past participle.

### The verbs that take être

The list is short enough to learn. Most of the verbs come in pairs of opposites.

| Verb | Meaning | Participle |
|---|---|---|
| *aller* | to go | *allé* |
| *venir* | to come | *venu* |
| *arriver* | to arrive | *arrivé* |
| *partir* | to leave | *parti* |
| *entrer* | to go in | *entré* |
| *sortir* | to go out | *sorti* |
| *monter* | to go up | *monté* |
| *descendre* | to go down, to get off | *descendu* |
| *rester* | to stay | *resté* |
| *tomber* | to fall | *tombé* |
| *retourner* | to go back to a place | *retourné* |
| *rentrer* | to go home, to come back in | *rentré* |
| *revenir* | to come back | *revenu* |
| *devenir* | to become | *devenu* |
| *naître* | to be born | *né* |
| *mourir* | to die | *mort* |
| *passer* | to pass by, to stop by | *passé* |

You know the present of most of these already. *Entrer*, *rentrer*, *arriver*, *monter*, *tomber*, *retourner* and *passer* are regular -er verbs. *Naître* and *mourir* are used for now only in the passé composé: *je suis né*, *il est mort*. A common memory aid in English is the made-up name DR MRS VANDERTRAMP, built from the first letters of sixteen of them: *devenir, revenir, monter, rester, sortir, venir, aller, naître, descendre, entrer, rentrer, tomber, retourner, arriver, mourir, partir*. Say it if it helps. *Passer* is the seventeenth.

### Building the tense

Use the present of *être*, as in stage 1, and the participle.

| Subject | *aller* | *partir* |
|---|---|---|
| *je* | *je suis allé(e)* | *je suis parti(e)* |
| *tu* | *tu es allé(e)* | *tu es parti(e)* |
| *il* | *il est allé* | *il est parti* |
| *elle* | *elle est allée* | *elle est partie* |
| *nous* | *nous sommes allés / allées* | *nous sommes partis / parties* |
| *vous* | *vous êtes allé(e)(s)* | *vous êtes parti(e)(s)* |
| *ils* | *ils sont allés* | *ils sont partis* |
| *elles* | *elles sont allées* | *elles sont parties* |

### Agreement with the subject

With *être*, the participle works like an adjective from Adjectives and agreement: it **agrees** with the subject, which means it changes to match the subject's gender and number. For a woman or a feminine subject, add *-e*: *elle est allée*. For several people, add *-s*: *ils sont allés*. For several women, add both: *elles sont allées*. A mixed group takes the masculine plural, as in the present.

The participle matches the person, so a man writes *je suis parti* and a woman writes *je suis partie*. The same goes for *tu* and *vous* when you talk to one person. With *on* meaning "we", you will often see the participle agree with the people it stands for: *on est allés*.

You almost never hear the agreement. *Allé*, *allée*, *allés* and *allées* are all /ale/. *Parti* and *partie* are both /paʁti/. This is a writing skill. The one pair you hear is *mort* /mɔʁ/ and *morte* /mɔʁt/.

### When the same verb takes avoir

Some of these verbs also exist with a **direct object**, the thing that receives the action. Then the helper is *avoir*, with no agreement with the subject. *Elle est sortie* ("she went out"), but *elle a sorti son téléphone* ("she took out her phone"). *Il est monté* ("he went up"), but *il a monté l'escalier* ("he climbed the stairs"). The verbs *sortir*, *monter*, *descendre*, *rentrer*, *retourner* and *passer* behave this way. *Elle a passé une heure ici* means "she spent an hour here".

Verbs with *avoir*, from the last two lessons, never agree with the subject: *elles ont mangé*, not *elles ont mangées*.

## Hear it

Dina travelled by train from Toronto to Montréal. She and her friend Alex are on the phone and use *tu*. Listen for *je suis* and *elle est* in front of the participles, and notice that you do not hear the agreement.

```fr
Alex: Alors, tu es arrivée à Montréal? | So, did you arrive in Montreal?
Dina: Oui! Je suis arrivée à six heures et demie. Le train est parti de Toronto à une heure. | Yes! I arrived at six thirty. The train left Toronto at one o'clock.
Alex: Tu es descendue à la gare? | Did you get off at the station?
Dina: Oui. Ensuite, je suis allée à l'hôtel en métro. | Yes. Then I went to the hotel by metro.
Alex: Le train est arrivé à l'heure? | Did the train arrive on time?
Dina: Non, il est arrivé en retard. Une dame est tombée sur le quai. Un agent est venu tout de suite. | No, it arrived late. A lady fell on the platform. An agent came right away.
Alex: Et ta famille? | And your family?
Dina: Mes parents sont restés à Toronto. Ma sœur est allée à Ottawa. | My parents stayed in Toronto. My sister went to Ottawa.
Alex: Tu es sortie hier soir? | Did you go out last night?
Dina: Oui, ma cousine est venue à l'hôtel. Nous sommes sorties et nous avons mangé une pizza. | Yes, my cousin came to the hotel. We went out and we ate a pizza.
```

## Try it

1. Write the passé composé with *être*: *Elle ___ (arriver) à midi.* *Nous, les deux sœurs, ___ (aller) au parc.* *Ils ___ (partir) à huit heures.* *Tu (a man) ___ (rester) à la maison.*
2. Add the right ending: *Marie est sorti___.* *Les enfants sont tombé___.* *Mes parents sont rentré___.*
3. *Avoir* or *être*? *Il ___ monté l'escalier.* *Il ___ monté à midi.*
4. Say in French: "I was born in 1990" (a woman speaking). "She came back at noon."
5. Which verb pair do you hear a difference in: *il est mort* and *elle est morte*, or *il est parti* and *elle est partie*?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Elle est arrivée à midi.* *Nous sommes allées au parc.* *Ils sont partis à huit heures.* *Tu es resté à la maison.*
2. *Marie est sortie.* *Les enfants sont tombés.* *Mes parents sont rentrés.*
3. *Il a monté l'escalier* (a direct object follows). *Il est monté à midi* (no object).
4. *Je suis née en 1990.* *Elle est revenue à midi.*
5. *Il est mort* /mɔʁ/ and *elle est morte* /mɔʁt/. The other pair sounds the same.

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Je suis arrivé à midi. | I arrived at noon.
Elle est partie à huit heures. | She left at eight o'clock.
Nous sommes allés au parc. | We went to the park.
Ils sont restés à la maison. | They stayed at home.
Tu es sortie hier soir? | Did you go out last night?
```

## Common mistakes

Using *avoir* with a movement verb: *j'ai allé*, *j'ai arrivé*. Say *je suis allé*, *je suis arrivé*.

Forgetting to agree the participle for a woman or for several people: *elle est parti*, *ils sont arrivé*. Write *elle est partie* and *ils sont arrivés*.

Agreeing the participle with *avoir*: *elle a mangée*. With *avoir* and a subject, the participle does not change.

## Key terms

- **Agreement**: a word changing its form to match the gender and number of another word, as *allée* matches *elle*.
- **Direct object**: the person or thing that receives the action of a verb, such as *l'escalier* in *il a monté l'escalier*.
