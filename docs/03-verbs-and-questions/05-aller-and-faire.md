# Aller and faire

<span class="tag tag-grammar">Grammar</span> <span class="tag tag-a1">A1</span>

After this lesson you can use *aller* ("to go") and *faire* ("to do", "to make"), say where you go and how you get there, and name common things you do, such as sport, cooking and errands.

Before this: [Regular -er verbs](02-regular-er-verbs.md), [Saying no: negation](03-negation.md), [Asking questions](04-asking-questions.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

*Aller* and *faire* are two of the most used verbs in French, and neither one follows the *-er* pattern. They are **irregular verbs**: their forms do not follow a pattern, so you learn each one as a set of six, as you did with *être* and *avoir*. You have already used *aller* without knowing it. *Comment allez-vous?* and *Je vais bien* are forms of *aller*, and *Ça va?* is *va*, the form for *il*, *elle* and *ça*.

### Aller

| Pronoun | Form | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| *je* | *vais* | /ʒə vɛ/ |
| *tu* | *vas* | /ty va/ |
| *il / elle / on* | *va* | /il va/ |
| *nous* | *allons* | /nuz alɔ̃/ |
| *vous* | *allez* | /vuz ale/ |
| *ils / elles* | *vont* | /il vɔ̃/ |

Listen for the /z/ link in *nous allons* and *vous allez*, and for the nasal vowel of *vont*. The forms *vais* and *vas* start with *v*, and *allons* and *allez* start with a vowel. That shift is typical of irregular verbs.

### Going to a place

*Aller* is followed by *à* ("to" or "at") and a place: *Je vais à Montréal.* When the place has an article, *à* joins it. The pairs *à le* and *à les* are not written. They become one word.

| With | Becomes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *à + le* | *au* | *Je vais au parc.* |
| *à + les* | *aux* | *Nous allons aux musées.* |
| *à + la* | *à la* (no change) | *Tu vas à la banque.* |
| *à + l'* | *à l'* (no change) | *Elle va à l'hôpital.* |

To go to a person's home or workplace, use *chez* (you know it from the sounds lessons): *Je vais chez Marc*, *Elle va chez le docteur*.

### How you get there

To say how you travel, use *en* with a vehicle and *à* with feet and bikes: *en voiture* "by car", *en métro*, *en train* "by train", *en autobus* "by bus", *à pied* "on foot", *à vélo* "by bike". The question is *Comment vas-tu au travail?* or, in speech, *Tu vas au travail comment?* The answer is *Je vais au travail en métro.*

### Faire

| Pronoun | Form | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| *je* | *fais* | /ʒə fɛ/ |
| *tu* | *fais* | /ty fɛ/ |
| *il / elle / on* | *fait* | /il fɛ/ |
| *nous* | *faisons* | /nu fəzɔ̃/ |
| *vous* | *faites* | /vu fɛt/ |
| *ils / elles* | *font* | /il fɔ̃/ |

Watch the spelling and the sound of *nous faisons*. The letters *ai* are said /ə/, like the weak vowel of *le*, and not /ɛ/. So the word sounds like "fuh-zon". The question *Qu'est-ce que tu fais?* means "What are you doing?"

*Faire* is the verb for activities. Many of its uses are set phrases, and you learn each one as a whole.

```fr
Je fais du sport. | I do sport. I exercise.
Il fait du ski. | He skis.
Nous faisons du vélo. | We cycle.
Tu fais la cuisine. | You cook.
Elle fait les courses. | She does the errands.
On fait la fête. | We party.
Vous faites le ménage. | You do the housework.
Fais attention! | Be careful!
```

The words *du* and *de la* in these phrases mean something like "some" or "a bit of" an activity. A later lesson explains them. For now, learn *faire du sport*, *faire du ski*, *faire du vélo* as three chunks. In a negative sentence, *du* becomes *de*: *Je ne fais pas de sport.* The same shift happens in *pas de café*, which you saw in the lesson on negation.

## Hear it

Nadia is a nurse, aged 36. At the end of her shift she tells her colleague Karim what she does after work, and they use *tu*. Listen for the forms of *aller* and *faire*, and for the /z/ links.

```fr
Karim: Qu'est-ce que tu fais après le travail? | What do you do after work?
Nadia: Je vais au parc. Je fais du sport. Et toi? | I go to the park. I do some sport. And you?
Karim: Moi, je vais à la banque, et je fais les courses. | Me, I go to the bank, and I do the errands.
Nadia: Tu vas au travail comment? | How do you get to work?
Karim: Je vais au travail en métro. Et toi? | I go to work by metro. And you?
Nadia: À vélo. Mais je ne vais pas à l'hôpital à pied! | By bike. But I don't walk to the hospital!
Karim: Le soir, nous allons chez Marc. On fait la fête. | In the evening, we go to Marc's. We're having a party.
Nadia: Super! Je ne fais pas le ménage, alors. | Great! I'm not doing the housework, then.
```

## Try it

1. Fill in *aller*: *Nous ___ au parc.* *Vous ___ à la banque?* *Elles ___ à l'hôpital.* *Je ___ au cinéma.*
2. Fill in *au*, *aux*, *à la* or *à l'*: *Je vais ___ cinéma.* *Tu vas ___ pharmacie.* *Nous allons ___ hôpital.* *Ils vont ___ musées.*
3. Fill in *faire*: *Tu ___ du sport.* *Nous ___ la cuisine.* *Vous ___ attention.* *Ils ___ le ménage.*
4. Say how you get to work or to school, and say what you do after.
5. Make *Je fais du sport* negative.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Nous allons au parc.* *Vous allez à la banque?* *Elles vont à l'hôpital.* *Je vais au cinéma.*
2. *Je vais au cinéma.* *Tu vas à la pharmacie.* *Nous allons à l'hôpital.* *Ils vont aux musées.*
3. *Tu fais du sport.* *Nous faisons la cuisine.* *Vous faites attention.* *Ils font le ménage.*
4. A model answer: *Je vais au travail en métro. Après, je fais du sport.*
5. *Je ne fais pas de sport.*

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Je vais au parc. | I'm going to the park.
Tu vas au travail comment? | How do you get to work?
Nous allons chez Marc. | We're going to Marc's.
Qu'est-ce que tu fais? | What are you doing?
Je fais du sport et je fais les courses. | I do sport and I do the errands.
```

## Common mistakes

Writing *à le parc* or *à les musées*. These become *au parc* and *aux musées*. *À la* and *à l'* do not change.

Saying *nous faisons* with /ɛ/. The *ai* is /ə/: "fuh-zon".

Using *en* with *pied* or *vélo*. Use *à pied* and *à vélo*, and keep *en* for things you sit inside, such as *en voiture*.

## In Quebec

*Faire l'épicerie* is a Quebec and Canadian way to say "to buy groceries". Usito marks it with the Q/C label, which means Quebec and Canada, and lists *faire les courses* and *faire le marché* as other ways to say it ([Usito: épicerie](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/d%C3%A9finitions/%C3%A9picerie)). You can use *faire les courses* anywhere. In Montreal you will also hear *magasiner* ("to shop"), which you met in [Regular -er verbs](02-regular-er-verbs.md).

## Key terms

- **Irregular verb**: a verb whose forms do not follow a pattern, so you learn each form.
- **Contraction**: two words that join into one, such as *à* + *le* = *au*.
