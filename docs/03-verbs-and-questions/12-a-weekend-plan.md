# A weekend plan

<span class="tag tag-speak">Speak</span> <span class="tag tag-a1">A1</span> <span class="tag tag-review">Review</span>

After this lesson you can hold a two-minute phone call in which you plan a weekend: propose, ask questions, accept or refuse, and fix days, times and places.

Before this: [Rhythm and intonation](01-rhythm-and-intonation.md), [Regular -er verbs](02-regular-er-verbs.md), [Saying no: negation](03-negation.md), [Asking questions](04-asking-questions.md), [Aller and faire](05-aller-and-faire.md), [Days, dates and big numbers](06-days-dates-and-big-numbers.md), [Telling the time](07-telling-time.md), [Making plans](08-making-plans.md). You need: a timer.

## The idea

This lesson has no new grammar. It puts the whole stage into one conversation, so you can see how the pieces fit. Dev is 28 and works in Toronto as a software tester. Sophie is 30 and lives in Montreal. They are friends and use *tu*. Dev calls Sophie to plan a visit.

Five new words come with the call. *Un marché* is a market, *un brunch* is a late-morning meal that mixes breakfast and lunch, *ensemble* means "together", *plutôt* means "rather" or "instead", and *tout le monde* means "everyone". It takes a singular verb: *tout le monde est là*.

## Hear it

Listen first with the translation hidden. Find three things: the day and time of Dev's train, what they do on Saturday, and what they do on Sunday. Then read along. Two spoken forms from [Casual questions in Quebec](10-quebec-casual-questions.md) appear in Sophie's and Dev's lines, marked as casual.

```fr
Dev: Salut Sophie! Ça va? | Hi Sophie! How's it going?
Sophie: Salut Dev! Ça va bien, et toi? | Hi Dev! Fine, and you?
Dev: Très bien. Je vais à Montréal cette fin de semaine. Tu es libre? | Very well. I'm going to Montreal this weekend. Are you free?
Sophie: Ben oui! Tu es à la gare à quelle heure? {qc} | Well, yes! What time are you at the station?
Dev: Vendredi, à 18 h 20. On se retrouve devant la gare? | Friday, at 6:20 p.m. Shall we meet in front of the station?
Sophie: Ça marche. Samedi, on fait quoi? Tu veux aller au marché? | That works. What do we do on Saturday? Do you want to go to the market?
Dev: Pourquoi pas? J'adore cuisiner. Et ensuite, on va au musée? | Why not? I love cooking. And then, shall we go to the museum?
Sophie: Moi, j'aime le cinéma. Tu veux aller au cinéma plutôt? | Me, I like the movies. Do you want to go to the movies instead?
Dev: Un film samedi soir, ouais, je veux bien. {qc} | A movie on Saturday night, yeah, sure.
Sophie: Super! Et dimanche, on fait un brunch avec tout le monde? | Great! And on Sunday, shall we have a brunch with everyone?
Dev: Dimanche matin, je ne peux pas avant dix heures. | On Sunday morning, I can't before ten.
Sophie: Pas de problème. Brunch à 11 h 30, alors. À vendredi! | No problem. Brunch at 11:30, then. See you Friday!
Dev: À vendredi, Sophie! Bisous. | See you Friday, Sophie! Kisses.
```

## Your turn

You are Dev. Play the call twice. In the first round, say Dev's lines with the book open. In the second, cover the lines and use only the prompts below. Then change one thing, such as the day or the activity, and say the call again.

1. Greet Sophie and ask how she is.
2. Say you are going to Montreal this weekend and ask if she is free.
3. Give the day and time of your train and propose where to meet.
4. Say you love cooking and propose the market.
5. Refuse a time on Sunday and give a reason.
6. End the call with *à* and the day.

Check yourself against this list: one *ne ... pas*, one *est-ce que* or a question word, one time in the 24-hour way, and one *tu veux* or *on se retrouve*.

## Try it

1. Answer from the call: when is Dev's train, and what does Sophie suggest instead of the museum?
2. Say Sophie's line *Tu es à la gare à quelle heure?* in a more formal way, with *vous* and inversion.
3. Say in French: "We are going to the market together on Saturday."
4. Say in French: "Everyone is free on Sunday."
5. Make up a reason for refusing a Saturday invitation, using *je suis occupé* or *je travaille*.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. Dev's train is on Friday at 18 h 20, and Sophie suggests a film at the cinema.
2. *À quelle heure êtes-vous à la gare?*
3. *Samedi, nous allons au marché ensemble.* or *Samedi, on va au marché ensemble.*
4. *Tout le monde est libre dimanche.*
5. For example: *Désolé, samedi, je ne peux pas. Je travaille.*

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Je vais à Montréal cette fin de semaine. | I'm going to Montreal this weekend.
Tu es libre samedi? | Are you free on Saturday?
On se retrouve devant la gare à 18 h 20? | Shall we meet in front of the station at 6:20 p.m.?
Dimanche, je ne peux pas avant dix heures. | On Sunday, I can't before ten.
Brunch à 11 h 30, ça marche. | Brunch at 11:30, that works.
```

## Common mistakes

Using *je veux* to accept an invitation. Say *je veux bien*.

Saying a 24-hour time with *et quart* or *et demie*. In the 24-hour way, say the minutes as a number: *dix-huit heures vingt*.

Forgetting the *de* in a negative sentence. Say *Je n'ai pas de carte*, and not "je n'ai pas une carte".

## In Quebec

Sophie lives in Montreal. In the call, *ben oui* and *ouais* are casual forms that you will hear from friends in Quebec. You understand them, and you answer in standard French.
