# Hearing times and plans

<span class="tag tag-listen">Listen</span> <span class="tag tag-a1">A1</span>

After this lesson you can pick the times, places and plans out of a train announcement, a voicemail and a bus announcement, and say what you heard.

Before this: [Telling the time](07-telling-time.md), [Days, dates and big numbers](06-days-dates-and-big-numbers.md), [Making plans](08-making-plans.md), [Aller and faire](05-aller-and-faire.md). You need: headphones, and a pencil and paper.

## The idea

Real announcements are fast and have background noise, and you will not catch every word. You do not need to. You need the three facts that matter: what, when, where. This lesson gives you a method and three short recordings to practise it on. All the times, lines and names in the recordings are invented for practice. They are not real timetables.

### The method

1. Listen once with the translation hidden. Do not write. Ask only: what kind of message is this?
2. Listen again. Write the numbers you hear, in figures: times, platforms, lines. Numbers are the easiest thing to catch, because you know them.
3. Listen a third time for the place names and the day. Write them as you hear them.
4. Open the translation and check. Mark what you missed, and listen to that part once more.

Two sound habits help. First, an announcement says the hour first and then the minutes, in the 24-hour way: *dix-huit heures quarante*. Second, the words before a time are small and fast (*à*, *est*, *dans*), so listen for the number and not for them.

### Words you will hear

A **gare** is a railway station, a **quai** is a platform, and a **billet** is a ticket. *Un départ* is a departure and *une arrivée* an arrival. A **retard** is a delay, and you know *en retard*. *Une ligne* is a transit line, and *un arrêt* is a stop. *Prochain* means "next" and *dernier* means "last". *La boîte vocale* is voicemail.

The announcement gives times as *départ à*, *arrivée à* and *retard de*: *départ à dix-huit heures quarante* means "departure at 6:40 p.m.", and *quinze minutes de retard* means "fifteen minutes late".

## Hear it

The first recording is a train announcement in a station in Ottawa. The second is a voicemail from Marc to his friend Léa, in *tu*. The third is a bus announcement in Montreal. For each one, follow the four steps of the method. The questions are in Try it.

```fr
Annonce: Mesdames et messieurs, bonsoir. Le train pour Montréal est au quai trois. Départ à dix-huit heures quarante. | Ladies and gentlemen, good evening. The train for Montreal is at platform three. Departure at 6:40 p.m.
Annonce: Le train de Toronto a quinze minutes de retard. Arrivée à dix-neuf heures vingt. | The train from Toronto is fifteen minutes late. Arrival at 7:20 p.m.
Annonce: Le dernier train pour Montréal est à vingt-deux heures. Merci. | The last train for Montreal is at 10 p.m. Thank you.
```

```fr
Boîte-vocale: Bonjour, vous êtes sur la boîte vocale de Léa. Laissez un message, s'il vous plaît. | Hello, you have reached Léa's voicemail. Please leave a message.
Marc: Salut Léa, c'est Marc. Je suis à la gare. Samedi, je suis libre. | Hi Léa, it's Marc. I'm at the station. On Saturday, I'm free.
Marc: On se retrouve à midi et demi devant le musée? Ça marche? | Shall we meet at half past noon in front of the museum? Does that work?
Marc: Mais dimanche, je ne peux pas. Je travaille. À samedi! | But on Sunday I can't. I'm working. See you Saturday!
```

```fr
Annonce: Ligne douze, direction gare. Prochain arrêt : rue Saint-Denis. | Line twelve, toward the station. Next stop: Saint-Denis Street.
Annonce: Attention, l'autobus a cinq minutes de retard. Merci de votre patience. | Attention, the bus is five minutes late. Thank you for your patience.
```

## Try it

1. At what time does the train for Montreal leave, and from which platform? Write the time as *18 h 40* style.
2. How late is the train from Toronto, and when does it arrive?
3. What is the time of the last train for Montreal?
4. In the voicemail, which day is Marc free, and which day is he not? Where do they meet, and at what time?
5. In the bus announcement, which line is it, and what is the next stop?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *18 h 40*, platform three (*quai trois*).
2. Fifteen minutes late. It arrives at *19 h 20*.
3. *22 h*.
4. He is free on Saturday (*samedi*) and not on Sunday (*dimanche*). They meet at half past noon (*12 h 30*) in front of the museum.
5. Line twelve (*ligne douze*). The next stop is *rue Saint-Denis*.

</details>

## Say it

Say each line as the announcer would, with an even beat and a clear number.

```fr
Le train est au quai trois. | The train is at platform three.
Départ à dix-huit heures quarante. | Departure at 6:40 p.m.
Le train a quinze minutes de retard. | The train is fifteen minutes late.
Le dernier train est à vingt-deux heures. | The last train is at 10 p.m.
Samedi, je suis libre. | On Saturday, I'm free.
```

## Common mistakes

Waiting to understand every word. Catch the numbers and the place first, then fill in the rest.

Writing a time as you hear the words, for example "dix-huit heures quarante" as 10.40. Subtract 12 from the hour to check: 18 h is six in the evening.

Mixing up *départ* and *arrivée*. Listen for the word before the time.

## In Quebec

Times in French in Quebec are written *18 h 40*, as the OQLF gives them, and said as in the recordings. This lesson has no new Quebec forms. Listen to the same recordings with the Quebec voice and then the France voice and compare the vowels you know.
