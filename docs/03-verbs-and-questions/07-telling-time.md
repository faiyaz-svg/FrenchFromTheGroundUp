# Telling the time

Before this: [Numbers from 0 to 100](../02-sounds-spelling-and-everyday-things/06-numbers-0-100.md), [Asking questions](04-asking-questions.md), [The vowels o and eu](../01-first-sounds-and-greetings/05-vowels-o-and-eu.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

French has two ways to say the time. The everyday way uses a 12-hour clock with words such as *quart* and *demie*. The official way uses a 24-hour clock with plain numbers and is the way of timetables, announcements and appointments. You need both, and the second one is easier.

### Asking and answering

To ask the time, say *Quelle heure est-il?* /kɛl œʁ ɛ til/ or, in speech, *Il est quelle heure?* A polite way is *Vous avez l'heure, s'il vous plaît?* The answer starts with *Il est* and the number of hours. *Heure* /œʁ/ is feminine, and it takes an *s* from two o'clock on.

| Time (p.m.) | Everyday | 24-hour |
|---|---|---|
| 1:00 | *Il est une heure.* | *Il est treize heures.* |
| 2:00 | *Il est deux heures.* | *Il est quatorze heures.* |
| 2:05 | *Il est deux heures cinq.* | *Il est quatorze heures cinq.* |
| 2:15 | *Il est deux heures et quart.* | *Il est quatorze heures quinze.* |
| 2:30 | *Il est deux heures et demie.* | *Il est quatorze heures trente.* |
| 2:45 | *Il est trois heures moins le quart.* | *Il est quatorze heures quarante-cinq.* |
| 2:50 | *Il est trois heures moins dix.* | *Il est quatorze heures cinquante.* |

Three special words are *midi* /midi/ for noon, *minuit* /minɥi/ for midnight, and *demie*. They work like this. *Midi* and *minuit* are masculine, so you say *midi et demi* and *minuit et demi*. For other hours, *demie* is feminine because it agrees with *heure*: *deux heures et demie*. *Quart* means "quarter". *Et quart* adds 15 minutes, and *moins le quart* takes 15 off the next hour. For the other times past the half hour, you say the next hour *moins* a number of minutes: *trois heures moins dix* is ten to three.

To say which half of the day you mean in the everyday way, add *du matin* "in the morning", *de l'après-midi* "in the afternoon" or *du soir* "in the evening": *trois heures de l'après-midi*, *huit heures du soir*.

### The 24-hour clock

In the 24-hour way, you count hours from 0 to 23 and then say the minutes as an ordinary number: *dix-huit heures quinze*, *vingt et une heures trente*. Nothing else is needed, and that is why timetables, trains and clinics use it. After 12, subtract 12 to find the everyday time: 18 h is six in the evening.

### Writing the time

In writing, a time is figures, then *h*, then the minutes: *14 h 30*, *8 h 15*, *18 h*. The OQLF says that *h* is a symbol that is always preceded and followed by a space, and that it is written with no full stop and no *s*. The colon, as in *14:30*, is for digital displays, and the OQLF does not accept it in letters and ordinary texts ([OQLF: écriture des heures](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/21516/la-typographie/nombres/ecriture-des-heures)). Minutes under ten may be written with or without a zero: *17 h 05* or *17 h 5*. A time that uses *quart*, *demi*, *midi* or *minuit* is written in letters in a text: *midi et demi*, *onze heures et quart*.

### Saying when

To give the time of an event, use *à*: *Le rendez-vous est à 14 h 30.* *À quelle heure est le rendez-vous?* A *rendez-vous* is an appointment or a meeting arranged for a time. *Un horaire* is a timetable or a schedule. Four words describe how you stand against the time: *tôt* "early", *tard* "late", *en avance* "ahead of time" and *en retard* "behind time", and *à l'heure* means "on time". Lengths of time are *une minute*, *un quart d'heure* (15 minutes) and *une demi-heure* (30 minutes). With *dans* ("in"), they say when something will happen: *Le docteur est là dans un quart d'heure.*

## Hear it

The first dialogue is in a clinic in Laval. A patient and a receptionist use *vous*. In the second, two friends wait for a bus in Gatineau and use *tu*. The timetable is invented for practice. Listen for the hour first, then the minutes.

```fr
Réceptionniste: Bonjour, madame. Votre rendez-vous est à quelle heure? | Hello. What time is your appointment?
Vous: À trois heures et demie, avec le docteur. | At half past three, with the doctor.
Réceptionniste: Il est trois heures et quart. Vous êtes en avance. | It's a quarter past three. You are early.
Vous: Merci. Le docteur est là dans un quart d'heure? | Thank you. Is the doctor here in a quarter of an hour?
Réceptionniste: Oui. Un instant, s'il vous plaît. | Yes. One moment, please.

Léa: Il est quelle heure? | What time is it?
Samir: Il est huit heures moins le quart. | It's a quarter to eight.
Léa: L'autobus est à huit heures dix. On est à l'heure! | The bus is at ten past eight. We're on time!
Samir: Oui. Mais demain, l'autobus est à neuf heures. | Yes. But tomorrow the bus is at nine.
```

Here is the bus timetable, written and said both ways.

| Written | Spoken (24-hour) | Spoken (everyday) |
|---|---|---|
| 7 h 15 | *sept heures quinze* | *sept heures et quart* |
| 7 h 45 | *sept heures quarante-cinq* | *huit heures moins le quart* |
| 8 h 10 | *huit heures dix* | *huit heures dix* |
| 12 h 30 | *douze heures trente* | *midi et demi* |

## Try it

1. Say these times the everyday way: *7 h 15*, *12 h 30*, *8 h 45*, *15 h*.
2. Write in figures: *dix-huit heures quinze*, *minuit et demi*, *une heure et quart*.
3. Which form follows the OQLF: *14:30*, *14h30* or *14 h 30*?
4. Say in French: "The meeting is at nine in the morning."
5. Ask a stranger for the time, politely, and answer your own question with the time right now.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Sept heures et quart.* *Midi et demi.* *Neuf heures moins le quart.* *Trois heures de l'après-midi.*
2. *18 h 15.* *0 h 30.* *1 h 15.*
3. *14 h 30*, with a space before and after the *h*.
4. *Le rendez-vous est à neuf heures du matin.*
5. *Vous avez l'heure, s'il vous plaît?* The answer depends on your clock. It starts with *Il est* and the hour.

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Il est quelle heure? | What time is it?
Il est huit heures et quart. | It's a quarter past eight.
Le rendez-vous est à quatorze heures trente. | The appointment is at 2:30 p.m.
Vous avez l'heure, s'il vous plaît? | Do you have the time, please?
Nous sommes à l'heure. | We are on time.
```

## Common mistakes

Writing *14:30* or *14h30*. In French text, use *14 h 30*, with a space on each side of the *h*.

Saying *deux heure* with no *s*. From two o'clock on, *heures* is plural. It sounds the same, so you must remember to write the *s*: *deux heures et demie*.

Using *et demie* after *midi*. Say *midi et demi*.

## In Quebec

The OQLF form *14 h 30* is the one to use in writing in Quebec. You say it aloud as *quatorze heures trente*.
