# Making plans

<span class="tag tag-speak">Speak</span> <span class="tag tag-a1">A1</span>

After this lesson you can invite someone, accept or refuse, suggest another day, and agree on a time and a place, with *tu* and with *vous*.

Before this: [Aller and faire](05-aller-and-faire.md), [Days, dates and big numbers](06-days-dates-and-big-numbers.md), [Telling the time](07-telling-time.md), [Asking questions](04-asking-questions.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

A plan has five moves: you propose, the other person answers, you may refuse and offer something else, you fix the time, and you fix the place. Each move has a few set phrases. Some of them use verbs that this book teaches later: *vouloir* "to want", *pouvoir* "can", *venir* "to come" and *se retrouver* "to meet". Learn those phrases whole, as **chunks**, which are phrases that you say as one block without taking them apart. Later lessons explain them: [Prendre, venir, boire and voir](../05-the-past-what-happened/02-prendre-venir-boire-voir.md), [Vouloir, pouvoir and devoir](../06-habits-memories-and-food/02-vouloir-pouvoir-devoir.md) and [Reflexive verbs](../06-habits-memories-and-food/04-reflexive-verbs.md).

### Proposing

To invite, you have several choices. Each row has a *tu* version and a *vous* version.

| With *tu* | With *vous* | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *Tu veux aller au cinéma?* | *Vous voulez aller au cinéma?* | Do you want to go to the movies? |
| *On peut aller au concert?* | *Vous pouvez venir au concert?* | Can we go to the concert? |
| *Ça te dit, un film ce soir?* | *Ça vous dit, un film ce soir?* | Do you feel like a movie tonight? |
| *Tu viens?* | *Vous venez?* | Are you coming? |

*Ça te dit?* means "Does that appeal to you?" and it is a friendly, casual way to suggest something. You can also use the present of *aller* for a plan: *On va au concert samedi?* means "Shall we go to the concert on Saturday?" The present of *aller* is enough for a plan here, and a later lesson adds the *aller* + infinitive pattern for the near future. After *tu veux* and *on peut*, the next verb is in the infinitive, the form that ends in *-er* for the verbs you know, as you learned in [Regular -er verbs](02-regular-er-verbs.md).

The new nouns are *un film* "a movie", *un concert*, *un match* "a game or match", *un spectacle* "a show", and *une soirée* "an evening, a party". *Ce soir* is "tonight". To suggest, use *proposer* "to suggest" and *inviter* "to invite": *Je propose vendredi. J'invite Marc.*

### Accepting

*Oui, je veux bien* means "Yes, I'd like to", and it is warmer than a plain *oui*. *Pourquoi pas?* means "Why not?" and *ça marche* means "that works, it's a deal". *D'accord* from stage 2 still works, and *avec plaisir* means "with pleasure".

### Refusing and offering something else

To refuse politely, say you are sorry, say that you cannot, and give a reason. Use *je ne peux pas* "I can't", and *Je suis occupé* (a man) or *Je suis occupée* (a woman) for "I'm busy". The opposite is *je suis libre* "I'm free", with the same form for a man and a woman. Then suggest a different time: *Demain?* or *Vendredi, ça marche?*

### Fixing the time and the place

*On se retrouve à 19 h 30 devant le cinéma?* means "Shall we meet at 7:30 in front of the movie theatre?" Learn *on se retrouve* as a chunk for "we meet (each other)", and say the time as you learned it: *dix-neuf heures trente* or *sept heures et demie*. With a manager or a stranger, *vous* is the safe choice: *Vous êtes libre vendredi?*

A short rule about *je veux*: it means "I want", and by itself it can sound blunt. Use *je veux bien* to accept and *je voudrais* (from [Ordering at a café](../02-sounds-spelling-and-everyday-things/08-ordering-at-a-cafe.md)) to ask politely.

## Hear it

Two coworkers, Camille and Dev, plan a drink after work, and they use *tu*. Then Dev asks his manager, Mme Roy, the same thing with *vous*. Listen for what each person says to propose, accept and refuse.

```fr
Camille: Salut, Dev! Ça te dit, un verre ce soir? | Hi, Dev! Do you feel like a drink tonight?
Dev: Ce soir? Désolé, je ne peux pas. Je suis occupé. | Tonight? Sorry, I can't. I'm busy.
Camille: Vendredi soir, ça marche? | Does Friday evening work?
Dev: Vendredi, oui, je veux bien! On se retrouve à dix-neuf heures? | Friday, yes, I'd like to! Shall we meet at seven?
Camille: Ça marche. Tu invites Marc? | That works. Will you invite Marc?
Dev: Pourquoi pas? J'invite Marc et Léa. | Why not? I'll invite Marc and Léa.

Dev: Madame Roy, vous êtes libre vendredi soir? Nous allons au restaurant. | Mrs. Roy, are you free Friday evening? We are going to a restaurant.
Mme-Roy: Avec plaisir! Mais je ne peux pas avant sept heures. | With pleasure! But I can't before seven.
Dev: Pas de problème. On se retrouve à sept heures et demie? | No problem. Shall we meet at half past seven?
Mme-Roy: D'accord. Vous venez avec Camille? | Okay. Are you coming with Camille?
```

## Try it

1. Invite a friend to a concert on Saturday in three ways.
2. Reply to *Tu veux aller au cinéma ce soir?* to accept, and then to refuse with a reason.
3. Change to *vous*: *Tu veux venir? Tu es libre vendredi? Ça te dit?*
4. Refuse an invitation for tonight and propose another day.
5. Say in French: "Shall we meet at 7:30 in front of the movie theatre?" in two ways of saying the time.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. For example: *Tu veux aller au concert samedi?* *On va au concert samedi?* *Ça te dit, un concert samedi?*
2. To accept: *Oui, je veux bien!* or *Ça marche.* To refuse: *Désolé, je ne peux pas. Je suis occupé.* (or *occupée*).
3. *Vous voulez venir? Vous êtes libre vendredi? Ça vous dit?*
4. For example: *Désolé, ce soir, je ne peux pas. Demain, ça marche?*
5. *On se retrouve à dix-neuf heures trente devant le cinéma?* and *On se retrouve à sept heures et demie devant le cinéma?*

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Tu veux aller au cinéma ce soir? | Do you want to go to the movies tonight?
Désolé, je ne peux pas. Je suis occupé. | Sorry, I can't. I'm busy.
Vendredi, ça marche? | Does Friday work?
Oui, je veux bien! | Yes, I'd like to!
On se retrouve à dix-neuf heures? | Shall we meet at seven?
```

## Common mistakes

Saying *je veux* when you accept. It sounds like "I want". Say *je veux bien*.

Using *tu* with a manager or a stranger. With them, use *vous*, and the chunks become *vous voulez* and *vous pouvez*.

Writing *libre* with an *-e* for a woman, as in "libree". The word has one form: *libre* for a man and a woman.

## In Quebec

People in Quebec use the same chunks. These chunks work in Quebec and in France. The casual Quebec forms that you will hear in a bar or at work are taught in [Casual questions in Quebec](10-quebec-casual-questions.md).
