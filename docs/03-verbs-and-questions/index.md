## Cheat sheet

Sounds. A rhythm group has an even beat and a lift on its last syllable. A statement falls, a yes or no question rises, and a question that starts with a question word falls. A weak *e* often nearly disappears in short words.

Regular -er verbs, *parler*. The endings *-e*, *-es* and *-ent* are silent. Use *j'* before a vowel sound, and *on* for "we".

| Pronoun | Form | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| *je* | *parle* | /ʒə paʁl/ |
| *tu* | *parles* | /ty paʁl/ |
| *il / elle / on* | *parle* | /il paʁl/ |
| *nous* | *parlons* | /nu paʁlɔ̃/ |
| *vous* | *parlez* | /vu paʁle/ |
| *ils / elles* | *parlent* | /il paʁl/ |

Verbs in *-ger* keep an *e* before *-ons*: *nous mangeons*.

*Aller* and *faire*.

| Pronoun | *aller* | *faire* |
|---|---|---|
| *je* | *vais* | *fais* |
| *tu* | *vas* | *fais* |
| *il / elle / on* | *va* | *fait* |
| *nous* | *allons* | *faisons* /fəzɔ̃/ |
| *vous* | *allez* | *faites* |
| *ils / elles* | *vont* | *font* |

*À* + *le* = *au*, *à* + *les* = *aux*, and *à la* and *à l'* do not change. Use *en* with a vehicle (*en voiture*) and *à* with feet and bikes (*à pied*, *à vélo*).

Negation.

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| *ne ... pas* | *Je ne parle pas français.* |
| *n'* before a vowel | *Je n'habite pas ici.* |
| *pas de* after *un*, *une*, *des* | *Je n'ai pas de carte.* |
| *ne ... jamais*, *rien*, *personne*, *plus* | *Je ne mange jamais le matin.* |
| casual speech drops *ne* | heard in speech, not written |

Questions.

| Way | Example |
|---|---|
| Intonation | *Tu habites à Montréal?* |
| *Est-ce que* | *Est-ce que tu habites à Montréal?* |
| Inversion (formal, with *vous*) | *Habitez-vous à Montréal?* |
| Question word | *Où habites-tu?* *Tu habites où?* |

Question words: *qui*, *que* and *quoi*, *où*, *quand*, *comment*, *combien (de)*, *pourquoi* (answer with *parce que*), *quel / quelle / quels / quelles*. In inversion, add *-t-* after a vowel with *il*, *elle* and *on*: *Parle-t-il?*

Dates and numbers. Days and months have no capital: *lundi*, *mars*. A date is *le 14 novembre 2026* or, in forms, *2026-11-14*. The first of the month is *le 1er mars*. Numbers: *cent*, *deux cents*, *deux cent un*, *mille* (no *s*), *un million*.

Time. Write times as *14 h 30*, with a space on each side of the *h*.

| Time | Everyday | 24-hour |
|---|---|---|
| 8:00 | *huit heures* | *huit heures* |
| 8:15 | *huit heures et quart* | *huit heures quinze* |
| 8:30 | *huit heures et demie* | *huit heures trente* |
| 8:45 | *neuf heures moins le quart* | *huit heures quarante-cinq* |
| 12:00 and 0:00 | *midi*, *minuit* | *douze heures*, *zéro heure* |

Key phrases.

```fr
Je parle français. J'habite à Toronto. | I speak French. I live in Toronto.
Je ne comprends pas. Je n'ai pas de carte. | I don't understand. I don't have a card.
Tu veux aller au cinéma? Ça te dit? | Do you want to go to the movies? Do you feel like it?
Oui, je veux bien! Ça marche. | Yes, I'd like to! That works.
Désolé, je ne peux pas. Je suis occupé. | Sorry, I can't. I'm busy.
On se retrouve à 19 h 30 devant le cinéma? | Shall we meet at 7:30 in front of the movie theatre?
Il est quelle heure? Vous avez l'heure? | What time is it? Do you have the time?
Le rendez-vous est à quelle heure? | What time is the appointment?
Où est-ce que tu habites? Pourquoi? Parce que j'aime ça. | Where do you live? Why? Because I like it.
Mon anniversaire est le premier mars. | My birthday is March 1st.
À plus tard! À samedi! Bisous. | See you later! See you Saturday! Kisses.
```

Casual Quebec, to recognise only: a small *-tu* after the verb makes a question, *chu* stands for *je suis*, *ben* for *bien*, and *pis* for *et* or *puis*. The lesson on casual questions in Quebec has examples.
