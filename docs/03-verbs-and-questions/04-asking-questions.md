## The idea

A **question** asks for information. French has two kinds: a **yes or no question**, which has the answer *oui* or *non*, and an **information question**, which starts with a question word such as "where" or "when". For each kind there are several ways to ask, and the choice depends on how formal you want to sound.

### Yes or no questions

There are three ways. Take *Tu habites à Montréal.*

| Way | Example | When |
|---|---|---|
| Intonation | *Tu habites à Montréal?* | speech, everyday |
| *Est-ce que* | *Est-ce que tu habites à Montréal?* | speech and writing, neutral |
| Inversion | *Habitez-vous à Montréal?* | writing and formal speech |

The first way is the one you learned in [Rhythm and intonation](01-rhythm-and-intonation.md): keep the word order of a statement and let your voice rise. The second way puts *est-ce que* /ɛs kə/ in front of a statement. The words mean something like "is it that", and they have no separate meaning of their own. Before a vowel sound, *que* loses its vowel: *Est-ce qu'il habite à Montréal?*

**Inversion** (*l'inversion*) is the third way: the verb comes before the pronoun and a hyphen joins them. For now, use it with *vous*, and recognise it with the other pronouns. You already know one question that is built this way: *Comment allez-vous?* is *vous allez* turned around. Other examples are *Parlez-vous français?* and *Avez-vous une carte?* With *tu* it looks the same: *Parles-tu français?* Writers and formal speakers use it. People use it much less in casual speech.

When a verb ends in a vowel (*parle*, *habite*, *a*) and the pronoun is *il*, *elle* or *on*, French adds a *-t-* so that the two vowels do not collide: *Parle-t-il français?* *Habite-t-elle ici?* *A-t-il un billet?* ("Does he have a ticket?") A verb that already ends in *t* or *s* does not need it: *Parlent-ils français?*

### Question words

Put the question word first, or last in casual speech. Here are the words of this lesson.

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *qui* | who | *Qui parle?* |
| *que*, *quoi* | what | *Qu'est-ce que c'est?* *Tu cherches quoi?* |
| *où* | where | *Où est-ce que tu habites?* |
| *quand* | when | *Tu voyages quand?* |
| *comment* | how | *Comment vous vous appelez?* |
| *combien* | how much, how many | *Combien de croissants?* |
| *pourquoi* | why | *Pourquoi tu étudies le français?* |

For "what", use *qu'est-ce que* /kɛs kə/ at the start of a question and *quoi* /kwa/ at the end: *Qu'est-ce que tu cherches?* *Tu cherches quoi?* You know *Qu'est-ce que c'est?* from the first stage as a chunk, and now you can see its parts. The form *Que cherches-tu?* has the same meaning in more formal writing.

*Pourquoi* asks "why", and the answer starts with *parce que* /paʁs kə/, which means "because". Before a vowel it becomes *parce qu'*: *Parce qu'il habite ici.* After *combien* you add *de* and the noun: *Combien de cafés?* *Tu as combien d'amis?*

Information questions follow the same three ways as before.

| Way | Example |
|---|---|
| Question word last (casual) | *Tu habites où?* |
| *Est-ce que* | *Où est-ce que tu habites?* |
| Inversion | *Où habitez-vous?* |

### Quel

*Quel* means "which" or "what" before a noun, and it agrees with the noun: *quel* (masculine), *quelle* (feminine), *quels* and *quelles* (plural). All four sound the same, /kɛl/. *Quel âge as-tu?* means "How old are you?" *Quelle est votre adresse?* *Quels sont vos cours?* For time, *à quelle heure* means "at what time": *Le train est à quelle heure?* ("What time is the train?")

## Hear it

The first dialogue is at the information desk of Gare Centrale in Montreal. Mme Roy asks a clerk about a train, and they use *vous*. The times and prices are invented for practice. In the second dialogue two friends use *tu*. Listen for the rise on yes or no questions and the fall on question-word questions.

```fr
Mme-Roy: Bonjour, monsieur. Est-ce que le train pour Ottawa est à dix heures? | Hello. Is the train to Ottawa at ten o'clock?
Employé: Oui, madame. Il est à dix heures. | Yes, ma'am. It is at ten o'clock.
Mme-Roy: Où est le quai? | Where is the platform?
Employé: Le quai trois. Un instant... Avez-vous un billet? | Platform three. One moment... Do you have a ticket?
Mme-Roy: Non. Ça coûte combien? | No. How much is it?
Employé: Ça fait trente dollars, madame. | Thirty dollars, ma'am.

Léa: Tu voyages avec qui? | Who are you travelling with?
Samir: Avec Marc, un collègue. | With Marc, a colleague.
Léa: Pourquoi vous voyagez en train? | Why are you travelling by train?
Samir: Parce que j'aime le train. Et toi, tu voyages quand? | Because I like trains. And you, when are you travelling?
Léa: Demain. À quelle heure est le train? | Tomorrow. What time is the train?
```

## Try it

1. Turn *Tu habites à Montréal.* into three questions: with intonation, with *est-ce que*, and with *vous* and inversion.
2. Fill in the question word, *qui*, *où*, *combien* or *pourquoi*: *___ est-ce que tu habites? À Gatineau.* *___ tu étudies le français? Parce que j'aime le Québec.* *___ de croissants? Trois.* *___ est-ce? C'est Marc.*
3. Fill in the form of *quel*: *___ âge as-tu?* *___ est votre adresse?* *___ sont vos cours?*
4. Which is correct, *Habite-t-elle ici?* or "Habite-elle ici?" Which is correct, *Parlent-ils français?* or "Parlent-t-ils français?"
5. Say in French, with *vous* and inversion: "Where do you live?"

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Tu habites à Montréal?* *Est-ce que tu habites à Montréal?* *Habitez-vous à Montréal?*
2. *Où* est-ce que tu habites? *Pourquoi* tu étudies le français? *Combien* de croissants? *Qui* est-ce?
3. *Quel* âge as-tu? *Quelle* est votre adresse? *Quels* sont vos cours?
4. *Habite-t-elle ici?* has the *-t-* because *habite* ends in a vowel. *Parlent-ils français?* does not, because *parlent* already ends in a *t*.
5. *Où habitez-vous?*

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Est-ce que tu habites à Montréal? | Do you live in Montreal?
Où habitez-vous? | Where do you live?
Tu cherches quoi? | What are you looking for?
Pourquoi tu étudies le français? | Why are you studying French?
Quel âge as-tu? | How old are you?
```

## Common mistakes

Mixing two ways in one question, such as "Est-ce que habitez-vous". Pick one way and keep it.

Using *quel* with the wrong form. It agrees with the noun that comes after it, so a feminine noun takes *quelle*.

Using *parce que* for "why". *Pourquoi* asks the question and *parce que* gives the answer.

## Key terms

- **Yes or no question**: a question that has *oui* or *non* as an answer.
- **Information question**: a question that begins with a question word.
- **Inversion**: putting the verb before the pronoun, joined by a hyphen, to make a question.
