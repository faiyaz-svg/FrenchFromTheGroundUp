## L'idée

A work email in French has the same parts as an English one, and a few French habits. The rules below follow the Office québécois de la langue française (OQLF), as of 2026. The full table of greetings and closings is on the [Email and letter formulas](../reference/email-formulas.md) page, so this lesson does not repeat it.

### The five parts

1. **The subject line** (*l'objet*). It says the action and one detail: *Report de la réunion du 12 mars*. The OQLF page on the [subject line](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/index.php?id=25514) asks for a clear subject, with the most important words first, a capital only on the first word and no final period.
2. **The greeting** (*la salutation*). *Madame, Monsieur,* for a first contact when you do not know the name. *Bonjour Madame Roux,* or *Bonjour Patrick,* when you know the person. The OQLF advises against an exclamation mark after *Bonjour*.
3. **The body**. Open with a short context, then state your request in the first paragraph. One subject per email, short sentences.
4. **The closing** (*la formule de fin*). *Cordialement,* is a safe choice in most work emails. For a manager, *Salutations distinguées,* is more formal.
5. **The signature block**: your name, your title, your organisation and your phone number, on separate lines.

### Words for the body

*Ci-joint* means "attached, enclosed". As an adverb at the start of a phrase it does not change: *Ci-joint le rapport.* *Vous trouverez ci-joint le rapport.* A file you attach is *une pièce jointe*, and you can write *en pièce jointe*: *Le rapport est en pièce jointe.* The OQLF [terminology entry](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/8362585/piece-jointe) gives *pièce jointe* as the French term (abbreviated *p. j.*). The [guide on ci-joint](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/index.php?id=21484) explains when it agrees. Mention the attachment in the text, so the reader looks for it.

*Faire parvenir* means "to send, to forward" and sounds a little more formal than *envoyer*: *Pourriez-vous me faire parvenir le rapport?* *Je vous ferai parvenir les documents.* (Only *faire* changes: *je ferai parvenir*.)

*À la suite de* means "following": *À la suite de votre appel, je vous écris...* The shorter *suite à* is very common in business writing too. I could not confirm a ruling from the OQLF on the two forms, so this book writes *à la suite de* and tells you that you will also see *suite à* in real emails.

*Dans l'attente de* means "while waiting for", and closes a request: *Dans l'attente de votre réponse, je vous remercie.* *Un destinataire* is the person who receives the email, and *un expéditeur* is the person who sends it. *Une copie conforme* is a copy sent to another person (often shortened to *cc* in email software).

### A model email

Vous with a supplier you know by name. Read it once, then look at where each part goes.

```fr
Objet : Report de la réunion du 12 mars | Subject: Postponing the meeting of March 12
Bonjour Madame Roux, | Hello Ms. Roux,
À la suite de votre appel, je vous écris pour déplacer notre réunion du 12 mars. | Following your call, I am writing to move our meeting of March 12.
Pourriez-vous me confirmer une autre date, de préférence la semaine prochaine? | Could you confirm another date, preferably next week?
Je vous fais parvenir ci-joint l'ordre du jour. | I am sending you the agenda attached.
Dans l'attente de votre réponse, je vous remercie pour votre compréhension. | While waiting for your reply, I thank you for your understanding.
Cordialement, | Best regards,
Nadine Gagnon | Nadine Gagnon
Gestionnaire de projet, Agence Alpha | Project manager, Alpha Agency
```

The model has about 55 words. Yours should be longer: add the reason for the change and one more thank-you sentence. The people, the agency and the dates are invented.

### Your writing task

Write an email of 80 to 100 words to a supplier (*vous*). You need to change a meeting. Use this plan.

1. *Objet :* an action and a date.
2. A greeting.
3. A first sentence that says why you write (*À la suite de...* or *Je vous écris au sujet de...*).
4. A request with *pourriez-vous* and a new date.
5. One sentence with *ci-joint* or *en pièce jointe*.
6. *Dans l'attente de votre réponse*, then your thanks.
7. A closing and a signature of three lines.

Check your email against the five parts. Count your words. Read it aloud once: if you stumble, the sentence is too long.

## Écoutez

Écoutez la réponse de Mme Roux. Listen for the new date and for the closing.

```fr
Objet : Réponse : report de la réunion du 12 mars | Subject: Reply: postponing the meeting of March 12
Bonjour Madame Gagnon, | Hello Ms. Gagnon,
Merci de votre message. Le 12 mars ne sera pas possible pour nous. | Thank you for your message. March 12 will not be possible for us.
Nous pourrons nous rencontrer le mardi 19 mars, à 10 h, en visioconférence. | We can meet on Tuesday, March 19, at 10, by video.
Je vous enverrai le lien la veille. | I will send you the link the day before.
Meilleures salutations, | Best regards,
Isabelle Roux | Isabelle Roux
Société Dufour | Dufour company
```

## Dites-le

Dites les phrases à voix haute.

```fr
Je vous écris au sujet de notre réunion. | I am writing to you about our meeting.
Pourriez-vous me faire parvenir le rapport? | Could you send me the report?
Je vous fais parvenir ci-joint l'ordre du jour. | I am sending you the agenda attached.
Dans l'attente de votre réponse, je vous remercie. | While waiting for your reply, I thank you.
```

## Erreurs fréquentes

Writing a subject line in capitals or with exclamation marks: *URGENT!!!*. Say the action and the date instead.

Writing *Bonjour!* followed by the name in a work email. The OQLF advises against an exclamation mark after *Bonjour*.

Forgetting to mention the attachment in the text. Write *Ci-joint* or *en pièce jointe*, so the reader looks for it.

## Au Québec

Quebec writes *courriel* for "email" and *pièce jointe* for "attachment". *Courriel* is the OQLF's recommended term, and *pièce jointe* is the term in its entry (see the entries linked above). In Quebec offices, a first name in the greeting (*Bonjour Patrick,*) is common between colleagues, as the OQLF page on greetings shows.
