## L'idée

Some common verbs do not use the infinitive as the stem of the futur simple. They have their own **stem** (*le radical*, the part of the verb before the ending). The endings are the same as in the last lesson: *`-ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont`*. Every stem ends in *r*, so the *r* sound still comes right before the ending. You learn the stems as a list. Once you know the stem, the six forms follow.

### The stems

| Infinitive | Stem | Futur simple (je) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| *être* | ser- | *je serai* | I will be |
| *avoir* | aur- | *j'aurai* | I will have |
| *aller* | ir- | *j'irai* | I will go |
| *faire* | fer- | *je ferai* | I will do |
| *pouvoir* | pourr- | *je pourrai* | I will be able |
| *vouloir* | voudr- | *je voudrai* | I will want |
| *devoir* | devr- | *je devrai* | I will have to |
| *venir* | viendr- | *je viendrai* | I will come |
| *voir* | verr- | *je verrai* | I will see |
| *savoir* | saur- | *je saurai* | I will know |
| *falloir* | faudr- | *il faudra* | it will be needed |
| *envoyer* | enverr- | *j'enverrai* | I will send |
| *recevoir* | recevr- | *je recevrai* | I will receive |
| *courir* | courr- | *je courrai* | I will run |

*Falloir* exists only with *il*: *Il faudra être là à 9 h.* ("We will have to be there at 9.") The verbs built on *venir* (*revenir*, *devenir*) and on *voir* follow the same pattern: *je reviendrai*, *je deviendrai*.

*Recevoir* is new, so here is its present: *je reçois, tu reçois, il reçoit, nous recevons, vous recevez, ils reçoivent*. The *c* has a cedilla (*ç*) before *o* so it keeps the sound /s/.

### Full forms

Here are two verbs in full, so you see that the endings do not change.

| | être | aller |
|---|---|---|
| je | serai | irai |
| tu | seras | iras |
| il / elle / on | sera | ira |
| nous | serons | irons |
| vous | serez | irez |
| ils / elles | seront | iront |

Pronounce the pairs with care. *Je serai* /ʒə səʁe/ and *j'irai* /ʒiʁe/ end in /e/. *Il aura* and *il ira* sound close, but *aura* has /oʁa/ and *ira* has /iʁa/.

### Set phrases and time words

Three set phrases use the same stems. *Il y aura* means "there will be": *Il y aura une réunion demain.* *Il fera beau* means "the weather will be nice". *Il faudra* comes from *falloir*. Two more words help with time: *prochainement* ("soon, in the near future") and *ultérieurement* ("later, at a later date"). Both are written and formal: *Les détails seront envoyés ultérieurement.* *Prochainement* also appears on signs and in announcements.

### Deadlines

For a **deadline** or due date, French has two phrases: *une échéance* (a due date, often for payments or tasks) and *une date limite* (a last date). A **delay** or time allowed is *un délai*: *Le délai est de deux semaines.* Avoid the false friend: *un délai* is not a "delay" in the sense of lateness. A late arrival is *un retard*.

A schedule of dates is *un échéancier* in Quebec, or *un calendrier*. The Office québécois de la langue française (OQLF) gives *calendrier* as its preferred word for a schedule fixed in advance. Its terminology entry for [calendrier](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/8871611/calendrier) notes that in Quebec the document is sometimes called *échéancier*, and that in management it is better to keep *échéancier* for a document that lists deadlines in order. Both words are fine in a work conversation.

To say something will be done ahead of time, use *à l'avance*: *Nous vous enverrons les documents à l'avance.*

## Écoutez

Écoutez le dialogue. Carole (45) is a project manager. She gives deadlines to a client, M. Fortin (50). They use *vous*. Listen for the stem before each ending.

```fr
Carole: Bonjour, monsieur Fortin. Voici l'échéancier du projet. | Hello, Mr. Fortin. Here is the schedule for the project.
Fortin: Merci. Quand est-ce que je recevrai le premier rapport? | Thank you. When will I receive the first report?
Carole: Vous le recevrez le 12 mars. C'est notre date limite. | You will receive it on March 12. That is our deadline.
Fortin: Et le prix final? | And the final price?
Carole: Nous confirmerons le prix ultérieurement. Il faudra deux semaines. | We will confirm the price later. It will take two weeks.
Fortin: Est-ce que vous serez à Montréal le 20? | Will you be in Montreal on the 20th?
Carole: Oui, j'irai à votre bureau, et mon adjoint viendra aussi. | Yes, I will go to your office, and my assistant will come too.
Fortin: Très bien. Je pourrai voir les résultats à ce moment-là? | Very good. Will I be able to see the results then?
Carole: Oui, vous verrez tout. Nous vous enverrons les documents à l'avance. | Yes, you will see everything. We will send you the documents ahead of time.
```

## À vous

1. Complétez : *Nous ___ (être) à Ottawa lundi.* *Tu ___ (avoir) les chiffres demain.* *Ils ___ (aller) au bureau.*
2. Écrivez au futur simple : *il faut*, *je peux*, *vous voulez*, *elles viennent*.
3. Répondez en une phrase : « Quand est-ce que vous recevrez le message? » (Choisissez une date.)
4. Corrigez les deux phrases du bloc.

```fr
J'enverai le message demain. {x}
Nous serrons là à midi. {x}
```

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Nous serons à Ottawa lundi.* *Tu auras les chiffres demain.* *Ils iront au bureau.*
2. *il faudra*, *je pourrai*, *vous voudrez*, *elles viendront*.
3. Example: *Je recevrai le message le 12 mars.*
4. *J'enverrai le message demain.* (*enverr-* has two *r*s.) *Nous serons là à midi.* (*ser-* has one *r*, and the ending is *-ons*.)

</details>

## Dites-le

Dites les phrases à voix haute.

```fr
Je serai au bureau à 9 h. | I will be at the office at 9.
Nous ferons le travail à l'avance. | We will do the work ahead of time.
Vous recevrez le message avant la date limite. | You will receive the message before the deadline.
Il faudra deux semaines. | It will take two weeks.
Ils viendront ultérieurement. | They will come later.
```

## Erreurs fréquentes

Using the infinitive as the stem for an irregular verb: *je ferai*, not *je faireai*. Learn the stem list.

Writing one *r* in the stems with two: *je verrai*, *j'enverrai*, *je pourrai*, *je courrai*. The double *r* gives the strong /ʁ/ sound.

Mixing up *je serai* (from *être*) and *je saurai* (from *savoir*). The vowels differ: /sə/ and /so/.

## Au Québec

In Quebec a work schedule is sometimes called *un échéancier*, and *une échéance* is a due date. The OQLF entry linked above prefers *calendrier* for a general schedule. Use whichever word your coworkers use.
