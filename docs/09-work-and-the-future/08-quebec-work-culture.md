## L'idée

This lesson describes practices. It does not give legal advice, and laws change, so each fact below has a link and is stated as of 2026. If you work in Quebec, read the official page for your own situation.

### French as the language of work

In Quebec, French is the normal language of work. The Charter of the French language is the main law on this. [Éducaloi](https://www.educaloi.qc.ca/en/capsules/language-workplace-quebec), a legal information group, summarises it: workers covered by Quebec law can work in French. Employers must provide staff communications and official workplace documents (such as conditions of employment and collective agreements) in French. An employer may require another language, such as English, only in limited cases, when the job truly needs it. People who speak little French can still work in Quebec when their employer follows the language rules. The page also says that these rules do not apply to public servants in federal institutions, who fall under federal law.

The Office québécois de la langue française (OQLF) runs the process called **francisation** (*la francisation*, "making French the common language of an organisation"). The [OQLF page for businesses](https://www.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/francisation/entreprises/) sets out the steps. In a [press release of 6 May 2024](https://www.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/office/communiques/2024/20240506_entreprises_25_49_personnes.aspx), the OQLF said that registration becomes mandatory for firms with 25 to 49 employees from 1 June 2025, and it invited them to register early. Its page for businesses says firms with 25 or more employees in Quebec over a six-month period register. The rules for each size of company change, so check the OQLF site.

The word *un organisme* means a public body or non-profit group, such as the OQLF.

### Contracts, unions and rights

*Un employeur* is the company or person who hires you. *Un contrat* (*un contrat de travail*) is the written agreement. *Un syndicat* is a union. *Une convention collective* is the agreement between the employer and the union, which sets pay and rules for the group. *Une assurance collective* is group insurance, a plan for medical or dental costs that a workplace offers.

The CNESST (the Commission des normes, de l'équité, de la santé et de la sécurité du travail) is the Quebec body for workplace rights and safety. Its page on [annual vacation](https://www.cnesst.gouv.qc.ca/en/working-conditions/leave/annual-vacation) is where to look for the vacation rules. As of 2026, a short summary: after one year of service, two weeks of paid vacation, and after three years, three weeks. During the first year, vacation builds up at one day per full month, up to two weeks. An employer can offer more. *Un congé parental* is parental leave, time off after a birth or adoption. The details of the amounts and lengths are on the official sites, so this book does not list them.

### Tu and vous at work

In many Quebec offices, colleagues use first names and *tu* with each other, even with a manager. With a client, an older person, or someone you have not met, *vous* is still the safe start. Listen to how people speak to you. If your colleague says *tu* to you, answer with *tu*. Using *tu* is called *le tutoiement*, and using *vous* is *le vouvoiement*. A manager can also say *on se tutoie?* ("shall we use tu?"). In writing to a client, keep *vous*. The reader should not copy the casual *tu* of an office into a first email to someone outside the company.

### After work: le 5 à 7

*Un 5 à 7* (*un cinq à sept*) is a social drink between 5 p.m. and 7 p.m., often after work. You met it in [Table culture in Quebec](../06-habits-memories-and-food/09-quebec-table-culture.md). At work, it can be an event at a bar or a restaurant, sometimes with a colleague's farewell or a company event. You can say no politely: *Merci, mais je ne peux pas ce soir.*

## Écoutez

Écoutez les deux dialogues. Camille (28) commence à travailler dans une entreprise de Montréal. Dans le premier, elle parle avec Mme Lafleur (47), des ressources humaines. Elles se disent *vous*. Dans le deuxième, Camille parle avec un collègue, Simon (32). Ils se disent *tu*.

```fr
Lafleur: Bienvenue, Camille. Voici votre contrat. | Welcome, Camille. Here is your contract.
Camille: Merci. Est-ce que tous les documents sont en français? | Thank you. Are all the documents in French?
Lafleur: Oui. Voici aussi les informations sur l'assurance collective. | Yes. Here is also the information on the group insurance.
Camille: Et les vacances? Combien de semaines est-ce que j'aurai? | And vacation? How many weeks will I have?
Lafleur: Après un an de service, vous aurez deux semaines. Vous pouvez vérifier sur le site de la CNESST. | After one year of service, you will have two weeks. You can check on the CNESST site.
Camille: D'accord. Je vous remercie. | All right. Thank you.

Simon: Salut Camille! Ici, on se tutoie. | Hi Camille! Here, we use tu with each other.
Camille: D'accord, merci. Et avec la directrice? | All right, thanks. And with the manager?
Simon: Elle aussi me tutoie. Mais avec les clients, on se vouvoie. | She uses tu with me too. But with clients, we use vous.
Simon: Ce soir, il y a un 5 à 7 au bar du coin. Tu viens? | Tonight there is a 5 à 7 at the bar on the corner. Are you coming?
Camille: Avec plaisir. Je viendrai vers 17 h 30. | With pleasure. I'll come around 5:30.
```

## À vous

1. Répondez aux questions : Qu'est-ce que Camille reçoit de Mme Lafleur? Combien de semaines de vacances Camille aura-t-elle après un an? Comment est-ce qu'on se parle dans ce bureau?
2. Choisissez *tu* ou *vous* : (a) à un client, (b) à un collègue qui dit *tu*, (c) à un directeur que vous ne connaissez pas.
3. Complétez : *Un ___ est un accord entre l'employeur et l'employé.* *Un ___ aide les employés à défendre leurs droits.* (*contrat*, *syndicat*, *organisme*)
4. Écrivez deux phrases pour refuser poliment un 5 à 7.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. Son contrat et les informations sur l'assurance collective. Deux semaines. Au bureau, on se tutoie; avec les clients, on dit *vous*.
2. (a) *vous*. (b) *tu*. (c) *vous*.
3. *Un contrat est un accord entre l'employeur et l'employé.* *Un syndicat aide les employés à défendre leurs droits.*
4. Example: *Merci pour l'invitation. Je ne peux pas ce soir, mais ce sera pour la prochaine fois.*

</details>

## Dites-le

Dites les phrases à voix haute.

```fr
Ici, on se tutoie. | Here, we use tu with each other.
Avec les clients, on se vouvoie. | With clients, we use vous.
Est-ce que tous les documents sont en français? | Are all the documents in French?
Il y a un 5 à 7 ce soir. Tu viens? | There is a 5 à 7 tonight. Are you coming?
```

## Erreurs fréquentes

Using *tu* with a client or a stranger because colleagues use it. In a first contact, start with *vous*.

Believing that every Quebec workplace uses *tu*. Many do, but some do not. Listen first.

Treating a source on Quebec rules as legal advice. This lesson only describes. Check official pages for your own case.

## Au Québec

The Charter of the French language and its rules on work are described above, with sources, as of 2026. French is the normal language of work, and an employer may require another language only in limited cases. Rules and company sizes change, so the OQLF and Éducaloi pages are the places to look for the current version.
