## L'idée

You already know one way to talk about what comes next: the **futur proche** (*le futur proche*, "near future"), as in *je vais travailler demain*. The **futur simple** (*le futur simple*, "simple future") is a second way. It is one word, not two: *je travaillerai*. It is the form you read in contracts, schedules and news, and the form you hear in promises and in speeches. In daily talk, many speakers use the futur proche for plans and keep the futur simple for promises, predictions and more formal speech.

### Building the form

The futur simple of a regular verb is the infinitive plus the endings *`-ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont`*. These are the present tense of *avoir* (*j'ai, tu as, il a, nous avons, vous avez, ils ont*) with the *av-* cut off from *nous* and *vous*. You hear the infinitive, then the ending.

1. Take the infinitive: *parler*, *finir*, *vendre*.
2. For a verb in *-re*, drop the final *e*: *vendr-*. Verbs in *-er* and *-ir* keep the whole infinitive.
3. Add the ending for the person.

| | parler | finir | vendre |
|---|---|---|---|
| je | parlerai | finirai | vendrai |
| tu | parleras | finiras | vendras |
| il / elle / on | parlera | finira | vendra |
| nous | parlerons | finirons | vendrons |
| vous | parlerez | finirez | vendrez |
| ils / elles | parleront | finiront | vendront |

Listen for the *r* before every ending: *parlerai* /paʁləʁe/. The *r* is what marks the futur simple. In careful speech the *e* inside the verb is often dropped: *je parlerai* sounds close to /ʒə paʁlʁe/. Watch the final sound of *je parlerai*: it is /e/, as in *été*. The conditionnel you meet in lesson 3 ends in /ɛ/ (*je parlerais*), as in *père*. Textbooks teach this contrast, and it is the only audible difference between the two tenses for *je*. In everyday speech many speakers pronounce the two endings alike, so the rest of the sentence also tells you which tense you hear.

### Spelling changes in -er verbs

A few *-er* verbs change their spelling, because the stem now has a silent *e* in the middle. You already met this with *acheter* in the present (*j'achète*). The futur simple uses the same change in every person, including *nous* and *vous*:

| Infinitive | Futur simple (je) | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| *acheter* | *j'achèterai* | *e* becomes *è* |
| *se lever* | *je me lèverai* | *e* becomes *è* |
| *appeler* | *j'appellerai* | the letter l doubles |
| *payer* | *je paierai* | *y* becomes *i* (*je payerai* is also correct) |
| *préférer* | *je préférerai* | no change in writing: the *é* stays |

*Manger* and *commencer* need no change in the futur simple: you write *je mangerai* and *je commencerai*. If you doubt a spelling, say the verb aloud. A doubled *l* or an *è* gives the open sound /ɛ/ before the *r*, as in *appellerai* /apɛləʁe/.

### What the futur simple is for

Three uses come first. A **plan**: *Je commencerai lundi.* A **promise**: *Je finirai ce soir.* A **prediction**: *Il neigera demain.* The futur simple also appears after *quand* ("when"), *dès que* ("as soon as"), *lorsque* ("when", a little more formal) and *aussitôt que* ("as soon as"), when the event has not happened yet. English uses the present here ("I'll call you when I arrive"), but French needs the future: *Je vous appellerai quand j'arriverai.* Watch this: *quand j'arrive* in French usually means a habit ("whenever I arrive").

## Écoutez

Écoutez le dialogue. Nadine (29) starts work at an agency in Gatineau. She talks with her manager, Étienne (52). They use *vous*. Listen for the *r* before each ending.

```fr
Étienne: Bonjour, Nadine. Quels sont vos objectifs pour le premier mois? | Hello, Nadine. What are your goals for the first month?
Nadine: Je rencontrerai toute l'équipe cette semaine. | I will meet the whole team this week.
Étienne: Très bien. Et ensuite? | Very good. And then?
Nadine: Je lirai les dossiers des clients. Je finirai les premiers projets d'ici vendredi. | I will read the client files. I will finish the first projects by Friday.
Étienne: Quand est-ce que vous commencerez à parler aux clients? | When will you start talking to clients?
Nadine: Dès que j'aurai les bons documents, je téléphonerai au premier client. | As soon as I have the right documents, I will phone the first client.
Étienne: Parfait. On prévoit une réunion avec lui lundi prochain. | Perfect. We are planning a meeting with him next Monday.
Nadine: D'ici là, je préparerai une présentation. | Until then, I will prepare a presentation.
Étienne: Je vous enverrai les chiffres demain. | I will send you the figures tomorrow.
Nadine: Merci. Plus tard, nous achèterons un nouvel écran pour l'équipe. | Thank you. Later, we will buy a new screen for the team.
```

## À vous

1. Complétez avec le futur simple : *Nous ___ (parler) au client demain.* *Tu ___ (finir) le projet ce soir.* *Elle ___ (vendre) sa voiture.*
2. Écrivez la forme de *je* : *acheter*, *appeler*, *payer*, *se lever*.
3. Choisissez : *Je vous appellerai dès que j'___ les chiffres.* (*ai* ou *aurai*)
4. Répondez : « Qu'est-ce que vous ferez ce week-end? » Dites trois choses avec le futur simple.

1. *Nous parlerons au client demain.* *Tu finiras le projet ce soir.* *Elle vendra sa voiture.*
2. *j'achèterai*, *j'appellerai*, *je paierai* (or *je payerai*), *je me lèverai*.
3. *aurai*. After *dès que*, French uses the futur simple for an event that has not happened yet.
4. Example: *Je dormirai tard. Je visiterai un musée. Je téléphonerai à mes parents.* (Any three correct futur simple sentences.)

## Erreurs fréquentes

Cutting the *r*: *je parlai* is not the futur simple. Write and say *je parlerai*.

Forgetting the changes in *-er* verbs: *j'achetterai* or *j'appelerai* are wrong. Write *j'achèterai* and *j'appellerai*.

Using the present after *quand* for a future event: *Je vous appelle quand j'arrive* means a habit. For one coming event, say *Je vous appellerai quand j'arriverai*.

## Termes clés

- **Futur simple**: the one-word future tense, made from the infinitive plus *`-ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont`*.
- **Futur proche**: the near future, made with *aller* plus an infinitive.
- **Spelling change**: a change in the stem of some *-er* verbs, such as *è* in *j'achèterai* or a doubled *l* in *j'appellerai*.
