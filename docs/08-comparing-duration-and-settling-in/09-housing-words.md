## The idea

Finding a place to live is a big part of settling in. French ads are short and use abbreviations, so you learn the words in three groups: the place, the money and the paper, and the move.

### The place

*Un logement* is the general word for a home you live in (an apartment, a house). *Un appartement* is an apartment. Inside, you find *un salon* (living room), *une cuisine*, *une salle à manger* (dining room), *une chambre* (bedroom), *une salle de bain* (bathroom), *un balcon* and *un sous-sol* (basement). The kitchen often comes with *un réfrigérateur* (fridge, and *le frigo* in speech) and *un lave-vaisselle* (dishwasher). *Une laveuse* is a washing machine. Usito marks that sense Q/C, with *lave-linge* and *machine à laver* as synonyms ([Usito: laveuse](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/définitions/laveuse)).

Three adjectives appear in many ads. *Meublé* means furnished. *Chauffé* means heated, and in an ad it usually means that heating is included in the rent. *Éclairé* means lit, and it usually means that electricity for lights is included. These are usual meanings, so ask what is included when you phone.

### Reading the "4 1/2"

Quebec ads count rooms with a number and sometimes a "1/2": *un 3 1/2*, *un 4 1/2*, *un 5 1/2*. You say *un trois et demi*, *un quatre et demi*. The whole number counts rooms such as the living room, the kitchen and the bedrooms. The "1/2" stands for a small space, most often a bathroom. The OQLF says that the fraction 1/2 is used most often for a *salle d'eau* (a small room with a toilet and a sink or shower) or a *cuisinette* (a very small kitchen), and that bathrooms and similar spaces are not usually counted as rooms in a description of a home ([OQLF: pièce](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/2077843/piece)). So *un 4 1/2* has four counted rooms and a bathroom. It often means a living room, a kitchen and two bedrooms, but ads do not all count the same way, so read the list of rooms.

### Money and paper

*Le loyer* is the rent: *950 $ par mois* (950 dollars a month). *Un bail* is the lease, the written agreement between *le propriétaire* (owner, landlord) and *le locataire* (tenant). *Un dépôt* is a deposit. Rules about leases and deposits belong to the Tribunal administratif du logement (TAL), and this book does not give legal advice. For a real lease, read the TAL pages at tal.gouv.qc.ca. *Une colocation* is a shared apartment, and *un colocataire* (*une colocataire*) is a roommate. The verb *louer* means "to rent", and it covers both sides: the tenant rents the apartment from the owner, and the owner rents it to the tenant.

### Moving

*Un déménagement* is a move, and *déménager* is the verb (you know it from [The futur proche](../04-people-places-and-directions/06-futur-proche.md)). Moving day in Quebec has a date. The TAL says that most leases in Quebec end on 30 June, so many moves happen on 1 July. A TAL notice also says that a new tenant's right to occupy a home starts on the first day of the lease ([Québec.ca: moves of 1 July, as of 2026](https://www.quebec.ca/nouvelles/actualites/details/demenagements-du-1er-juillet-le-tribunal-administratif-du-logement-au-bout-du-fil-71438)). You will hear *le 1er juillet* or *le jour du déménagement* in conversations in June.

## Hear it

Here are two invented ads, to read aloud. The prices are made up for practice.

```fr
4 1/2 à louer, Rosemont. Deux chambres, salon, cuisine, balcon. Chauffé, éclairé. Libre le 1er juillet. 950 $ par mois. | 4 1/2 for rent, Rosemont. Two bedrooms, living room, kitchen, balcony. Heated, with lights included. Free on July 1. 950 dollars a month.
3 1/2 meublé, Verdun. Sous-sol, une chambre, salon, cuisine. Non chauffé. 700 $ par mois. | Furnished 3 1/2, Verdun. Basement, one bedroom, living room, kitchen. Not heated. 700 dollars a month.
```

Now a short story about moving day, told by Léa, 29, to a friend with *tu*. Listen for *le propriétaire*, *le bail* and *déménager*.

```fr
Léa: Le premier juillet, nous avons déménagé dans un 4 1/2. | On July 1st, we moved into a 4 1/2.
Léa: Le propriétaire m'a donné les clés le matin. Le bail commence le premier juillet. | The landlord gave me the keys in the morning. The lease starts on July 1st.
Léa: L'appartement est chauffé, mais il n'est pas meublé. | The apartment is heated, but it isn't furnished.
Léa: J'ai un salon, deux chambres, une cuisine et un balcon. | I have a living room, two bedrooms, a kitchen and a balcony.
Léa: Mon colocataire a apporté le réfrigérateur et la laveuse. | My roommate brought the fridge and the washing machine.
Léa: Quel déménagement! Nous avons fini à dix heures du soir. | What a move! We finished at ten at night.
```

## Try it

1. Read the first ad aloud. Then answer in English: how many bedrooms, is it heated, and from when is it free?
2. What does the "1/2" in *un 4 1/2* stand for, most often?
3. Match each word to its meaning: *le loyer*, *le bail*, *le propriétaire*, *le locataire*. The meanings are: tenant, lease, rent, owner.
4. Say in French: "The apartment is furnished." "It is not heated." "My roommate rents a 3 1/2."
5. Write a short ad of 20 to 30 words for a 4 1/2 in your city. Use at least three words from the lesson.

2. A bathroom (or a small space such as a *salle d'eau* or a *cuisinette*), which is not counted as a full room.
3. *Le loyer* is the rent, *le bail* is the lease, *le propriétaire* is the owner, *le locataire* is the tenant.
4. *L'appartement est meublé.* *Il n'est pas chauffé.* *Mon colocataire loue un 3 1/2.*
5. Example: *4 1/2 à louer, centre-ville. Deux chambres, salon, cuisine. Balcon. Chauffé. Libre le 1er juillet. 1 000 $ par mois.*

C'est un 4 1/2 chauffé et éclairé. | It's a heated 4 1/2 with lights included.

## Common mistakes

Counting the bathroom as a full room: *un 4 1/2* is not five rooms.

Assuming *chauffé* always means everything is included. Ask about heating and lights when you phone.

Using *louer* for only one side. *Je loue un appartement* can mean you are the tenant or the owner, so add *à* when it matters: *Je loue mon appartement à un ami.*

## In Quebec

*Un 4 1/2* and *une laveuse* are Quebec habits. The first-of-July moving day, as the TAL describes it, is a pattern in Quebec because so many leases end on 30 June. If you move in a different month, the lease can start on another date.

## Key terms

- **Participle used as an adjective**: a past participle that describes a noun and agrees with it, such as *meublé*, *chauffé* and *éclairé* (*une cuisine éclairée*).
