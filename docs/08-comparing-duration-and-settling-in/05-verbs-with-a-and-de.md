# Verbs followed by à and de

<span class="tag tag-grammar">Grammar</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can put a second verb after the first with the right small word (*commencer à courir*, *essayer d'arrêter*), and you know which common verbs take *à*, which take *de* and which take nothing.

Before this: [Vouloir, pouvoir and devoir](../06-habits-memories-and-food/02-vouloir-pouvoir-devoir.md), [Regular -er verbs](../03-verbs-and-questions/02-regular-er-verbs.md), [Prendre, venir, boire and voir](../05-the-past-what-happened/02-prendre-venir-boire-voir.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

When two verbs follow each other, the second one is in the **infinitive** (*l'infinitif*), the form you find in a dictionary: *parler*, *finir*, *vendre*. The first verb carries the person and the tense: *Je veux parler.* *Nous avons fini de manger.*

Between the two verbs there is sometimes nothing, sometimes *à* and sometimes *de*. No rule predicts which one, so you learn it verb by verb. There is only a rough tendency: verbs of starting, continuing, learning, helping and succeeding like *à*, and verbs of finishing, stopping, deciding, refusing, forgetting and trying like *de*. Use the lists below and say the whole chunk each time (*commencer à*, *finir de*), so the small word comes with the verb.

### Verbs with nothing between them

You already know these: *vouloir* (*je veux partir*), *pouvoir*, *devoir*, *aimer*, *adorer*, *détester*, *préférer*, *aller* (*je vais manger*), *il faut* and *espérer*. They go straight to the infinitive.

### Verbs followed by à

| Verb | Example |
|---|---|
| *commencer à* | *Elle commence à travailler.* |
| *continuer à* | *Nous continuons à travailler.* |
| *apprendre à* | *J'apprends à parler français.* |
| *réussir à* | *Elle a réussi à trouver un appartement.* |
| *hésiter à* | *N'hésitez pas à appeler.* |
| *aider quelqu'un à* | *Ma sœur m'aide à choisir.* |

*Aider* has the person right after it, and *à* comes after the person. The OQLF says *commencer à* is by far the usual form: its page on [commencer](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/22316/la-syntaxe/les-prepositions/preposition-apres-un-verbe/prepositions-a-employer-apres-le-verbe-commencer) says *commencer de* exists, but it is much less common and sounds formal or literary.

### Verbs followed by de

| Verb | Example |
|---|---|
| *finir de* | *J'ai fini de manger.* |
| *essayer de* | *Elle essaie de dormir.* |
| *décider de* | *Nous avons décidé de partir.* |
| *arrêter de* | *Il arrête de parler.* |
| *refuser de* | *Je refuse de payer.* |
| *accepter de* | *Il a accepté de venir.* |
| *oublier de* | *Tu as oublié de fermer la porte.* |
| *rêver de* | *Je rêve de voyager.* |
| *promettre de* | *Elle promet de téléphoner.* |
| *avoir besoin de* | *J'ai besoin de dormir.* |
| *avoir envie de* | *Nous avons envie de sortir.* |
| *venir de* | *Je viens de finir.* |

The last three are chunks you met already. *Ça permet de* means "it makes it possible to": *Ça permet de parler plus vite.* Before a vowel, *de* becomes *d'*: *essayer d'arrêter*, *décider d'acheter*.

For one verb at a time, the OQLF keeps a section on the [preposition after a verb](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/la-syntaxe/les-prepositions/preposition-apres-un-verbe) with pages on verbs such as *commencer* and *hésiter*.

### Nouns after the same verbs

Some of these verbs also take a noun. *Rêver de* works with both: *rêver de voyager*, *rêver de vacances*. Two others are mostly used with a noun in this book: *profiter de* (*profiter de l'été*, "to enjoy the summer") and *se souvenir de* (*Je me souviens de cette soirée*, "I remember that evening").

### Spelling, negation and agreement

1. *Commencer* adds a cedilla before *-o*: *je commence*, *nous commençons*. This keeps the *c* sound soft.
2. *Essayer* can change *y* to *i*: *j'essaie* or *j'essaye*, *nous essayons*, *vous essayez*. Both spellings are correct.
3. In the passé composé, put the first verb in the past and leave the infinitive alone: *J'ai commencé à courir.* *Elle a décidé de partir.*
4. For a negative, put *ne ... pas* around the first verb: *Je ne commence pas à travailler avant neuf heures.* To make the infinitive itself negative, keep *ne pas* together before it: *J'ai décidé de ne pas manger de biscuits.*

## Hear it

Lina is 41 and works as an accountant in Laval. She tells a friend, in the *tu* form, how she started running, a new verb: *courir* (to run). Listen for *à* or *de* between every pair of verbs.

```fr
Amie: Alors, Lina, tu fais du sport maintenant? | So, Lina, you're doing sports now?
Lina: Oui! J'ai décidé d'être plus active. J'ai commencé à courir en janvier. | Yes! I decided to be more active. I started running in January.
Lina: Au début, j'ai hésité à sortir quand il faisait froid. | At first, I hesitated to go out when it was cold.
Amie: Et maintenant? | And now?
Lina: Maintenant, je continue à courir trois fois par semaine. Ma sœur m'a aidée à choisir mes chaussures. | Now I keep running three times a week. My sister helped me choose my shoes.
Lina: J'ai aussi essayé d'arrêter de manger des biscuits le soir. | I also tried to stop eating cookies in the evening.
Amie: Et ça marche? | And is it working?
Lina: C'est difficile! Hier, j'ai oublié de fermer la boîte, et j'ai mangé tous les biscuits. | It's hard! Yesterday I forgot to close the box, and I ate all the cookies.
Amie: Tu rêves de courir un marathon? | Do you dream of running a marathon?
Lina: Non, j'ai seulement envie de continuer. | No, I just want to keep going.
```

## Try it

1. Add *à* or *de*. *J'ai commencé ___ apprendre le français.* *Nous avons fini ___ manger.* *Elle réussit ___ parler vite.* *Tu essaies ___ dormir?*
2. Choose *à*, *de* or nothing. *Je veux ___ partir.* *Il a décidé ___ rester.* *Ils continuent ___ travailler.* *Nous allons ___ manger.*
3. Say in French: "I have to stop eating cookies." "She decided to leave." "He is learning to swim."
4. Fix the mistake in each line.

```fr
J'ai fini à manger. {x}
Elle commence de travailler. {x}
Elle a oublié à fermer la porte. {x}
```

5. Write three true sentences about yourself with *commencer à*, *apprendre à* and *essayer de*.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *à*, *de*, *à*, *de*.
2. nothing (*vouloir*), *de*, *à*, nothing (*aller*).
3. *Je dois arrêter de manger des biscuits* (*devoir* takes the infinitive directly, and *arrêter* takes *de*). *Elle a décidé de partir.* *Il apprend à nager.*
4. *J'ai fini de manger.* *Elle commence à travailler.* *Elle a oublié de fermer la porte.*
5. Example: *J'ai commencé à étudier le français en septembre. J'apprends à cuisiner. J'essaie de dormir huit heures.* Your answers are different, so check that each verb has the right small word.

</details>

## Say it

```fr
J'ai commencé à courir en janvier. | I started running in January.
Nous avons décidé de partir. | We decided to leave.
J'essaie d'arrêter de manger des biscuits. | I'm trying to stop eating cookies.
Elle apprend à nager. | She's learning to swim.
Tu as oublié de fermer la porte. | You forgot to close the door.
```

## Common mistakes

Choosing the small word from English: *finir à manger*. Learn the chunk with the verb: *finir de*.

Leaving out the small word: *j'ai commencé courir*. *Commencer* always needs *à* or *de* before an infinitive, and the usual one is *à*.

Putting *de* before a vowel without dropping the vowel: *essayer de arrêter*. Say *essayer d'arrêter*.

## Key terms
