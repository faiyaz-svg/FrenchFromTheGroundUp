# Small talk: weather and hockey

## The idea

**Small talk** (*le bavardage*, and the verb is *bavarder*, "to chat") is light conversation about a safe topic (*un sujet*) with a colleague, a neighbour, a driver or someone in an elevator. It is not about the information. It says "I see you, and I'm friendly". The weather, the weekend and sport are the three usual topics in Canada, and every one of them works in a few steps.

### Five steps

1. Greet the person: *Bonjour, Julien.* (*Vous* or *tu*, as you would anyway.)
2. Make a comment: *Quelle tempête!* *Il fait froid, non?* *Il a beaucoup neigé hier soir.*
3. Answer the other person's comment with something small of your own: *Oui, vraiment. Il y avait de la poudrerie.*
4. Ask a follow-up question, so it is their turn: *Et vous? Vous êtes venue en métro?* *Et toi, tu as regardé le match?*
5. Close politely: *Bonne journée!* *À demain!*

The follow-up in step 4 keeps the conversation alive. A good one asks about something the other person has said, with *Et vous?* or *Et toi?* or with the question words from [Asking questions](../03-verbs-and-questions/04-asking-questions.md): *Qu'est-ce que vous avez fait?*

### Asking the other person to agree

A comment sounds friendlier when you invite agreement. The careful form is *n'est-ce pas?* ("isn't it?", "didn't it?"), put at the end: *Il a beaucoup neigé, n'est-ce pas?* It is correct and a bit formal, so you will use it with a stranger or at work. A shorter way is *non?*: *Il fait froid, non?* You will also hear *hein?* at the end of a sentence in Quebec. Usito lists *hein* as a word used to look for the listener's agreement, and links it to *n'est-ce pas* ([Usito: hein](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/définitions/hein)). It is casual, so write *hein* only in a casual message. Say it with people you know, and listen for it in the second dialogue below.

### Words that move the conversation

| French | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *au fait* | change the subject, "by the way" | *Au fait, tu as vu Marc?* |
| *d'ailleurs* | add something related, "besides", "in fact" | *J'adore le hockey. D'ailleurs, mon fils joue le samedi.* |
| *il paraît que* | pass on something you heard | *Il paraît qu'il va neiger demain.* |

After *il paraît que*, the verb is in the usual tense (here, the futur proche). The phrase tells the listener you are not sure.

### Hockey as a topic

Hockey is a common subject. You can ask *Tu as regardé le match hier soir?* or say *Mon fils joue au hockey depuis cinq ans.* Many people in Montréal follow *les Canadiens de Montréal*, the city's NHL team, which has an [official site](https://www.nhl.com/canadiens/). A safe way into the topic is to ask a question and listen. Leave your opinion about players, referees or results until you know how the other person feels. The next lesson gives you the hockey words.

## Hear it

Mme Gagnon is 58 and her colleague Julien is 33. They work in Montréal and use *vous*. They wait for the elevator after a snowstorm. Listen for the weather comment, the follow-up question and *n'est-ce pas*.

```fr
Gagnon: Bonjour, Julien. Il a beaucoup neigé hier soir, n'est-ce pas? | Good morning, Julien. It snowed a lot last night, didn't it?
Julien: Oui, vraiment. Il y avait de la poudrerie, et il faisait moins quinze. Vous êtes venue en métro? | Yes, really. There was blowing snow, and it was minus fifteen. Did you come by subway?
Gagnon: Oui, mais le métro était en retard. Et vous? | Yes, but the subway was late. And you?
Julien: Moi, j'ai marché. Au fait, vous avez passé une bonne fin de semaine? | I walked. By the way, did you have a good weekend?
Gagnon: Oui, merci. J'ai regardé un match de hockey avec mon fils. | Yes, thanks. I watched a hockey game with my son.
Julien: Ah oui? Il paraît qu'il va neiger encore demain. | Oh yes? I hear it's going to snow again tomorrow.
Gagnon: Vraiment? D'ailleurs, mes enfants adorent la neige. | Really? In fact, my children love the snow.
Julien: Ils ont de la chance! Ah, voilà l'ascenseur. Bonne journée, madame. | They're lucky! Oh, here's the elevator. Have a good day, madam.
```

Now Denis, 50, drives a bus in Montréal, and Chantal, 45, takes it every morning. They use *tu*, and the lines marked casual have *hein*.

```fr
Denis: Salut, Chantal! Tu as regardé le match hier soir? | Hi, Chantal! Did you watch the game last night?
Chantal: Un peu. J'ai travaillé tard, alors j'ai vu seulement la fin. | A little. I worked late, so I only saw the end.
Denis: Moi, j'ai tout regardé. C'était un bon match, hein? {qc} | I watched all of it. It was a good game, eh?
Chantal: Oui. Mon fils joue au hockey depuis cinq ans. | Yes. My son has played hockey for five years.
Denis: Ah oui? Il joue le samedi matin? | Oh yes? Does he play on Saturday mornings?
Chantal: Tous les samedis, à sept heures! | Every Saturday, at seven!
Denis: Quelle heure! Au fait, tu descends à la prochaine? | What a time! By the way, are you getting off at the next one?
Chantal: Oui. Bonne journée, Denis! | Yes. Have a good day, Denis!
Denis: Toi aussi. À demain! | You too. See you tomorrow!
```

## Try it

1. Someone says *Il fait froid, hein?* Write an answer of two short sentences that ends with a follow-up question.
2. Choose *au fait*, *d'ailleurs* or *il paraît*. *___ qu'il va neiger demain.* *___, tu as vu Marc hier?* *J'adore le hockey. ___, mon fils joue le samedi.*
3. Add a tag to each comment: *Il fait beau, ___?* (with a stranger at work) *Il fait beau, ___?* (with a friend, casual).
4. Say in French: "It snowed a lot last night, didn't it?" "Did you watch the game last night?" "It's going to snow again tomorrow, I hear."
5. Role-play with your voice. You meet a colleague (*vous*) at the elevator. Do the five steps: greet, comment on the weather, answer, ask a follow-up, close. Record it, then listen.

## Say it

```fr
Quelle tempête! Il a beaucoup neigé, n'est-ce pas? | What a storm! It snowed a lot, didn't it?
Et vous, vous avez passé une bonne fin de semaine? | And you, did you have a good weekend?
Au fait, tu as regardé le match hier soir? | By the way, did you watch the game last night?
Il paraît qu'il va neiger demain. | I hear it's going to snow tomorrow.
Bonne journée! À demain! | Have a good day! See you tomorrow!
```

## Common mistakes

Ending a small talk after one comment. Always add a follow-up question, such as *Et vous?*

Using *hein* with a stranger at work or in a written message. Use *n'est-ce pas* or *non*.

Using *au fait* to add a related idea. It changes the subject. For a related idea, use *d'ailleurs*.

## In Quebec

*Hein?* at the end of a sentence is a feature you will hear. It invites agreement, as *n'est-ce pas* does. Treat it as casual and use it with friends, and use *n'est-ce pas* or *non* in a more formal setting. Keep the hockey talk friendly: ask about the game and let the other person say what they think.
