# Comparatives

<span class="tag tag-grammar">Grammar</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can compare two people, places or things with *plus*, *moins*, *aussi* and *autant de*, and you can choose between *meilleur* and *mieux*.

Before this: [Adjectives and agreement](../04-people-places-and-directions/01-adjectives-agreement.md), [This, that, and moi, toi](../04-people-places-and-directions/04-this-that-and-moi-toi.md), [Adverbs ending in -ment](../06-habits-memories-and-food/07-adverbs-in-ment.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

A **comparative** (*le comparatif*) is a form that says one thing has more, less or the same amount of a quality as another thing. English builds it with "-er" or "more": taller, more expensive. French uses a small word before the adjective and the word *que* ("than" or "as") after it. The pattern has the same shape every time, so you learn it once.

### Adjectives and adverbs

| Idea | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| more | *plus* + adjective + *que* | *Montréal est plus petit que Toronto.* |
| less | *moins* + adjective + *que* | *Le bus est moins rapide que le métro.* |
| as ... as | *aussi* + adjective + *que* | *Ma sœur est aussi grande que moi.* |

The adjective still agrees with the first thing you mention, as in [Adjectives and agreement](../04-people-places-and-directions/01-adjectives-agreement.md): *Léa est plus grande que Paul*, *Les trains sont plus lents que les avions*. The word *que* becomes *qu'* before a vowel: *Il est plus fatigué qu'elle.*

The same pattern works with adverbs, the words that describe how a verb is done: *Tu parles plus vite que moi.* *Il travaille moins rapidement que sa collègue.* The adverb never changes.

After *que*, use the stressed pronouns you learned in stage 4: *que moi*, *que toi*, *que lui*, *qu'elle*, *que nous*, *que vous*, *qu'eux*, *qu'elles*. Never say *que je*: *Elle est plus grande que moi*.

In *plus intéressant*, you link the two words with liaison: /plyz ɛ̃teʁesɑ̃/. In *plus grand*, the *s* is silent: /ply gʁɑ̃/.

### Nouns and verbs

With a noun, use *de* (*d'*) and the same three ideas: *plus de*, *moins de*, and *autant de* for "as much" or "as many". *Autant de* has no *aussi* form.

| Idea | Example |
|---|---|
| more | *Il y a plus de gens à Toronto qu'à Montréal.* |
| less | *J'ai moins de temps que toi.* |
| as much, as many | *Elle a autant d'amis que Léa.* |

With a verb, put the comparison word after the verb and no *de*: *Je travaille plus que toi*, *Il dort moins que moi*, *Tu manges autant que lui.*

### Meilleur and mieux

English has "better" for both *bon* (good) and *bien* (well). French keeps them apart.

- *Meilleur* is the comparative of the adjective *bon*. It agrees: *meilleur*, *meilleure*, *meilleurs*, *meilleures*. *Ce café est meilleur que ce thé.*
- *Mieux* is the comparative of the adverb *bien*. It never changes. *Tu cuisines mieux que moi.*

Test: if the word describes a noun (a café, a soup), use *meilleur*. If it describes a verb (cook, speak), use *mieux*. You met *mieux* in [Adverbs ending in -ment](../06-habits-memories-and-food/07-adverbs-in-ment.md). *Aller mieux* means "to be better" in health: *Je vais mieux, merci.* Never say *plus bon* or *plus bien*; those forms don't exist. The adjective *mauvais* has *pire* ("worse"): *Le temps est pire aujourd'hui.* *Plus mauvais* is also correct.

### Words for comparing

A few adjectives come in pairs: *rapide* and *lent* (fast, slow), *léger* and *lourd* (light, heavy), *riche* and *pauvre* (rich, poor), *fort* (strong, and also "loud" in *parlez plus fort*) and *haut* (high, tall). *Différent* (*de*) means "different (from)", *pareil* means "the same", and *proche* (*de*) means "close (to)". *Plein* means "full".

## Hear it

Priya lives in Toronto and Samuel lives in Montréal. They are old friends, so they use *tu*. Listen for *que* after each comparison, and for the three forms *meilleure*, *meilleur* and *mieux*.

```fr
Priya: Alors, Samuel, Montréal ou Toronto? Quelle ville est la meilleure? | So, Samuel, Montréal or Toronto? Which city is the best?
Samuel: Ça dépend. Je trouve Montréal moins chère que Toronto. | It depends. I find Montréal less expensive than Toronto.
Priya: Oui, mon appartement est plus petit que ton appartement, mais il est plus cher! | Yes, my apartment is smaller than your apartment, but it's more expensive!
Samuel: Mais ton quartier est plus proche du centre-ville. Mon bus est plus lent que ton métro. | But your neighbourhood is closer to downtown. My bus is slower than your subway.
Priya: C'est vrai. Il y a plus de cafés chez toi, et le café est meilleur qu'ici. | That's true. There are more cafés where you live, and the coffee is better than here.
Samuel: Tu trouves? En tout cas, tu cuisines mieux que moi. | You think so? Anyway, you cook better than I do.
Priya: Merci! Et l'hiver? Il fait aussi froid à Montréal qu'à Toronto? | Thanks! And winter? Is it as cold in Montréal as in Toronto?
Samuel: Non, il fait plus froid ici. Mais on a autant de bons restaurants que vous! | No, it's colder here. But we have as many good restaurants as you do!
```

## Try it

1. Complete with *plus*, *moins* or *aussi*, to match the English. *Le métro est ___ rapide que le bus* (faster). *Un vélo est ___ lourd qu'une voiture* (lighter, so "less heavy"). *Ma mère est ___ grande que moi* (as tall).
2. Choose *meilleur(e)(s)* or *mieux*. *Cette soupe est ___ que la soupe d'hier.* *Karim cuisine ___ que moi.* *Ces pâtes sont ___ que ces pizzas.*
3. Say in French: "I have more time than you." "She has as many friends as Léa." "There are fewer people here."
4. Fix the mistake in each line.

```fr
Ce film est plus bon que l'autre. {x}
Elle est plus grande de moi. {x}
Il y a plus gens ici que là-bas. {x}
```

5. Write three comparisons between two places you know, one with *plus*, one with *moins* and one with *autant de*.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *plus*, *moins*, *aussi*.
2. *meilleure* (the soup is feminine), *mieux* (it describes the verb *cuisiner*), *meilleures* (feminine plural *pâtes*).
3. *J'ai plus de temps que toi.* *Elle a autant d'amis que Léa.* *Il y a moins de gens ici.*
4. *Ce film est meilleur que l'autre.* *Elle est plus grande que moi.* *Il y a plus de gens ici que là-bas.*
5. Example: *Toronto est plus grand que Sudbury. Sudbury est moins cher que Toronto. Il y a autant de neige à Sudbury qu'à Montréal.*

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Je suis plus fatigué que toi. | I'm more tired than you.
Ce café est meilleur que ce thé. | This coffee is better than this tea.
Tu parles mieux que moi. | You speak better than me.
Il y a moins de gens ici qu'à Toronto. | There are fewer people here than in Toronto.
Elle a autant d'amis que lui. | She has as many friends as him.
```

## Common mistakes

Saying *plus bon* for "better". Use *meilleur* with a noun and *mieux* with a verb.

Using *de* instead of *que* after a comparison: *plus grand de moi*. The word that follows is *que*.

Using *aussi* with a noun: *aussi d'amis*. With nouns, say *autant de*.
