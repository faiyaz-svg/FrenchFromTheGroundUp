# The relative pronouns qui and que

<span class="tag tag-grammar">Grammar</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can join two short sentences into one with *qui* and *que* (*qu'*), as in *la femme qui parle* and *le film que j'aime*.

Before this: [Direct object pronouns](../07-pronouns-and-getting-things-done/01-direct-object-pronouns.md), [The passé composé: no, questions and agreement](../05-the-past-what-happened/06-pc-negation-questions-agreement.md), [Adjectives and agreement](../04-people-places-and-directions/01-adjectives-agreement.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

You know *qui* and *que* as question words: *Qui parle?* (Who is talking?) and *Qu'est-ce que tu fais?* In this lesson they do a different job. A **relative pronoun** (*un pronom relatif*) joins two sentences by replacing a word in the second one. The word it replaces is called the **antecedent** (*l'antécédent*): it comes right before the pronoun and says who or what you are talking about.

Take two sentences: *J'ai un ami. Il habite à Gatineau.* The word *il* repeats *un ami*. Replace it with *qui* and join them: *J'ai un ami qui habite à Gatineau.* Here *un ami* is the antecedent.

### Qui is the subject

Use **qui** when the word you replace is the subject of the second sentence, which means it does the action. The next word is a verb.

*La femme qui parle est ma tante.* (The woman who is talking is my aunt.)
*Il y a une boulangerie qui ouvre à six heures.* (There is a bakery that opens at six.)

The verb after *qui* agrees with the antecedent: *les gens qui habitent ici*, *une femme qui parle*. The word *qui* never loses its *i*, so it is never *qu'*: *la femme qui arrive*.

### Que is the object

Use **que** when the word you replace is the direct object of the second sentence, which means it receives the action. The next word is a subject (a noun or a pronoun) and then a verb.

*C'est le film. J'aime ce film.* becomes *C'est le film que j'aime.* (Here is the film that I like.)
*C'est un restaurant que tout le monde aime.* (It's a restaurant that everyone likes.)

*Que* becomes *qu'* before a vowel sound: *le livre qu'elle lit*, *les amis qu'on invite*.

English can drop the pronoun ("the film I like"). French cannot: you must say *que*.

### How to choose

1. Find the two sentences and the word that repeats.
2. Ask what that word does in the second sentence. If it does the action (it is the subject), use *qui*. If it receives the action (it is the object), use *que*.
3. Quick check: after *qui* you find a verb (a small word such as *ne*, *me* or *nous* can come in between: *la personne qui nous aide*). After *que* you find a subject and then a verb.

### The participle agrees with que

In the passé composé with *avoir*, the participle agrees with a direct object that comes **before** the verb, as you saw in [The passé composé: no, questions and agreement](../05-the-past-what-happened/06-pc-negation-questions-agreement.md). *Que* stands for that direct object, so the participle agrees with the antecedent:

*La lettre que j'ai écrite est sur la table.* (*lettre* is feminine, so *écrite*.)
*Les amis que j'ai invités sont arrivés.* (plural, so *invités*.)
*Le film que j'ai vu était long.* (masculine, so no change.)

After *qui*, the verb agrees with the subject in the usual way and nothing else changes.

### Only a glance ahead

After a preposition, French uses *qui* for people: *la personne avec qui je travaille*. This book only shows you the shape here. *Dont* and *où* as relative words come in stage 10, and *ce qui* and *ce que* come in stage 12. Keep your own sentences inside *qui* and *que* for now.

### Words for kinds and places

*Un genre*, *une sorte* and *un type* mean "kind" or "sort": *Quel genre de musique écoutes-tu?* *Une manière*, *une façon* and *un moyen* are "way" or "means": *C'est une bonne façon d'apprendre.* *Un endroit* and *un lieu* mean "place". *Un endroit* is the usual word in speech.

## Hear it

Ravi is 38 and drives a taxi in Brampton, Ontario. A passenger from Gatineau asks about the neighbourhood, and they use *vous*. Listen for *qui* before a verb and for *que* or *qu'* before a subject.

```fr
Passagère: Bonjour. Vous habitez à Brampton depuis longtemps? | Hello. Have you lived in Brampton for a long time?
Ravi: Depuis quinze ans. C'est un quartier que j'aime beaucoup. | For fifteen years. It's a neighbourhood that I like a lot.
Passagère: Vous aimez quoi, surtout? | What do you like especially?
Ravi: Les gens qui habitent ici sont sympas. Il y a un restaurant que ma femme adore. | The people who live here are nice. There is a restaurant that my wife loves.
Ravi: Il y a aussi une boulangerie qui ouvre à six heures. C'est l'endroit que mes enfants préfèrent. | There is also a bakery that opens at six. It's the place my children prefer.
Passagère: Vous parlez français? | Do you speak French?
Ravi: Oui, j'ai un ami qui habite à Gatineau. Nous parlons français ensemble. | Yes, I have a friend who lives in Gatineau. We speak French together.
Passagère: Et quel genre de musique écoutez-vous dans votre taxi? | And what kind of music do you listen to in your taxi?
Ravi: La musique que mes clients aiment! | The music that my customers like!
```

## Try it

1. Choose *qui*, *que* or *qu'*. *La femme ___ parle est ma tante.* *Le café ___ je bois est chaud.* *J'ai un collègue ___ habite à Laval.* *Le film ___ elle regarde est long.*
2. Join the two sentences with *qui* or *que*. *J'ai un ami. Il parle quatre langues.* *C'est la lettre. J'ai écrit la lettre.* *C'est un restaurant. Tout le monde aime ce restaurant.*
3. Write the participle in the right form. *La ville que nous avons* (visiter). *Les amis que j'ai* (inviter). *Le livre que tu as* (lire).
4. Fix the mistake in each line.

```fr
Le film qui j'aime est drôle. {x}
C'est la femme que parle français. {x}
Les clés que j'ai perdu sont ici. {x}
```

5. Finish three sentences that start with *J'ai un ami qui*, *J'aime les gens qui* and *Mon café préféré est un endroit que*.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *qui* (*la femme* does the talking), *que* (*je bois le café*), *qui* (*le collègue* lives in Laval), *qu'* (*elle regarde le film*, and *elle* starts with a vowel sound).
2. *J'ai un ami qui parle quatre langues.* *C'est la lettre que j'ai écrite.* *C'est un restaurant que tout le monde aime.*
3. *visitée* (*ville* is feminine), *invités* (plural), *lu* (masculine, no change).
4. *Le film que j'aime est drôle* (*j'aime* has its own subject). *C'est la femme qui parle français* (*la femme* does the action). *Les clés que j'ai perdues sont ici* (feminine plural).
5. Example: *J'ai un ami qui habite à Ottawa. J'aime les gens qui parlent français. Mon café préféré est un endroit que mes amis aiment aussi.*

</details>

## Common mistakes

Using *qui* where *que* is needed: *le film qui j'aime*. After the pronoun there is a subject (*j'*), so use *que*.

Using *que* where *qui* is needed: *la femme que parle*. The word replaced is the subject, so use *qui*.

Forgetting the agreement with *que* in the passé composé. After *la lettre que j'ai*, the participle is *écrite* (feminine), because it agrees with the antecedent.

## Key terms

- **Relative pronoun**: a word such as *qui* or *que* that joins two sentences by replacing a word in the second one.
- **Antecedent**: the word that a relative pronoun refers to (*la femme* in *la femme qui parle*).
