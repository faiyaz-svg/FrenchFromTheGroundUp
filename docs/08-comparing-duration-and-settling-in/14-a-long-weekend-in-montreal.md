# A long weekend in Montreal

<span class="tag tag-quebec">Quebec</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span> <span class="tag tag-review">Review</span>

After this lesson you can plan a long weekend in Montréal in speech and in a short note, using the comparisons, time words, relative pronouns, verb patterns and weather words of stage 8.

## The idea

This lesson has no new grammar and no new rules. It brings back the whole stage in one conversation and one short note, so you can see what you can do now. Think of it as a check on yourself. You will listen to two friends plan a trip, say their lines aloud, and then write a note of your own. When something feels shaky, the table below tells you which lesson to go back to.

The story is simple. Ayesha lives in Toronto and wants to visit her friend Marc in Montréal around the Fête nationale. Between them they decide how she travels, where she sleeps, what they do and what the weather will be. Each decision uses a pattern from this stage, so one short conversation touches almost every lesson.

### New words for a trip

*Un forfait* is a package, a set of services for one price: *un forfait train et hôtel*. *Un laissez-passer* is a pass, a card or ticket that lets you travel or enter. *Un circuit* is a planned route or tour: *un circuit à vélo*. *Une file d'attente* is a line of people waiting. To wait in line, Quebec French says *faire la file*. Usito marks *faire la file* as Q/C and gives *faire la queue* as the same thing ([Usito: file](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/définitions/file)), so both are understood. *Une longue fin de semaine* is a weekend made longer by a holiday. This is how people talk about it, and a calendar tells you when it happens. In 2026, 24 June is a Wednesday and 1 July is a Wednesday ([Quebec government holiday list](https://www.quebec.ca/gouvernement/travailler-gouvernement/remuneration-avantages-sociaux/conditions-travail/jours-feries)), so look at your calendar for the year you travel.

## Hear it

Ayesha is 32 and lives in Toronto. Marc is 34 and lives in Montréal. They are friends and use *tu*. They plan a weekend around the Fête nationale. Listen for each pattern in the table above.

```fr
Ayesha: Salut Marc! Alors, tu es prêt pour la Fête nationale? | Hi Marc! So, are you ready for the Fête nationale?
Marc: Oui! Ça fait trois ans que j'habite à Montréal, et c'est la première fois que tu viens! | Yes! I've lived in Montréal for three years, and it's the first time you're coming!
Ayesha: C'est vrai. J'ai décidé de prendre le train. C'est moins cher que l'avion, et c'est plus facile. | It's true. I decided to take the train. It's cheaper than the plane, and it's easier.
Marc: Tu arrives vendredi? Il y a un train qui arrive à dix-huit heures. | Are you arriving Friday? There's a train that arrives at six p.m.
Ayesha: Parfait. J'ai hâte de voir la ville que tu aimes beaucoup. | Perfect. I can't wait to see the city that you like a lot.
Marc: Samedi, on fait un circuit à vélo dans le Vieux-Montréal. Il paraît qu'il va faire beau, vingt-cinq degrés. | On Saturday, we'll do a bike tour in Old Montréal. I hear it's going to be nice, twenty-five degrees.
Ayesha: Et le jour de la Fête nationale? | And on the day of the Fête nationale?
Marc: Il y a des fêtes dans les rues. Il y a toujours une grande file d'attente au métro, alors on marche. | There are parties in the streets. There's always a big line at the subway, so we walk.
Ayesha: Je peux dormir chez toi? | Can I sleep at your place?
Marc: Bien sûr. J'ai un 4 1/2, chauffé et éclairé. Le salon est petit, mais c'est le meilleur sofa de la ville! | Of course. I have a heated 4 1/2. The living room is small, but it's the best sofa in the city!
Ayesha: Super. J'ai oublié d'acheter un laissez-passer pour le métro. | Great. I forgot to buy a pass for the subway.
Marc: On peut acheter un laissez-passer là-bas. | We can buy a pass there.
Ayesha: Et le restaurant que j'adore, il est encore ouvert? | And the restaurant that I love, is it still open?
Marc: Oui, c'est le restaurant le plus connu du quartier. | Yes, it's the best-known restaurant in the neighbourhood.
Ayesha: On y va samedi soir! À vendredi! Prends soin de toi. | We'll go there Saturday night! See you Friday! Take care.
```

Now Ayesha writes a short note to her sister Zara. It is a model note of about 70 words, invented for the book.

```fr
Salut Zara, | Hi Zara,
Je pars vendredi pour Montréal. Marc habite là depuis trois ans. | I'm leaving Friday for Montréal. Marc has lived there for three years.
Je vais faire un circuit à vélo, visiter le Vieux-Montréal et manger au restaurant que j'adore. | I'm going to do a bike tour, visit Old Montréal and eat at the restaurant that I love.
Il paraît qu'il va faire vingt-cinq degrés. J'ai décidé de rester trois jours. | I hear it's going to be twenty-five degrees. I decided to stay three days.
Tu veux venir l'an prochain? | Do you want to come next year?
Bises, | Kisses,
Ayesha | Ayesha
```

## Try it

Work through the five tasks in order. Do tasks 1 to 4 with the answers closed, then check them. Task 5 is the main one: it has two parts, and the second part is a note that you write yourself.


1. Say three comparisons about the trip: the train and the plane, Montréal and Toronto, a café and another café.
2. Complete with *depuis*, *il y a*, *pendant* or *dans*. *Marc habite à Montréal ___ trois ans.* *Je pars ___ deux jours.* *J'ai habité à Toronto ___ cinq ans.* *Nous sommes arrivés ___ dix minutes.*
3. Join with *qui* or *que*. *C'est un restaurant. J'adore ce restaurant.* *C'est un train. Il arrive à six heures.*
4. Add *à* or *de* (or nothing). *J'ai décidé ___ prendre le train.* *J'ai oublié ___ acheter un billet.* *Je veux ___ visiter le Vieux-Montréal.* *Nous commençons ___ marcher.*
5. Role-play and note, in two parts.
    1. Speak. Play the dialogue and say Ayesha's lines out loud, with a recording of Marc's lines or a partner. Use the mic button to check yourself. Stop and replay any line you cannot say at normal speed.
    2. Write. Write a note of 60 to 80 words to a friend about a trip you plan, in *tu*. It must have: *depuis* or *il y a* once, one sentence with *que* and a noun (*le restaurant que j'adore*), one verb followed by *de* or *à*, one weather sentence, and a closing such as *Bises* or *À bientôt*. Count your words, then tick each item in the list against your note.

## Common mistakes

Using the passé composé with *depuis* for something that is still true: *Marc a habité là depuis trois ans*. Use the present: *Marc habite là depuis trois ans*.

Choosing *plus bon* or *le plus bon*: use *meilleur* and *le meilleur*.

Forgetting the small word after *décider* or *oublier*: *j'ai décidé prendre*. Say *j'ai décidé de prendre*.

## In Quebec

*Faire la file* is Quebec and Canadian usage, and *faire la queue* has the same sense. Both are understood. In 2026, check your calendar for the Fête nationale (24 June) and the Fête du Canada (1 July) before you plan the trip, because the weekend around them changes each year.
