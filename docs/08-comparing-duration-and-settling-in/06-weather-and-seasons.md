# Weather and seasons

After this lesson you can say what the weather is, give a temperature in degrees Celsius, name the four seasons and understand a short radio forecast for Montréal and Québec City, including Canadian winter words such as *la poudrerie* and *le verglas*.

Before this: [Aller and faire](../03-verbs-and-questions/05-aller-and-faire.md), [Partitive articles: du, de la, des](../06-habits-memories-and-food/01-partitive-articles.md), [Everyday nouns in Quebec](../05-the-past-what-happened/09-quebec-everyday-nouns.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

To ask about the weather, say *Quel temps fait-il?* (What is the weather like?) or, in speech, *Il fait quel temps?* The answer uses one of three patterns.

### Pattern 1: il fait + adjective

*Faire* describes the general weather, and *il* stands for nothing in particular, like "it" in English. The word after it is an adjective that doesn't change.

| French | English |
|---|---|
| *Il fait beau.* | The weather is nice. |
| *Il fait mauvais.* | The weather is bad. |
| *Il fait chaud.* | It's hot. |
| *Il fait froid.* | It's cold. |
| *Il fait frais.* | It's cool. |
| *Il fait doux.* | It's mild. |
| *Il fait humide.* | It's humid. |
| *Il fait sec.* | It's dry. |

Watch the difference from [Avoir and il y a](../02-sounds-spelling-and-everyday-things/07-avoir-and-il-y-a.md): *Il fait froid* describes the air, and *J'ai froid* says that you feel cold.

### Pattern 2: a verb for rain, snow and ice

Some weather has its own verb, always in the *il* form: *il pleut* (*pleuvoir*, to rain), *il neige* (*neiger*, to snow) and *il gèle* (*geler*, to freeze). Never add *faire*: say *il pleut*, not *il fait pleut*. In the past, *il a neigé* and *il a plu*.

### Pattern 3: il y a + noun

For things you can see or feel, use *il y a* with the partitive from [Partitive articles: du, de la, des](../06-habits-memories-and-food/01-partitive-articles.md): *il y a du soleil*, *il y a du vent*, *il y a des nuages*, *il y a du brouillard* (fog), *il y a de la glace*, *il y a un orage* (a thunderstorm), *il y a une tempête* (a storm). The same ideas have adjectives: *nuageux* (cloudy), *ensoleillé* (sunny), *pluvieux* (rainy), *venteux* (windy). They describe the sky, the day or the week: *Le ciel est nuageux. La journée est ensoleillée.*

The nouns *la pluie* (rain), *la neige* (snow), *un nuage* (cloud), *le vent* (wind) and *la glace* (ice) also work on their own: *La pluie arrive ce soir.* *La météo* is the forecast.

Three sounds: *brouillard* /bʁujaʁ/ ends with a silent *d*, *verglas* /vɛʁglɑ/ with a silent *s*, and *automne* /otɔn/ has a silent *m* in the middle.

### Temperatures and seasons

Canada uses degrees Celsius. For a positive temperature, say the number and *degrés*: *Il fait vingt degrés* (20 °C). For a negative one, say *moins* before the number: *Il fait moins quinze* (-15 °C), *il fait moins vingt*. *Zéro* is *zéro*: *Il fait zéro.* In writing, a space goes between the number and the symbol: 20 °C. *La température* is the temperature, and *un degré* is a degree.

The four seasons (*les saisons*, one is *une saison*) are *le printemps* (spring), *l'été* (summer), *l'automne* (autumn) and *l'hiver* (winter). The word for "in" depends on the season: *au printemps*, but *en été*, *en automne* and *en hiver*.

### What you wear

You know many winter clothes from [Everyday nouns in Quebec](../05-the-past-what-happened/09-quebec-everyday-nouns.md): *une tuque*, *des mitaines*, *un foulard*, *des bottes*, *un manteau*. Add *un gant* (a glove with separate fingers), *un imperméable* (a raincoat) and *un parapluie* (an umbrella, which you know).

## Hear it

First, an invented radio forecast, read slowly. Listen for the temperature after *il fait* and for *il y a*.

```fr
Voix: Voici la météo. À Montréal, le ciel est nuageux ce matin. Il y a du vent et il fait moins huit. | Here is the weather. In Montréal, the sky is cloudy this morning. There is wind and it's minus eight.
Voix: L'après-midi, il neige. Il y a de la glace sur les trottoirs. | In the afternoon, it snows. There is ice on the sidewalks.
Voix: À Québec, il y a de la poudrerie et il fait moins douze. Attention au verglas ce soir. | In Québec City, there is blowing snow and it's minus twelve. Watch out for the ice tonight.
Voix: Demain, il fait beau et froid, avec du soleil. | Tomorrow, it's nice and cold, with sun.
```

Now a phone call between two friends, with *tu*. Jules is in Québec City and Amina is in Montréal.

```fr
Amina: Allô, Jules! Il fait quel temps chez toi? | Hello, Jules! What's the weather like at your place?
Jules: Mauvais! Il a beaucoup neigé cette nuit. Maintenant, il y a de la poudrerie. | Bad! It snowed a lot last night. Now there is blowing snow.
Amina: Il fait froid? | Is it cold?
Jules: Oui, il fait moins vingt. Je ne vois rien. | Yes, it's minus twenty. I can't see anything.
Amina: Ici, il pleut et il y a du verglas. Les trottoirs, c'est de la glace! | Here it's raining and there is ice. The sidewalks are all ice!
Jules: Tu as ta tuque et tes mitaines? | Do you have your hat and your mittens?
Amina: Oui, et mon imperméable! J'ai froid quand même. | Yes, and my raincoat! I'm cold anyway.
```

## Try it

1. Say the weather in French. Rain. Snow. 30 degrees. Minus 20 degrees. Wind.
2. Complete. *Il ___ chaud.* *Il y a ___ soleil.* *J'___ froid.* *Il ___ beaucoup en hiver.* (Use *neiger*.)
3. Read each temperature aloud: -20 °C, 0 °C, 25 °C, -5 °C.
4. Fix the mistake in each line.

```fr
Il fait pleut. {x}
Je suis froid dehors. {x}
Il y a le soleil. {x}
```

5. Write two sentences about today's weather where you are and what coat or hat you have with you.

4. *Il pleut.* *J'ai froid dehors.* *Il y a du soleil.*
5. Example: *Il fait moins dix et il y a du vent. J'ai mon manteau et ma tuque.*

## Say it

```fr
Il fait quel temps aujourd'hui? | What's the weather like today?
Il fait moins quinze et il neige. | It's minus fifteen and it's snowing.
Il y a du vent et des nuages. | There is wind and there are clouds.
En hiver, il fait froid. | In winter, it's cold.
Au printemps, il pleut souvent. | In spring, it rains often.
```

## Common mistakes

Adding *faire* to the weather verbs: *il fait pleut*. Say *il pleut*, *il neige*.

Mixing *avoir* and *faire* for cold and hot: *je fais froid*. People have *chaud* or *froid* (*j'ai froid*), and the weather *fait* it (*il fait froid*).

Using the definite article after *il y a*: *il y a le soleil*. Say *il y a du soleil*.

## In Quebec

Quebec winters have words you will hear all the time. The OQLF defines *la poudrerie* as snow that is already on the ground and gets lifted and pushed by the wind, and labels the word as used in Québec and Canada ([OQLF: poudrerie](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/8363806/poudrerie)). When there is a lot of it, you can hardly see ahead. *Le verglas* is a layer of ice. Environment and Climate Change Canada's French glossary uses *verglas* for the clear ice that forms when freezing rain (*pluie verglaçante*) hits the ground and objects ([Environment Canada glossary](https://www.canada.ca/fr/environnement-changement-climatique/services/conditions-meteorologiques-ressources-outils-generaux/glossaire.html)). *Une bordée de neige* is a heavy snowfall in one go. Usito marks *bordée* as a Quebec and Canadian use ([Usito: bordée](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/définitions/bordée)). *Une tempête de neige* is a snowstorm.
