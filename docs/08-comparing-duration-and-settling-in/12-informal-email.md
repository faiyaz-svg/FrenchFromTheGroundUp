# An informal email

<span class="tag tag-write">Write</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can write a friendly email of 90 to 120 words to a friend, with a subject line, a greeting, news, an invitation, two questions and a closing.

Before this: [A message about yesterday](../05-the-past-what-happened/11-message-about-yesterday.md), [The futur proche](../04-people-places-and-directions/06-futur-proche.md), [A short booking email](../07-pronouns-and-getting-things-done/11-short-booking-email.md), [Depuis, il y a and pendant](03-depuis-il-y-a-pendant.md). You need: a keyboard or a pencil.

## The idea

An informal email (*un courriel*) to a friend has the same parts as any email, with a warmer tone. You use *tu*, short sentences and the words you would say to the person. Each part has a job.

| Part | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *l'objet* | the subject line, short | *Objet : Fin de semaine à Montréal?* |
| the greeting | opens, with the name | *Salut Sam,* |
| the opening | asks how the person is, says you missed them | *Comment vas-tu? Tu me manques!* |
| the news | what you did, in the past | *Cette semaine, j'ai beaucoup travaillé.* |
| the invitation | what you want to do | *Tu veux venir à Montréal?* |
| the questions | give the person something to answer | *Tu es libre vendredi?* |
| the closing | ends with warmth, then your name | *J'ai hâte de te voir! Bises, Nina* |

### Greeting and closing

The OQLF says that in an email the greeting can be *Bonjour* followed by the name, and it lists forms from the most formal to the most familiar, such as *Bonjour Lucie* and *Chère Céline* ([OQLF: formules d'appel dans le courriel](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/24944/la-redaction-et-la-communication/redaction-administrative-et-commerciale/correspondance/courriel/formules-dappel-dans-le-courriel)). For a friend, *Salut* with the name is natural: *Salut Sam,* The comma after the name is the usual choice. Keep the closing in the same tone as the greeting: warm for a friend, polite for a boss. Common friendly closings are *À bientôt* (see you soon), *Bises* (kisses, which you know from [Texting to make plans](../03-verbs-and-questions/09-texting-to-make-plans.md)), *Prends soin de toi* (take care) and *Bonne continuation* (all the best for what you are doing). *Bonne continuation* is a wish for the future and is more neutral than *bises*.

### Useful phrases

*Avoir hâte de* + infinitive means "to look forward to, to be eager to": *J'ai hâte de te voir.* It uses *avoir*, so there is no *être*. *Hâte* is a noun here, and the verb is *avoir*, like *avoir faim*.

*Manquer* works the other way around from English. *Tu me manques* literally says "you are missing to me" and means "I miss you". The one who is missed is the subject: *Sam me manque.* *Il me manque.* *Vous nous manquez.*

*Garder le contact* means "to keep in touch": *On garde le contact, d'accord?* *Prendre soin de* means "to take care of": *Prends soin de toi.* *Prenez soin de vous* is the *vous* form. *Des nouvelles* means "news about someone": *Je n'ai pas de tes nouvelles.*

### Which tenses

Use the passé composé for what you did and the imparfait for the background, as in [Imparfait or passé composé](../06-habits-memories-and-food/06-imparfait-vs-passe-compose.md): *Mardi, il faisait froid, alors j'ai pris le métro.* Use the futur proche for plans: *Je vais organiser un souper.* Use the present for invitations: *Tu veux venir?*

### How to write it

1. Write the subject line last, in four to six words.
2. Greet with *Salut* and the name, then ask how the person is.
3. Write two or three past sentences about your week.
4. Add one plan with the futur proche, and the invitation.
5. Add two questions.
6. Close with a friendly phrase and your name on its own line.
7. Read it aloud once, and check each verb ending.

## Hear it

Nina writes to her friend Sam in Toronto. This is a model email, invented for the book. Listen for the tenses: past for the week, futur proche for the plan and present for the invitation.

```fr
Objet : Fin de semaine à Montréal? | Subject: Weekend in Montréal?
Salut Sam, | Hi Sam,
Comment vas-tu? Ça fait un mois que je n'ai pas de tes nouvelles. Tu me manques! | How are you? It's been a month since I had news from you. I miss you!
Cette semaine, j'ai beaucoup travaillé. Lundi, j'ai fini un gros projet, et mardi, il faisait très froid, alors j'ai pris le métro. | This week, I worked a lot. On Monday, I finished a big project, and on Tuesday, it was very cold, so I took the subway.
Hier, j'ai vu Julie. Elle m'a dit qu'elle va déménager en juillet. | Yesterday, I saw Julie. She told me she is going to move in July.
Je vais organiser un souper samedi soir. Tu veux venir à Montréal pour la fin de semaine de la Fête du Travail? | I'm going to organize a dinner on Saturday night. Do you want to come to Montréal for the Labour Day weekend?
Tu peux prendre le train vendredi, non? Il paraît qu'il va faire beau. | You can take the train on Friday, right? I hear it's going to be nice.
J'ai hâte de te voir! Prends soin de toi. | I can't wait to see you! Take care.
Bises, | Kisses,
Nina | Nina
```

## Try it

1. Put the parts of an email in order: closing, news, greeting, invitation, subject line, questions.
2. Choose a greeting and closing for each. A friend: *Salut Sam,* or *Madame,*? A boss you do not know well: *À bientôt!* or *Cordialement,*? (*Cordialement* means "kind regards".)
3. Say in French: "I miss you." "I can't wait to see you." "Take care of yourself."
4. Fix the mistake in each line.

```fr
Je suis hâte de te voir. {x}
Je manque Sam. {x}
Prend soin de toi. {x}
```

5. Writing task. Write an email of 90 to 120 words to a friend. Use *tu*. Include the subject line, a greeting, two verbs in the passé composé, one verb in the imparfait, one futur proche, one invitation, two questions and a closing. Count your words.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. Subject line, greeting, news, invitation, questions, closing. (The subject line comes first on the page even though you write it last.)
2. *Salut Sam,* for a friend. *Cordialement,* for a boss. *À bientôt!* fits a friend.
3. *Tu me manques.* *J'ai hâte de te voir.* *Prends soin de toi.*
4. *J'ai hâte de te voir.* (*Avoir hâte*, not *être*.) *Sam me manque.* (The person you miss is the subject.) *Prends soin de toi.* (The *tu* imperative of *prendre* keeps its *s*.)
5. Check the model against your email: tenses, two questions and the closing. Read it aloud once.

## Say it

```fr
Salut Sam, comment vas-tu? | Hi Sam, how are you?
Tu me manques! | I miss you!
J'ai hâte de te voir. | I can't wait to see you.
Tu veux venir à Montréal? | Do you want to come to Montréal?
Prends soin de toi. À bientôt! | Take care. See you soon!
```

## Common mistakes

Saying *Je suis hâte*. Use *j'ai hâte*, with *avoir*.

Saying *Je manque Sam* for "I miss Sam". Say *Sam me manque*.

Mixing *tu* and *vous* in the same email. Choose *tu* for a friend and keep it to the end.

## In Quebec

The OQLF recommends *courriel*, as in [Quebec words: dépanneur and café](../02-sounds-spelling-and-everyday-things/09-quebec-dep-and-cafe-words.md). In an informal email, friends write *Salut* and end with *À bientôt* or *Bises*. Keep casual spellings such as *chu* for speech and texts, as you learned in [Casual questions in Quebec](../03-verbs-and-questions/10-quebec-casual-questions.md), and write full forms in an email.
