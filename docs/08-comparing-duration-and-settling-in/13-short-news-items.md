# Short news items

<span class="tag tag-read">Read</span> <span class="tag tag-a2">A2</span>

After this lesson you can read a short local news item in French and say who did what, where and when, using words such as *selon*, *a annoncé* and *plusieurs*.

Before this: [Weather and seasons](06-weather-and-seasons.md), [Past time words](../05-the-past-what-happened/07-past-time-words.md), [Notices and instructions](../07-pronouns-and-getting-things-done/12-notices-and-instructions.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

A news item (*une nouvelle*, in the plural *des nouvelles*) is short, factual and written in a fixed order. If you know the order, you can find the key facts even when some words are new. The four items below are written for this book and are invented: the events, numbers and people are not real, and no real person is named. They imitate the style of local news in Montréal, Gatineau, Québec City and Ottawa. For real news, use the stage media list.

### Four questions

Read each item with four questions, which you know from [Asking questions](../03-verbs-and-questions/04-asking-questions.md): *Qui?* (who), *Quoi?* (what), *Où?* (where) and *Quand?* (when). The first sentence usually answers most of them. The rest gives details and who said it.

### Steps for reading

1. Read the headline (*le titre*) and say what the item is about in one word: storm, strike, festival.
2. Read the first sentence and find the four answers. The verb is often in the passé composé, because the news reports what happened.
3. Find *selon* ("according to"): it tells you the source.
4. Read the details, and skip the words that you do not need. Do not look up every word.
5. Say the item back in two short sentences.

### Words of the news

*Un journaliste* is a reporter. *Annoncer* is "to announce": *Le maire a annoncé* (the mayor announced). *Un maire* leads *une municipalité* (a municipality, the local government of a town), and *un citoyen* is a resident or citizen. *Selon* means "according to": *Selon la municipalité, la rue est fermée.* *Plusieurs* means "several". *Une perturbation* is a disruption, and *des travaux* are roadworks or construction. *Un accident* is an accident, *un blessé* is an injured person and *une victime* is a victim. *Un festival* is a festival, and *une grève* is a strike.

You also see *à cause de* ("because of"): *un retard à cause des travaux*. *Demander à quelqu'un de* + infinitive means "to ask someone to do something", a pattern like those in [Verbs followed by à and de](05-verbs-with-a-and-de.md).

## Hear it

Read each item aloud, then check the English. In each one, find the four answers.

Item 1, Québec City.

```fr
Tempête de neige à Québec | Snowstorm in Québec City
Une tempête de neige est arrivée à Québec hier soir. Il a beaucoup neigé. | A snowstorm arrived in Québec City last night. It snowed a lot.
Plusieurs écoles ont fermé ce matin. | Several schools closed this morning.
La municipalité demande aux citoyens de rester à la maison. | The municipality asks residents to stay home.
Sur l'autoroute, un accident a fait un blessé. | On the highway, an accident left one person injured.
```

Item 2, Montréal.

```fr
Perturbation dans le métro | Disruption in the subway
Il y a une perturbation sur la ligne verte ce matin. | There is a disruption on the green line this morning.
Il y a un retard de vingt minutes à cause des travaux. | There is a delay of twenty minutes because of roadworks.
Selon la municipalité, le service revient à la normale à dix heures. | According to the municipality, service returns to normal at ten o'clock.
```

Item 3, Gatineau.

```fr
Un festival de musique à Gatineau | A music festival in Gatineau
Un festival de musique commence vendredi. Plusieurs concerts sont gratuits. | A music festival starts on Friday. Several concerts are free.
Le maire a annoncé que le festival commence à six heures. | The mayor announced that the festival starts at six o'clock.
Selon la municipalité, il y a des travaux près du parc, alors les citoyens peuvent prendre l'autobus. | According to the municipality, there are roadworks near the park, so residents can take the bus.
```

Item 4, Ottawa.

```fr
Grève des chauffeurs d'autobus à Ottawa | Bus drivers' strike in Ottawa
Les chauffeurs d'autobus ont arrêté de travailler lundi matin. | The bus drivers stopped working on Monday morning.
La grève dure depuis deux jours. | The strike has lasted for two days.
Selon les chauffeurs, ils vont continuer à parler avec leur patron cette semaine. | According to the drivers, they are going to keep talking with their boss this week.
Plusieurs citoyens ont pris le vélo. | Several residents took their bikes.
```

## Try it

1. Item 1: write the four answers (who, what, where, when).
2. True or false? Item 2: the delay is twenty minutes. Item 3: all the concerts are free. Item 4: the strike started on Friday.
3. Match each word to its meaning: *une grève*, *un blessé*, *une perturbation*, *un maire*. The meanings are: injured person, strike, disruption, mayor.
4. Find *selon* in each of the four items and write who the source is.
5. Write a three-sentence news item of your own, invented, about your street. Use *selon* and *plusieurs* once each, and put the headline first.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. A snowstorm (what) arrived in Québec City (where) last night (when). The municipality (who) asks residents to stay home.
2. True. False (several concerts are free, not all). False (the drivers stopped on Monday morning, and the strike has lasted two days).
3. *Une grève* is a strike, *un blessé* is an injured person, *une perturbation* is a disruption, *un maire* is a mayor.
4. Item 1 has no *selon*. Item 2: the municipality. Item 3: the municipality. Item 4: the drivers.
5. Example: *Travaux dans ma rue.* *Des travaux ont commencé lundi.* *Selon la municipalité, plusieurs rues sont fermées.*

</details>

## Common mistakes

Reading every word. Find the four answers first and skip the details you do not need.

Treating the news as true because it sounds real. The items in this book are invented, and the stage media list is the place for real news.

Writing *selon moi* when you mean the source of a fact. *Selon la municipalité* names the source. *Selon moi* means "in my opinion".

## In Quebec

Local news in Quebec uses words you will meet on transit notices and signs too, such as *des travaux* and *une perturbation*. For real news, use the media page and the links in the stage reading list. *ICI OHdio* has short clips.
