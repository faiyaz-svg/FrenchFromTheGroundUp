After this lesson you can talk about a hockey game with the usual Quebec words, and name the main holidays of the Quebec year with their dates.

## The idea

This lesson has two parts: the words of hockey, and the calendar of holidays. Both come up in small talk, so you need to follow them.

### Hockey words

| French | English |
|---|---|
| *un joueur / une joueuse* | player |
| *une équipe* | team |
| *un entraîneur* | coach |
| *un partisan* | fan |
| *une patinoire* | rink |
| *un but* | goal |
| *un gardien de but* | goalie |
| *une rondelle* | puck |
| *un bâton* | stick |
| *un chandail* | jersey |
| *une victoire* | win |
| *une défaite* | loss |
| *le pointage* | score |

A few of these are worth a note, with their sources.

- *Un gardien de but* is the preferred term. The OQLF says it is well established in Quebec usage, and that the English borrowing *goaler* (and its French-looking forms *goaleur* and *goaleuse*) is to be avoided in French ([OQLF: gardien de but](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/8870060/gardien-de-but)). People often say *le gardien* for short.
- *Une rondelle* is the puck ([OQLF: rondelle](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/8356162/rondelle)).
- *Un chandail* has a Quebec and Canadian sense: the shirt worn by players or their fans in a team's colours. Usito marks it Q/C ([Usito: chandail](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/définitions/chandail)). *Chandail* also means a sweater.
- *Le pointage* is the number of goals or points scored in a game. Usito marks that sense Q/C and gives *la marque* and *le score* as synonyms ([Usito: pointage](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/définitions/pointage)).
- To score is *marquer un but*. The OQLF calls *scorer un but* a hybrid borrowing to avoid, and *marquer un but* is the preferred form ([OQLF: marquer un but](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/redirection/ficheuid/8350579)).

With these you can say *L'équipe a gagné*, *Le gardien a arrêté la rondelle*, *Le pointage est de trois à deux*, or ask *Qui a marqué?* ("Who scored?"). A *partisan* supports a team, and *un match* is a game.

### The holiday calendar

*Un jour férié* is a public holiday. The Quebec government lists the holidays of its own employees on its [page on holidays](https://www.quebec.ca/gouvernement/travailler-gouvernement/remuneration-avantages-sociaux/conditions-travail/jours-feries). Other workplaces have their own rules, so ask at work. That list has 13 days, including Good Friday, Easter Monday, 2 January and some days around Christmas and the new year. The table shows the days named in this lesson, with the dates for 2026 (as of 2026).

| Holiday | Rule | In 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| *le jour de l'An* | 1 January | Thursday 1 January |
| *la Journée nationale des patriotes* | the Monday before 25 May | Monday 18 May |
| *la Fête nationale du Québec* | 24 June | Wednesday 24 June |
| *la Fête du Canada* | 1 July | Wednesday 1 July |
| *la Fête du Travail* | first Monday of September | Monday 7 September |
| *l'Action de grâce* | second Monday of October | Monday 12 October |
| *Noël* | 25 December | Friday 25 December |

The Canada page says that the *Loi constitutionnelle de 1867* (the British North America Act, as it was first called) created Canada on 1 July 1867 ([Canada Day history](https://www.canada.ca/fr/patrimoine-canadien/services/fete-canada-histoire.html)). *La Fête nationale* on 24 June is the national holiday of Québec. Say these names with their dates in French: *Le vingt-quatre juin, c'est la Fête nationale.* *Le premier juillet, c'est la Fête du Canada.* Many people also move house on 1 July, which [the next housing lesson](09-housing-words.md) explains.

*Pâques* (Easter) falls in spring, and the date changes each year. *Le réveillon* is a festive meal on the night of 24 December (*le réveillon de Noël*) or 31 December (*le réveillon du jour de l'An*). *Les Fêtes* is the whole period around Noël and the new year: *Bonnes Fêtes!* is a greeting in December.

One more date marks a season, not a holiday. The OQLF says that *le temps des sucres*, the sugar-maple season, lasts about four to five weeks in Quebec, from mid-March to the end of April ([OQLF: temps des sucres](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/redirection/ficheuid/8358302)). That is when people visit *une cabane à sucre*, as in [Table culture in Quebec](../06-habits-memories-and-food/09-quebec-table-culture.md).

Three words for a street party: *un défilé* (a parade), *un feu d'artifice* (fireworks) and *un drapeau* (a flag).

## Hear it

Étienne is 19. He watches a hockey game at home with his grandmother Odette, and they use *tu*. Listen for *un but*, *le pointage* and *gagner*. Later, Étienne tells a friend about 24 June, with *tu*.

```fr
Étienne: Mamie, regarde! Le joueur a marqué un but! | Gran, look! The player scored a goal!
Odette: Ah oui? Quel est le pointage maintenant? | Oh yes? What's the score now?
Étienne: Deux à un. Le gardien est très fort ce soir. | Two to one. The goalie is very strong tonight.
Odette: Et ton chandail, tu l'as acheté où? | And your jersey, where did you buy it?
Étienne: Au magasin près de chez moi. Je suis un partisan depuis dix ans. | At the shop near my place. I've been a fan for ten years.
Odette: Moi, j'aime la patinoire de mon enfance, c'était mieux! | Me, I like the rink from my childhood, it was better!
Étienne: Mamie, c'est la victoire! L'équipe a gagné! | Gran, it's the win! The team won!
```

```fr
Étienne: Tu fais quoi le vingt-quatre juin, pour la Fête nationale? | What are you doing on June 24th for the Fête nationale?
Amir: Je vais au défilé avec ma famille. Il y a aussi un feu d'artifice le soir? | I'm going to the parade with my family. Will there be fireworks in the evening?
Étienne: Il paraît que oui. Mon drapeau est déjà prêt! | I hear so. My flag is already ready!
Amir: Et le premier juillet, c'est la Fête du Canada. Quelle semaine! | And July 1st is Canada Day. What a week!
```

## Try it

1. Say the date of each holiday in French: *le jour de l'An*, *la Fête nationale*, *la Fête du Canada*, *Noël*.
2. Give the English for *un but*, *une défaite*, *une patinoire* and *un entraîneur*.
3. Complete. *Le joueur a ___ un but.* *L'équipe a ___ le match.* *Le ___ est de trois à deux.*
4. Write the date of the Fête du Travail and of the Action de grâce this year (use a calendar).
5. Say two things that *chandail* can mean.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *le premier janvier*, *le vingt-quatre juin*, *le premier juillet*, *le vingt-cinq décembre*.
2. goal, loss, rink, coach.
3. *marqué*, *gagné*, *pointage*.
4. The first Monday of September and the second Monday of October. In 2026: *le lundi 7 septembre* and *le lundi 12 octobre*.
5. A team shirt (the Quebec and Canadian sense, marked Q/C in Usito) and a sweater.

## Common mistakes

Saying *goaler* or *scorer un but* in careful French. These are casual anglicisms that the OQLF says to avoid. Use *gardien de but* and *marquer un but*.

Thinking *chandail* means "sweater" only. In a hockey conversation it is the jersey.

Mixing the two holidays: 24 June is the Fête nationale du Québec, and 1 July is the Fête du Canada.
