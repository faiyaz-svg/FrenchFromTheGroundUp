# Gender and articles

<span class="tag tag-grammar">Grammar</span> <span class="tag tag-a1">A1</span>

After this lesson you can say whether a noun is masculine or feminine, choose between *le*, *la*, *l'*, *un* and *une*, and use the endings *-tion*, *-ment*, *-age* and *-eur* as hints.

Before this: [Spelling rules and liaison](03-spelling-sound-rules-and-liaison.md), [Être and introducing yourself](../01-first-sounds-and-greetings/10-etre-and-introductions.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

A **noun** is a word for a person, a place or a thing: *café*, *table*, *ami*. In French every noun has a **gender**, which is either **masculine** or **feminine**. English does not do this. The gender has nothing to do with the thing itself: a table is not female. It is a label that the noun carries, and the words around it must match it.

For people, the gender usually follows the person: *un homme* is a man and *une femme* is a woman, *un garçon* is a boy and *une fille* is a girl. For things, the gender is a fact to learn with the noun. This is why this book always shows a noun with its article.

### The articles

An **article** is a short word that comes before a noun. French has two kinds. A **definite article** points to a particular thing, like English "the". An **indefinite article** introduces any one thing, like English "a" or "an".

| | Masculine | Feminine | Before a vowel or h muet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Definite ("the") | *le* /lə/ | *la* /la/ | *l'* (both genders) |
| Indefinite ("a") | *un* /œ̃/ | *une* /yn/ | *un* or *une* |

The indefinite article shows the gender in the sound: *un* has a nasal vowel and *une* ends in /n/. Use that to hear which one you have. Before a vowel, *le* and *la* both turn into *l'* (elision from the last lesson), so the gender hides: *l'hôtel* is masculine and *l'idée* is feminine. When you learn a noun that starts with a vowel, learn its *un* or *une* as well, or the label (m.) or (f.), as the New words tables do.

The plural articles *les* and *des* come in the next lesson.

### Definite or indefinite?

Use *un* or *une* when you introduce a thing for the first time, or when any one of them will do. Use *le*, *la* or *l'* when both people already know which one, or when there is only one. In "Voilà un livre" you are showing a book. In "Le livre est sur la table" you mean the book you both know about.

```fr
Camille: Voilà un lit pour la chambre. | Here is a bed for the bedroom.
Cliente: Merci. Et le sac, c'est pour la cuisine? | Thank you. And the bag, is it for the kitchen?
Camille: Oui, c'est un sac pour la cuisine. | Yes, it's a bag for the kitchen.
```

### Endings that help

Some endings give a good hint. They are "usually" rules, not laws, so each has exceptions.

| Ending | Gender | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| *-tion* | feminine | *une question* /kɛstjɔ̃/, *une conversation* |
| *-ment* | masculine | *un moment* /mɔmɑ̃/, *un appartement* |
| *-age* | masculine | *un voyage*, *un garage* |
| *-eur* | masculine for most people and machines | *un ordinateur*, *un docteur* |
| *-e* (most nouns) | often feminine | *une table*, *une chaise*, *une maison* |
| a consonant or other vowel | often masculine | *un livre*, *un stylo*, *un sac* |

Watch out for the common exceptions. *La peur*, *la fleur* and *l'heure* (f.) end in *-eur* and are feminine. *Un problème*, *un système* and *un programme* end in *-e* and are masculine. Look for these in the New words table and learn them as set pairs.

Some nouns for people stay the same for a man and a woman, and only the article changes: *un ami* and *une amie* sound alike apart from *un* /œ̃n/ and *une* /yn/. Other pairs use two different words, such as *un garçon* and *une fille*.

### Say it with the article

The best habit is to never learn a bare noun. Learn *une maison*, not *maison*. When you meet a new noun, say the article with it three times. When you look a word up, check the gender in the dictionary: it is marked *n.m.* (masculine) or *n.f.* (feminine). Quebec and France mostly agree on gender, and [Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/), the free Quebec dictionary, marks it for each entry.

## Hear it

Listen for *un* with a nasal vowel and *une* ending in /n/, and for *l'* in front of a vowel.

```fr
Un moment, une idée. | A moment, an idea.
Un programme, un système, un problème. | A program, a system, a problem.
Une maison, une ville, un pays. | A house, a city, a country.
Une chambre, une cuisine, un magasin. | A bedroom, a kitchen, a shop.
Un lit, un vélo, un exemple. | A bed, a bike, an example.
L'hôtel, l'idée, la maison. | The hotel, the idea, the house.
```

## Try it

1. Write the definite article: ___ maison, ___ pays, ___ hôtel, ___ idée.
2. Write *un* or *une*: ___ moment, ___ question, ___ cuisine, ___ système.
3. Use the endings to guess the gender, then check the New words table: *garage*, *conversation*, *appartement*, *docteur* (doctor).
4. Why do *l'hôtel* and *l'idée* look the same in the article?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *La maison*, *le pays*, *l'hôtel* (m.), *l'idée* (f.).
2. *Un moment*, *une question*, *une cuisine*, *un système*.
3. *Un garage* (*-age*), *une conversation* (*-tion*), *un appartement* (*-ment*), *un docteur* (*-eur*).
4. Both start with a vowel, so *le* and *la* become *l'*. The gender is hidden, so you need to learn it with the noun.

</details>

## Common mistakes

Learning a noun without its article, then guessing later. Learn *une table* as one unit.

Treating the endings as laws. *La peur* is feminine even though it ends in *-eur*, and *un problème* is masculine even though it ends in *-e*.

Thinking that the gender of a thing tells you anything about it. A table is feminine in French and a desk (*un bureau*) is masculine, and this is only a label.

## Key terms

- **Noun**: a word for a person, a place or a thing.
- **Gender**: the label masculine or feminine that every French noun carries.
- **Article**: a short word before a noun that shows its gender and whether it is a particular thing or any one.
- **Definite article**: *le*, *la*, *l'* (and *les*): points to a particular thing, like "the".
- **Indefinite article**: *un*, *une* (and *des*): introduces any one thing, like "a".
