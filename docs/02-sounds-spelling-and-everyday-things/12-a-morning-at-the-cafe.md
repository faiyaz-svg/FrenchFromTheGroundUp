# A morning at the café

<span class="tag tag-read">Read</span> <span class="tag tag-a1">A1</span> <span class="tag tag-review">Review</span>

After this lesson you can read a 100-word description of a café and its menu board, find the numbers, prices and people in it, and describe a place with *il y a*, *être* and *avoir*.

Before this: every lesson in this stage, especially [Avoir and il y a](07-avoir-and-il-y-a.md), [Ordering at a café](08-ordering-at-a-cafe.md) and [Hearing numbers and prices](10-hearing-numbers-and-prices.md). You need: a pencil.

## The idea

This is the review lesson of the stage. It has no new grammar. You read one text and one menu, then you answer questions that bring back the sounds, the articles, the plurals, the numbers, *avoir*, *il y a* and the café phrases. The text only describes a place: it says what there is and who is there. Verbs for actions, such as "to serve" or "to drink", come in stage 3, so this text has none.

### How to read it

1. Play the text once with the translation hidden. Listen only for numbers and names.
2. Read it once without the sound. Underline every *il y a*, every form of *être* and every form of *avoir*.
3. Play it again, line by line, and say each line out loud with the right liaison.
4. Open the translation only for the lines you could not follow.

If a noun is new, look at its article. The article gives you the gender, and *les* or *des* tells you it is plural.

## Hear it

The café is our own invention: Chez Mireille in the city of Sherbrooke, in Quebec. It is not a real place. Read, then listen.

```fr
Le café Chez Mireille est dans une petite rue de Sherbrooke. | The café Chez Mireille is in a small street in Sherbrooke.
Il y a douze tables et un grand comptoir. | There are twelve tables and a big counter.
Il y a aussi une terrasse avec six tables. | There is also a terrace with six tables.
Mireille est la patronne. | Mireille is the boss.
Elle a trois employés : Karim, un garçon de vingt ans, Julie, une fille de dix-huit ans, et Marc. | She has three employees: Karim, a twenty-year-old, Julie, an eighteen-year-old, and Marc.
Karim est derrière le comptoir. | Karim is behind the counter.
Julie est devant la porte, avec un menu. | Julie is in front of the door, with a menu.
Marc a soixante-dix ans : il est toujours ici! | Marc is seventy years old: he is always here!
Il y a beaucoup de clients. | There are a lot of customers.
Il y a des amis, des gens et des enfants. | There are friends, people and children.
Les clients ont faim et soif. | The customers are hungry and thirsty.
Le chocolat chaud est très bon. | The hot chocolate is very good.
```

The menu board hangs behind the counter. Prices are in dollars, with a comma for the cents and the sign after the number.

| Boissons | Prix |
|---|---|
| *un café* | 3,00 $ |
| *un thé* | 3,00 $ |
| *un chocolat chaud* | 4,25 $ |
| *un jus* | 3,50 $ |
| *une bière* | 6,00 $ |

| Nourriture | Prix |
|---|---|
| *un croissant* | 2,75 $ |
| *un gâteau* | 4,00 $ |
| *une soupe* | 5,50 $ |
| *un sandwich* | 7,50 $ |
| *une salade* | 8,00 $ |

A short exchange at the counter uses the board. It is *vous*, because Karim does not know the customer.

```fr
Karim: Bonjour! Qu'est-ce que je vous sers? | Hello! What can I get you?
Vous: Je voudrais une soupe et un thé, s'il vous plaît. | I would like a soup and a tea, please.
Karim: Pour ici ou pour emporter? | For here or to take away?
Vous: Sur place. C'est combien? | For here. How much is it?
Karim: Ça fait huit dollars cinquante. | That comes to eight dollars fifty.
Vous: Voilà. Merci! | Here you are. Thank you!
```

## Try it

1. How many tables are there in all, inside and on the terrace?
2. Who is the boss? Who is seventy years old?
3. Karim says the price for a soup and a salad. Write it in words.
4. Which words in the text are plural? Look for *les* and *des* and write the singular of each noun: *les clients*, *des amis*, *des enfants*.
5. Write three sentences about a café, a shop or a room you know, with *il y a*, *être* and *avoir*.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. Eighteen: twelve inside and six on the terrace.
2. Mireille is the boss (*la patronne*). Marc is seventy (*il a soixante-dix ans*).
3. Soup 5,50 $ and salad 8,00 $ make 13,50 $: *treize dollars cinquante*.
4. *Les clients* (*le client*), *des amis* (*un ami*), *des enfants* (*un enfant*).
5. Example: *Il y a six tables. Le café est petit. J'ai faim.* Check that each noun has an article.

</details>

### A quick review quiz

These questions bring back each lesson. Write the answers before you open the box.

1. Say the sound of *c* in *merci* and in *café*.
2. Do you pronounce the *s* of *les amis*?
3. Is *un problème* masculine or feminine?
4. Write the plural of *un journal* and of *un cadeau*.
5. Say 71 and 91 in words.
6. Complete: *Tu ___ vingt ans.* *Nous ___ faim.*
7. What do you say to answer *Pour ici ou pour emporter?*
8. In Quebec, what is *le souper*, and what is the standard word for a soft drink on a menu?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. /s/ in *merci* and /k/ in *café*.
2. Yes, as /z/: *les amis* /lez ami/.
3. Masculine, despite the *-e*.
4. *Des journaux* and *des cadeaux*.
5. *Soixante et onze* and *quatre-vingt-onze*.
6. *Tu as vingt ans.* *Nous avons faim.*
7. *Sur place* or *pour emporter*.
8. The evening meal. The standard words for a soft drink are *une boisson gazeuse* (the OQLF term for a soft drink) or *une boisson*; *liqueur* is the common Quebec word.

</details>

## Common mistakes

Dropping the article in a list. Write *Il y a des tables et des chaises*, not *Il y a tables et chaises*.

Skipping *il y a* and writing *c'est douze tables*. Use *il y a* to say what a place has.

Writing a menu price as $3.00. Write 3,00 $.

## In Quebec

A menu in Quebec may say *boisson gazeuse* or *liqueur* for a soft drink, and *breuvage* for a drink. You met the three in the Quebec words lesson. In a café in Sherbrooke you may also hear *déjeuner* for breakfast and *dîner* for lunch.
