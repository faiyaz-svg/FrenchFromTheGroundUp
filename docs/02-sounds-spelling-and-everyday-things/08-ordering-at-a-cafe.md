# Ordering at a café

<span class="tag tag-speak">Speak</span> <span class="tag tag-a1">A1</span>

After this lesson you can order a drink and something to eat at a café counter, say whether it is for here or to take away, ask the price, and understand the answer.

Before this: [Numbers from 0 to 100](06-numbers-0-100.md), [Gender and articles](04-gender-and-articles.md), [Plurals, des and les](05-plurals-and-des-les.md), [Greetings and politeness](../01-first-sounds-and-greetings/08-greetings-and-politeness.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

At a café counter you only need a few set phrases, and most of the words are nouns you learn with their articles. A **chunk** is a group of words you learn and say as one piece, without taking it apart. This lesson is built on chunks. You will not need the grammar behind them yet, and each one is explained later in the book.

### The phrases you say

| French | Meaning | When |
|---|---|---|
| *Bonjour.* | hello | always first |
| *Je voudrais un café, s'il vous plaît.* | I would like a coffee, please | to order |
| *Sur place.* / *Pour emporter.* | for here / to take away | the answer to "for here or to go?" |
| *C'est tout.* | that's all | when you are done |
| *C'est combien?* | how much is it? | to ask the price |
| *Merci. Bonne journée.* | thank you, have a good day | to finish |

*Je voudrais* /ʒə vudʁɛ/ means "I would like". It is the polite way to ask for something, and you use it with *s'il vous plaît* (please). *Je veux*, "I want", is too blunt for a counter. You can learn *je voudrais* as one chunk. It is a form of the verb *vouloir*, explained in The conditionnel for politeness<!-- link: ../09-work-and-the-future/03-conditionnel-politeness.md -->, so for now do not try to change it.

### The phrases you hear

The worker will say these. You do not say them, you answer them.

| French | Meaning | Your answer |
|---|---|---|
| *Qu'est-ce que je vous sers?* | what can I get you? (literally, what do I serve you) | *Je voudrais* and the item |
| *Pour ici ou pour emporter?* | for here or to take away? | *Sur place* or *pour emporter* |
| *Et avec ça?* | anything else? (literally, and with that) | *C'est tout* or one more item |
| *Ça fait six dollars cinquante.* | that comes to $6.50 | *Voilà* and pay |
| *Vous désirez?* | what would you like? | *Je voudrais* and the item |

The first four are chunks, and *Vous désirez?* is another way of asking the first question. You learn to recognise it and answer it, but you do not need to say it.

### Counter words

You need nouns for what you order. Learn each with its article, as in lesson 4.

For drinks: *un café*, *un thé*, *un chocolat chaud*, *un lait*, *un jus*, *une bière*. For food: *un croissant*, *un sandwich*, *un gâteau*, *une soupe*, *une salade*. For things you hold: *un verre* (a glass), *une tasse* (a cup), *une bouteille* (a bottle). You also say *un verre d'eau* (a glass of water) and *une bouteille d'eau* as chunks, because the *de* + noun pattern has its own lesson. At the end, *l'addition* is the bill and *un dollar* is a dollar.

### Saying prices

Say the dollars, the word *dollars*, then the cents as a plain number. The amount 4,50 $ is *quatre dollars cinquante*. For 1,25 $ say *un dollar vingt-cinq*, and notice that *dollar* is singular for one. For 12 $ you can say *douze dollars*. Use the numbers from lesson 6.

In writing, French puts the amount first, a comma before the cents and a space before the dollar sign: 4,50 $. This is the French way to write it, and the next lessons show it again.

### Putting it together

```fr
Julien: Bonjour! Qu'est-ce que je vous sers? | Hello! What can I get you?
Vous: Bonjour. Je voudrais un chocolat chaud et un croissant, s'il vous plaît. | Hello. I would like a hot chocolate and a croissant, please.
Julien: Pour ici ou pour emporter? | For here or to take away?
Vous: Sur place, merci. | For here, thank you.
Julien: Et avec ça? | Anything else?
Vous: C'est tout. C'est combien? | That's all. How much is it?
Julien: Ça fait six dollars cinquante. | That comes to six dollars fifty.
Vous: Voilà. Merci! | Here you are. Thank you!
Julien: Merci à vous, bonne journée! | Thank you, have a good day!
```

The scene is a café in Vieux-Québec. Julien is a 30-year-old barista, and you are a visitor, so the dialogue uses *vous*. In Montreal the worker may start with "Bonjour, hi", and [Bonjour-hi and other Montreal greetings](../01-first-sounds-and-greetings/09-bonjour-hi-montreal-greetings.md) tells you how to answer.

## Hear it

Listen for the lift on *Et avec ça?* and for the price at the end of each exchange.

```fr
Je voudrais un café, s'il vous plaît. | I would like a coffee, please.
Je voudrais un thé et un gâteau. | I would like a tea and a cake.
Je voudrais une soupe et une salade. | I would like a soup and a salad.
Je voudrais un jus et un sandwich. | I would like a juice and a sandwich.
Je voudrais un verre d'eau. | I would like a glass of water.
Ça fait quatre dollars cinquante. | That comes to four dollars fifty.
Ça fait douze dollars vingt-cinq. | That comes to twelve dollars twenty-five.
Ça fait un dollar quatre-vingt-quinze. | That comes to one dollar ninety-five.
```

## Try it

1. Order a hot chocolate and a croissant, then say it is for here.
2. Write 4,50 $ and 12,25 $ in words.
3. The worker says *Et avec ça?* and you want nothing more. What do you say?
4. Write the price of 7,75 $ in words. Then say how you answer *Pour ici ou pour emporter?* if you want to leave.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Je voudrais un chocolat chaud et un croissant, s'il vous plaît. Sur place.*
2. *Quatre dollars cinquante.* *Douze dollars vingt-cinq.*
3. *C'est tout.*
4. *Sept dollars soixante-quinze.* *Pour emporter.*

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Bonjour. Je voudrais un café, s'il vous plaît. | Hello. I would like a coffee, please.
Pour emporter. C'est tout. | To take away. That's all.
C'est combien? | How much is it?
Merci, bonne journée! | Thank you, have a good day!
```

## Common mistakes

Saying *je veux* to order. It sounds blunt at a counter, so say *je voudrais*.

Putting the cents before the dollars. Say *quatre dollars cinquante*, with the cents last.

Leaving out *s'il vous plaît*. A request without it sounds rude, so add it at the end of the order.

## In Quebec

Prices in Quebec are in dollars and cents, as in the rest of Canada, and you say the cents as a plain number. French writes 4,50 $, with a comma and a space before the dollar sign. The next lessons add some casual Quebec words for money and food: [Quebec words: dépanneur and café](09-quebec-dep-and-cafe-words.md).
