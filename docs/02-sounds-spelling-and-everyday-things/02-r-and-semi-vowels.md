# The French r and the semi-vowels

<span class="tag tag-sounds">Sounds</span> <span class="tag tag-a1">A1</span>

After this lesson you can say the French /ʁ/, and the semi-vowels /j/, /w/ and /ɥ/, in words such as *rue*, *bien*, *moi* and *huit*.

Before this: [French consonants](01-consonants.md), [Vowels a, i, ou and u](../01-first-sounds-and-greetings/03-vowels-a-i-ou-u.md), [Nasal vowels](../01-first-sounds-and-greetings/06-nasal-vowels.md). You need: a glass of water, for the gargle.

## The idea

This lesson has two parts. First the French *r*, which is the sound English speakers find strangest. Then three **semi-vowels**: sounds that start like a vowel but are said so quickly, right before another vowel, that they work like consonants. English has two of them already, the *y* of "yes" and the *w* of "water". French has a third one, which is new.

### The French r: /ʁ/

The French *r* is made at the back of the mouth, not with the tip of the tongue. The back of your tongue rises toward the soft flap that hangs at the back of your throat (the **uvula**), and the air squeezes through with a soft friction. There is no tongue-tip movement and no English "r" lip rounding.

1. Say /g/ slowly, as in *gare*. Feel the back of your tongue touch the roof of your mouth.
2. Say it again, but stop right before the tongue touches. Let air pass through the narrow gap. You should hear a soft rasp.
3. Keep the tongue tip down, behind your lower front teeth. The tip does nothing.
4. Add a vowel before and after: *ara*, *oro*, *iri*. Then say *rue* /ʁy/, *merci* /mɛʁsi/, *bonjour* /bɔ̃ʒuʁ/.

If step 2 does not work, gargle a little water, then say a /g/ with your mouth empty: the spot that moved is the right place. Another way is to start from a gentle cough. At the end of a word the sound is light, so say *bonjour* with an /ʁ/ that fades, not a hard English "r".

The letters *r* and *rr* both say /ʁ/: *Paris* /paʁi/, *une sœur* /sœʁ/, *la porte*. Do not roll it with the tongue tip.

### The semi-vowel /j/

The sound /j/ is the English *y* of "yes". It is written in four ways. The letter *y* before a vowel says /j/: *un voyage* /vwajaʒ/ (a trip). The letter *i* before a vowel says /j/: *bien* /bjɛ̃/ (the letters *ien* say /jɛ̃/, the nasal vowel from the last stage). The letters *ill* after a vowel usually say /j/: *une fille* /fij/ (a girl, or a daughter). Final *-ail* and *-eil* also end in /j/: *le travail* /tʁavaj/ (work).

Watch out: a few common words with *ill* say /il/ instead: *la ville* (city), *mille* (thousand), *tranquille* (quiet). They are exceptions, so learn them one by one.

### The semi-vowel /w/

The sound /w/ is the English *w*, said with the lips more rounded and tense. It is written *ou* before a vowel: *oui* /wi/. It is also in the spelling *oi*, which says /wa/: *moi* /mwa/, *toi* /twa/, *le soir* /swaʁ/ (the evening), *la voiture* /vwatyʁ/ (the car). And *oin* says /wɛ̃/: *loin* /lwɛ̃/ (far).

### The semi-vowel /ɥ/

The sound /ɥ/ is new. It is /y/ (the *u* of *tu*) said quickly before another vowel. Round your lips as if you were going to whistle, say /y/, and slide at once into the next vowel. The spelling is *u* before a vowel and the group *ui*: *lui* /lɥi/ (him), *la nuit* /nɥi/ (the night), *huit* /ɥit/ (eight, taught in lesson 6), *un fruit* /fʁɥi/ (a piece of fruit).

*Oui* /wi/ and *huit* /ɥit/ look alike on paper and sound different. In /w/ the lips push forward and open like a small circle. In /ɥ/ the lips are pushed forward and held tight, as for /y/. If you say *huit* like "weet", you are saying /w/.

## Hear it

Listen for the soft rasp at the back of your throat in the first block, and for the lip shape in the second.

```fr
Rue, merci, bonjour. | Street, thank you, hello.
Paris, Montréal. | Paris, Montreal.
Une sœur, une fille. | A sister, a girl.
Le soir, la nuit. | The evening, the night.
```

```fr
Oui, huit. | Yes, eight.
Lui, Louis. | Him, Louis.
Moi, toi, lui. | Me, you, him.
Un voyage, le travail. | A trip, work.
Un fruit, une voiture. | A fruit, a car.
Loin, bien. | Far, well.
```

Play *oui*, *huit* and *Louis* three times each. Then say *lui* and *Louis* so that the second sound is different: /ɥi/ with a tight, whistling shape, and /wi/ with a rounder one.

## Try it

1. Which sound begins *rue*? Which letters make it?
2. Write the semi-vowel in *fille*, *moi* and *huit* as /j/, /w/ or /ɥ/.
3. Say *oui* and *huit* three times in a row. Which one needs rounded lips held tight?
4. A word has *ill* and ends in /il/. Name one.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. /ʁ/, written *r*.
2. *Fille* /fij/ has /j/. *Moi* /mwa/ has /w/. *Huit* /ɥit/ has /ɥ/.
3. *Huit*, with the tight lips of /y/.
4. *Ville* /vil/, *mille* or *tranquille*.

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Moi, je suis de Paris. | I'm from Paris.
Toi, tu es de Montréal. | You are from Montreal.
Le soir, la nuit, la voiture. | The evening, the night, the car.
Huit fruits. | Eight pieces of fruit.
Un voyage loin de la ville. | A trip far from the city.
```

## Common mistakes

Saying the English "r" with the tongue curled back or the lips rounded. French /ʁ/ is made at the back of the mouth with the tongue tip down.

Rolling the *r* with the tip of the tongue. That sound is used in some places, but a rolled /r/ is not the standard sound, so say /ʁ/.

Saying *huit* as "weet". The lips are tight and rounded as for /y/.

## In Quebec

The OQLF says the standard *r* in Quebec is the same back-of-the-mouth sound, /ʁ/, as elsewhere in French. It describes the rolled *r* made with the tongue tip as old-fashioned or tied to some regions, and says it lasted longest in western Quebec ([OQLF: prononciation de la lettre r](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/24521/la-prononciation/prononciation-de-certaines-lettres/prononciation-de-la-lettre-r)). You may hear it from older speakers and in old songs. You do not need to say it.

For *oi*, the OQLF says it sounds like /wa/ in many words and is longer when a consonant closes the syllable, as in *soir* ([OQLF: la suite de lettres oi](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/24522/la-prononciation/prononciation-de-certaines-lettres/prononciation-de-la-suite-de-lettres-oi)). [Quebec vowels and diphthongs](../12-real-montreal/06-quebec-vowels-and-diphthongs.md) goes back to this later.

## Key terms

- **Semi-vowel**: a sound that starts like a vowel but is said so fast before another vowel that it acts like a consonant: /j/, /w/, /ɥ/.
- **Uvula**: the small soft flap at the back of the throat, where the French /ʁ/ is made.
