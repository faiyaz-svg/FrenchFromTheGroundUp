## The idea

A form is the first piece of French writing you will do in real life: a library card, a gym sign-up, a doctor's office. The words on a form are short and repeat from one form to the next. Each label names the information you give, so if you know the label you know what to write. This lesson teaches the labels, then the three things that take care: the date, the address and the signature.

### The labels

| Label | What to write | English |
|---|---|---|
| *Nom* | your family name | last name |
| *Prénom* | your first name | first name |
| *Date de naissance* | day, month, year in numbers | date of birth |
| *Âge* | a number | age |
| *Adresse* | number and street | address |
| *Ville* | the city | city |
| *Province* | *QC*, *ON* | province |
| *Code postal* | letter, number, letter, space, number, letter, number | postal code |
| *Téléphone* | a phone number | phone |
| *Courriel* | your email address | email |
| *Nationalité* | *canadienne*, *canadien* | nationality |
| *Profession* | your job, or *étudiant* (student) | occupation |
| *État civil* | *célibataire*, *marié* or *mariée* | marital status |
| *Signature* | you sign | signature |

A **form** is *un formulaire*. A box that you must fill in is marked *obligatoire* (required). Many forms print an asterisk next to it. *Un champ* is the box itself.

The label *Nom* means your family name, and *Prénom* means your first name: it is the opposite order to the English "first name, last name", so read each label before you write. In your Anki deck, learn *prénom* with *nom* as a pair.

### Dates in numbers

A date of birth on a form is written in numbers. Months with names (*mars* is March, *avril* is April) come later in the book, so this lesson only uses numbers. Two orders are common. One is *JJ/MM/AAAA*, day, month, year, with *JJ* for *jour* (day), *MM* for *mois* (month) and *AAAA* for *année* (year). The other is *AAAA-MM-JJ*, year first, which is the order of the international standard ISO 8601, from the largest part to the smallest ([OQLF: norme ISO 8601](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/8391391/norme-iso-8601)). The OQLF calls a date such as 03/02/01 ambiguous, because its meaning depends on the country. So 14 March 1998 is 14/03/1998 in the first order and 1998-03-14 in the second.

Watch out: the same numbers mean different dates in different orders, and a form from another country may put the month first. Read the hint under the box, which tells you the order, before you write. When you say your birth date aloud, use words once stage 3 has taught the month names.

### Addresses

The OQLF gives this order for a Quebec address: building number, a comma, the street name, then the apartment after another comma, as in *45, rue Laval, app. 4*. It accepts the short form of *appartement* shown there, and advises against the symbol # and the abbreviation *no* before a number ([OQLF: numéro d'appartement ou de bureau](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/25048/la-redaction-et-la-communication/redaction-administrative-et-commerciale/correspondance/consignes-dadressage/elements-de-ladresse/numero-dappartement-ou-de-bureau-dans-ladresse)). The city, the province and the postal code come next, in that order. The OQLF writes the postal code in capitals with a space between the two groups, for example *G1V 2N3*, and puts it after the city and province ([OQLF: code postal](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/25052/la-redaction-et-la-communication/redaction-administrative-et-commerciale/correspondance/consignes-dadressage/elements-de-ladresse/code-postal-dans-ladresse)).

On a form the boxes are already separate, so you fill each one. The street word comes first: *rue* (street), *avenue* (avenue) or *boulevard*. Canada Post uses a different order for the apartment: it puts the apartment number before the street number, with a hyphen, as in *4-45 rue Laval*, and it asks for capitals, an abbreviated province such as *QC* or *ON*, and no # sign ([Canada Post: how to address mail](https://www.canadapost-postescanada.ca/cpc/en/support/kb/sending/general-information/how-to-address-mail-and-parcels.page)). The OQLF advises against the hyphen form. If a form shows boxes, follow the boxes.

In Ontario the French labels are the same. A form from a French-language school or a government office in Ottawa asks for *Province*, and you write *ON*.

### Titles and the signature

Some forms ask for a title of address: *M.* for *monsieur* and *Mme* for *madame*. The OQLF writes the titles in full in the opening and closing of a letter and on the envelope, so you mostly see the short forms on forms and lists ([OQLF: abréviation de madame, de mademoiselle et de monsieur](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/21435/les-abreviations-et-les-symboles/les-abreviations/cas-particuliers-dabreviations/abreviation-de-madame-de-mademoiselle-et-de-monsieur)). *Mme* has no full stop and *M.* has one.

The last box is the *signature*. Sign by hand, with the name you wrote above.

## Hear it

Listen for the labels, then follow the form in the table. The scene is a front desk at a public library in Gatineau, with *vous*. The employee is Madame Roy, and you are signing up for a card. Our own example, not a real form.

```fr
Employée: Bonjour, madame. Voilà le formulaire. | Hello. Here is the form.
Vous: Merci. Mon nom, c'est Tremblay. | Thank you. My family name is Tremblay.
Employée: Ça s'écrit comment? | How is that spelled?
Vous: T, R, E, M, B, L, A, Y. Et mon prénom, c'est Nadia. | T, R, E, M, B, L, A, Y. And my first name is Nadia.
Employée: Merci. La date de naissance, ici, en chiffres. Et l'adresse, là. | Thank you. The date of birth here, in numbers. And the address there.
Vous: Voilà. Mon courriel, c'est Nadia point Tremblay arobase exemple point c, a. | Here. My email is nadia.tremblay@exemple.ca.
Employée: Parfait. Et la signature, ici, s'il vous plaît. Merci! | Perfect. And the signature here, please. Thank you!
```

## Try it

1. What does each label ask for: *prénom*, *code postal*, *obligatoire*, *courriel*?
2. Write 3 January 2001 as JJ/MM/AAAA and as AAAA-MM-JJ.
3. Write this address the OQLF way: number 48, *rue* Principale, apartment 12, Ottawa, Ontario.
4. Fill in the library-card form for yourself, in the table order, with your own information.

<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Prénom*: first name. *Code postal*: postal code. *Obligatoire*: required. *Courriel*: email address.
2. 03/01/2001 and 2001-01-03.
3. *48, rue Principale, app. 12, Ottawa, ON* followed by your postal code.
4. Your own answers. Check that the date is in numbers, that the comma follows the building number, and that the code has a capital letter, a number, a capital letter, a space, then number, capital letter, number.

## Writing task

Fill in the library-card form with your own information, using the labels in the table. Then write your name and address out in words, as in the model. Use between 25 and 40 words in total, counting the form answers and the sentences.

Model for Nadia (about 30 words):

*Nom : Tremblay. Prénom : Nadia. Date de naissance : 14/03/1998. Adresse : 45, rue Laval, app. 4. Ville : Gatineau. Province : QC. Code postal : J8X 2B3. Mon adresse : quarante-cinq, rue Laval, appartement quatre.*

## Common mistakes

Mixing up *nom* and *prénom*. *Nom* is your family name and *prénom* is your first name.

Writing the date in the English order, 03/14/1998. Read the hint under the box first, and give the day before the month, or use AAAA-MM-JJ.

Using # for an apartment number. The OQLF advises against it, because # does not mean "number" in French. Write *app. 4*.

## In Quebec

Quebec forms use *courriel* for email, as the OQLF recommends ([OQLF: courriel](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/8353974/courriel)). The province code is *QC* for Québec and *ON* for Ontario.
