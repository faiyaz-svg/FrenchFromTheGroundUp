# Numbers from 0 to 100

<span class="tag tag-words">Words</span> <span class="tag tag-a1">A1</span>

After this lesson you can say, hear and write the numbers from 0 to 100, including 21, 71 and 91, and you know when the last consonant of *six*, *huit* and *dix* is pronounced.

Before this: [Spelling rules and liaison](03-spelling-sound-rules-and-liaison.md), [Nasal vowels](../01-first-sounds-and-greetings/06-nasal-vowels.md), [The French r and the semi-vowels](02-r-and-semi-vowels.md). You need: a pencil and paper.

## The idea

A **number** (*un nombre*) is the quantity itself. A **digit** (*un chiffre*) is one of the symbols 0 to 9, and a phone or bus number is a *numéro*. The French count in a way that has a few oddities, so this lesson goes in five steps: 0 to 16, then 17 to 19, then the tens, then 21 to 69, then 70 to 100. Say each step out loud before you move on.

### 0 to 16

Learn these as separate words. They do not follow a pattern.

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 *zéro* /zeʁo/ | 1 *un* /œ̃/ | 2 *deux* /dø/ | 3 *trois* /tʁwa/ |
| 4 *quatre* /katʁ/ | 5 *cinq* /sɛ̃k/ | 6 *six* /sis/ | 7 *sept* /sɛt/ |
| 8 *huit* /ɥit/ | 9 *neuf* /nœf/ | 10 *dix* /dis/ | 11 *onze* /ɔ̃z/ |
| 12 *douze* /duz/ | 13 *treize* /tʁɛz/ | 14 *quatorze* /katɔʁz/ | 15 *quinze* /kɛ̃z/ |
| 16 *seize* /sɛz/ | | | |

Watch out for the *p* of *sept*, which is silent, and for the *t*, which is pronounced. Say /sɛt/, with no /p/.

### 17 to 19

From 17 you say ten plus the unit: *dix-sept* /disɛt/ (17), *dix-huit* /dizɥit/ (18), *dix-neuf* /diznœf/ (19). The *x* of *dix* sounds like /z/ before the vowel of *huit* and *neuf*.

### The tens

| 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 60 | 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *vingt* /vɛ̃/ | *trente* /tʁɑ̃t/ | *quarante* /kaʁɑ̃t/ | *cinquante* /sɛ̃kɑ̃t/ | *soixante* /swasɑ̃t/ | *cent* /sɑ̃/ |

*Trente*, *quarante*, *cinquante* and *soixante* all end in /ɑ̃t/. *Vingt* has a silent *g* and *t* when it stands alone.

### 21 to 69

Say the ten, then the unit: *vingt-deux* (22), *trente-trois* (33), *quarante-quatre* (44), *cinquante-cinq* (55), *soixante-six* (66). In writing, a hyphen joins the two parts.

The numbers that end in 1 use *et* ("and") with no hyphens: *vingt et un* (21), *trente et un* (31), *quarante et un* (41), *cinquante et un* (51), *soixante et un* (61). Only 21, 31, 41, 51, 61 and 71 do this. The numbers 81 and 91 do not.

A note on spelling: this book uses the traditional spelling, with a hyphen between the parts except around *et*, so *vingt et un*. The 1990 spelling reform allows a hyphen between all the parts (*vingt-et-un*), and both spellings are correct ([OQLF: écriture des déterminants numéraux](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/23494/la-typographie/nombres/ecriture-des-determinants-numeraux)).

### 70 to 100

French builds these on 60 and 80. For 70 to 79, say 60 plus 10 to 19: *soixante-dix* (70), *soixante et onze* (71), *soixante-douze* (72), up to *soixante-dix-neuf* (79). For 80 to 99, say four twenties (*quatre-vingts*, 80), then add: *quatre-vingt-un* (81, no *et*), *quatre-vingt-deux* (82), up to *quatre-vingt-dix* (90), *quatre-vingt-onze* (91) and *quatre-vingt-dix-neuf* (99). Then *cent* (100).

Watch out: *quatre-vingts* has an *s* only when no other number follows it. With a number after it, there is no *s*: *quatre-vingt-trois*.

In Montreal you say *soixante-dix*, which the OQLF lists as its preferred term ([OQLF: soixante-dix](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/8870053/soixante-dix)); a Belgian or Swiss speaker may say *septante*, which Usito describes as common usage in Belgium and Switzerland ([Usito: septante](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/définitions/septante)).

### Final consonants in *cinq*, *six*, *sept*, *huit*, *neuf*, *dix*

The OQLF describes these in its pronunciation pages ([OQLF: prononciation des nombres](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/la-prononciation/prononciation-des-nombres)). The rules for a number before a noun:

| Number | Before a consonant | Before a vowel | At the end |
|---|---|---|---|
| *six*, *dix* | silent: *six jours* /si ʒuʁ/ | /z/: *six amis* /siz ami/ | /s/: *dix* /dis/ |
| *huit* | /t/: *huit livres* /ɥit livʁ/ (silent only in a few exceptions) | /t/: *huit amis* /ɥit ami/ | /t/: *huit* /ɥit/ |
| *cinq* | /k/ or silent (both heard): *cinq livres* /sɛ̃k livʁ/ or /sɛ̃ livʁ/ | /k/: *cinq amis* /sɛ̃k ami/ | /k/: *cinq* /sɛ̃k/ |
| *sept* | /t/: *sept livres* /sɛt livʁ/ | /t/: *sept amis* /sɛt ami/ | /t/: *sept* /sɛt/ |
| *neuf* | /f/: *neuf livres* /nœf livʁ/ | /f/, but /v/ before *ans* and *heures*: *neuf ans* /nœv ɑ̃/ (nine years) | /f/: *neuf* /nœf/ |

Say the last consonant when the number stands alone, as in a phone number. *Vingt* is /vɛ̃/ alone and before a consonant, but the *t* is heard from 21 to 29: *vingt et un* /vɛ̃t e œ̃/, *vingt-deux* /vɛ̃tdø/.

## Hear it

Listen for the changing *x* of *six* and *dix* and for the *t* in *huit*.

```fr
Un, deux, trois, quatre, cinq. | One, two, three, four, five.
Six, sept, huit, neuf, dix. | Six, seven, eight, nine, ten.
Onze, douze, treize, quatorze, quinze, seize. | Eleven, twelve, thirteen, fourteen, fifteen, sixteen.
Six chats, six amis. | Six cats, six friends.
Dix livres, dix amis. | Ten books, ten friends.
Huit livres, huit amis. | Eight books, eight friends.
```

Now numbers in short exchanges. The first is at a front desk, with *vous*. Our own examples.

```fr
Vous: Bonjour, le poste quarante-deux, s'il vous plaît. | Hello, extension forty-two, please.
Standardiste: Un instant. C'est le poste quarante-deux? | One moment. Is it extension forty-two?
Vous: Oui, quarante-deux. Merci beaucoup. | Yes, forty-two. Thank you very much.
```

```fr
Chauffeur: C'est l'autobus quatre-vingt-cinq. | It's bus eighty-five.
Vous: Merci. Et le soixante et onze? | Thank you. And the seventy-one?
Chauffeur: Le soixante et onze est ici. | The seventy-one is here.
```

## Try it

1. Write in words: 15, 21, 33, 48, 76, 80, 91, 100.
2. Say in French: 17, 18, 19. What happens to the *x* of *dix* in 18 and 19?
3. Say *six chats*, *six amis* and *six* alone. Which sound does the *x* make each time?
4. What is the difference between 71 and 81 in words?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Quinze*, *vingt et un*, *trente-trois*, *quarante-huit*, *soixante-seize*, *quatre-vingts*, *quatre-vingt-onze*, *cent*.
2. *Dix-sept*, *dix-huit*, *dix-neuf*. In 18 and 19 the *x* is /z/ before the vowel.
3. *Six chats* /si ʃa/: silent. *Six amis* /siz ami/: /z/. *Six* alone /sis/: /s/.
4. 71 is *soixante et onze*, with *et*. 81 is *quatre-vingt-un*, with no *et*.

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Zéro, un, deux, trois, quatre, cinq. | Zero, one, two, three, four, five.
Dix, vingt, trente, quarante, cinquante, soixante. | Ten, twenty, thirty, forty, fifty, sixty.
Soixante-dix, quatre-vingts, quatre-vingt-dix, cent. | Seventy, eighty, ninety, one hundred.
Vingt et un, trente et un, soixante et onze. | Twenty-one, thirty-one, seventy-one.
```

## Common mistakes

Dropping the *et* in 21, 31, 41, 51, 61 and 71, as in *vingt-un*. Write *vingt et un* and *soixante et onze*.

Writing *quatre-vingts-trois* with an *s* before another number. Write *quatre-vingt-trois*.

Pronouncing the *x* of *six* and *dix* as /s/ before a consonant. Say *six chats* /si ʃa/.

## In Quebec

Quebec uses *soixante-dix*, *quatre-vingts* and *quatre-vingt-dix*, as France does, and the OQLF gives *soixante-dix* as its term ([source above](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/8870053/soixante-dix)).

## Key terms

- **Cardinal number**: a number used to count, such as *trois* or *vingt*.
- **Digit**: one of the symbols 0 to 9, *un chiffre*.
