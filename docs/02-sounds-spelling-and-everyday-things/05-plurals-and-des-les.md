# Plurals, des and les

<span class="tag tag-grammar">Grammar</span> <span class="tag tag-a1">A1</span>

After this lesson you can make a noun plural, choose between *les* and *des*, and hear whether a speaker means one thing or several from the article.

Before this: [Gender and articles](04-gender-and-articles.md), [Spelling rules and liaison](03-spelling-sound-rules-and-liaison.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

**Singular** means one, and **plural** means more than one. English adds *-s* to most nouns: "book, books". French does the same on paper, but the *-s* is silent, so *un livre* /livʁ/ and *des livres* /de livʁ/ sound almost the same. In speech the plural is heard in the article: *le* becomes *les*, and *un* or *une* becomes *des*. This is one of the most useful things to learn for listening, because you will hear the article long before you see the noun.

### The plural articles

| Singular | Plural | Sound change |
|---|---|---|
| *le*, *la*, *l'* | *les* /le/ | the vowel opens from /ə/ or /a/ to /e/ |
| *un*, *une* | *des* /de/ | the nasal vowel or /yn/ becomes /e/ |

*Les* is the plural of *le*, *la* and *l'* (the). *Des* is the plural of *un* and *une*: it means "some" or nothing at all in English. English says "I see books" with no word before "books", and French needs *des*: *Voilà des livres*. You cannot leave the article out in French.

Before a vowel, both articles link with /z/: *les amis* /lez ami/, *des amis* /dez ami/. This is the required liaison from [lesson 3](03-spelling-sound-rules-and-liaison.md), and it also lets you hear the plural when the noun starts with a vowel.

The verb changes too. *Être* has a singular form and a plural form: *le livre est ici* and *les livres sont ici* (*ici* means "here"). The *-s* in *livres* is silent, so *est* and *sont* are the clues you can hear.

### Making the plural on paper

1. **Most nouns add -s.** *Une table* becomes *des tables*. The *s* is silent.
2. **Nouns ending in -eau or -eu add -x.** *Un cadeau* (a gift) becomes *des cadeaux*, *un jeu* (a game) becomes *des jeux*, and *un bureau* becomes *des bureaux*. The *x* is silent too.
3. **Nouns ending in -al change to -aux.** *Un journal* (a newspaper) becomes *des journaux* /ʒuʁno/, and *un animal* becomes *des animaux* /animo/. The sound changes as well as the spelling: /al/ becomes /o/.
4. **Nouns that already end in -s, -x or -z do not change.** *Un prix* (a price) is *des prix*, *un cours* (a class) is *des cours*, and *un pays* is *des pays*. Only the article shows the plural.
5. **Irregular plurals.** *Un œil* (an eye) becomes *des yeux* /jø/, and this one you learn by itself. *Un œil* is said /œj/ and *des yeux* is said /jø/, so the vowel and the order of the sounds change.

A few nouns also have an odd plural in speech: *un œuf* /œf/ becomes *des œufs* /dez ø/, as lesson 3 showed.

Watch out for *une personne*. It is a feminine noun even when it names a man, so you say *une personne* and *des personnes*. The gender follows the word, not the person.

```fr
Karim: Voilà des cadeaux pour la fête. | Here are some gifts for the party.
Nadia: Et les journaux, c'est pour le magasin? | And the newspapers, are they for the shop?
Karim: Oui, les journaux sont pour le magasin. | Yes, the newspapers are for the shop.
Nadia: Deux journaux, des stylos, un livre. | Two newspapers, some pens, one book.
```

### Les or des?

Use *les* when you mean particular things that both people know, or things in general. Use *des* when you introduce several things or when any of them will do. This is the same difference as between *le* and *un*: the sentence *Les journaux sont pour le magasin.* names the newspapers already on the table, and *Voilà des journaux.* shows some for the first time.

## Hear it

Listen to the vowel in the article. The noun may sound the same in the singular and the plural, but the article does not.

```fr
Le livre, les livres. | The book, the books.
Un sac, des sacs. | A bag, some bags.
Une table, des tables. | A table, some tables.
L'ami, les amis. | The friend, the friends.
Un journal, des journaux. | A newspaper, some newspapers.
Un cadeau, des cadeaux. | A gift, some gifts.
Un œil, des yeux. | An eye, some eyes.
Un prix, des prix. | A price, some prices.
```

Play each pair twice and say it. Then close the page and listen again: can you hear which line is singular and which plural from the article alone?

## Try it

1. Write the plural: *un cours*, *un message*, *un animal*, *un jeu*, *une personne*.
2. Which word tells you the number in *des prix* and in *un prix*?
3. Choose *est* or *sont*: *Les cadeaux ___ pour la fête.* *Le journal ___ ici.*
4. You hear /le ʃa/ and /de ʃa/. Write the two phrases.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Des cours* (no change), *des messages*, *des animaux*, *des jeux*, *des personnes*.
2. The article, because *prix* is the same in both: *un prix*, *des prix*.
3. *Les cadeaux sont pour la fête.* *Le journal est ici.*
4. *Les chats* and *des chats*.

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Les livres sont ici. | The books are here.
Voilà des cadeaux pour les amis. | Here are some gifts for the friends.
Un journal, des journaux. | A newspaper, some newspapers.
Les yeux, les œufs, les prix. | The eyes, the eggs, the prices.
```

## Common mistakes

Listening for a final *s* on the noun. It is silent, so listen to the article: *les* and *des* are the plural markers.

Leaving out *des* where English leaves out "some". *Voilà des livres*, not *voilà livres*.

Adding *-s* to *-al* nouns: *des journals*. Write *des journaux*.

## In Quebec

Plurals work the same way in Quebec as in France. In careful French, *des* becomes *de* before an adjective that comes in front of a plural noun (*de beaux jours*, "fine days"). You meet this rule in [Partitive articles: du, de la, des](../06-habits-memories-and-food/01-partitive-articles.md), so for now keep *des*.
