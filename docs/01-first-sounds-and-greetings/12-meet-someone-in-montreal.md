# Meet someone in Montreal

<span class="tag tag-speak">Speak</span> <span class="tag tag-a1">A1</span> <span class="tag tag-review">Review</span>

After this lesson you can hold a first conversation of about a dozen lines: greet someone, say who you are and where you are from, order a drink, spell your name and email address, and say goodbye.

Before this: [Greetings and politeness](08-greetings-and-politeness.md), [Bonjour-hi and other Montreal greetings](09-bonjour-hi-montreal-greetings.md), [Être and introducing yourself](10-etre-and-introductions.md), [Your first slow dialogues](11-first-slow-dialogues.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

This lesson has no new grammar. It puts the stage together in one conversation. You will hear a dialogue first, then play your own side of it, and then change it so that it fits you.

### The scene

Chloé is a university student in Montreal. She goes to a French conversation evening at a café, where learners and native speakers sit together. The other people are young adults like her, and the group uses *tu*. She sits next to a person who arrived from Toronto this week. That person is you. The role-play below follows the order you practised: greeting, names, origin, drinks, email, goodbye.

## Hear it

First listen with the translation hidden. Listen for three things: the two cities, the drinks and the email address.

```fr
Chloé: Salut! Ça va? | Hi! How's it going?
Vous: Ça va bien, merci. Et toi? | Fine, thanks. And you?
Chloé: Très bien. Je m'appelle Chloé. Et toi? | Very well. My name is Chloé. And you?
Vous: Je m'appelle Marc. Enchanté. | My name is Marc. Nice to meet you.
Chloé: Enchantée. Tu es de Montréal? | Nice to meet you. Are you from Montreal?
Vous: Non, je suis de Toronto. Je suis canadien. Et toi? | No, I'm from Toronto. I'm Canadian. And you?
Chloé: Moi, je suis de Gatineau. | I'm from Gatineau.
Vous: Un thé, s'il te plaît? | A tea, please?
Chloé: Oui, voilà. Merci beaucoup. | Yes, here you are. Thank you very much.
Vous: De rien. Ça s'écrit comment, Chloé? | You're welcome. How do you spell Chloé?
Chloé: C, H, L, O, E accent aigu. | C, H, L, O, E acute.
Vous: Merci. Et ton courriel? | Thank you. And your email?
Chloé: Chloé point Roy arobase exemple point c, a | chloe.roy@exemple.ca
Vous: Merci, Chloé. Bonne fin de semaine! | Thanks, Chloé. Have a good weekend!
Chloé: Merci, toi aussi. À bientôt! | Thanks, you too. See you soon!
```

## Your turn

You are Marc in the dialogue. Cover the lines that start with *Vous* and say them from memory while the other lines play. Then change the dialogue so that it is true for you: your own name, your own city, your own nationality and your own drink. Say the new version twice.

```fr
Chloé: Salut! Ça va? | Hi! How's it going?
Chloé: Je m'appelle Chloé. Et toi? | My name is Chloé. And you?
Chloé: Enchantée. Tu es de Montréal? | Nice to meet you. Are you from Montreal?
Chloé: Moi, je suis de Gatineau. | I'm from Gatineau.
Chloé: Oui, voilà. Merci beaucoup. | Yes, here you are. Thank you very much.
Chloé: C, H, L, O, E accent aigu. | C, H, L, O, E acute.
Chloé: Merci, toi aussi. À bientôt! | Thanks, you too. See you soon!
```

### A version with vous

Now imagine you meet a coworker's parent, who is older. Use *vous* instead of *tu*. The sentences change in three places: *tu es* becomes *vous êtes*, *Et toi?* becomes *Et vous?*, and *s'il te plaît* becomes *s'il vous plaît*.

```fr
Madame-Roy: Bonjour. Comment allez-vous? | Hello. How are you?
Vous: Très bien, merci. Et vous? | Very well, thank you. And you?
Madame-Roy: Ça va bien. Vous êtes de Toronto? | Very well. Are you from Toronto?
Vous: Oui, je suis de Toronto. Enchanté, madame. | Yes, I'm from Toronto. Nice to meet you.
Madame-Roy: Enchantée. Au revoir, bonne journée! | Nice to meet you. Goodbye, have a good day!
```

## Try it

1. Say your side of the first dialogue from memory, with your own details.
2. Change this line to *vous*: *Tu es de Montréal? Et toi?*
3. Spell your first name aloud, and then spell an email address of your choice with *arobase* and *point*.
4. Say what *À bientôt* means and when you use it.

<summary>Answer</summary>

1. Check: you give your name with *je m'appelle*, your city with *je suis de*, a nationality with the right ending, and a drink with *s'il te plaît*.
2. *Vous êtes de Montréal? Et vous?*
3. Example: *Marc point Roy arobase exemple point c, a*. Say each letter of what is not a word.
4. "See you soon". You say it when you leave someone you expect to see again.

## Common mistakes

Changing *tu es* to *vous êtes* but leaving *Et toi?* in the same conversation. Change every *tu* word together.

Stopping when you do not know a word. Use *Pardon?* or repeat the last word with a rising voice, and keep talking.

Reading the dialogue instead of saying it. Cover the lines, say them, and look only to check.

## In Quebec

In a Montreal café you may hear "Bonjour, hi" from the worker. Answer with *bonjour* and keep going in French. If someone answers your *merci* with *bienvenue*, you will know what it means.
