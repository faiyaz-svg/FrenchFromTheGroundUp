# The vowels o and eu

<span class="tag tag-sounds">Sounds</span> <span class="tag tag-a1">A1</span>

After this lesson you can say the four rounded vowels /o/, /ɔ/, /ø/ and /œ/, and pick the right one from the spelling.

Before this: [The e vowels](04-vowels-e-family.md). You need: a mirror.

## The idea

The rounded vowels come in two pairs, and each pair has a closed vowel and an open vowel, as the e vowels did. The [vowel chart](../assets/figures/01-first-sounds-and-greetings/vowel-chart.svg) shows them. The back pair, /o/ and /ɔ/, is made with the tongue back. The front pair, /ø/ and /œ/, is made with the tongue forward, like /e/ and /ɛ/, but with the lips rounded. Rounding is the key move: your lips push forward into a small circle.

### The four sounds

| Symbol | Spellings | Examples | Closed or open |
|---|---|---|---|
| /o/ | o at the end of a word or before /z/, ô, au, eau | *eau* /o/ (water), *beau* /bo/ (nice), *trop* /tʁo/ | closed |
| /ɔ/ | o before a pronounced consonant | *bol* /bɔl/ (bowl), *porte* /pɔʁt/ (door), *pomme* /pɔm/ (apple) | open |
| /ø/ | eu at the end of a word or before /z/ | *deux* /dø/ (two), *bleu* /blø/ (blue), *peu* /pø/ (little), *Monsieur* /məsjø/ (Mr, sir) | closed |
| /œ/ | eu or œu before a pronounced consonant | *sœur* /sœʁ/ (sister), *peur* /pœʁ/ (fear), *fleur* /flœʁ/ (flower), *heure* /œʁ/ (hour) | open |

The spellings *au* and *eau* look like two or three vowels, but each says one sound, /o/. Likewise *eu* and *œu* each say one vowel. In *Monsieur* the *eu* says /ø/ in the last syllable, and the first syllable is the weak /ə/ you met last lesson.

### How to make each sound

1. For /o/, say the English "oh", then stop the glide. Round your lips into a small circle and hold them still.
2. For /ɔ/, drop your jaw a little from /o/, with the lips less tight. It is close to the vowel in English "off".
3. For /ø/, say /e/ (the vowel of *thé*) and keep your tongue where it is. Now round your lips as for /o/. The vowel you hear is /ø/.
4. For /œ/, say /ɛ/ (the vowel of *père*) and keep the tongue. Round your lips and open the jaw a little. The vowel you hear is /œ/.

If you hear /o/ instead of /ø/, your tongue has gone back. Return to /e/ and keep it forward.

## Hear it

Listen for how open the mouth is. *Eau* and *deux* are closed. *Bol* and *sœur* are open and short.

```fr
Eau. Beau. Bol. | Water. Nice. Bowl.
Deux. Bleu. Peu. | Two. Blue. Little.
Sœur. Peur. Fleur. | Sister. Fear. Flower.
Une heure. | One hour.
```

Compare the pairs *peu* /pø/ and *peur* /pœʁ/, and *beau* /bo/ and *bol* /bɔl/. The vowel is the main difference you can hear. A short rule helps with *eu*: at the end of a word or before /z/ it is /ø/, and before other pronounced consonants it is usually /œ/. Say each word twice and check the rule against the audio.

## Try it

1. Name the vowel in each word, /o/, /ɔ/, /ø/ or /œ/: *eau*, *bol*, *deux*, *sœur*.
2. Say *peu* and *peur*. Which has the more open vowel?
3. Which two words rhyme in their vowel: *bleu*, *deux*, *peur*?
4. Describe in two steps how you make /ø/ from /e/.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Eau*: /o/. *Bol*: /ɔ/. *Deux*: /ø/. *Sœur*: /œ/.
2. *Peur* /pœʁ/.
3. *Bleu* and *deux*, which both have /ø/.
4. Say /e/ and keep the tongue still. Then round your lips.

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Deux bols et une pomme. | Two bowls and an apple.
La sœur et la mère sont ici. | The sister and the mother are here.
L'eau est bleue. | The water is blue.
Une heure, deux fleurs. | One hour, two flowers.
```

## Common mistakes

Spreading the lips on *deux* and *sœur*. These vowels need rounded lips, so say /e/ and /ɛ/ first and then round.

Reading *eau* as three sounds. It says one vowel, /o/.

Sliding the vowel at the end of *beau*, as English "no" ends. Hold /o/ still.
