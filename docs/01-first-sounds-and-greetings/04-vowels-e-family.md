# The e vowels

<span class="tag tag-sounds">Sounds</span> <span class="tag tag-a1">A1</span>

After this lesson you can say the three e vowels /e/, /ɛ/ and /ə/, choose the right one from the spelling, and hear the difference between *les* and *lait*.

Before this: [The vowels a, i, ou and u](03-vowels-a-i-ou-u.md). You need: a mirror.

## The idea

The letter *e* is the busiest letter in French spelling. It stands for three different vowels, and the spelling around it tells you which one. All three are made in the front of the mouth, with spread or relaxed lips. The [vowel chart](../assets/figures/01-first-sounds-and-greetings/vowel-chart.svg) places /e/ above /ɛ/: the tongue is higher for /e/ and the jaw is more open for /ɛ/. The third vowel, /ə/, is a weak sound in the middle.

### The three sounds

| Symbol | Name | Spellings | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| /e/ | closed e | é, the endings *-er* and *-ez*, *et*, *les* | *thé* /te/ (tea), *café* /kafe/, *et* /e/ (and), *les* /le/ |
| /ɛ/ | open e | è, ê, ai, ei, *e* before a pronounced consonant | *père* /pɛʁ/ (father), *mère* /mɛʁ/ (mother), *lait* /lɛ/ (milk), *merci* /mɛʁsi/ |
| /ə/ | weak e | *e* at the end of a short word or syllable | *le* /lə/, *je* /ʒə/ (I), *de* /də/ (of, from), *petit* /pəti/ |

The accent on é and è is the sign you saw in the first lesson, and here it does its work: *é* says /e/ and *è* says /ɛ/. The spelling *ai*, as in *lait*, also says /ɛ/. You do not hear the *a* and the *i* separately.

### How to make /e/ and /ɛ/

1. Smile, with your lips spread and your tongue high and forward, as for /i/.
2. Lower your jaw a little. This is /e/, the vowel of *thé*. Hold it still, because there is no glide at the end as in English "day".
3. Lower your jaw further, keeping the lips relaxed. This is /ɛ/, the vowel of *père*. It is close to the vowel in English "bed".
4. Practise the switch: /e/, /ɛ/, /e/, /ɛ/, watching the jaw in a mirror.

### How to make /ə/

1. Relax the face. Your tongue rests in the middle of the mouth.
2. Round your lips slightly, as if you started to say /y/ and stopped.
3. Say *le* and *de* quickly. The vowel is short and weak.

/ə/ is the unstable vowel of French. You say it in full in *le* and *je*, but in fast speech it often disappears: *petit* sounds close to /pti/. A final *e* in writing is usually silent, so *rue* /ʁy/ has no /ə/ at the end. Spelling rules and liaison<!-- link: ../02-sounds-spelling-and-everyday-things/03-spelling-sound-rules-and-liaison.md --> gives more detail.

## Hear it

Listen for the jaw. In *les* it is nearly closed, and in *lait* it is more open. The two words differ by this one vowel.

```fr
Les. Lait. Les. Lait. | The. Milk. The. Milk.
Le thé, le café. | The tea, the coffee.
Le père et la mère. | The father and the mother.
Je suis, le thé, de rien. | I am, the tea, you're welcome.
```

The pair *les* /le/ and *lait* /lɛ/ is the main minimal pair of this lesson. Play it three times, then say it. Listen next to a third word, *le* /lə/: it has the weakest vowel and the shortest sound.

## Try it

1. Name the vowel (/e/, /ɛ/ or /ə/) in each word: *thé*, *père*, *le*, *lait*, *café*.
2. Which two words in the list differ only in the e vowel: *les*, *lait*, *le*?
3. *Mère* and *merci* both have /ɛ/. What in the spelling tells you?
4. Say *je* and *de* three times each. Is the vowel short and relaxed?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Thé*: /e/. *Père*: /ɛ/. *Le*: /ə/. *Lait*: /ɛ/. *Café*: /e/ (the last vowel).
2. *Les* /le/ and *lait* /lɛ/ differ only in the vowel. *Le* /lə/ differs from both.
3. In *mère* it is the accent grave. In *merci* it is the *e* before the pronounced consonants *r* and *c*.
4. Your check: the vowel is short, the lips slightly round, and nothing is stressed.

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Le lait et le thé. | The milk and the tea.
Le père et la mère sont ici. | The father and the mother are here.
Merci, le café est petit. | Thank you, the coffee is small.
```

## Common mistakes

Gliding on /e/, so that *thé* ends in an English "ay" sound. Hold the vowel still.

Saying the final *e* in a word like *rue* or *petite*. A final *e* is usually silent.

Reading *ai* as two vowels. In *lait* the spelling *ai* is one vowel, /ɛ/.

## In Quebec

## Key terms

- **Open vowel**: a vowel said with the jaw lowered, such as /ɛ/.
- **Closed vowel**: a vowel said with the jaw nearly closed, such as /e/.
