# Greetings and politeness

After this lesson you can greet people, say goodbye, thank someone, ask for something politely, apologise, ask how someone is, and choose between *tu* and *vous*.

Before this: [The alphabet and spelling your name](07-alphabet-and-spelling-names.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

A greeting is a set phrase. You learn each one whole, with its sound, and you do not need to understand the grammar inside yet. Several of them contain a small word that carries a lot: *vous* or *tu*, the two French words for "you". Choosing between them is the first real decision in a French conversation, so we start there.

### Tu and vous

*Vous* is the polite "you". You use it with strangers, with people older than you, with people in a position of authority, and at work with someone you do not know. *Vous* is also the word for "you" when you speak to more than one person, whatever their age. *Tu* is the familiar "you". You use it with friends, family, children, and people your own age in a casual place.

The plain rule is: when in doubt, use *vous*. A polite *vous* rarely offends. If the other person says *tu* to you, you may answer with *tu*. English has only "you", so this choice has no English match. Your ear will learn it by use.

### Hello and goodbye

*Bonjour* is hello for the day, and it works with everyone, from early morning to late afternoon. *Bonsoir* is the evening version. *Salut* is a casual hello or bye among friends. For goodbye, *au revoir* is the standard form, and *bonne journée* wishes someone a good day.

On a personal phone line, *Allô?* is a normal way to answer. The OQLF gives it in its advice on [answering a call](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/23257/la-redaction-et-la-communication/communications-telephoniques/recevoir-un-appel), and notes that it is also written *allo*. At work, the advice is different: give the name of the company, then *bonjour*.

### Thank you, please and sorry

| You want to say | French | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Thank you | *merci*, *merci beaucoup* | everyone |
| You're welcome | *de rien* | neutral and common |
| Please | *s'il vous plaît* (with *vous*), *s'il te plaît* (with *tu*) | match the person |
| Excuse me (to get attention or pass) | *excusez-moi* | with *vous* |
| Sorry, pardon | *pardon* | everyone |

*S'il vous plaît* and *s'il te plaît* are one phrase with two endings: *vous* or *te*. The choice follows the person you are speaking to. The lesson [Bonjour-hi and other Montreal greetings](09-bonjour-hi-montreal-greetings.md) adds more on how people answer *merci* in Quebec.

### How are you?

*Ça va?* is the everyday question: "How is it going?" It works with *tu* and in many casual places. The polite form is *Comment allez-vous?*, which asks how the person is, with *vous*. You answer with *Ça va bien* or, a little more formally, *Très bien, merci*. Often people add *Et vous?* or *Et toi?*, which both mean "and you?" (*vous* with a stranger, *toi* with a friend). Lesson 10 explains them.

## Hear it

Listen for the pattern in each exchange: greeting, request, thanks. In the first dialogue the speakers use *vous*. In the second they know each other and use *tu*.

```fr
Employée: Bonjour, madame. | Hello, ma'am.
Vous: Bonjour. Comment allez-vous? | Hello. How are you?
Employée: Très bien, merci. | Very well, thank you.
Vous: Un café, s'il vous plaît. | A coffee, please.
Employée: Voilà. | Here you are.
Vous: Merci beaucoup. Au revoir, bonne journée. | Thank you very much. Goodbye, have a good day.
Employée: De rien. | You're welcome.

Léa: Salut, Samir! Ça va? | Hi, Samir! How's it going?
Samir: Ça va bien, merci. Et toi? | Fine, thanks. And you?
Léa: Ça va. Un thé, s'il te plaît? | Good. A tea, please?
Samir: Voilà. | Here you go.
Léa: Merci. Salut! | Thanks. Bye!
```

## Try it

1. Choose *tu* or *vous* for each person: a bus driver you do not know, your friend Samir, your professor, a group of three classmates.
2. Say "please" to Samir, and then to the bus driver.
3. Which phrase says "good day" when you leave?
4. You bump someone in a crowd. Which do you say, *excusez-moi* or *pardon*?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. The bus driver: *vous*. Samir: *tu*. The professor: *vous*. The three classmates: *vous*, because the word is also the plural.
2. To Samir: *s'il te plaît*. To the bus driver: *s'il vous plaît*.
3. *Bonne journée*.
4. Both are polite. *Pardon* is the quick word for a small bump, and *excusez-moi* is the usual way to get someone's attention.

</details>

```fr
Bonjour, madame. Comment allez-vous? | Hello, ma'am. How are you?

```

## Common mistakes

Using *tu* with a stranger or a person in authority. *Vous* is the safe choice.

Saying *bonjour* with stress on the first syllable. Give both syllables the same weight and lift on the end.

Using the wrong form of "please", such as *s'il vous plaît* to a close friend or *s'il te plaît* to a stranger. Match *vous* or *te* to the person.

## Key terms

- **Vous**: the polite word for "you", and the word for "you" when you speak to more than one person.
- **Tu**: the familiar word for "you", used with friends, family and children.
- **Register**: how formal or casual your language is, such as polite *vous* or casual *tu*.
