# The alphabet and spelling your name

<span class="tag tag-words">Words</span> <span class="tag tag-a1">A1</span>

After this lesson you can say the French names of the letters, name the accents, and spell your name and an email address aloud.

Before this: [Typing accents on your keyboard](02-typing-accents.md), [Nasal vowels](06-nasal-vowels.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

The French alphabet has the same 26 letters as English, but the names of the letters are different. You need them every time you spell a name on the phone, at a hotel desk or in an email address. Most of the names use vowels you have already practised, so this lesson is also a review of /e/, /ɛ/, /i/ and /y/.

### The letter names

| Letter | Name | Letter | Name | Letter | Name |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | /a/ | J | /ʒi/ | S | /ɛs/ |
| B | /be/ | K | /ka/ | T | /te/ |
| C | /se/ | L | /ɛl/ | U | /y/ |
| D | /de/ | M | /ɛm/ | V | /ve/ |
| E | /ə/ | N | /ɛn/ | W | /dubləve/ |
| F | /ɛf/ | O | /o/ | X | /iks/ |
| G | /ʒe/ | P | /pe/ | Y | /igʁɛk/ |
| H | /aʃ/ | Q | /ky/ | Z | /zɛd/ |
| I | /i/ | R | /ɛʁ/ | | |

Four names trip up English speakers. *E* is /ə/, the weak vowel of *le*. *G* is /ʒe/, close to the English name of *J*, and *J* is /ʒi/, close to the English name of *G*. *I* is /i/ and *U* is /y/, so the letter *U* uses the rounded vowel of *tu*. *W* is said "double v" and *Y* is said "i grec", which means "Greek i". Letters with accents are named by the letter and then the accent, which you will practise below.

## Hear it

Listen for the vowel at the end of each name. Most names have a clear /e/, /ɛ/ or /i/, and the last syllable of the string is the one that rises.

```fr
A, B, C, D, E, F, G | A, B, C, D, E, F, G
H, I, J, K, L, M, N | H, I, J, K, L, M, N
O, P, Q, R, S, T, U | O, P, Q, R, S, T, U
V, W, X, Y, Z | V, W, X, Y, Z
```

### The accents, by name

You met the five accents in the first lesson. To say an accented letter, say the letter and then the accent: *é* is "e accent aigu", *è* is "e accent grave", *ê* is "e accent circonflexe", *ë* is "e tréma" and *ç* is "c cédille".

```fr
E accent aigu. | E acute accent.
E accent grave. | E grave accent.
E accent circonflexe. | E circumflex accent.
E tréma. | E diaeresis.
C cédille. | C cedilla.
```

### Spelling your name

A **surname** is a family name. To spell it, say each letter, and say *deux* before a doubled letter: *Dubois* is D, U, B, O, I, S, and a name with two *l* is said with *deux l*. Listen to the pace: one letter per beat, with a short pause between groups of three or four. The question *Ça s'écrit comment?* means "How is that written?"

```fr
Employée: Votre nom, s'il vous plaît? | Your surname, please?
Vous: Dubois. | Dubois.
Employée: Ça s'écrit comment? | How is that spelled?
Vous: D, U, B, O, I, S. | D, U, B, O, I, S.
Employée: Merci. | Thank you.
```

### Spelling an email address

An email address has symbols you need names for. The OQLF (the Quebec language office) names them on its page about [saying web and email addresses](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/23538/la-prononciation/prononciation-de-mots-particuliers/prononciation-des-adresses-web-et-des-adresses-de-courriel).

| Symbol | Say | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| @ | *arobase* | The OQLF lists *a commercial*, *arobas* and *arobase*. This book uses *arobase* |
| . | *point* | The OQLF advises against the English word "dot" |
| - | *trait d'union* | The name the OQLF page uses for the hyphen. The page does not use *tiret* (dash) |
| _ | *trait de soulignement* | Also *soulignement* |

For *marie.dubois@exemple.ca* you say: *Marie point Dubois arobase exemple point c, a*. You say the word parts as words and spell only what is not a word.

```fr
Marie point Dubois arobase exemple point c, a | marie.dubois@exemple.ca
```

## Try it

1. Spell *Léa* aloud, and name the accent.
2. Spell *Marc* and *Nadia* aloud, then record yourself. Check that the *I* in *Nadia* is a bright /i/.
3. Read this address aloud: *paul.tremblay@exemple.ca*.
4. How do you say the accent in *école* in French?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. L, E accent aigu, A. The accent is the *accent aigu*.
2. *Marc*: M, A, R, C. *Nadia*: N, A, D, I, A. The *I* is /i/, a bright spread vowel.
3. *Paul point Tremblay arobase exemple point c, a*.
4. *Accent aigu*, on the *é*. The letter is "e accent aigu".

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Je m'appelle Marie. | My name is Marie.
M, A, R, I, E. | M, A, R, I, E.
Ça s'écrit comment? | How is that spelled?
Marie point Dubois arobase exemple point c, a | marie.dubois@exemple.ca
```

## Common mistakes

Saying English letter names. French *E* is /ə/, *I* is /i/ and *U* is /y/, so *U* has the rounded vowel of *tu*.

Mixing up *G* /ʒe/ and *J* /ʒi/. The first vowel is /e/ and the second is /i/.

Saying English words such as "dot" in an address. The OQLF advises against them and gives *point* and *trait de soulignement*.

## In Quebec

The OQLF names *a commercial*, *arobas* and *arobase* for @, and the book uses *arobase*. If someone says one of the others, it means the same sign. The OQLF advises *point* and not the English words "dot" and "slash" when you say an address in French.

## Key terms

- **Surname**: a family name, the last part of someone's name.
- **Letter name**: the word you say for a letter when you spell, such as /ʒe/ for *G*.
