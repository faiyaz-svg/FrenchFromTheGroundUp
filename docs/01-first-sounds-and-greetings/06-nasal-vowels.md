# Nasal vowels

<span class="tag tag-sounds">Sounds</span> <span class="tag tag-a1">A1</span>

After this lesson you can say the nasal vowels /ɑ̃/, /ɛ̃/ and /ɔ̃/, hear the difference between *bon*, *banc* and *bain*, and know when an *n* or *m* makes a vowel nasal.

Before this: [The vowels o and eu](05-vowels-o-and-eu.md). You need: your fingers, to feel your nose.

## The idea

All the vowels so far are **oral vowels**: the air leaves through the mouth only. A **nasal vowel** is a vowel where air also leaves through the nose. Your soft palate (the soft part at the back of the roof of your mouth) drops, and the vowel sounds as if you have a cold. English has no true nasal vowels, so this is a new move.

The most important rule is about the letter *n*. In a nasal vowel the *n* or *m* is a mark, not a sound. In *bon* /bɔ̃/ (good) you do not say an /n/ at the end: your tongue never touches the roof of your mouth. If you hear a clear /n/, you have said the English "bon", not the French one.

### The four nasal vowels

| Symbol | Spellings | Examples | Built from |
|---|---|---|---|
| /ɑ̃/ | an, am, en, em | *blanc* /blɑ̃/ (white), *dans* /dɑ̃/ (in), *grand* /gʁɑ̃/ | /a/ with a dark sound, lips relaxed |
| /ɛ̃/ | in, im, ain, ein, ien | *vin* /vɛ̃/ (wine), *pain* /pɛ̃/ (bread), *bien* /bjɛ̃/ (well), *matin* /matɛ̃/ (morning) | /ɛ/ |
| /ɔ̃/ | on, om | *bon* /bɔ̃/ (good), *non* /nɔ̃/ (no), *maison* /mɛzɔ̃/ (house) | /ɔ/ with rounded lips |
| /œ̃/ | un, um | *brun* /bʁœ̃/ (brown), *un* /œ̃/ (a, one) | /œ/ |

The mark above the vowel in IPA, the tilde, means "nasal". The chart shows all four in the box at the right of the [vowel chart](../assets/figures/01-first-sounds-and-greetings/vowel-chart.svg). /ɑ̃/ is the vowel in *blanc*, and it does not sound like the oral vowel /a/, so do not say /a/ plus a nose sound.

### How to make a nasal vowel

1. Say the oral vowel, for example /ɔ/ as in *bol*.
2. Hold your nose lightly. Say it again and feel nothing.
3. Lower your soft palate, as when you start to say "ng" in "sing", and keep your tongue off the roof of your mouth.
4. Say the vowel again and feel the buzz in your nose. This is /ɔ̃/. The word *bon* is /b/ plus this vowel, and nothing after it.

Do the same with /ɛ/ for /ɛ̃/ and with the dark /ɑ/ for /ɑ̃/.

### When a vowel stays oral

The vowel is only nasal when *n* or *m* ends the syllable. When *n* is followed by a vowel, or doubled, the *n* is pronounced and the vowel is oral. Compare *bon* /bɔ̃/ with *bonne* /bɔn/ (good, feminine). The first has a nasal vowel and no /n/. The second has an oral vowel and a clear /n/ at the end.

## Hear it

Listen for the nose. *Bon*, *banc* and *bain* are the classic three-way pair. They differ by the nasal vowel only, and the lips are the clue: rounded for *bon*, relaxed for *banc* and *bain*.

```fr
Bon. Banc. Bain. | Good. Bench. Bath.
Un pain blanc. | A white loaf.
Un bon vin. | A good wine.
Le matin, bien. | The morning, well.
Bon. Bonne. | Good (masculine). Good (feminine).
```

Play *bon*, *banc* and *bain* three times each. Then say them with your fingers on your nose. You should feel the buzz on each nasal vowel and no extra /n/ after it.

## Try it

1. Which nasal vowel is in *pain*, *blanc*, *bon*, *brun*? Name it as /ɛ̃/, /ɑ̃/, /ɔ̃/ or /œ̃/.
2. Does *bonne* have a nasal vowel? How can you tell?
3. Say *bon* and *banc*. What part of your mouth changes?
4. Say *vin blanc* three times, with no /n/ at the end of either word.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Pain*: /ɛ̃/. *Blanc*: /ɑ̃/. *Bon*: /ɔ̃/. *Brun*: /œ̃/.
2. No. The *n* is doubled and followed by *e*, so you pronounce the *n* and the vowel is oral: /bɔn/.
3. The lips: rounded for *bon*, relaxed for *banc*. The tongue also moves.
4. Your check: the vowel buzzes in your nose and your tongue never touches the roof of your mouth.

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Un pain blanc et un bon vin. | A white loaf and a good wine.
Bien, merci, bonjour. | Fine, thank you, hello.
Le matin, le bain est bon. | In the morning, the bath is good.
```

## Common mistakes

Adding an /n/ or /m/ after the vowel, as in English "bon" or "pan". The tongue stays off the roof of the mouth.

Mixing the spellings: *an* and *en* both say /ɑ̃/, and *in* and *ain* both say /ɛ̃/. Learn the sound first, then the spellings.

Making the vowel nasal when *n* is doubled or followed by a vowel. Say *bonne* with a clear /n/.

## In Quebec

The chart shows a fourth nasal vowel, /œ̃/, in *brun* and *un*. The contrast between /ɛ̃/ and /œ̃/ (for example /bʁɛ̃/ against /bʁœ̃/) is described as fading in standard French, in favour of /ɛ̃/ ([SFU French linguistics course notes](https://www.sfu.ca/fren270/Phonologie/page4_6.html)). Quebec French is described as keeping the two vowels apart. In Montreal you will hear /œ̃/ in *un* and *brun* more often than in Paris. Both pronunciations are understood everywhere, so say whichever comes naturally.

## Key terms

- **Oral vowel**: a vowel where the air leaves through the mouth only.
- **Nasal vowel**: a vowel where air also leaves through the nose. French writes it with a vowel plus *n* or *m*, and the *n* or *m* is not pronounced as a consonant.
