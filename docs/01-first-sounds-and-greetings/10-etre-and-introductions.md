# Être and introducing yourself

<span class="tag tag-grammar">Grammar</span> <span class="tag tag-a1">A1</span>

After this lesson you can say your name, where you are from and your nationality, use all six forms of the verb *être* with their subject pronouns, and answer *Et vous?*.

Before this: [Greetings and politeness](08-greetings-and-politeness.md), [Nasal vowels](06-nasal-vowels.md). You need: nothing.

## The idea

You can already say hello. To say who you are, you need a verb. A **verb** is the word that says what someone is or does. *Être* /ɛtʁ/ means "to be", and it is the most used verb in French. The **subject** of a sentence is the person or thing the verb is about, and a **pronoun** is a short word that stands for a person, such as "I" or "she". In "I am Marc", "I" is the subject and a pronoun, and "am" is the verb.

A verb changes its ending with the subject, and to **conjugate** a verb means to give its forms for each subject. English does this too ("I am", "you are", "she is"). *Être* has six forms, and they do not follow a pattern, so you learn them as a set.

### The subject pronouns

| Pronoun | Meaning | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| *je* | I | yourself |
| *tu* | you | one person, informal (lesson 8) |
| *il* | he, it | a man, or a masculine thing |
| *elle* | she, it | a woman, or a feminine thing |
| *nous* | we | you and others |
| *vous* | you | one person, polite, or several people |
| *ils* | they | several men, or a mixed group |
| *elles* | they | several women |

There is a ninth word, *on*, which in speech often means "we" and takes the same verb form as *il* and *elle*. You will meet it fully in [Regular -er verbs](../03-verbs-and-questions/02-regular-er-verbs.md). For now you only need the eight above.

### The present of être

| Pronoun | Form | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| *je* | *suis* | /ʒə sɥi/ |
| *tu* | *es* | /ty ɛ/ |
| *il / elle* | *est* | /il ɛ/, /ɛl ɛ/ |
| *nous* | *sommes* | /nu sɔm/ |
| *vous* | *êtes* | /vuz ɛt/ |
| *ils / elles* | *sont* | /il sɔ̃/, /ɛl sɔ̃/ |

Listen for what is silent. In *tu es* and *il est*, the final *s* and *t* are silent, so you hear only /ɛ/. In *vous êtes*, a /z/ links *vous* to *êtes*, and [Spelling rules and liaison](../02-sounds-spelling-and-everyday-things/03-spelling-sound-rules-and-liaison.md) explains that link. *Ils sont* ends in the nasal vowel /ɔ̃/ from lesson 6, with no /n/ and no /t/.

### Introducing yourself

*Je m'appelle Marc* /ʒə mapɛl maʁk/ means "My name is Marc". Learn it as a chunk, a phrase you say whole, for now. A later stage explains the pieces. After your name, you say where you are from with *je suis de* and a place: *Je suis de Toronto*.

For your nationality, you use *je suis* and an adjective, a word that describes a person or thing. The adjective changes for a man and a woman. A man says *je suis canadien* /kanadjɛ̃/ and a woman says *je suis canadienne* /kanadjɛn/. The feminine adds *-ne*, and the nasal vowel of *canadien* becomes oral vowel plus a clear /n/, as in *bon* and *bonne*. Other pairs add an *-e* too: *québécois* and *québécoise*, *français* and *française*. In *française* /fʁɑ̃sɛz/ the *-e* also brings back a /z/ sound that *français* /fʁɑ̃sɛ/ does not have. This is a pattern to hear now. The full rule comes in Adjectives and agreement<!-- link: ../04-people-places-and-directions/01-adjectives-agreement.md -->.

*Enchanté* and *enchantée* both mean "nice to meet you", and they sound the same. A man writes *enchanté* and a woman writes *enchantée*. To return a question, say *Et vous?* (and you?) with a stranger and *Et toi?* (and you?) with a friend.

## Hear it

Listen for the lift at the end of each question and for the silent final letters in *tu es* and *il est*. The first dialogue uses *vous*, and the second uses *tu*.

```fr
Marc: Bonjour, je m'appelle Marc. Et vous? | Hello, my name is Marc. And you?
Léa: Je m'appelle Léa. Enchantée. | My name is Léa. Nice to meet you.
Marc: Enchanté. Vous êtes de Montréal? | Nice to meet you. Are you from Montreal?
Léa: Non, je suis de Toronto. Et vous? | No, I'm from Toronto. And you?
Marc: Je suis de Gatineau. Je suis canadien. | I'm from Gatineau. I'm Canadian.

Samir: Salut, je m'appelle Samir. Et toi? | Hi, my name is Samir. And you?
Nadia: Salut, je m'appelle Nadia. Tu es de Québec? | Hi, my name is Nadia. Are you from Quebec City?
Samir: Oui, je suis québécois. Et toi? | Yes, I'm from Quebec. And you?
Nadia: Je suis canadienne, de Toronto. | I'm Canadian, from Toronto.
```

## Try it

1. Fill in the form of *être*: *Nous ___ à Montréal.* *Elles ___ canadiennes.* *Tu ___ de Toronto.*
2. Say your own name, your city and your nationality in three sentences.
3. A woman says "nice to meet you" to a man she meets for the first time. What does she write?
4. What is the subject pronoun in *Ils sont de Gatineau*, and what does it stand for?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. *Nous sommes à Montréal.* *Elles sont canadiennes.* *Tu es de Toronto.*
2. Example: *Je m'appelle Léa. Je suis de Toronto. Je suis canadienne.* Use *canadien* for a man.
3. *Enchantée.* The ending matches the speaker, not the person she is meeting.
4. *Ils*, which stands for several men or a mixed group.

</details>

## Say it

```fr
Je m'appelle Marc. | My name is Marc.
Je suis de Toronto. | I'm from Toronto.
Tu es de Montréal? | Are you from Montreal?
Nous sommes canadiens. | We are Canadian.
Enchanté. Et vous? | Nice to meet you. And you?
```

## Common mistakes

Writing *je es* or *tu est*. Each pronoun has its own form, so learn the six as a set.

Saying the final *s* of *tu es* or the *t* of *il est*. Both are silent.

Using the masculine adjective for a woman. A woman says *je suis canadienne*, with the clear /n/.

## In Quebec

A person from Quebec is called *québécois* (man) or *québécoise* (woman). In casual speech the spoken forms of *être* get shorter, and [Casual questions in Quebec](../03-verbs-and-questions/10-quebec-casual-questions.md) shows how.

## Key terms

- **Verb**: a word that says what someone is or does.
- **Subject**: the person or thing a verb is about.
- **Pronoun**: a short word that stands for a person or thing, such as *je* or *elle*.
- **Conjugate**: to give the forms of a verb for each subject.
- **Adjective**: a word that describes a person or thing, such as *canadien*.
