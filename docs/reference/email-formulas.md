# Email and letter formulas

Subject lines, greetings, openings, requests, thanks, closings and four model emails, following the Office québécois de la langue française (OQLF) and its Banque de dépannage linguistique (BDL), as of 2026. The OQLF pages used here do not describe any difference with France, so none is claimed. Rules are summarised in our own words, with a link at each point of use.

## Subject lines

The OQLF page on the [subject line of an email](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/index.php?id=25514) asks for a clear, precise subject, put the most important words first, one subject per email, and a capital only on the first word, with no final period. A noun for the action (request, reminder, cancellation) plus a detail works well. A deadline can go in.

| Use | Avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *Demande de rendez-vous pour le 12 mars* | *Rendez-vous* | The first says the action and the date |
| *Annulation de la réunion du 15 mars* | *Réunion* | One word does not say what happened |
| *Rappel : paiement à faire avant le 27 septembre* | *Urgent!!!* | No alarm words, no exclamation marks |
| *Réservation d'une chambre du 12 au 14 décembre* | *RÉSERVATION* | Capitals read as shouting |

If your software adds `RE` to a reply, keep only one.

## Greetings by situation

The OQLF page on [greetings in an email](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/24944/la-redaction-et-la-communication/redaction-administrative-et-commerciale/correspondance/courriel/formules-dappel-dans-le-courriel) lists the forms below. The comma after *Bonjour* may be left out. The page advises against an exclamation mark after *Bonjour*.

| Situation | Greeting | Note |
|---|---|---|
| First contact, name unknown | *Madame, Monsieur,* | The two words go one above the other |
| First contact, name known | *Madame Villeneuve,* or *Bonjour, Madame Villeneuve.* | Write the title in full, with a capital |
| Person with a job title | *Madame la Directrice,* | |
| Known person, formal | *Bonjour, Madame.* | |
| Colleague | *Bonjour Patrick,* | A first name alone is for contacts you know |
| Warm tone or a close colleague | *Chère Sabrina,* or *Chères collègues,* | Only for someone you know very well |
| Title or gender unknown | *Bonjour,* or *Bonjour Alex Tremblay,* | Neutral. Leave the title out of the closing too |
| Friend | *Salut Julie,* | Casual. This form is not on the OQLF page |

From most formal to most familiar, the page gives this order: *Madame,* then *Bonjour, Madame.* then *Bonjour, Céline.* then *Chère Céline,*. In a same-day exchange about one file, the greeting can be dropped, but the page advises against dropping it every time. A first name or *Rebonjour* works after the first message.

## Opening sentences and requests

The OQLF page on the [body of an email](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/index.php?id=24945) says to open with a short context (the date of the last exchange) and to state the action you want in the first paragraph. Keep one subject per message, one idea per paragraph, and short sentences.

```fr
Je vous écris au sujet de notre rencontre du 3 mars. | I am writing about our meeting of March 3.
Je vous contacte au sujet de ma réservation. | I am contacting you about my booking.
Suite à votre message du 5 mars, je vous confirme ce qui suit. | Following your message of March 5, I confirm the following.

Pourriez-vous me faire parvenir le rapport d'ici le 15 mars? | Could you send me the report by March 15?
Pourriez-vous me confirmer la date de la réunion? | Could you confirm the date of the meeting?
Je voudrais savoir si une chambre est libre le 12 décembre. | I would like to know if a room is free on December 12.
Je vous demande de bien vouloir corriger la facture. | I ask you to please correct the invoice.
```

For a link, write words that name the destination, not *cliquez ici*, and keep a short answer in the text itself. Emoticons (*binettes*) suit personal emails only.

## Thanks

```fr
Merci de votre message. | Thank you for your message.
Merci de votre réponse rapide. | Thank you for your quick reply.
Je vous remercie pour votre aide. | Thank you for your help.
Merci d'avance de votre collaboration. | Thank you in advance for your cooperation.
Merci beaucoup, c'est très gentil. | Thank you very much, that is very kind.
```

The last line is for a friend or a familiar colleague.

## Closings by register

The OQLF page on [closings in an email](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/index.php?id=24946) says email closings are simpler than letter closings, that a short one takes a comma, and that a long letter closing is fine for a first contact or an official message. The page on [closings in a letter](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/index.php?id=22101) adds the rules for long forms.

| Register | Closing | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Short, everyday work | *Merci et bonne journée,* | Quick thanks and goodbye |
| Short, everyday work | *Bonne journée,* | Routine message |
| Short, everyday work | *Salutations,* or *Meilleures salutations,* | Neutral, any colleague or client |
| Short, respectful | *Salutations distinguées,* or *Respectueusement,* | A manager or a person in authority |
| Short, personal | *Cordialement,* | Warm and fine in work emails as well |
| Short, personal | *Amicalement,* or *Amitiés,* | Friends and close colleagues |
| Long, formal | *Je vous prie d'agréer, Madame, mes salutations distinguées.* | First contact, official message |
| Long, formal | *Je vous prie de croire, Madame la Directrice, à l'expression de ma considération.* | A person in authority |
| Long, formal | *Je vous prie de recevoir, cher collègue, mes plus cordiales salutations.* | A colleague at the same level |

Three rules from the letter page:

- Repeat the greeting word in the long closing (*Madame*, *Madame la Directrice*) between commas. If you opened with only *Bonjour,*, leave the title out of the closing.
- The BDL says not to put *expression de* or *assurance de* before *salutations*. Write *agréer mes salutations distinguées*, not *l'expression de mes salutations distinguées*, a form you will still see in many letters.
- Avoid *sentiments* in work messages. Use *salutations*, *considération* or *respect*. Closings with *hommage* are dated.

## Signature block

The email page says to end with the closing, then your name and contact details.

```fr
Cordialement,

Prénom Nom
Titre ou fonction
Organisation
514 555-0100
```

The phone number is a placeholder. Use your own.

## Out-of-office reply

The BDL page on the [automatic out-of-office reply](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/index.php?id=24680) says to give the return date at the start (or say there is none), a person to contact for absences of more than a day, and to end with a short farewell and the signature block. We adapt its model wording.

```fr
Objet : Absence du bureau jusqu'au 4 janvier | Subject: Out of the office until January 4
Bonjour, | Hello,
Je suis actuellement absente du bureau jusqu'au 4 janvier. | I am currently out of the office until January 4.
Pour toute demande urgente, veuillez communiquer avec Marc Roy à marc.roy@exemple.ca ou au 514 555-0101. | For any urgent request, please contact Marc Roy at marc.roy@exemple.ca or at 514 555-0101.
Cordialement, | Best regards,
Prénom Nom | First name Last name
```

A man writes *absent*. *Actuellement* means "currently" in French (see the [false friends](false-friends.md) page).

## Four model emails

The people, dates and numbers are invented.

### Booking

Vous with a hotel you have not contacted before.

```fr
Objet : Demande de réservation du 12 au 14 décembre | Subject: Booking request for December 12 to 14
Madame, Monsieur, | Dear Sir or Madam,
Je voudrais réserver une chambre double du 12 au 14 décembre 2026. | I would like to book a double room from December 12 to 14, 2026.
Pourriez-vous me confirmer le prix et les conditions d'annulation? | Could you confirm the price and the cancellation terms?
Je vous remercie d'avance de votre réponse. | Thank you in advance for your reply.
Cordialement, | Best regards,
Prénom Nom | First name Last name
```

### Work request

A colleague you know. The ask and the date come first.

```fr
Objet : Demande du rapport trimestriel avant le 15 mars | Subject: Request for the quarterly report before March 15
Bonjour Patrick, | Hello Patrick,
Je t'écris au sujet de notre rencontre du 3 mars. | I am writing to you about our meeting of March 3.
Pourrais-tu me faire parvenir le rapport avant le 15 mars? | Could you send me the report by March 15?
J'en ai besoin pour la réunion du 18 mars. | I need it for the meeting on March 18.
Merci et bonne journée, | Thanks and have a good day,
Sophie | Sophie
```

Many Quebec workplaces use *tu* between colleagues. If you are not sure, use *vous* until the other person uses *tu*.

### Reply to a client

Vous, a named client, an answer and the next step.

```fr
Objet : Votre demande du 3 mars | Subject: Your request of March 3
Bonjour, Madame Tremblay. | Hello, Ms. Tremblay.
Merci de votre message du 3 mars. | Thank you for your message of March 3.
Votre commande sera livrée le 10 mars. | Your order will be delivered on March 10.
Si vous avez d'autres questions, n'hésitez pas à m'écrire. | If you have other questions, do not hesitate to write to me.
Meilleures salutations, | Best regards,
Prénom Nom | First name Last name
Service à la clientèle | Customer service
```

### Formal complaint

First contact with a company, long closing, one complaint, one request.

```fr
Objet : Plainte concernant la facture 12345 | Subject: Complaint about invoice 12345
Madame, Monsieur, | Dear Sir or Madam,
Je vous écris au sujet de la facture 12345 du 2 novembre 2026, qui indique un montant de 250,00 $ au lieu de 205,00 $. | I am writing about invoice 12345 of November 2, 2026, which shows 250.00 $ instead of 205.00 $.
Je vous demande de bien vouloir corriger cette erreur et de m'envoyer une nouvelle facture avant le 30 novembre. | I ask you to please correct this error and send me a new invoice by November 30.
Je vous remercie pour votre attention. | Thank you for your attention.
Je vous prie d'agréer, Madame, Monsieur, mes salutations distinguées. | Yours sincerely.
Prénom Nom | First name Last name
```
