# Quebec and France words

Everyday words that differ between Quebec and France, with the English and a register note. These are regional standard and casual usages, not errors. Every Quebec word below was checked against the free [Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/) dictionary, the OQLF Banque de dépannage linguistique or the OQLF terminology bank in November 2026. Words I could not confirm are left out.

The book teaches the neutral form first. A word with no label is neutral and fine in speech and writing. Casual words are marked, and the casual lines in the examples carry the casual tag.

## Meal names

<div class="warning" markdown>

The three meal names shift between Quebec and France. Check the hour before you accept an invitation.

| Meal | Quebec | France |
|---|---|---|
| Breakfast | *le déjeuner* | *le petit-déjeuner* |
| The midday meal | *le dîner* | *le déjeuner* |
| The evening meal | *le souper* | *le dîner* |

Usito defines *déjeuner* as the morning meal, *dîner* as the midday meal and *souper* as the evening meal, which is the Quebec system. The France column is the usage you will meet in France and in most textbooks. If someone invites you to *dîner*, ask *À quelle heure?* (At what time?).

</div>

| Quebec | France | English | Register | Source |

| *bienvenue* (after *merci*) | *de rien*, *je vous en prie* | you're welcome | casual | [Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/définitions/bienvenue) |

Notes on rows that need a word of care:

- *Magasiner* and *faire les magasins* both mean to go round the shops. Usito also lists *faire des courses* for the same sense. France uses *faire du shopping* freely.
- *Courriel* began in Quebec. Usito says it has an official recommendation in France too, so you may see it there, but *e-mail* and *mail* are what people say.
- *Stationnement* also exists in France, but *parking* is more common there. *Dépanneur* in France means a repairer, so *dépanneur* for a shop is Quebec only.
- *Présentement* was long thought typical of Quebec, but Usito notes it is found often in the press in France, Belgium and Switzerland. Use *actuellement* if you want a form that goes everywhere.
- *Tantôt* in Quebec means a while ago or in a while on the same day. In France it was used for "this afternoon" and is old-fashioned.
- *Char*, *chum* and *bienvenue* as a reply to *merci* are marked by Usito as criticized in careful standard French, because they come from English. They are very common in everyday Quebec speech. Understand them, and use the standard form in writing and at work: *voiture*, *petit ami*, *de rien*.
- *Chandail* also means a knitted sweater, a sense found in France as well. The sports jersey sense is Quebec and Canada.
- *Piasse* is a casual word for a dollar. In a shop, say *dollars*.

```fr
On se voit la fin de semaine prochaine? | Shall we meet next weekend?
J'aime magasiner au centre-ville. | I like to shop downtown.
Je t'envoie un courriel ce soir. | I will send you an email tonight.
Il y a un dépanneur au coin de la rue. | There is a convenience store at the corner of the street.
Mon cellulaire est sur la table. | My phone is on the table.
Le stationnement est plein. | The parking lot is full.
Nous prenons le traversier pour aller à Lévis. | We take the ferry to Lévis.
Mets ta tuque, il fait très froid. | Put on your hat, it is very cold.
Je ne suis pas disponible présentement. | I am not available at the moment.
Je t'ai vu sortir tantôt. | I saw you go out a while ago.
À tantôt! | See you later!

Marc: On prend mon char? {qc} | Shall we take my car?
Marc: Mon chum arrive à six heures. {qc} | My boyfriend arrives at six.
Marc: Ça coûte dix piasses. {qc} | That costs ten dollars.
Marc: Je suis icitte depuis une heure. {qc} | I have been here for an hour.
Vous: Merci beaucoup. | Thank you very much.
Serveur: Bienvenue! {qc} | You're welcome!
Serveur: Je vous en prie. | You're welcome.
```
