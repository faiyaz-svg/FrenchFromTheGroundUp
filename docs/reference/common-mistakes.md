## Stage 1: First sounds and greetings

### [How French spelling, sounds and rhythm work](../01-first-sounds-and-greetings/01-french-sound-map.md)

Stressing the first syllable, as English does. French gives every syllable about the same weight and lifts on the last one.

Reading every letter. In *petit* the final *t* is silent, so you say /pəti/.

Treating an accent as optional. *Thé* without its accent is a spelling error, and the drills mark it "almost".

### [Typing accents on your keyboard](../01-first-sounds-and-greetings/02-typing-accents.md)

Pressing a dead key and the letter at the same moment. A dead key waits, so press it first, release it, and then press the letter.

Choosing the wrong accent. *É* says /e/ and *è* says /ɛ/. Say the word first, then choose.

Typing *oe* for *œ*. The ligature is a single letter, and *coeur* is a spelling error.

### [The vowels a, i, ou and u](../01-first-sounds-and-greetings/03-vowels-a-i-ou-u.md)

Saying /u/ for *u*. In *tu* the tongue stays forward. Start from /i/ and round your lips.

Gliding at the end of /i/ and /u/ as English does ("see" becomes "see-y"). Hold the vowel still.

Spelling /u/ with a single *u*. The sound /u/ is written *ou*, as in *vous*.

### [The e vowels](../01-first-sounds-and-greetings/04-vowels-e-family.md)

Gliding on /e/, so that *thé* ends in an English "ay" sound. Hold the vowel still.

Saying the final *e* in a word like *rue* or *petite*. A final *e* is usually silent.

Reading *ai* as two vowels. In *lait* the spelling *ai* is one vowel, /ɛ/.

### [The vowels o and eu](../01-first-sounds-and-greetings/05-vowels-o-and-eu.md)

Spreading the lips on *deux* and *sœur*. These vowels need rounded lips, so say /e/ and /ɛ/ first and then round.

Reading *eau* as three sounds. It says one vowel, /o/.

Sliding the vowel at the end of *beau*, as English "no" ends. Hold /o/ still.

### [Nasal vowels](../01-first-sounds-and-greetings/06-nasal-vowels.md)

Adding an /n/ or /m/ after the vowel, as in English "bon" or "pan". The tongue stays off the roof of the mouth.

Mixing the spellings: *an* and *en* both say /ɑ̃/, and *in* and *ain* both say /ɛ̃/. Learn the sound first, then the spellings.

Making the vowel nasal when *n* is doubled or followed by a vowel. Say *bonne* with a clear /n/.

### [The alphabet and spelling your name](../01-first-sounds-and-greetings/07-alphabet-and-spelling-names.md)

Saying English letter names. French *E* is /ə/, *I* is /i/ and *U* is /y/, so *U* has the rounded vowel of *tu*.

Mixing up *G* /ʒe/ and *J* /ʒi/. The first vowel is /e/ and the second is /i/.

Saying English words such as "dot" in an address. The OQLF advises against them and gives *point* and *trait de soulignement*.

### [Greetings and politeness](../01-first-sounds-and-greetings/08-greetings-and-politeness.md)

Using *tu* with a stranger or a person in authority. *Vous* is the safe choice.

Saying *bonjour* with stress on the first syllable. Give both syllables the same weight and lift on the end.

Using the wrong form of "please", such as *s'il vous plaît* to a close friend or *s'il te plaît* to a stranger. Match *vous* or *te* to the person.

### [Bonjour-hi and other Montreal greetings](../01-first-sounds-and-greetings/09-bonjour-hi-montreal-greetings.md)

Taking "Bonjour, hi" as a sign that your French is not good enough. It is a common shop greeting that offers a choice.

Using *bienvenue* to answer *merci* when you speak. Use *de rien*, *je vous en prie* or *il n'y a pas de quoi* instead, and recognise *bienvenue*.

Saying *je vous en prie* to a friend. It is formal, so use *de rien* with friends.

### [Être and introducing yourself](../01-first-sounds-and-greetings/10-etre-and-introductions.md)

Writing *je es* or *tu est*. Each pronoun has its own form, so learn the six as a set.

Saying the final *s* of *tu es* or the *t* of *il est*. Both are silent.

Using the masculine adjective for a woman. A woman says *je suis canadienne*, with the clear /n/.

### [Your first slow dialogues](../01-first-sounds-and-greetings/11-first-slow-dialogues.md)

Reading the translation first. Keep it hidden, listen twice, and check at the end.

Trying to catch every word. Listen for names, places and greetings, and let the rest pass.

Giving up after one listen. A first listen is for the shape of the conversation, and the second and third are for the details.

### [Meet someone in Montreal](../01-first-sounds-and-greetings/12-meet-someone-in-montreal.md)

Changing *tu es* to *vous êtes* but leaving *Et toi?* in the same conversation. Change every *tu* word together.

Stopping when you do not know a word. Use *Pardon?* or repeat the last word with a rising voice, and keep talking.

Reading the dialogue instead of saying it. Cover the lines, say them, and look only to check.


## Stage 2: Sounds, spelling and everyday things

### [French consonants](../02-sounds-spelling-and-everyday-things/01-consonants.md)

Puffing air on /p/, /t/ and /k/, as in English "pot". Relax and use less air: the paper should hardly move.

Saying *th* as in English "think" in *thé*. The letters *th* are /t/ in French.

Pronouncing final consonants in *petit*, *trop*, *grand* and *chez*. Learn the CaReFuL letters, and treat the rest as silent unless a word is an exception.

### [The French r and the semi-vowels](../02-sounds-spelling-and-everyday-things/02-r-and-semi-vowels.md)

Saying the English "r" with the tongue curled back or the lips rounded. French /ʁ/ is made at the back of the mouth with the tongue tip down.

Rolling the *r* with the tip of the tongue. That sound is used in some places, but a rolled /r/ is not the standard sound, so say /ʁ/.

Saying *huit* as "weet". The lips are tight and rounded as for /y/.

### [Spelling rules and liaison](../02-sounds-spelling-and-everyday-things/03-spelling-sound-rules-and-liaison.md)

Linking after *et*, as in "ay-t un". *Et* is always followed by a break: *un garçon et un homme*.

Treating every h the same. An h muet allows elision and liaison (*l'homme*, *les hommes*), and an h aspiré blocks both (*le héros*).

Linking everything, in an effort to sound French. Required links are enough at this level, and the optional ones are often skipped in real speech.

### [Gender and articles](../02-sounds-spelling-and-everyday-things/04-gender-and-articles.md)

Learning a noun without its article, then guessing later. Learn *une table* as one unit.

Treating the endings as laws. *La peur* is feminine even though it ends in *-eur*, and *un problème* is masculine even though it ends in *-e*.

Thinking that the gender of a thing tells you anything about it. A table is feminine in French and a desk (*un bureau*) is masculine, and this is only a label.

### [Plurals, des and les](../02-sounds-spelling-and-everyday-things/05-plurals-and-des-les.md)

Listening for a final *s* on the noun. It is silent, so listen to the article: *les* and *des* are the plural markers.

Leaving out *des* where English leaves out "some". *Voilà des livres*, not *voilà livres*.

Adding *-s* to *-al* nouns: *des journals*. Write *des journaux*.

### [Numbers from 0 to 100](../02-sounds-spelling-and-everyday-things/06-numbers-0-100.md)

Dropping the *et* in 21, 31, 41, 51, 61 and 71, as in *vingt-un*. Write *vingt et un* and *soixante et onze*.

Writing *quatre-vingts-trois* with an *s* before another number. Write *quatre-vingt-trois*.

Pronouncing the *x* of *six* and *dix* as /s/ before a consonant. Say *six chats* /si ʃa/.

### [Avoir and il y a](../02-sounds-spelling-and-everyday-things/07-avoir-and-il-y-a.md)

Saying *je suis vingt ans* or *je suis faim*. Age and feelings use *avoir*: *j'ai vingt ans*, *j'ai faim*.

Leaving out *ans* when you give your age. English can say "I'm twenty", but French says *j'ai vingt ans*.

Writing *il a* as *il à* or the other way around. They sound the same, so check what the sentence needs: a verb (*il a*) or a small word meaning "to" (*à*).

### [Ordering at a café](../02-sounds-spelling-and-everyday-things/08-ordering-at-a-cafe.md)

Saying *je veux* to order. It sounds blunt at a counter, so say *je voudrais*.

Putting the cents before the dollars. Say *quatre dollars cinquante*, with the cents last.

Leaving out *s'il vous plaît*. A request without it sounds rude, so add it at the end of the order.

### [Quebec words: dépanneur and café](../02-sounds-spelling-and-everyday-things/09-quebec-dep-and-cafe-words.md)

Taking *dîner* to mean the evening meal in Quebec. It is the midday meal, and the evening meal is *souper*.

Writing *piasse* in an email to a stranger. It is casual, so write *dollar* in writing.

Thinking *liqueur* is alcoholic everywhere. In Quebec it is also a soft drink, and in France it is a sweet alcoholic drink. Listen to the context.

### [Hearing numbers and prices](../02-sounds-spelling-and-everyday-things/10-hearing-numbers-and-prices.md)

Writing the price the English way, $4.95. In French writing, put the sign after the number, with a space, and use a comma: 4,95 $.

Hearing *quatre-vingt-quinze* as 80 plus 5. It is 4 times 20 plus 15, which is 95.

Mixing up *soixante* with *cinquante* in a fast price. Listen for the vowel: /swa/ against /sɛ̃/.

### [Filling in forms](../02-sounds-spelling-and-everyday-things/11-filling-in-forms.md)

Mixing up *nom* and *prénom*. *Nom* is your family name and *prénom* is your first name.

Writing the date in the English order, 03/14/1998. Read the hint under the box first, and give the day before the month, or use AAAA-MM-JJ.

Using # for an apartment number. The OQLF advises against it, because # does not mean "number" in French. Write *app. 4*.

### [A morning at the café](../02-sounds-spelling-and-everyday-things/12-a-morning-at-the-cafe.md)

Dropping the article in a list. Write *Il y a des tables et des chaises*, not *Il y a tables et chaises*.

Skipping *il y a* and writing *c'est douze tables*. Use *il y a* to say what a place has.

Writing a menu price as $3.00. Write 3,00 $.


## Stage 3: Verbs and questions

### [Rhythm and intonation](../03-verbs-and-questions/01-rhythm-and-intonation.md)

Stressing a syllable in the middle of a group, as English does. Give every syllable the same beat and lift only at the end.

Adding a vowel after a silent final *e*, so *chaise* becomes "chaise-uh". The *e* is silent and the word ends on the consonant.

Making a question-word question rise. A question that starts with *où*, *quand* or *comment* falls.

### [Regular -er verbs](../03-verbs-and-questions/02-regular-er-verbs.md)

Saying the final *-ent* of *ils parlent*. The ending is silent, and the verb sounds like *il parle*.

Leaving out the article after *aimer*. French says *J'aime le café*, not "J'aime café".

Writing "mangons" for *nous*. Verbs in *-ger* need the *e*: *nous mangeons*.

### [Saying no: negation](../03-verbs-and-questions/03-negation.md)

Writing *pas un café* for "no coffee". After a negative, *un*, *une* and *des* become *de*: *Je n'ai pas de café.*

Leaving out *n'* before a vowel in writing. In speech you may hear no *ne* at all, but in writing you use *je n'habite pas*.

Using *oui* to answer a negative question when you mean "yes, I do". Use *si*.

### [Asking questions](../03-verbs-and-questions/04-asking-questions.md)

Mixing two ways in one question, such as "Est-ce que habitez-vous". Pick one way and keep it.

Using *quel* with the wrong form. It agrees with the noun that comes after it, so a feminine noun takes *quelle*.

Using *parce que* for "why". *Pourquoi* asks the question and *parce que* gives the answer.

### [Aller and faire](../03-verbs-and-questions/05-aller-and-faire.md)

Writing *à le parc* or *à les musées*. These become *au parc* and *aux musées*. *À la* and *à l'* do not change.

Saying *nous faisons* with /ɛ/. The *ai* is /ə/: "fuh-zon".

Using *en* with *pied* or *vélo*. Use *à pied* and *à vélo*, and keep *en* for things you sit inside, such as *en voiture*.

### [Days, dates and big numbers](../03-verbs-and-questions/06-days-dates-and-big-numbers.md)

Capitalising days and months, as in English. French writes *lundi* and *mars* with a lowercase first letter, except at the start of a sentence.

Writing the month before the day, as in "November 14". French says *le 14 novembre*.

Adding an *s* to *mille*. It never changes: *deux mille*.

### [Telling the time](../03-verbs-and-questions/07-telling-time.md)

Writing *14:30* or *14h30*. In French text, use *14 h 30*, with a space on each side of the *h*.

Saying *deux heure* with no *s*. From two o'clock on, *heures* is plural. It sounds the same, so you must remember to write the *s*: *deux heures et demie*.

Using *et demie* after *midi*. Say *midi et demi*.

### [Making plans](../03-verbs-and-questions/08-making-plans.md)

Saying *je veux* when you accept. It sounds like "I want". Say *je veux bien*.

Using *tu* with a manager or a stranger. With them, use *vous*, and the chunks become *vous voulez* and *vous pouvez*.

Writing *libre* with an *-e* for a woman, as in "libree". The word has one form: *libre* for a man and a woman.

### [Texting to make plans](../03-verbs-and-questions/09-texting-to-make-plans.md)

Using texting abbreviations with *vous*. They are for friends and family. At work, write full words.

Putting a space before *?* and *!*. In French text in this book, write *Tu viens?*, with no space.

Using *bisous* with a manager or a stranger. Keep it for family and close friends.

### [Casual questions in Quebec](../03-verbs-and-questions/10-quebec-casual-questions.md)

Taking the second *tu* of *Tu veux-tu?* for a second "you". It is a question marker.

Writing casual forms in work emails, school papers or formal letters. They are for speech and for friends.

Judging the speaker. Casual forms are a register of Quebec French, and they are not an error.

### [Hearing times and plans](../03-verbs-and-questions/11-hearing-times-and-plans.md)

Waiting to understand every word. Catch the numbers and the place first, then fill in the rest.

Writing a time as you hear the words, for example "dix-huit heures quarante" as 10.40. Subtract 12 from the hour to check: 18 h is six in the evening.

Mixing up *départ* and *arrivée*. Listen for the word before the time.

### [A weekend plan](../03-verbs-and-questions/12-a-weekend-plan.md)

Using *je veux* to accept an invitation. Say *je veux bien*.

Saying a 24-hour time with *et quart* or *et demie*. In the 24-hour way, say the minutes as a number: *dix-huit heures vingt*.

Forgetting the *de* in a negative sentence. Say *Je n'ai pas de carte*, and not "je n'ai pas une carte".


## Stage 4: People, places and directions

### [Adjectives and agreement](../04-people-places-and-directions/01-adjectives-agreement.md)

Putting every adjective before the noun, as in English. Most go after: *une femme intelligente*, not *une intelligente femme*.

Forgetting the feminine consonant. *Petit* is silent at the end, but *petite* ends in /t/, so a woman who is small is *petite*, and you say it.

Writing *beau* before a vowel. Say *un bel hôtel*, and write it the same way.

### [Colours, sizes and where adjectives go](../04-people-places-and-directions/02-colours-sizes-and-position.md)

Putting the colour before the noun. A colour goes after: *un sac noir*.

Adding *-s* to *orange* or *marron* in the plural. They never change: *des sacs orange*.

Keeping *clair* and *foncé* separate from the colour. The pair is one unit, so *une veste bleu clair* has no *-e* on *bleu* or *clair*.

### [Possessives and family](../04-people-places-and-directions/03-possessives-and-family.md)

Matching the possessive to the owner as in English. *Son père* is "his father" or "her father", because *son* matches *père*.

Saying *ma amie*. Use *mon amie*, because *amie* starts with a vowel sound.

Forgetting that *un*, *une* and *des* turn into *de* after *ne ... pas*. Say *Je n'ai pas de sœur*.

### [This, that, and moi, toi](../04-people-places-and-directions/04-this-that-and-moi-toi.md)

Writing *ce amie* or *ce hôtel*. Use *cette amie* and *cet hôtel*.

Using *je* or *tu* after a preposition. Say *avec moi*, not *avec je*.

Saying *c'est ils*. After *c'est*, use *eux* for men and *elles* for women: *ce sont eux*, *ce sont elles*.

### [Prepositions of place](../04-people-places-and-directions/05-prepositions-of-place.md)

Writing *de le parc*. French always joins them: *du parc*.

Using *à* with a country. Say *en France*, *au Canada*, *aux États-Unis*, and keep *à* for cities.

Saying *au États-Unis*. The name is plural, so it takes *aux*.

### [The futur proche](../04-people-places-and-directions/06-futur-proche.md)

Changing the second verb. Say *je vais manger*, not *je vais mange*.

Putting *pas* after the infinitive. *Pas* goes after *vais*: *Je ne vais pas manger*.

Reading *dans une heure* as "within an hour". It means "one hour from now".

### [C'est or il est](../04-people-places-and-directions/07-c-est-vs-il-est.md)

Using *il est* before *un*, as in *il est un médecin*. When there is an article, use *c'est*.

Putting an article after *il est* with a job. Say *Il est médecin*, not *il est un médecin*.

Mixing up *il y a* and *c'est*. *Il y a* says something is there. *C'est* tells you what it is.

### [Asking for directions](../04-people-places-and-directions/08-asking-directions.md)

Saying *où est les toilettes*. The plural needs *où sont*.

Adding *-ez* to a friend. Use *tourne* with *tu* and *tournez* with *vous*.

Forgetting *jusqu'au*. Say *jusqu'au feu*, not *jusqu'à le feu*.

### [Metro maps and signs](../04-people-places-and-directions/09-metro-maps-and-signs.md)

Confusing *poussez* and *tirez*. Both end in /e/. Remember that *poussez* sounds like "push".

Using *direction* as a place. It means "towards", so it is followed by the last station of the line.

Writing the colour wrongly after *ligne*. Say *la ligne verte*, not *la ligne vert*.

### [The Quebec accent and street words](../04-people-places-and-directions/10-quebec-accent-and-street-words.md)

Copying the Quebec vowels for every word. Keep the standard vowels in your own speech and learn to hear the others.

Writing *char*, *lumière* or *chum* in a formal text. Use *voiture*, *feu* and *copain* there.

Saying the loose vowel before /ʁ z v ʒ/. The OQLF says those consonants do not trigger the loosening, so *rouge* keeps its tense /u/.

### [Following directions](../04-people-places-and-directions/11-following-directions.md)

Trying to translate every word. Catch the direction words and landmarks first.

Mixing up *gauche* and *droite* under pressure. Write *G* and *D* beside each word as you hear it.

Hearing *place* and thinking "seat". In this lesson it means "square".

### [A short self-introduction](../04-people-places-and-directions/12-short-self-introduction.md)

Writing *J'aime musique* with no article. After *aimer*, the noun has one.

Writing *Je habite*. Before a vowel sound, *je* becomes *j'*: *J'habite*.

Writing *Je n'ai pas des frères*. After the negative, use *de*: *Je n'ai pas de frères*.

### [A day in Montreal](../04-people-places-and-directions/13-a-day-in-montreal.md)

Trying to catch every word in the guide's talk. Catch the places and the verbs first.

Missing *nous allons* at the start of each sentence. It tells you what will happen next.

Mixing up *c'est* and *il est* in your own description. Use *c'est* for a general opinion and *il est* for a particular thing.


## Stage 5: The past: what happened

### [Regular -ir and -re verbs](../05-the-past-what-happened/01-regular-ir-and-re-verbs.md)

Writing *ils finient* instead of *ils finissent*. The *-iss-* stays in all three plural forms of a *finir* verb.

Adding *t* to *vendre* in the singular, as in *il vendt*. The forms are *je vends*, *tu vends*, *il vend*.

Using *-iss-* with *partir*: *nous partissons* does not exist. Say *nous partons*, *nous sortons*, *nous dormons*.

### [Prendre, venir, boire and voir](../05-the-past-what-happened/02-prendre-venir-boire-voir.md)

Writing *ils prenent* with one *n*. The plural has two: *ils prennent*, *ils viennent*.

Using *nous boivons* or *vous boivez*. The stem becomes *buv-*: *nous buvons*, *vous buvez*.

Using *venir de* with a past tense after it, as in *je viens de fini*. After *venir de*, the verb stays in the infinitive: *je viens de finir*.

### [The passé composé with avoir](../05-the-past-what-happened/03-passe-compose-avoir.md)

Using the infinitive instead of the participle: *j'ai parler*. The two sound the same, so check the spelling: after *j'ai* write *-é*, *-i* or *-u*.

Putting the participle before *avoir*, or leaving out *avoir*. Say *j'ai mangé*, not *mangé* alone and not *je mangé*.

Using *être* as the helper because English says "I am finished". With the verbs in this lesson, *avoir* is the helper. The verbs that take *être* come in [The passé composé with être](../05-the-past-what-happened/05-passe-compose-etre.md).

### [Irregular past participles](../05-the-past-what-happened/04-irregular-participles.md)

Saying *eu* like *peu*. In *j'ai eu* the vowel is /y/, as in *tu*.

Writing *j'ai prendu* or *j'ai faisé*. Regular endings do not apply here, so use *pris* and *fait*.

Conjugating *ouvrir* like *finir*: *nous ouvrissons*. The present is *j'ouvre*, *nous ouvrons*, like an -er verb.

### [The passé composé with être](../05-the-past-what-happened/05-passe-compose-etre.md)

Using *avoir* with a movement verb: *j'ai allé*, *j'ai arrivé*. Say *je suis allé*, *je suis arrivé*.

Forgetting to agree the participle for a woman or for several people: *elle est parti*, *ils sont arrivé*. Write *elle est partie* and *ils sont arrivés*.

Agreeing the participle with *avoir*: *elle a mangée*. With *avoir* and a subject, the participle does not change.

### [The passé composé: no, questions and agreement](../05-the-past-what-happened/06-pc-negation-questions-agreement.md)

Putting *pas* after the participle: *j'ai mangé pas*. Put *ne* before the helper and *pas* right after it: *je n'ai pas mangé*.

Putting *personne* in the middle: *je n'ai personne vu*. It goes last: *je n'ai vu personne*.

Keeping *un* after *pas*: *Je n'ai pas vu un film*. It becomes *de*: *Je n'ai pas vu de film*.

### [Past time words](../05-the-past-what-happened/07-past-time-words.md)

Putting *dernière* before the noun: *la dernière semaine* means "the final week". For "last week" say *la semaine dernière*.

Using *hier* for the day before a moment in a story. Say *la veille*.

Using *depuis* for "ago": *depuis trois jours, j'ai vu Julie*. For "ago" use *il y a*.

### [Telling about your weekend](../05-the-past-what-happened/08-telling-about-your-weekend.md)

Using *visiter* for a person: *j'ai visité ma tante*. Use *visiter* for places and *voir* for people: *j'ai vu ma tante*.

Using *j'ai allé* for "I went". Say *je suis allé*.

Starting every sentence with *et*: *et j'ai mangé, et j'ai dormi*. Use the linking words: *d'abord*, *ensuite*, *puis*, *enfin*.

### [Everyday nouns in Quebec](../05-the-past-what-happened/09-quebec-everyday-nouns.md)

Using *frigidaire* or *sacoche* in a formal email. Both are casual. Write *réfrigérateur* and *sac à main*.

Treating a Quebec word as an error in France, or the reverse. Neither variety is wrong. The same word can mean something else: a *gilet* is not the same garment in Montréal and in Paris.

Reading *tantôt* only as "later". In Quebec, the verb tense decides whether it means earlier or later.

### [Listening to a story](../05-the-past-what-happened/10-listening-to-a-story.md)

Trying to understand every word on the first play. The first listen is for the frame, and the details come on later plays.

Writing the wrong helper because you could not hear it. When a participle ends in /e/ or /i/ and the helper is unclear, listen for *je suis* or *il est*, which go with movement verbs, and *j'ai* or *il a*, which go with the others.

Confusing *heureusement* with *heureux*. *Heureusement* is "luckily" and goes with an event. *Heureux* is "happy" and goes with a person.

### [A message about yesterday](../05-the-past-what-happened/11-message-about-yesterday.md)

Mixing *matin* and *matinée*, or *jour* and *journée*. Use *matin* and *jour* for a point or a count (*hier matin*, *trois jours*), and *matinée* and *journée* for the stretch of time (*toute la journée*).

Writing *fatigant* for a tired person. A person who is tired is *fatigué*. *Fatigant* describes the thing or the day that tires you.

Forgetting the accent: *j'ai oublie*, *j'ai rate*. The participles end in *é*: *oublié*, *raté*.

### [A weekend in Quebec City](../05-the-past-what-happened/12-a-weekend-in-quebec-city.md)

Switching to the present in the middle of a story: *Samedi, nous avons visité Québec, puis nous mangeons.* Keep the passé composé until the story is over.

Using *avoir* with *partir*, *arriver* or *rentrer*. These take *être*.

Forgetting to agree the participle when *nous* or *elles* is feminine: *nous sommes parties* for two women.


## Stage 6: Habits, memories and food

### [Partitive articles: du, de la, des](../06-habits-memories-and-food/01-partitive-articles.md)

Leaving out the article: *Je bois café*. French needs *du café*, even when English says "I drink coffee".

Keeping *du* after a negative: *Je ne bois pas du café*. After *ne ... pas* use *de*: *Je ne bois pas de café*.

Using *du* with a verb of liking: *J'aime du café*. Use the definite article: *J'aime le café*.

### [Vouloir, pouvoir and devoir](../06-habits-memories-and-food/02-vouloir-pouvoir-devoir.md)

Putting the second verb in a conjugated form: *Je veux mange*. After *vouloir*, *pouvoir* and *devoir*, the second verb stays in the infinitive: *Je veux manger*.

Reading *il ne faut pas* as "you don't have to". It means "you must not".

Writing *je préfère* with *é* in every form. The accent is *è* before a silent ending: *je préfère*, *ils préfèrent*, but *nous préférons*.

### [Food, shopping and quantities](../06-habits-memories-and-food/03-food-shopping-and-quantities.md)

Using the article after a quantity word: *un kilo des pommes de terre*. Use *de* alone: *un kilo de pommes de terre*.

Writing *j'achetes* or *il achete*. Write *è* when the ending is silent: *j'achète*, *il achète*.

Mixing *une livre* and *un livre*. The pound is feminine and the book is masculine.

### [Reflexive verbs](../06-habits-memories-and-food/04-reflexive-verbs.md)

Leaving out the pronoun: *Je lève à 7 h*. A reflexive verb always keeps *me*, *te* or *se*: *Je me lève à 7 h*.

Using *avoir* in the passé composé: *Je me suis couché*, not *Je m'ai couché*. Reflexive verbs always take *être*.

Dropping the pronoun after *aller*: *Je vais coucher tôt*. Keep it and make it match the subject: *Je vais me coucher tôt*.

### [The imparfait](../06-habits-memories-and-food/05-imparfait.md)

Using the passé composé for a habit: *J'ai joué tous les jours*. For a repeated past action, use *je jouais tous les jours*.

Building the stem from the infinitive: *je finais*. Use the *nous* form: *nous finissons*, so *je finissais*.

Forgetting the soft *g*: *je mangais*. Write *je mangeais*.

### [Imparfait or passé composé](../06-habits-memories-and-food/06-imparfait-vs-passe-compose.md)

Using the passé composé for the scene: *Il a fait froid et il y a eu beaucoup de monde*. For weather and crowds in a story, use *il faisait froid* and *il y avait beaucoup de monde*.

Using the imparfait for a completed event: *Je suis entré et je prenais un café*. Two events one after the other both take the passé composé.

Choosing the tense from the English: "I was" is not always the imparfait. *J'ai été content quand j'ai gagné* is correct for a reaction at one moment.

### [Adverbs ending in -ment](../06-habits-memories-and-food/07-adverbs-in-ment.md)

Writing *facilment* or *seulment*. The *-e* of the feminine stays: *facilement*, *seulement*.

Writing *evidentment*. For adjectives in *-ent*, the adverb is *-emment*: *évidemment*. For *-ant*, it is *-amment*: *constamment*.

Using *bon* for *bien*: *Il parle bon*. After a verb that describes how, use the adverb: *Il parle bien*.

### [At the restaurant](../06-habits-memories-and-food/08-at-the-restaurant.md)

Calling the main course *l'entrée*. In French, *l'entrée* is the starter and *le plat* is the main dish.

Saying *Je veux un café* to the waiter. It is blunt. Use *je voudrais* or *je vais prendre*.

Forgetting *de* with a quantity: *une bouteille eau*. Use *une bouteille d'eau*.

### [Table culture in Quebec](../06-habits-memories-and-food/09-quebec-table-culture.md)

Using *dîner* for the evening meal in Quebec. Use *souper* for supper, and keep *dîner* for lunch.

Writing *un 5 à 7*. The OQLF says to write *un cinq à sept*.

Giving a tip percentage as a fixed rule. Customs differ, so look at what the terminal suggests or ask.

### [Casual speech at the resto](../06-habits-memories-and-food/10-casual-speech-at-the-resto.md)

Writing casual forms in an email or a formal message: *c'est-tu* or *y'a*. Write the standard forms, and keep the casual ones for speaking and for recognising.

Using casual words with a stranger, an older person or a manager. With them, use *vous* and the standard words.

Translating *correct* as "right". In casual Quebec speech it often means "fine" or "OK".

### [Describing your childhood](../06-habits-memories-and-food/11-describing-your-childhood.md)

Using the passé composé for the habits: *Chaque dimanche, nous avons mangé chez ma grand-mère*. Use the imparfait: *nous mangions*.

Writing *je jouait* or *nous jouais*. Match the ending to the subject: *je jouais*, *il jouait*, *nous jouions*.

Writing *mes grand-parents*. The first part also takes an *s*: *mes grands-parents*.

### [Menus and recipes](../06-habits-memories-and-food/12-menus-and-recipes.md)

Reading *cuire* as a noun. It is a verb, "to cook", and it has the participle *cuit* from the restaurant lesson.

Treating *les pâtes* (pasta) and *la pâte* (batter, dough) as the same word. The singular is *la pâte*, and *les pâtes* is the plural for pasta.

Forgetting that *de* follows a quantity in a list of ingredients: *une pincée de sel*, not *une pincée du sel*.

### [Dinner with friends](../06-habits-memories-and-food/13-dinner-with-friends.md)

Forgetting the partitive: *J'apporte vin*. Say *J'apporte du vin*.

Using *du* after a negative: *Je ne bois pas du vin*. Use *Je ne bois pas de vin*.

Asking *Tu veux de vin?* The offer needs the partitive: *Tu veux du vin?*


## Stage 7: Pronouns and getting things done

### [Direct object pronouns](../07-pronouns-and-getting-things-done/01-direct-object-pronouns.md)

Putting the pronoun after the verb, as English does: *Je vois la*. The pronoun goes before: *Je la vois*.

Putting the pronoun before the helper verb: *Je le vais appeler*. With *aller*, *pouvoir* and *vouloir* the pronoun goes before the infinitive: *Je vais l'appeler*.

Forgetting the agreement in writing: *Les clés? Je les ai pris*. A pronoun before the verb makes the participle agree: *Je les ai prises*.

### [Indirect object pronouns](../07-pronouns-and-getting-things-done/02-indirect-object-pronouns.md)

Using *le* or *la* for a verb with *à*: *Je la téléphone*. The verb takes *à*, so use *lui*: *Je lui téléphone*.

Using *lui* for a thing: *Je lui donne* means "I give him (her)". For a thing with *à*, you will need *y* (next lesson).

Making the participle agree with an indirect object: *Je leur ai parlés*. It stays *parlé*.

### [The pronouns y and en](../07-pronouns-and-getting-things-done/03-y-and-en.md)

Dropping *en* when the number stays: *J'ai deux* for "I have two". Say *J'en ai deux*.

Using *y* for a person: *J'y téléphone*. For a person use *lui* or *leur*: *Je lui téléphone*.

Making the participle agree with *en*: *J'en ai pris deux* never *J'en ai prises*. The participle stays masculine singular with *en*.

### [The imperative](../07-pronouns-and-getting-things-done/04-imperative.md)

Keeping the *s* in the *tu* form of an -er verb: *Parles!* Say *Parle!* The *s* only comes back before *y* or *en*: *Vas-y!*

Putting the pronoun before the verb in a positive command. Put it after, with a hyphen: *Prends-le!* In the negative it goes first: *Ne le prends pas!*

Saying *Ne pas bouger*, a notice form, to a person. When you speak to someone, use *Ne bougez pas.*

### [Savoir, connaître, dire, lire and écrire](../07-pronouns-and-getting-things-done/05-savoir-connaitre-dire-lire-ecrire.md)

Using *savoir* with a person or place: *Je sais Montréal*. Use *connaître*: *Je connais Montréal*.

Using *connaître* before a verb: *Je connais nager*. Use *savoir*: *Je sais nager*.

Saying *vous disez*. The form is *vous dites*.

### [The body, health and the pharmacy](../07-pronouns-and-getting-things-done/06-body-health-and-pharmacy.md)

Saying *J'ai mal à ma tête*, a word-for-word copy of English. Use the article: *J'ai mal à la tête*.

Writing *à* and *le* apart before a masculine body part. They join: *J'ai mal au dos*.

Using *je sens* alone for "I feel ill": *Je me sens mal*. The reflexive *me* is needed when you talk about yourself.

### [At the doctor and the pharmacy](../07-pronouns-and-getting-things-done/07-doctor-and-pharmacy.md)

Using the past for something still true: *J'ai eu mal depuis deux jours*. After *depuis* the pain continues, so use the present: *J'ai mal depuis deux jours*.

Saying *des conseils* when you mean one piece of advice. One piece is *un conseil*.

Using *depuis* with a number of days but forgetting the number: *depuis jours*. Say *depuis deux jours*.

### [At a hotel or an Airbnb](../07-pronouns-and-getting-things-done/08-hotel-or-airbnb.md)

Saying *Je veux une chambre*. It sounds blunt. Use *Je voudrais* or *Pouvez-vous*.

Forgetting the pronoun before the infinitive: *Pouvez-vous apporter moi une couverture?* Say *Pouvez-vous m'apporter une couverture?*

Writing *Le chambre est sale*. *Chambre* is feminine: *La chambre est sale.*

### [Health care in Quebec for a visitor](../07-pronouns-and-getting-things-done/09-health-care-in-quebec-as-a-visitor.md)

Saying *Je vais à urgences*. The word is plural and takes *aux*: *Je vais aux urgences.*

Forgetting the agreement when a woman speaks: *Je suis tombé*. She writes *Je suis tombée*, because *tomber* uses *être*.

Saying *J'ai besoin un médecin*. After *avoir besoin* you need *de*: *J'ai besoin d'un médecin.*

### [Phone messages and announcements](../07-pronouns-and-getting-things-done/10-phone-messages-and-announcements.md)

Saying *Rappelez moi* in writing, without a hyphen. In a positive command, the pronoun is joined by a hyphen: *rappelez-moi*.

Mixing up *rappeler* ("to call back") and *se rappeler* ("to remember"), which looks the same. *Se rappeler* is reflexive.

Writing *Appuyez le 1*. You press *on* the number: *appuyez sur le 1*.

### [A short booking email](../07-pronouns-and-getting-things-done/11-short-booking-email.md)

Using *tu* with a business: *Peux-tu me confirmer*. Use *vous*: *Pouvez-vous me confirmer*.

Writing *Bonjour!* at the top of a formal email. The OQLF discourages the exclamation mark here, so write *Bonjour,* and start the next line with a capital.

Forgetting the space before the colon in *Objet :*. French puts a non-breaking space before the colon.

### [Notices and instructions](../07-pronouns-and-getting-things-done/12-notices-and-instructions.md)

Reading *Ne pas fumer* as a question or a statement about a person. It is an instruction to anyone who reads it.

Using *vous* commands for a friend: *Mettez le papier dans le bac* is for a notice. To a friend, say *Mets le papier dans le bac.*

Mixing up *les déchets* (waste) with *le recyclage* (recycling). They go in different bins.

### [Solving problems while travelling](../07-pronouns-and-getting-things-done/13-solving-problems-while-travelling.md)

Using *je* with *volé*: *J'ai volé mon sac* means "I stole my bag". Use *on m'a volé mon sac* for "someone stole my bag".

Forgetting the pronoun and repeating the noun: *Pouvez-vous réparer le chauffage?* is correct but heavy. In speech, *Pouvez-vous le réparer?* is more natural.

Writing *en panne* with an adjective ending: *en pannes*. The phrase does not change.


## Stage 8: Comparing, duration and settling in

### [Comparatives](../08-comparing-duration-and-settling-in/01-comparatives.md)

Saying *plus bon* for "better". Use *meilleur* with a noun and *mieux* with a verb.

Using *de* instead of *que* after a comparison: *plus grand de moi*. The word that follows is *que*.

Using *aussi* with a noun: *aussi d'amis*. With nouns, say *autant de*.

### [Superlatives](../08-comparing-duration-and-settling-in/02-superlatives.md)

Using *dans* for "in the": *le plus grand parc dans la ville*. Use *de*: *le plus grand parc de la ville*.

Saying *le plus bon* for "the best". Use *le meilleur* with a noun and *le mieux* with a verb.

Forgetting the second article when the adjective follows the noun: *la rue plus populaire*. Say *la rue la plus populaire*.

### [Depuis, il y a and pendant](../08-comparing-duration-and-settling-in/03-depuis-il-y-a-pendant.md)

Using the passé composé with *depuis* for something that is still true: *j'ai habité ici depuis deux ans*. Use the present: *j'habite ici depuis deux ans*.

Using *depuis* for "ago": *je suis arrivé depuis deux jours*. Use *il y a*.

Using *pendant* for something that is still going on: *je travaille ici pendant un an*. Use *depuis*.

### [The relative pronouns qui and que](../08-comparing-duration-and-settling-in/04-relative-qui-que.md)

Using *qui* where *que* is needed: *le film qui j'aime*. After the pronoun there is a subject (*j'*), so use *que*.

Using *que* where *qui* is needed: *la femme que parle*. The word replaced is the subject, so use *qui*.

Forgetting the agreement with *que* in the passé composé. After *la lettre que j'ai*, the participle is *écrite* (feminine), because it agrees with the antecedent.

### [Verbs followed by à and de](../08-comparing-duration-and-settling-in/05-verbs-with-a-and-de.md)

Choosing the small word from English: *finir à manger*. Learn the chunk with the verb: *finir de*.

Leaving out the small word: *j'ai commencé courir*. *Commencer* always needs *à* or *de* before an infinitive, and the usual one is *à*.

Putting *de* before a vowel without dropping the vowel: *essayer de arrêter*. Say *essayer d'arrêter*.

### [Weather and seasons](../08-comparing-duration-and-settling-in/06-weather-and-seasons.md)

Adding *faire* to the weather verbs: *il fait pleut*. Say *il pleut*, *il neige*.

Mixing *avoir* and *faire* for cold and hot: *je fais froid*. People have *chaud* or *froid* (*j'ai froid*), and the weather *fait* it (*il fait froid*).

Using the definite article after *il y a*: *il y a le soleil*. Say *il y a du soleil*.

### [Small talk: weather and hockey](../08-comparing-duration-and-settling-in/07-small-talk-weather-and-hockey.md)

Ending a small talk after one comment. Always add a follow-up question, such as *Et vous?*

Using *hein* with a stranger at work or in a written message. Use *n'est-ce pas* or *non*.

Using *au fait* to add a related idea. It changes the subject. For a related idea, use *d'ailleurs*.

### [Hockey and holidays in Quebec](../08-comparing-duration-and-settling-in/08-quebec-hockey-and-holidays.md)

Saying *goaler* or *scorer un but* in careful French. These are casual anglicisms that the OQLF says to avoid. Use *gardien de but* and *marquer un but*.

Thinking *chandail* means "sweater" only. In a hockey conversation it is the jersey.

Mixing the two holidays: 24 June is the Fête nationale du Québec, and 1 July is the Fête du Canada.

### [Housing words](../08-comparing-duration-and-settling-in/09-housing-words.md)

Counting the bathroom as a full room: *un 4 1/2* is not five rooms.

Assuming *chauffé* always means everything is included. Ask about heating and lights when you phone.

Using *louer* for only one side. *Je loue un appartement* can mean you are the tenant or the owner, so add *à* when it matters: *Je loue mon appartement à un ami.*

### [Phoning about an apartment](../08-comparing-duration-and-settling-in/10-phoning-about-an-apartment.md)

Using *tu* with the owner. Use *vous*, *je vous appelle* and *s'il vous plaît*.

Saying *Je veux visiter*. Use *Je voudrais visiter*, which is polite.

Mixing *à louer* and *à vendre*. An apartment for rent is *à louer*.

### [Listening at real speed](../08-comparing-duration-and-settling-in/11-real-speed-intro.md)

Writing reduced forms in a formal text or an email to a boss: *t'as*, *faut*, *chais pas*. Write the standard forms.

Trying to translate every word in a fast clip. Catch the key words first and replay.

Treating fillers as part of the message. *Euh* and *en fait* carry little meaning, so let them pass.

### [An informal email](../08-comparing-duration-and-settling-in/12-informal-email.md)

Saying *Je suis hâte*. Use *j'ai hâte*, with *avoir*.

Saying *Je manque Sam* for "I miss Sam". Say *Sam me manque*.

Mixing *tu* and *vous* in the same email. Choose *tu* for a friend and keep it to the end.

### [Short news items](../08-comparing-duration-and-settling-in/13-short-news-items.md)

Reading every word. Find the four answers first and skip the details you do not need.

Treating the news as true because it sounds real. The items in this book are invented, and the stage media list is the place for real news.

Writing *selon moi* when you mean the source of a fact. *Selon la municipalité* names the source. *Selon moi* means "in my opinion".

### [A long weekend in Montreal](../08-comparing-duration-and-settling-in/14-a-long-weekend-in-montreal.md)

Using the passé composé with *depuis* for something that is still true: *Marc a habité là depuis trois ans*. Use the present: *Marc habite là depuis trois ans*.

Choosing *plus bon* or *le plus bon*: use *meilleur* and *le meilleur*.

Forgetting the small word after *décider* or *oublier*: *j'ai décidé prendre*. Say *j'ai décidé de prendre*.


## Stage 9: Work and the future

### [The futur simple: regular verbs](../09-work-and-the-future/01-futur-simple-regular.md)

Cutting the *r*: *je parlai* is not the futur simple. Write and say *je parlerai*.

Forgetting the changes in *-er* verbs: *j'achetterai* or *j'appelerai* are wrong. Write *j'achèterai* and *j'appellerai*.

Using the present after *quand* for a future event: *Je vous appelle quand j'arrive* means a habit. For one coming event, say *Je vous appellerai quand j'arriverai*.

### [The futur simple: irregular verbs](../09-work-and-the-future/02-futur-simple-irregular.md)

Using the infinitive as the stem for an irregular verb: *je ferai*, not *je faireai*. Learn the stem list.

Writing one *r* in the stems with two: *je verrai*, *j'enverrai*, *je pourrai*, *je courrai*. The double *r* gives the strong /ʁ/ sound.

Mixing up *je serai* (from *être*) and *je saurai* (from *savoir*). The vowels differ: /sə/ and /so/.

### [The conditionnel for politeness](../09-work-and-the-future/03-conditionnel-politeness.md)

Mixing the futur simple and the conditionnel: *je voudrai* is not French. Say *je voudrais* /vudʁɛ/.

Forgetting the *r*: *je parlais* is the imparfait. For "I would speak", say and write *je parlerais*.

Using *je veux* at a counter. It sounds like an order. Use *je voudrais*.

### [Si clauses and hypotheses](../09-work-and-the-future/04-si-clauses-hypotheses.md)

Putting the conditionnel or the futur simple after *si* is wrong. Compare:

```fr
Si j'aurais le temps, je viendrais. {x}
Si j'avais le temps, je viendrais.
Si tu viendras, nous finirons. {x}
Si tu viens, nous finirons.
```

Using *éventuellement* for "eventually". It means "possibly". For "eventually", use *finalement*.

### [Phone calls at work](../09-work-and-the-future/05-phone-calls-at-work.md)

Saying *Qui êtes-vous?* on the phone. It is blunt. Say *C'est de la part de qui?*

Saying *Je vous transfère* without a warning. Say *Un instant, s'il vous plaît* first.

Confusing the two meanings of *un poste*: an extension (*poste 245*) and a job (*un poste de directeur*).

### [Serving the public](../09-work-and-the-future/06-serving-the-public.md)

Saying *Vous manquez un document* for "you are missing a document". Say *Il vous manque un document.*

Waiting for the person to ask for French or English. Offer both first: *Hello! Bonjour!*

Using *tu* with a member of the public at a government counter or in a bilingual office. Use *vous*, even with a young person.

### [Office and meeting words](../09-work-and-the-future/07-office-and-meeting-words.md)

Mixing *un fichier* and *un dossier*. A computer file is *un fichier*. A case or folder of papers is *un dossier*.

Writing *un meeting* in formal French. Use *une réunion*.

Using *un salaire* for time off. Pay is *un salaire*, time off is *un congé*.

### [Work culture in Quebec](../09-work-and-the-future/08-quebec-work-culture.md)

Using *tu* with a client or a stranger because colleagues use it. In a first contact, start with *vous*.

Believing that every Quebec workplace uses *tu*. Many do, but some do not. Listen first.

Treating a source on Quebec rules as legal advice. This lesson only describes. Check official pages for your own case.

### [Listening to phone calls](../09-work-and-the-future/09-listening-to-phone-calls.md)

Mixing up *reporter* and *rapporter*. *Reporter* is "to postpone". *Rapporter* means "to bring back" or "to report".

Hearing a time as a number only. In a call, *9 h 30* is nine thirty, and *15 h* is three o'clock. Convert before you write.

Stopping the audio at each new word. Keep going, and fill the gap on the second play.

### [Work email basics](../09-work-and-the-future/10-work-email-basics.md)

Writing a subject line in capitals or with exclamation marks: *URGENT!!!*. Say the action and the date instead.

Writing *Bonjour!* followed by the name in a work email. The OQLF advises against an exclamation mark after *Bonjour*.

Forgetting to mention the attachment in the text. Write *Ci-joint* or *en pièce jointe*, so the reader looks for it.

### [Workplace documents](../09-work-and-the-future/11-workplace-documents.md)

Reading *un poste* as an extension in a job ad. In a job description, it is the position.

Reading *doit* as a suggestion. In a policy, *doit* means "must".

Reading *exigence* as "exigency" in English. It means a requirement, something the candidate must meet.

### [Invitations and follow-ups](../09-work-and-the-future/12-invitations-and-follow-ups.md)

Writing an action with no name or no date: *Le budget sera envoyé.* Say who and when: *Chantal enverra le budget avant le 27 mars.*

Putting a capital on the day or the month: *le Mardi 20 Mars* is wrong. Write *le mardi 20 mars*.

Writing the time as *10h00* or *10:00*. Write *10 h*.

### [A day at the office](../09-work-and-the-future/13-a-day-at-the-office.md)

Using *tu* with a manager you do not know yet. Use *vous* until the other person says *tu*.

Using *je veux* at a client call. Use *je voudrais*.

Putting the futur simple after *si* is wrong:

```fr
Si j'aurai le temps, je ferai le compte rendu. {x}
Si j'ai le temps, je ferai le compte rendu.
```


## Stage 10: Complex sentences and requests

### [The plus-que-parfait](../10-complex-sentences-and-requests/01-plus-que-parfait.md)

Using the passé composé for an action that comes first in a past story: *Quand je suis arrivée, la réunion a commencé.* says the meeting began at the moment you arrived. Say *avait commencé*.

Forgetting the agreement with *être*: *elle était arrivé* is wrong. Say *elle était arrivée*.

Putting *avoir* in the présent: *j'ai fini* is the passé composé. The plus-que-parfait is *j'avais fini*.

### [The relative pronouns dont and où](../10-complex-sentences-and-requests/02-relative-dont-and-ou.md)

Repeating *de* after *dont*: *le livre dont je parle de* is wrong. *Dont* already contains *de*.

Using a possessive after *dont*: *la femme dont son fils est médecin* is wrong. Say *la femme dont le fils est médecin*.

Using *quand* after *jour*, *année* or *moment*: say *le jour où*, not *le jour quand*.

### [The relative pronoun lequel](../10-complex-sentences-and-requests/03-relative-lequel.md)

Using *que* after a preposition: *la personne avec que je travaille* is wrong. Say *avec qui* for a person and *avec lequel* for a thing.

Forgetting the contraction: *le projet à lequel je pense* is wrong. Say *auquel*. The feminine *à laquelle* does not change.

Using *duquel* where *dont* works: *le livre duquel je parle* is correct but heavy. Say *le livre dont je parle*.

### [The subjunctive after il faut que](../10-complex-sentences-and-requests/04-subjunctive-il-faut-que.md)

Using the présent after *il faut que*.

```fr
Il faut que tu finis. {x}
```

Say *il faut que tu finisses*.

Forgetting *que*.

```fr
Il faut je parte. {x}
```

Say *il faut que je parte*, or *il faut partir* for a general rule.

Using the same-subject form with *que*.

```fr
Je veux que je parte. {x}
```

When the subject does not change, use the infinitive: *je veux partir*.

### [The subjunctive: wishes and feelings](../10-complex-sentences-and-requests/05-subjunctive-vouloir-que-emotion.md)

Using the indicative after a wish or a feeling.

```fr
Je veux que tu viens. {x}
```

Say *je veux que tu viennes*.

Using *que* and the subjunctive with one subject.

```fr
Je veux que je parte. {x}
```

Say *je veux partir*.

Using the subjunctive after *espérer*.

```fr
J'espère qu'il vienne. {x}
```

Say *j'espère qu'il viendra*.

### [Pronoun order](../10-complex-sentences-and-requests/06-pronoun-order.md)

Putting *lui* before *le*.

```fr
Je lui le donne. {x}
```

Say *je le lui donne*. The direct object (*le, la, les*) comes first when *lui* or *leur* is the second pronoun.

Using the imperative order in the negative.

```fr
Ne donne-le-moi pas. {x}
```

In the negative, say *ne me le donne pas*.

Forgetting the agreement in the passé composé.

```fr
La facture, je la lui ai envoyé. {x}
```

Say *envoyée*.

### [Agreeing and disagreeing in meetings](../10-complex-sentences-and-requests/07-agreeing-in-meetings.md)

Using the subjunctive after *je suis convaincu que*: say *c'est*, not *ce soit*. After *je ne suis pas sûr que*, the subjunctive is right (*ce soit*).

Using the indicative after *je propose que*:

```fr
Je propose que nous ouvrons. {x}
```

*Je propose que* needs the subjunctive. Say *que nous ouvrions*.

Saying *je suis d'accord à vous*: say *je suis d'accord avec vous*.

### [At the bank](../10-complex-sentences-and-requests/08-at-the-bank.md)

Confusing *un retrait* and *un dépôt*. A *dépôt* puts money in, a *retrait* takes money out.

Forgetting *que* after *il faut*.

```fr
Il faut vous signez ici. {x}
```

Say *il faut que vous signiez ici*.

### [Music and TV in Quebec](../10-complex-sentences-and-requests/09-quebec-music-and-tv.md)

Using *un série*. *Série* is feminine: *une série*.

Using *un émission*. *Émission* is feminine: *une émission*.

Adding a possessive after *dont*: *l'artiste dont sa voix* is wrong. Say *l'artiste dont la voix*.

### [A radio interview](../10-complex-sentences-and-requests/10-radio-interview.md)

Trying to understand every word. Listen for the question, the verbs and one number instead, then listen again.

Skipping the second pass. The first pass gives the topic; the second gives the details.

Looking at the English too early. Read it only after the second pass.

### [A formal request](../10-complex-sentences-and-requests/11-formal-request.md)

Writing the subject line in capitals or with exclamation marks: *DEMANDE URGENTE!!!* is wrong. Write *Demande d'une attestation de résidence*.

Writing *l'expression de mes salutations distinguées*. The Banque de dépannage advises against it. Write *mes salutations distinguées*.

Writing *Bonjour!* with an exclamation mark to a first contact. The OQLF page advises against it. Write *Madame, Monsieur,* or *Bonjour,*.

### [Banking and admin words](../10-complex-sentences-and-requests/12-banking-and-admin-words.md)

Using *annuler* for a contract: for a subscription or a contract, say *résilier*. *Annuler* is for an appointment or a booking.

Writing *un impôt* for a sales tax. Sales taxes are *des taxes*.

Using *impôt* for a fine. A fine is *une amende*; *un impôt* is a tax.

### [Handling a request](../10-complex-sentences-and-requests/13-handling-a-request.md)

Starting with an apology and forgetting to say that you received the message. Begin with *Nous avons bien reçu votre message*, then apologize.

Promising a date you cannot keep. Write a date you can respect, or give a range such as *entre le 5 et le 8 décembre*.

Using *tu* with a client or a citizen. Replies use *vous*.

Oublier l'accord. Le participe s'accorde avec le sujet : *adoptées*.

```

La loi a adoptée. {x} | Wrong: the passive needs être.
```

Oublier *que*. En français, *que* est obligatoire : *Il dit qu'il est fatigué.*

```fr
Il dit il est fatigué. {x} | Wrong: que is missing.
```

Garder le pronom de la phrase d'origine. Si Marc dit « Je suis en retard », vous ne dites pas *Marc dit que je suis en retard*, mais *Marc dit qu'il est en retard*.

### [Reported speech in the past](../11-reporting-and-opinions/03-reported-speech-past.md)

Garder le futur : *Il a dit qu'il viendra demain.* Après un verbe au passé, le futur devient le conditionnel : *Il a dit qu'il viendrait le lendemain.*

Garder *demain* et *hier* dans un récit au passé. Dites *le lendemain* et *la veille*.

Confondre l'imparfait et le conditionnel à l'oral. *il était* et *il serait* ne sont pas pareils : écoutez le /ʁ/.

### [The gérondif and the present participle](../11-reporting-and-opinions/04-gerondif-and-participe-present.md)

Faire le gérondif avec deux sujets différents. Le sujet de *en parlant* est toujours le sujet du verbe principal.

Accorder le participe présent. Le participe est invariable (*jouant*). Seul l'adjectif verbal s'accorde (*des enfants charmants*).

```fr
des enfants jouants {x} | Wrong: the participle never takes -s (jouant).
```

Commencer par les reproches : *Votre service est nul.* Commencez par les faits : *Le 3 novembre, j'ai commandé...*

### [Opinion columns](../11-reporting-and-opinions/12-opinion-columns.md)

Lire une phrase ironique au premier degré. *Bravo* peut vouloir dire le contraire. Regardez le contexte.

## Stage 13: Subjunctive and argument

### [The subjunctive: doubt and opinion](../13-subjunctive-and-argument/01-subjunctive-doubt-opinion.md)

Mettre l'indicatif après un doute : « Je doute qu'il a raison. » Après *douter que*, le subjonctif est obligatoire : *Je doute qu'il ait raison.*

Garder l'indicatif quand *penser* est négatif : « Je ne pense pas qu'il vient. » La phrase négative demande le subjonctif : *Je ne pense pas qu'il vienne.*

Mettre le subjonctif après *il me semble que* : « Il me semble qu'il soit en retard. » Avec *me*, on emploie l'indicatif : *Il me semble qu'il est en retard.*

### [The subjunctive after conjunctions](../13-subjunctive-and-argument/02-subjunctive-conjunctions.md)

Mettre le subjonctif après *après que* : « Après qu'il ait fini ». Il s'agit d'un fait passé : *après qu'il a fini*.

Garder *que* avec un seul sujet : « Je pars avant que je dîne ». Avec un seul sujet, on dit *je pars avant de dîner*.

Ajouter *ne* après *sans que* : « sans qu'il ne le sache ». Après *sans que*, ce *ne* est une erreur : *sans qu'il le sache*.

### [Concession](../13-subjunctive-and-argument/03-concession.md)

Employer l'indicatif après *bien que* : « Bien que le budget est petit ». Écrivez *bien que le budget soit petit*.

Mettre le futur après *même si* : « Même si tu viendras ». *Même si* suit les règles de *si* : *même si tu viens*.

Mettre un verbe conjugué après *malgré* : « Malgré il pleut ». *Malgré* est suivi d'un nom : *malgré la pluie*.

### [The past subjunctive](../13-subjunctive-and-argument/04-subjonctif-passe.md)

Mettre le subjonctif présent pour une action finie : « Je regrette que nous abandonnions ». Pour une action finie, écrivez *Je regrette que nous ayons abandonné*.

Oublier l'accord avec *être* : « Je suis content qu'elles soient parti ». Avec *être*, le participe s'accorde : *qu'elles soient parties*.

## Stage 16: Debate, news and test readiness

### [Structuring an argument](../16-debate-news-and-test-readiness/01-structuring-an-argument.md)

Commencer un nouvel argument dans la conclusion. La conclusion répond à la question et ouvre sur autre chose; elle ne prouve plus rien.

Écrire un paragraphe sans exemple. Un argument sans exemple reste une opinion : ajoutez toujours un fait ou une scène.

Annoncer trois parties, puis en écrire deux. Le plan de l'introduction doit correspondre au développement.

### [Emphasis and inversion](../16-debate-news-and-test-readiness/02-emphasis-and-inversion.md)

Dire « C'est moi qui a fait » : après *c'est moi qui*, le verbe s'accorde avec *moi*. Écrivez *c'est moi qui ai fait*.

Écrire « Elle a faite réparer la toiture » : le participe *fait* est invariable devant un infinitif. Écrivez *elle a fait réparer la toiture*.

Oublier le trait d'union dans l'inversion : « viendra elle ». Écrivez *viendra-t-elle*.

### [Advanced news vocabulary](../16-debate-news-and-test-readiness/03-news-vocabulary-advanced.md)

Écrire « le changement climatique » au singulier pour parler du phénomène : on emploie surtout le pluriel, et l'OQLF le met dans le titre de son vocabulaire. Écrivez *les changements climatiques*.

Confondre *un déficit* et *la dette* : le déficit est annuel, la dette est la somme de tous les déficits.

Écrire « la main d'œuvre » sans trait d'union : écrivez *la main-d'œuvre*.

### [Discussing current events](../16-debate-news-and-test-readiness/04-current-events-discussion.md)

Employer *actuellement* pour dire « actually » : *actuellement* veut dire « en ce moment ». Pour « actually », écrivez *en fait*.

Dire *un média est objectif* pour dire qu'il est d'accord avec vous : *objectif* veut dire qu'il présente les faits sans prendre parti.

Oublier le subjonctif après *J'ai peur que* : « J'ai peur que c'est faux ». Écrivez *j'ai peur que ce soit faux*.

### [Formal debate](../16-debate-news-and-test-readiness/05-formal-debate.md)

Attaquer la personne au lieu de l'argument : « Vous n'y connaissez rien ». Dites plutôt *je mets cet argument en cause*.

Parler à l'autre camp à la deuxième personne : « tu as tort ». Dans un débat formel, on s'adresse à la présidence.

Dépasser le temps de parole. Une conclusion d'une minute suffit : une phrase de bilan et une demande.

### [Radio debates](../16-debate-news-and-test-readiness/06-radio-debates.md)

Prendre *un sophisme* pour *un mensonge* : un sophisme est un raisonnement faux, pas toujours un mensonge.

Dire « sur le fonds » : écrivez *sur le fond*.

Confondre *une surenchère* avec une simple opinion forte : la surenchère augmente sans cesse la dose pour dépasser l'autre.

### [TV news and documentaries](../16-debate-news-and-test-readiness/07-tv-news-and-documentaries.md)

Confondre *un reporter* et *un présentateur* : le reporter est sur place; le présentateur est au studio.

Traduire *un fait divers* par « a fact » : c'est une petite nouvelle de la vie courante, souvent un accident ou un incendie.

S'arrêter à chaque mot inconnu : continuez, puis revenez à la deuxième écoute.

### [Debates in Quebec today](../16-debate-news-and-test-readiness/08-quebec-today-debates.md)

Dire « le Québec est contre l'immigration » ou une phrase du même genre : les positions varient d'une personne et d'un groupe à l'autre. Dites *certains pensent que..., d'autres pensent que...*

Présenter un argument comme un fait : *les critiques disent que la loi limite les services* est un argument; le texte de la loi est un fait.

Ne pas dire la date d'un chiffre : écrivez *selon le rapport de 2025* ou *en 2026*.

### [Editorials](../16-debate-news-and-test-readiness/09-editorials.md)

Croire qu'un éditorial donne les faits de façon neutre : il défend une position. Cherchez le journal ou l'auteur qui parle.

Prendre une litote pour une critique : *ce n'est pas mauvais* veut dire « c'est bon ».

Confondre *s'indigner* et *s'insurger* : on *s'indigne* d'une injustice (réaction) et on *s'insurge contre* quelque chose (révolte).

### [An opinion piece](../16-debate-news-and-test-readiness/10-opinion-piece.md)

Entrer dans le sujet sans accroche : « Le stationnement est fermé ». Commencez par un fait ou une question qui donne envie de lire.

Mettre trois formules dans le même texte : une seule suffit, à la fin.

Écrire une concession qui prend le pas sur la thèse : après *certes*, revenez vite à votre idée avec *mais* ou *cela dit*.

### [Oral test practice](../16-debate-news-and-test-readiness/11-oral-test-practice.md)

Rester silencieux pour chercher le mot parfait : dites *comment dire* et contournez avec *en d'autres termes*.

Mélanger *tu* et *vous* devant un examinateur : choisissez *vous* au début et gardez-le.

Mémoriser un monologue mot à mot : un texte appris sonne faux, et vous perdez le fil à la première interruption. Apprenez le plan, pas le texte.

### [The final error clinic](../16-debate-news-and-test-readiness/12-final-error-clinic.md)

Employer « actuellement » pour traduire « actually » : *actuellement* veut dire « en ce moment ». Écrivez *en fait*.

Dire « je vais appliquer pour ce poste » : l'OQLF déconseille cet emploi de *appliquer*. Écrivez *je vais postuler pour ce poste*.

Mettre un temps du futur ou de l'indicatif après *il faut que* : « il faut que je vais ». Écrivez *il faut que j'aille*.

### [Capstone](../16-debate-news-and-test-readiness/13-capstone.md)

Mélanger *tu* et *vous* dans une même scène : choisissez-en un et gardez-le jusqu'à la fin de la scène.

Écrire un courriel formel sans formule de politesse finale : ajoutez *Veuillez agréer* ou une formule équivalente.

Lire ses diapositives à voix haute : l'écran donne les mots-clés; votre voix donne les phrases.
