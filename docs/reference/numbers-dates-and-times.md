# Numbers, dates and times

This page holds how to say and write numbers, money, days, dates, times and phone numbers in French. Use it when you are unsure of a form or need to check the spelling.

## Patterns from 21 to 100

From 21 to 69, say the ten, then the unit: *vingt-deux*, *trente-cinq*, *soixante-neuf*. The unit *un* is joined with *et* and no hyphen: *vingt et un*, *trente et un*, *soixante et un*.

From 70 to 79, keep counting from 60: *soixante et onze* (60 + 11), *soixante-douze*, *soixante-dix-sept*. From 80 to 99, count from 4 x 20: *quatre-vingt-un* (no *et*), *quatre-vingt-deux*, *quatre-vingt-onze*, *quatre-vingt-dix-neuf*.

Say these out loud.

```fr
Il y a vingt et un étudiants. | There are 21 students.
Elle a soixante et onze ans. | She is 71 years old.
Il a quatre-vingts ans. | He is 80 years old.
Il a quatre-vingt-un ans. | He is 81 years old.
Ça fait quatre-vingt-dix-neuf. | That makes 99.
```

Watch the *s* on *quatre-vingts*. It is there only when nothing follows it: *quatre-vingts* (80), but *quatre-vingt-un* (81). The same holds for *cent*.

## Hundreds, thousands and millions

| Number | French | Spelling rule |
|---|---|---|
| 100 | *cent* | No *un* in front |
| 101 | *cent un* | |
| 200 | *deux cents* | *Cents* takes an *s* when nothing follows |
| 201 | *deux cent un* | No *s* when a number follows |
| 1 000 | *mille* | No *un* in front |
| 2 000 | *deux mille* | *Mille* never takes an *s* |
| 2 500 | *deux mille cinq cents* | |
| 1 000 000 | *un million* | |
| 2 000 000 | *deux millions* | *Million* is a noun and takes an *s* |

*Million* is followed by *de* before a noun: *un million de dollars*, *deux millions de personnes*. *Mille* is not: *mille dollars*.

```fr
Le loyer est de mille deux cents dollars. | The rent is 1,200 dollars.
Ils ont deux cents employés. | They have 200 employees.
Ils ont deux cent un employés. | They have 201 employees.
La ville a un million de visiteurs par année. | The city has a million visitors a year.
```

### Hyphens and the 1990 spelling

The traditional spelling puts a hyphen only between parts under 100 that are not joined by *et*: *trente-cinq*, *cent trente-cinq*, *vingt et un*. The 1990 spelling reform allows a hyphen between every part: *vingt-et-un*, *cent-trente-cinq*. Both are accepted. This book uses the traditional spelling. Source: [OQLF, Banque de dépannage linguistique, écriture des déterminants numéraux](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/23494/la-typographie/nombres/ecriture-des-determinants-numeraux), checked 2026.

## Ordinals

An **ordinal number** (*un nombre ordinal*) gives the position: first, second, third. Except for *premier*, add *-ième* to the number (and drop a final silent *e*: *quatre* becomes *quatrième*).

| Number | French | Written short |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | *premier* (m.), *première* (f.) | 1er, 1re |
| 2nd | *deuxième* (also *second*, *seconde*) | 2e |
| 3rd | *troisième* | 3e |
| 4th | *quatrième* | 4e |
| 5th | *cinquième* | 5e |
| 6th | *sixième* | 6e |
| 7th | *septième* | 7e |
| 8th | *huitième* | 8e |
| 9th | *neuvième* | 9e |
| 10th | *dixième* | 10e |
| 20th | *vingtième* | 20e |
| 21st | *vingt et unième* | 21e |
| 100th | *centième* | 100e |

Watch the spelling: *cinq* becomes *cinquième* (a *u* is added) and *neuf* becomes *neuvième* (*f* becomes *v*).

```fr
C'est mon premier cours de français. | This is my first French class.
Elle habite au troisième étage. | She lives on the third floor.
C'est la deuxième rue à droite. | It is the second street on the right.
```

## Writing numbers

Write thousands with a space (a non-breaking space), and decimals with a comma: 1 250 for one thousand two hundred fifty, and 3,5 for three and a half. A point is not used. The dollar sign follows the number, after a space: 4,50 $. Percent also takes a space: 15 %.

| English | French |
|---|---|
| 1,250 | 1 250 |
| 3.5 | 3,5 |
| $4.50 | 4,50 $ |
| 15% | 15 % |

Say the comma as *virgule*: *trois virgule cinq*.

## Money

Say the dollars, then the cents. The cents are said as a plain number after the dollars. The amounts below are 4,50 $, 1 $ and 0,25 $.

```fr
Ça coûte quatre dollars cinquante. | It costs $4.50.
Ça fait un dollar. | That is one dollar.
C'est vingt-cinq cents. | It is 25 cents.
Ça fait dix-neuf dollars quatre-vingt-dix-neuf. | That is $19.99.
```

*Dollar* takes an *s* from two on: *deux dollars*. In casual Quebec speech, *une piasse* means a dollar, and it is common in speech but not in writing.

```fr
Ça coûte cinq piasses. {qc} | It costs five bucks.
```

## Days and months

Days and months are written with a lowercase first letter in French.

| Days | Months | |
|---|---|---|
| *lundi* | *janvier* | *juillet* |
| *mardi* | *février* | *août* |
| *mercredi* | *mars* | *septembre* |
| *jeudi* | *avril* | *octobre* |
| *vendredi* | *mai* | *novembre* |
| *samedi* | *juin* | *décembre* |
| *dimanche* | | |

A day without an article means one day, this coming one: *lundi, j'ai un rendez-vous* (this Monday). With *le* it means every week: *le lundi, je travaille à la maison* (on Mondays).

```fr
Lundi, j'ai un rendez-vous. | On Monday (this coming one), I have an appointment.
Le lundi, je travaille à la maison. | On Mondays, I work at home.
Je pars en juillet. | I am leaving in July.
```

## Dates

The order is day, month, year. Use *le* before the date. The first of the month takes the ordinal *premier* (written 1er), and the other days take a plain number.

| English | French | Said |
|---|---|---|
| July 1 | le 1er juillet | *le premier juillet* |
| March 5, 2027 | le 5 mars 2027 | *le cinq mars deux mille vingt-sept* |
| December 31 | le 31 décembre | *le trente et un décembre* |

```fr
Le premier juillet, c'est la fête du Canada. | July 1 is Canada Day.
Mon rendez-vous est le cinq mars deux mille vingt-sept. | My appointment is on March 5, 2027.
Elle est née le trente et un décembre. | She was born on December 31.
```

All-digit dates are risky: 05/03/2027 is March 5 in French, but May 3 in the United States. The international form is year, month, day: 2027-03-05.

### Years

Say a year as a whole number. In writing, there is no space in a year: 1999, 2027. In speech, a year such as 1999 is also said in hundreds (*dix-neuf cent quatre-vingt-dix-neuf*); both are heard.

```fr
En deux mille vingt-sept, je serai à Montréal. | In 2027, I will be in Montreal.
Il est arrivé en mille neuf cent quatre-vingt-dix-neuf. | He arrived in 1999.
```

## Times

In writing, the hour and minutes are separated by *h* (for *heure*), with a space on each side: 14 h 30. There is no period after *h*. Say the number of hours, *heures*, then the minutes. The 24-hour form is used on schedules, tickets and in writing.

| Written | Said, 24-hour |
|---|---|
| 8 h | *huit heures* |
| 14 h | *quatorze heures* |
| 14 h 30 | *quatorze heures trente* |
| 18 h 45 | *dix-huit heures quarante-cinq* |
| 0 h | *minuit* |

In casual speech, the 12-hour clock is common. You add *du matin*, *de l'après-midi* or *du soir* if the time is not clear. The halves and quarters have special words.

| Time | Casual 12-hour French |
|---|---|
| 1:00 | *une heure* |
| 2:15 | *deux heures et quart* |
| 2:30 | *deux heures et demie* |
| 2:45 | *trois heures moins le quart* |
| 2:50 | *trois heures moins dix* |
| 12:00 | *midi* |
| 12:30 | *midi et demi* |

```fr
Le train part à quatorze heures trente. | The train leaves at 14:30.
Le rendez-vous est à deux heures et demie de l'après-midi. | The appointment is at 2:30 in the afternoon.
On se voit à huit heures du matin. | We will meet at 8 in the morning.
Le magasin ferme à dix-huit heures quarante-cinq. | The store closes at 18:45.
Il est midi et demi. | It is 12:30.
```

*Une heure* is singular, and *heures* takes an *s* from two on. For writing minutes under 10, see [the OQLF page on writing the time](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/21516/la-typographie/nombres/ecriture-des-heures).

## Phone numbers

A North American number has an area code, then 3 digits, then 4: 514 555-1234. In speech you will hear it digit by digit, and you will also hear the last four digits read as two numbers (*douze trente-quatre*). Both are heard, so be ready for either. When you say your own number, digit by digit is the safest.

```fr
Mon numéro, c'est le cinq-un-quatre, cinq-cinq-cinq, un-deux-trois-quatre. | My number is 514 555-1234.
Mon numéro, c'est le cinq-un-quatre, cinq-cinq-cinq, douze trente-quatre. | My number is 514 555-1234 (last four in pairs).
```

A toll-free number begins with 1 800: *un huit cent*.
